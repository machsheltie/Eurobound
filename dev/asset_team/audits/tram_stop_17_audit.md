## Q1, how well does it represent the location? Score: 3/5
Painted: a shelter, a bench, a "TRAMVAJ 17" sign, two framed boards, pigeons, tracks, trees. That is the doc's visit-1 state (l.49, "neat and functional"). But the doc's identity is a stop that is a shrine in waiting, with "pigeons everywhere" (l.44), "rusted edges" (l.41), a timetable "yellowed paper behind cracked plastic... dated 1994" (l.42), a route map faded except the highlighted #17 (l.43), and #17 tracks "visibly less worn" (l.45). The plate has nice clean boards with a legible-looking map and no cracked 1994 date, no obsessive highlighting, no Jiri, no shrine hints. The tracks are all equally weathered.

## Q2, how well does it lean into the design requirements? Score: 2/5
Palette (l.56-58): Tram Green #2E8B57 is a muted verdigris on the frame only. Gray metal #708090 is present. Shrine Candle Orange #FF8C00 appears only as lit-board glow at night. Cobblestone Tan #D2B48C dominates the ground and Sky Blue #87CEEB is barely visible. Satire: effectively none. This is a charming, respectable European tram shelter, exactly the "Prague it up" result the author rejected. Nothing in the image says futile persistence, sunk cost, or a city that absorbed a man as an attraction. The doc is a sincere tragedy rather than a debauchery joke, but the 47,000-entry obsession, the pigeons and the 1994 schedule are all visual jokes the plate leaves out.

## Verdict: REDO (exterior; no interior needed)
