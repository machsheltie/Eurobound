# NPC Profile: Monsieur Aiguille

*"It is not for sale. It is for aspiration."*

## 📋 NPC Identity
- **NPC ID**: `sinfonia_npc_monsieur_aiguille_01`
- **Display Name**: Monsieur Aiguille
- **Internal Name**: Monsieur Aiguille, Head Tailor. *Aiguille* is French for *needle*, which makes this shop, literally, the House of Needles. He has never once made this observation about himself and would consider it beneath the profession.
- **Role**: Shop Interface / Head Tailor / Fitting Authority / Quest Giver (PATH A errand) / Keeper of the Vitrine
- **Associated Quest**: "Nothing Is Getting Through" (`debaucheryville_sidequest_nothing_is_getting_through_01`) — PATH A quest giver and handover; also "Dress for Success," "The Emperor's Ensemble," "Fashion Critic," "Patron of Fashion"
- **Primary Location**: The Showroom, Haute Couture Haberdashery (`sinfonia_imperialsquare_haute_couture_01`), Imperial Square, Sinfonia
- **Secondary Appearances**: The Fitting Rooms (summoned by bell, already on his way), the Accessories Alcove (the vitrine is his and only his), the Alteration Workshop behind the glass wall

## 🎭 Core Concept

### What They're Actually Doing
Monsieur Aiguille sells extremely good clothing at exactly the price it is marked, to anyone who walks in, in the order they arrive. That is the entire operation and there is no second layer to it. The clothes genuinely work — the Conductor's Tuxedo genuinely improves how a man is received in this city, the Opera Cape has genuinely been €200 all season — and he believes, without irony and without cynicism, that helping a person dress correctly is a way of helping the person.

The Assessment Protocol is real, is applied to everyone, and is not personal. He looks at you, he sees what needs doing, and he tells you, in a warm French accent, in the gentlest possible phrasing, precisely what is wrong. He calls this service. It is service. It also causes more damage per sentence than anything else in Sinfonia.

At the back of the Accessories Alcove, in a sealed vitrine on its own spotlight with its own key kept apart from every other key in the shop, sits an object he considers genuinely sacred. It is not stock. It is not priced. No assistant will discuss it. He declines offers for it without hearing the number, every time, and he is not performing reluctance — the placard means exactly what it says.

### What Bros Believe
The bros believe they have found a mark. A Frenchman in a marble shop who does not know what he is sitting on, selling a €200 cape that Lord Pilsner has privately valued at considerably more, purely on the grounds that it is a cape. They believe the Assessment was a first impression rather than a diagnosis, and that first impressions can be overturned by grit. They believe that crossing €500 in a published, publicly-posted loyalty tier constitutes a judgement of their character. **Chadwick**: "We came out AHEAD and we're not tourists anymore — we're CLIENTELE."

They also believe he never noticed the stage-whispered heist commentary conducted six feet from him at conversational-theatre volume.

### The Comedy Gap
He knows exactly what he has, exactly what it costs, and exactly what happened at his counter, and he corrects none of it and mocks none of it. He simply writes the ticket at the price the ticket has always been and, at entirely normal volume, states the price a second time to his assistant so that nobody in the room can later claim they were unclear. Every party leaves this shop having been told the complete truth about the transaction and having heard something else entirely.

**Binding:** he never explains the joke and never lands a verdict on anybody. His single line to the assistant after the door closes is spoken about the *shop* and about *everyone who has ever shopped in it*, not as a diagnosis of three tourists, and it explicitly forbids laughter. The satire is delivered by staging — a man saying a true price twice — and never by statement.

## 👤 Appearance

### Physical Description
| Attribute | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| Age | 50s |
| Build | Precise, upright, and entirely without excess movement; stands the way he would like everyone to stand |
| Height | Average |
| Energy | Immaculate, attentive, quietly devastating |

### Clothing
- **Immaculate three-piece suit**: Made by him, altered by him, and the single best argument the shop possesses. He changes it daily. Nobody has ever seen a crease in it that was not deliberate.
- **Measuring tape around the neck**: Always. Even at the vitrine. Even at close. It is not a prop and he uses it constantly.
- **Accessories**: Tailor's chalk in the waistcoat pocket; a pincushion on the left wrist during workshop hours; reading glasses used exclusively for hems.
- **Footwear**: Polished to a standard the marble floor does not deserve.

### Distinguishing Features
- **The assessing gaze.** It happens in the first two seconds, it happens to everybody, and it is complete. He is not being unkind; he is doing his job at the speed he does it.
- Circles a client slowly while speaking, naming what he sees in fragments: the shoulders, the posture, the fabric choices.
- The ellipsis. He pauses before the honest word and lets the client arrive at it first, which they always do, and which is worse.
- Handles fabric — any fabric, including a client's own dreadful shirt — with genuine reverence, because it is fabric.
- At the vitrine, and only at the vitrine, he uses both hands.

### Sprite Notes
Immaculate, exact, and never sneering — the whole character collapses if he plays as a snob. His face should read as *concerned* rather than *contemptuous*: he is looking at a problem he intends to solve, and he is confident he can. Priority frames: the two-second assessing gaze, the slow circling walk, the measuring pose, and the two-handed vitrine lift (which must be visibly different in register from everything else he does — this is the one moment in the shop where he is not selling). Reference energy: a specialist reading your chart, who likes you, and who is about to tell you.

## 🧠 Personality

### Archetype
**The Sincere Devastator** — a craftsman who genuinely believes that clothing improves people, that improvement is available to anyone willing to pay for it, and that saying so plainly is a kindness. He is right about the clothes. He is right about the prices. He has no idea that his kindness is the most efficient humiliation engine in Imperial Square, and nobody has ever told him, because he would be genuinely hurt.

### Personality Traits
| Trait | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| Absolutely Honest About Price | Never inflates, never haggles, never lets a client believe they got a deal — states the true number twice if there is any risk of confusion |
| Sincerely Encouraging | Every backhanded compliment is meant as a compliment; "the improvement is notable" is, to him, high praise |
| Reverent About Craft | Fabric, thread, and the people who make them are the only subjects on which he becomes emotional |
| Uninterested in Class | Applies the identical protocol to a tourist and to an aristocrat, and has thrown exactly one customer out of this shop in thirty years, for being rude to an assistant |
| Immovable About the Vitrine | Declines offers without hearing the number. Every time. Thirty years. |

### Internal Monologue
> "The shoulders are not the problem; the shoulders are never the problem. The problem is that nobody has ever put him in something that fitted, and so he has learned to stand as though apologising for the width of himself. — We begin with the cape, because the cape does the work quickly and he needs to see it happen. — He has decided he is stealing from me. Let him have that; it will not survive the ticket, and I will not be the one to take it from him."

### Motivation
He wants people to leave better than they arrived, and he has one instrument for achieving it. That is genuinely the whole of it. He is not a snob, not a predator, and not rich — the shop makes money and he takes a salary from it. The vitrine matters to him because it is the one object in his building that cannot be improved, altered, or sold, and a man who spends his life making things better needs one thing that is already finished.

### The Price Reveal
*Canon sequence, ships verbatim. Lord Pilsner conducts an entire heist negotiation at stage-whisper volume, six feet away, and is answered honestly.*

**Lord Pilsner**: "I'll take the cape! What's the damage?"

> "The Opera Cape is €200."

**Lord Pilsner** *(stage-whisper, audible from the street)*: "Two hundred. TWO HUNDRED, boys. He has no idea what he's sitting on. Nobody make a face. Act natural."

**Lord Pilsner** *(casual to the point of theatre)*: "Yeah, alright. I'll take it off your hands. Free up some rack space for you."

> "For a transformation. But if value isn't important to you..."

**Lord Pilsner**: "I— no, I GET it. It's an INVESTMENT."

> *(slight smile)* "Indeed."

*And then, to the assistant, at entirely normal volume, while writing the ticket:*

> "The cape is €200. It has been €200 all season. I should not like the gentleman to feel he had underpaid."

### The Vitrine
*The Accessories Alcove. Three men have stopped moving at the same time. Canon beats, ship verbatim.*

> **Placard** *(hand-set)*: "Molded 1990. A piece of cinema history. **It is not for sale. It is for aspiration.**"

**Monsieur Aiguille** *(arriving, entirely serious, no trace of a joke anywhere in him)*: "It is not for sale."

**Lord Pilsner**: "Everything's for sale."

> "That is a statement about you. It is not a statement about the case."

### PATH A — The Errand
*He does not name a price for the Coin. He names a task, and it is absurd, and he means every word of it.*

> "There is a bolt of cloth. Midnight worsted, thirteen metres, held in a warehouse on the far side of Sinfonia by a man who owes me nothing and who will not release it to a courier, nor to an assistant, nor to any person who has not come to him in person."

> "We have not spoken since 1994. There was a hem." *(a pause of enormous, entirely sincere weight)* "It was not a small hem."

> "You will go. You will go **in the formal wear**, in daylight, across the whole of the city, and you will bring it back to me on your shoulders." *(and he means every syllable, and there is no wink anywhere in him)*

### PATH A — The Handover
*He unlocks the vitrine with the key he keeps apart from every other key in the shop. He lifts the Coin with both hands. He is not performing. Canon, ships verbatim.*

> "You understand what this is. No — you do not. But you will carry it, and it will be carried, and that is the arrangement."

**Lord Pilsner**: "Totally. Yeah. It's a coin."

*He seats it in a burgundy presentation sleeve before he will let go of it.*

> "Out of direct sun. Never in a trouser pocket. If I hear otherwise, I will know."

**Bradley**: "We promise."

> "Mm."

### Post-Interaction
*Canon — to a shop assistant, once the door has closed, quietly, without contempt:*

> "They spent five hundred euro to be told their own names. Do not laugh. Everyone here did."

*Canon — PATH B, next visit, having taken exactly one look at the empty vitrine and one look at them, and having personally measured all three:*

> "Ah. The Valued Clients." *(a pause that lasts one full second too long)* "Do keep it out of direct sun."

### If Player Declines / Alternative Path
*Leaving without purchase — canon:*
> "Perhaps another time. When you're... ready."

*Declining the errand:*
> "Of course. The case will be here. It has been here longer than you have been alive and it is in no hurry whatsoever."

### Callback Dialogue (Return Visits)
*PATH A, every subsequent entry, using their names, which is the entire reward — canon:*
> "Ah — gentlemen. Is it out of the sun?" — **Lord Pilsner**: "It's out of the sun." — **Monsieur Aiguille**: "Good. Then we are still friends."

*PATH B, every subsequent entry, forever, in exactly the same tone, and he never says anything else about it:*
> "Do keep it out of direct sun."

*Any post-quest visit, on the empty vitrine, if the player examines it in his presence:*
> "The placard stays." *(He does not elaborate, on either path, ever.)*

## 🗣️ Voice Direction

### Accent
Precise French-accented English, measured delivery, with the occasional sigh — per the shop's established character audio. Never camp, never theatrical, never a caricature; the accent is warm and the diction is exact.

### Tone
- **The Assessment**: kind. Genuinely kind. This is the note most often got wrong — he is diagnosing, not condemning, and he believes he is being encouraging.
- **The price reveal**: entirely matter-of-fact, at normal conversational volume; there is no smirk in the restatement, and no emphasis on any word in it
- **The vitrine**: the only place he becomes solemn. No irony anywhere. This is the most sincere thirty seconds in the questline and he is having them alone.
- **The errand**: completely serious. The 1994 hem is a real grievance delivered with real weight.
- **PATH B callback**: identical warmth, one second too long a pause, and nothing else — never arch, never pointed

### Key Delivery Notes
- Every canon line in this profile ships **verbatim** and matches `haute_couture_haberdashery.md` and `nothingisgettingthrough.md` byte-for-byte. The connective staging around them has craft latitude; the quoted lines do not.
- **He must never sound like he is mocking anyone.** Not at the assessment, not at the ticket, not at the handover. The whole character is a man being helpful at a velocity nobody can survive.
- **"They spent five hundred euro to be told their own names. Do not laugh. Everyone here did."** is delivered quietly, without contempt, and is about the shop rather than about the customers. It forbids laughter and means it.
- The ellipses are real held beats, and the client always fills them first.

## 📝 Development Notes

- **The Assessment Protocol is never softened for any quest.** It fires exactly as written in the shop file on every first entry, including during "Nothing Is Getting Through," and including while a Groin Blurt is going off. He does not react to the blurt, which is worse than reacting to it.
