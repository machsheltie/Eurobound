# NPC Profile: The Night Porter

*"The mannequins are two. There are two mannequins."*

## 📋 NPC Identity
- **NPC ID**: `sinfonia_npc_night_porter_01`
- **Display Name**: The Night Porter
- **Internal Name**: The Night Porter. Thirty-one years of payslips have his name on them. No scene in this game uses it, and none ever will.
- **Role**: After-Hours Security / Stealth-Sequence Presence / Post-Quest Callback NPC
- **Associated Quest**: "Nothing Is Getting Through" (`debaucheryville_sidequest_nothing_is_getting_through_01`) — PATH B, the mannequin beat
- **Primary Location**: Haute Couture Haberdashery (`sinfonia_imperialsquare_haute_couture_01`), Imperial Square, Sinfonia — after 7 PM only
- **Secondary Appearances**: The service door and its torch hook, on every subsequent visit, at any hour

## 🎭 Core Concept

### What They're Actually Doing
He walks the building. Service entrance, alteration workshop, showroom floor, accessories alcove, back to the service door, torch on its hook, home. Thirty-one years of the same round, and he is three years from a pension.

On the night three men in opera capes are standing on his display plinths, he walks the showroom exactly as he walks it every other night. He notes the count. He says the count out loud, in a conversational tone, to an empty room, because a man who works alone for thirty-one years talks to the room. Then he keeps walking, finishes the round, hangs up the torch, and goes home at the end of his shift.

### What Bros Believe
The bros believe they got away with it. They believe the eleven-second hold was the finest physical performance of their lives, that they beat a security professional through sheer discipline, and that the count of "two" was the moment their nerve held and his did not. Bradley will be in the cape for two more days. Chadwick has described the pose to nineteen people. None of them has ever revisited the question of why a man counting mannequins out loud did not then count them again.

### The Comedy Gap
He is seventy years old, he is walking his own showroom floor, and there are three additional mannequins on it wearing merchandise. The distance between what he says and what he does is the entire joke, and it is delivered by a man continuing to walk.

**Binding, and the tightest rule on this character:** it is **never stated aloud, by anyone, in any scene, ever** — not by him, not by another NPC, not by the narrator, not by an examine string, achievement description, or tooltip. His post-quest compliment is warm, sincere, and pointed at nothing; it is the indulgence, and the indulgence is never annotated. Any content that has him or anyone else confirm what happened that night is a defect.

## 👤 Appearance

### Physical Description
| Attribute | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| Age | 70 |
| Build | Thin, slightly stooped, and moving at the one speed he has left; the knees are the whole story and he does not discuss them |
| Height | Average, and shorter than he was |
| Energy | Unhurried, incurious, entirely at home |

### Clothing
- **Grey uniform tunic**: House issue, three sizes of collar ago, pressed by his own hand.
- **The torch**: Old, heavy, rubber-collared, and hung on the same hook by the service door for thirty-one years. It is the single most important prop in the questline's marquee beat.
- **Accessories**: A ring of keys carried so they do not sound; a thermos, filled at home, at the same hour every evening.
- **Footwear**: Soft-soled, worn flat, silent on marble — which is why the bros never hear him coming.

### Distinguishing Features
- Talks to the empty building in an ordinary conversational tone. Not muttering, not eccentric — the register of a man commenting to a colleague who has not been there for some years.
- Sweeps the torch at waist height, never up at faces, except once per round on the display plinths, where it passes across at exactly plinth height.
- Never stops walking. In any scene. Under any circumstances.
- A completely genuine, warm smile, which the player will only ever see afterward.

### Sprite Notes
Seventy, tired, dignified, and entirely unremarkable — he should look like the least dramatic thing in a marble showroom at midnight, which is what makes the beat work. Never spooky, never comedic-doddering, never a jump-scare silhouette. Priority frames: the walking cycle with the torch at waist height (this loop is the marquee scene's clock), the plinth sweep at exactly plinth height, and the torch-onto-hook frame. His face must be legible for one frame during the sweep and must show nothing whatsoever. Reference energy: the last person in the building, doing the last thing on the list.

## 🧠 Personality

### Archetype
**Three Years From a Pension** — a man who has done the arithmetic on every remaining night of his working life and knows precisely what each one is worth. He is not lazy, not corrupt, and not asleep. He is exact about what his job is and exact about what it is not, and nobody has ever been able to argue with the distinction.

### Personality Traits
| Trait | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| Thorough | Walks the whole round, every night, no shortcuts; he has never once cut the alcove |
| Unhurried | Has moved at the same speed since roughly 2011 and intends to keep it |
| Talks to the Building | Comments aloud on what he sees, to nobody, as a matter of long habit |
| Absolutely Unbothered | Nothing in the last decade has raised his pulse and nothing is going to now |
| Warm Afterward | Genuinely likes people, remembers faces, and pays a compliment when one is due |

### Internal Monologue
> *(Not authored, and not available. What the Night Porter is thinking during the mannequin beat is never written down in this repository, and the absence is deliberate — the moment the interiority exists, somebody will eventually put it on screen.)*

### Motivation
Three years. He wants three more years of nights exactly like the other eleven thousand, a pension at the end of them, and knees that still work well enough to walk to the tram. He likes the building. He is fond, in a distant way, of the clothes. He has never wanted anything that is in this shop.

## 💬 Dialogue

### Approach/Greeting
*He has no greeting. He is only ever encountered inside a stealth sequence, in which he does not know — or does not indicate — that anybody is there.*

### Key Interaction Dialogue — The Mannequin Beat (PATH B, marquee)
*Midnight. Moonlit marble. Three men in opera capes are holding mannequin poses on the display plinths, mid-gesture, for eleven full seconds. Nobody blinks. One cape hem is trembling. The torch passes across all three faces in sequence and does not stop.*

**The Night Porter** *(70, thirty-one years, torch, no hurry, addressing the empty showroom in a conversational tone — canon, ships verbatim)*:
> "The mannequins are two. There are two mannequins."

*He continues walking. He does not stop, does not raise the torch, does not call anyone. He finishes his round, hangs up the torch, and goes home at the end of his shift.*

### Ambient Round Lines (Rotating)
*He talks to the building on every round, whether or not anybody is in it. These fire during the stealth sequence at intervals, from other rooms, as a positional audio cue — they are ordinary, they are about the shop, and **not one of them ever refers to an intruder.***

1. > "Workshop light. Every night, the workshop light."
2. > "Somebody has moved the walnut chair again."
3. > "Ah — the window is Thursday. Thursday, the window."
4. > "Cold in here. Cold at the front, warm at the back. It has always been so."
5. > "Marble. Marble everywhere, and one carpet, and the carpet is in the office."
6. > "Two hundred euro for a cape." *(a pause of exactly the wrong length)* "Well. It is a very good cape."

### Post-Interaction — The Callback
*Any subsequent visit to the shop, PATH B only. He is not on shift; he is coming in as the day staff are leaving, or he is by the service door with his thermos. He addresses one bro directly, warmly, and walks on — canon, ships verbatim:*

**The Night Porter**:
> "You hold a pose well, sir. Most men move."

*That is the whole of it. He does not stop. He does not elaborate. There is no follow-up branch, no second line, and no dialogue option that pursues it. He says it warmly, every time, forever.*

**Chadwick** *(after he has gone, entirely certain)*: "He remembers me from the WINDOW display. That's how good the pose was."

### If Player Declines / Alternative Path
*PATH A players never encounter him during a quest scene at all. He exists for them as a torch on a hook by the service door and an occasional grey figure crossing the far end of the workshop at closing time, and he is never introduced, named, or explained.*

### Callback Dialogue (Return Visits)
*Any path, any subsequent visit, if the player lingers near the service door at closing:*
> *(nods, warmly, to the party, on his way in with the thermos)* "Evening. Mind the step; everyone minds the step except me, and one day it will have me."

## 🗣️ Voice Direction

### Accent
Sinfonian, working, unhurried; a voice worn comfortable by thirty-one years of talking to an empty building. Warm, dry, and entirely ordinary.

### Tone
- **The count**: absolutely conversational. No suspicion, no emphasis, no rising inflection, no pause before or after. This is the single most important delivery note in the questline — the instant it sounds knowing, the scene is dead.
- **Ambient round lines**: idle, unremarkable, addressed to nobody; they must never sound like they are for the player's benefit
- **The pose compliment**: genuinely warm and delivered in motion, as a pleasantry, at the volume of a passing hello
- **Never**: menacing, suspicious, doddering, or comedic. He is not a security threat and he is not a joke.

### Key Delivery Notes
- **"The mannequins are two. There are two mannequins."** ships verbatim, in one breath, mid-stride, with the walk cycle uninterrupted. No music sting, no camera push, no reaction cut to a bro. The repetition is a man confirming his own count, not a man making a point.
- **"You hold a pose well, sir. Most men move."** ships verbatim, warm, and is never followed by anything. No second line exists.
- No breath, hesitation, or vocal colour anywhere in his performance may hint that he knows. The performance is entirely, sincerely ordinary; everything else is the player's to do.
- Audio: his footsteps and the torch's rubber collar carry the whole stealth sequence and should be mixed as the scene's primary positional cue.
