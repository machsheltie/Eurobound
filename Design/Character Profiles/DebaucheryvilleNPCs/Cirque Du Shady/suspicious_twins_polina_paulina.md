# NPC Profile: The Suspicious Twins — Polina & Paulina

*"Dab it." / "He hasn't SAID it." / "Dab it anyway."*

## 📋 NPC Identity
- **NPC ID**: `debaucheryville_npc_polina_01` (Polina) — the one holding the card
- **NPC ID**: `debaucheryville_npc_paulina_01` (Paulina) — the one holding the marker
- **Display Name**: Suspicious Twins (Polina & Paulina)
- **Internal Name**: Polina & Paulina — sisters, thirteen months apart, dressed identically since they were nineteen because it is funnier and because it wins bets. The district has called them twins for so long that correcting it would cost them money
- **Role**: Couchsurf Hosts (paired) / Ambient Duo / Trinket Source / Keepers of American Tourist Bingo
- **Associated Quest**: Couchsurfing Chronicles — Debaucheryville entry #4; `debaucheryville_sidequest_nothing_is_getting_through_01` (rejection-system participants)

- **Secondary Appearances**: Their flat (couchsurf interior); the Three-Crown Keg Haus, where the bar flies remember Paulina and ask about her out loud

## 🎭 Core Concept

### What They're Actually Doing

They are running a bet. They have been running it for years. American Tourist Bingo is a real, laminated, wet-erase drinking game with a real card and real rules and a genuinely competitive local field, and Polina and Paulina are its reigning authorities. They attend the Cirque's knife-throwing rounds because tourists who volunteer for a knife act are the highest-yield squares on the board. Taking two of them home was a play. They compared notes over breakfast because comparing notes is how you settle the wager. Nobody was seduced; a card was filled.

### What Bros Believe

That they had, in Chadwick's canonical phrasing, "European twins, bro." That the argument they woke up to was two women fighting over them romantically. That FlagMan's system does not have a category for this level of success. Not one of the three registers the laminated card on the kitchen table, the marker, or the phrase "who won" being used in the strict competitive sense.

### The Comedy Gap

The bros think they had a threesome. The twins think they won a drinking game. **No one wins.** The entire gap is contained in one word — "won" — which is said out loud, in front of them, repeatedly, with a scoring implement in hand, and is heard as flattery. The satire targets the conquest-mythology of men who cannot conceive of being the object of anyone else's game.

## 👤 Appearance

### Physical Description
| Attribute | Description |
|-----------|--------------|
| Age | Late 20s (Polina) and late 20s (Paulina), which is exactly how they prefer it phrased |
| Build | Matched by choice, not by birth — same posture, same walk, deliberately synchronized |
| Height | Paulina is visibly two centimetres taller. Nobody has ever mentioned it. This is a running gag and must never be acknowledged in dialogue |
| Energy | Coordinated, entertained, keeping score |

### Clothing
- **Identical outfits, never quite**: The same outfit down to the last detail except for exactly one deliberate difference each night — a ring, a shoe colour, a hair clip. Regulars use it to tell them apart. Tourists never notice it exists
- **The card pocket**: Polina's jacket has an inside pocket sized precisely for a laminated A5 card. It is the most-used pocket either of them owns
- **Accessories**: Polina — the American Tourist Bingo card, laminated, corners rounded from years of handling. Paulina — the wet-erase marker, uncapped with her teeth, capped on her sleeve
- **Footwear**: Boots. Same boots. One pair has a different lace and that is the tell for tonight

### Distinguishing Features
- Finish each other's sentences, badly and on purpose, because the mistiming makes tourists lean in
- Look at each other before answering anything — every single time, without exception
- Polina taps the card. Paulina uncaps the marker. In that order. It is the closest thing they have to a catchphrase
- Both laugh at the same moment and neither laugh is at the joke you made

### Sprite Notes

Silhouette them as a *pair* — they should almost never be rendered alone, and the composition should read as one unit at thumbnail size. The single deliberate outfit difference should be visible at close zoom and invisible at distance; that is the whole art brief. Paulina's two-centimetre height advantage should be drawn accurately and never framed as a joke by the camera. Card and marker are hero props: readable, worn, obviously real objects. Do not glamorize or vamp them — they are two people at a circus who are having a much better night than you are.

## 🧠 Personality

### Archetype

**The Scorekeepers** — a two-person institution that treats an entire tourist district as a board game and has never once cheated at it, because cheating would ruin the season. They are not predatory and they are not cruel; they are competitive. Every person they meet is either a square or not a square, and being a square is not an insult, it is a category.

### Personality Traits
| Trait | Description |
|-------|--------------|
| Rule-Bound (Polina) | Insists on the letter of the card. If the square says a thing, the thing must be established, not assumed |
| Result-Bound (Paulina) | Insists on the spirit of the card. If everyone in the room knows, dab it |
| Perpetually Adjudicating | Roughly forty percent of their dialogue is the two of them arguing about whether a square counts |
| Genuinely Fond | They like tourists. Tourists are the season. There is real warmth under the scoring and it never reads as an apology |
| Immune To Flattery | Compliments are logged, not received. Several are squares |

### Internal Monologue

**Polina:**
> "He is a free space. He is not a *square*, he is a free space, and Paulina is going to try to claim him as three squares, and I am going to make her read the card out loud."

**Paulina:**
> "It's three squares. 'Mentions college.' 'Mentions the flag guy.' 'Says the word synergy.' He did all of it before we left the tent. I'm dabbing all three and she can appeal."

### Motivation

They are defending a title. American Tourist Bingo has a field, a season, and — per canon — a place in local drinking culture that predates the bros' entire trip. Polina wants the record to be clean. Paulina wants the win. Both want the Cirque's knife-throwing rounds to keep delivering volunteers, because volunteers are where the rare squares live.

## 💬 Dialogue

### Approach/Greeting

**`performance_circle`, immediately after the player clears the knife-throwing dodge round (canonical meet)**:

**Paulina** (already applauding, marker still in hand):
> "He *volunteered*. Polina. Polina, he volunteered for the knives."

**Polina** (card out, unhurried):
> "That's the corner square. That's been open since March."

### Key Interaction Dialogue

*The bros, arriving in the conversation approximately one full beat behind it.*

**Chadwick**: "So — are you two, like, from here? Because we're kind of cultural ambassadors. Unofficially."

*The twins look at each other. They always look at each other first.*

**Polina**: "Say the second word again."

**Chadwick**: "...Ambassadors?"

**Paulina** (uncapping the marker with her teeth):
> "Ambassadors. That's a square."

**Polina**: "That is *not* a square, that's 'diplomat.'"

**Paulina**: "It's the same square."

**Polina**: "It is one word off and you know it."

**Paulina** (to the bros, warmly, genuinely): "Come with us, we need a tiebreak."

### Morning Scene (Couchsurf, canonical)

*Their flat. Both of them are already up, at the kitchen table, arguing about who "won" you. The card is on the table between them. There was a bet. You were the prize.*

**Paulina**: "Four squares. I had four."

**Polina**: "You had three and a *guess*."

**Paulina**: "He said 'synergize.' I heard him say it in the taxi."

**Polina** (checking a second, older card she has kept for cross-reference):
> "...Fine. Four."

*They compare notes. They laugh. Neither of them looks up.*

**Paulina** (finally noticing he is awake, entirely friendly):
> "Morning! You were very good. There's bread."

### Post-Interaction
> **Polina**: "Thank you, genuinely. You were a strong card."
> **Paulina**: "You were a *great* card. Ignore her, she's still appealing."

### If Player Declines / Alternative Path
> **Polina**: "That's fine." *(card back in the pocket)*
> **Paulina**: "That's a square too, actually. 'Chickens out.' — Don't look at me like that, it's on the card, it's ALWAYS been on the card."

### Herp Rejection Scene (`the_herp` active)

*An infected bro approaches the twins at the `performance_circle`. They do not recoil, they do not laugh at him, and they do not say no — because saying no is not the procedure. Polina taps the card. Paulina uncaps the marker. In that order.*

**Polina** (finding the square without looking, because she knows where it is):
> "Bottom row. Third from the left."

*The square reads **HERPES**. It has been on the card since before the bros' plane landed. It is not a new square, it was not added for them, and the laminate around it is as worn as everything else.*

**Paulina** (marker hovering): "Dab it."

**Polina**: "He hasn't SAID it."

**Paulina**: "Look at him."

**Polina**: "The card says *establishes*. Establishing is a verbal standard. Ask him."

**Paulina** (turning, pleasantly, as though asking the time):
> "Sorry — is that herpes?"

**Bradley**: "It's poison ivy. There was DEFINITELY foliage at that beach party."

*A pause. The twins look at each other. They always look at each other first.*

**Paulina**: "Dab it."

**Polina**: "...Dab it."

*She dabs it. The row completes. Paulina raises the card over her head without a word. Somewhere behind them, at the Cirque bar, four people cheer for a reason the bros will never learn, and someone buys a round.*

**Polina** (already walking, card back in the pocket, over her shoulder):
> "Thank you. Genuinely. That square has been open for two seasons."

*Bro delusion, immediately after:*
- **Bradley**: *"Bros. They CHEERED. We got a bar to cheer."*
- **Chadwick**: *"European twins raised a sign about us. That's basically a banner. That's a stadium moment."*
- **Lord Pilsner**: *"FlagMan's system doesn't even HAVE a flag for a crowd reaction. We're off the chart. Again."*

### Post-Cure Callback (Coin equipped)

*He returns to the `performance_circle` with `item_gold_circle_coin`. Polina taps the card out of pure reflex. Paulina does not uncap the marker.*

**Paulina** (looking at the Coin, then at the card, then at Polina):
> "There's no square for that."

**Polina**: "There is not."

**Paulina**: "Do we make a square for that?"

**Polina** (flat, final, card going back into the pocket):
> "We do not make a square for him. He does not get a *square*, Paulina, he gets to be the reason the bottom row is retired."

*She holds the card up so he can see it. The bottom-row square is dabbed. The dab is permanent — it went on with the wrong marker, months ago, and it never came off.*

**Polina**:
> "Congratulations on the — whatever that is. It's beautiful. You're still on the card. You will always be on the card. That's how laminate works."

**Paulina** (genuinely delighted, capping the marker on her sleeve):
> "See you at the knives!"

### Callback Dialogue (Return Visits)
> **Paulina**: "The card's behind the bar now. Framed. There's a little card next to the card explaining the card."
> **Polina**: "It does not name you."
> **Paulina**: "It describes you *thoroughly*."

## 🗣️ Voice Direction

### Accent

Local Debaucheryville, matched deliberately — they have spent a decade sounding like each other and it is a bit, not a birth fact. Fluent, quick English used mostly for adjudication. Any local-language exchanges between them should never be subtitled; the bros are not entitled to the ruling.

### Tone
- Polina: precise, procedural, mock-severe; the register of a referee who loves the rulebook
- Paulina: warm, fast, opportunistic; she is having the best night of anyone present
- Together: overlapping, interrupting, and completely comfortable — they are not performing for the tourist, the tourist is watching a private league match
- Herp rejection: entirely procedural, no disgust, no cruelty. The horror for the bros is that it is *administrative*

### Key Delivery Notes
- The word "won" in the morning scene must be said in the strict competitive sense with zero romantic colour — it is the whole joke and it dies if it is played coy
- "Sorry — is that herpes?" is asked in the tone of asking the time. Politely. No emphasis on any word
- The mandatory look-at-each-other before every response must survive VO direction; it is their defining beat
- Never direct either of them as seductive or predatory. They are competitors, and the bros are equipment
