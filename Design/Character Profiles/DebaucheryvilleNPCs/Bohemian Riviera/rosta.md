*"Three today. Personal best is five. I keep a chart."*

## 🎭 Core Concept

### What They're Actually Doing
Rosťa works the riverbank at Fauxst Beach with a boat hook, and has for eleven seasons. When the catapult fires, a portable toilet enters The Bacchanus, drifts, and Rosťa retrieves it. He walks it to the bank, empties it, tips it, and stands it upright on the embankment, where it stays, because a thing that stands up stays standing. That is the entire job. He is good at it in the specific, unglamorous way a person becomes good at anything done several thousand times: he knows the current, he knows which units float door-up and which float door-down, and he knows that the hook goes under the vent flange rather than the handle because the handle comes off. He keeps a chart. The chart is a paper tally of units retrieved per day, kept in a plastic sleeve, running since Season 11. His personal best is five in one afternoon, set in Season 9 — before the chart, which is why the chart exists. He is not sad about any of this and would find the suggestion that he might be genuinely puzzling.

### What Bros Believe
The bros do not see a riverbank worker. They see a groundskeeper. Somewhere between the splash and the walk of shame, the port-o-potty monuments stop reading as retrieved litter and start reading as *installed*, and the man installing them becomes, in the bros' account of the afternoon, a curator — a keeper of the beach's records, quietly maintaining a permanent exhibit of the men who have flown. Chadwick is confident the chart is a leaderboard. Lord Pilsner asks, more than once and with total sincerity, whether the units are arranged in order. Bradley, who has been standing closest to the actual chart the entire time, does not mention that the numbers only go up to five.

### The Comedy Gap
Rosťa answers every question he is asked, plainly, and no answer lands. He tells them the number. He tells them the personal best. He tells them, when asked, exactly what happens to a unit after he stands it up, which is nothing — it stands there, and then it keeps standing there, and by Day 6 someone's laundry is on it. He never once dresses any of it up, and the bros never once hear it undressed. He is the least invested person on the riverbank and he ends the day with an accurate count of what happened; they are the most invested people on the riverbank and they leave believing they've been archived.

### Clothing
- **Chest waders, rolled down to the knee**: Rolled down because the water rarely justifies rolling them up, and rolled down permanently because rolling them up and back down twice a day is more work than getting wet
- **A shirt that has been in the river**: Not once — routinely, for years; the color it started as is no longer recoverable and he does not appear to have noticed it changing
- **Accessories**: The boat hook, which never leaves his hand in any pose, standing, walking, or talking; the chart in its plastic sleeve, clipped inside the waders at the hip
- **Footwear**: The waders are the footwear; there is no separate shoe and there has never needed to be one

### Distinguishing Features
- Permanently damp from mid-thigh down, in every state, in every weather, at every hour — this is a continuity constant, not a launch-day condition
- Hooks under the vent flange, never the handle, in every retrieval animation — a small, correct, professional detail
- Sets the unit down, checks that it stands, and takes one step back to confirm it before turning away; the step back is the tell that he has done this thousands of times
- Looks at the water rather than at whoever is talking to him, without any of it reading as rudeness

### Sprite Notes
Read him as a man doing the twelve-thousandth repetition of a task he solved in Season 3. Everything in his posture should be economical — no wasted motion, no strain, no reaction to the smell, no reaction to the crowd. The boat hook is in-frame in every pose including idle and conversation; it is effectively part of his silhouette. Reference: a lock keeper on a working canal, or a beach lifeguard's exact opposite number — someone whose job also happens at the water's edge and actually happens.

## 🧠 Personality

### Archetype
**The Man With The Chart** — an entirely competent, entirely unbothered riverbank worker who has organized a genuinely absurd job into a routine, a number, and a record, and who experiences none of the absurdity because he is the one doing it correctly. He is not weary, not bitter, and not secretly philosophical about the river. He is a man at work who will answer your question and then get back to it.

### Personality Traits
| Trait | Description |
|-------|--------------|
| Utterly Unbothered | Eleven seasons of the same task; the launch, the splash, the smell, and the crowd are all just weather to him |
| Mildly Competent, Precisely | Brings the same accurate, unhurried skill to each retrieval that a mail carrier brings to a route — solved, repeatable, done right |
| Keeps A Chart | Tracks daily retrievals on paper in a plastic sleeve; the record is honest, the record is small, and he will show it to anyone who asks |
| Flatly Truthful | Answers exactly what he was asked with exactly what is true, at conversational volume, without ever once selling it |
| Immune To Being Impressed | Has watched eleven seasons of men fly into a river; the count is the only part of it he retains |

### Internal Monologue
> "Door-down. That one'll fill. — I can hook it before the bend if I go now, or I let it run to the shallows and walk out to it in ten minutes and stay dry. Ten minutes. — Two today. Two is a Tuesday."

### Motivation
He is paid to keep the riverbank clear and the units off the water, and he does that. Underneath it, the chart: not ambition, not a grievance, just the small, real satisfaction of a man who found a way to make a shapeless job into a number that goes up and down and can be compared to last week. He set five in Season 9 without a chart and could not prove it, which bothered him exactly enough to start writing it down, and has not bothered him since.

## 💬 Dialogue

### Approach/Greeting
**Approached at the retrieval station on any filming day, without looking up from the water**:
> "Three today. Personal best is five. I keep a chart."

**If the player stays put after that** *(still watching the water)*:
> "You get used to the smell. You do not get used to the door latch."

### Key Interaction Dialogue
*Mid-retrieval, hooking a drifting unit under the vent flange. He talks while he works and does not stop working to do it.*

**Chadwick**: "So you, what — you *curate* these? Like a — that's a permanent installation, that's what that is."

**Rosťa** *(walking the unit in, evenly)*:
> "They stand up. That's the whole trick. If they stand up, they stay."

**Lord Pilsner** *(indicating the embankment line)*: "And they're in *order*, right? Chronological? Tell me they're chronological."

**Rosťa** *(setting the unit down, checking it, stepping back)*:
> "They're where the bank is flat."

### If the Player Asks To See the Chart
*He unclips the sleeve, holds it out at arm's length, and lets the player take as long as they want with it. There is nothing on it but dates and single digits.*

> "Season nine I had five in an afternoon. Weather helped. Nobody wrote it down, so it's not on there. That's why there's a chart."

### If the Player Offers To Help
> "No. You'd fall in, and then I'd have two things."

### Post-Interaction
*Turning back to the water, the conversation over from his end the moment it stopped:*
> "If yours went in door-down, it'll come up past the bend. Give it an hour."

### If Player Declines / Alternative Path
*The player walks off without engaging. He does not look up.*
> "Mm."

### Callback Dialogue (Return Visits)
*Later in the game, at the monument line, boat hook in hand:*
> "Oh. You. — Yours is the third one along. Somebody put a towel on it. I left the towel."

### Hidden Details / Easter Eggs
- **The chart is examinable.** Dates down the left, single digits down the right, in the same pen since Season 11. No fours. Two fives, both crossed out and rewritten as threes with no explanation anywhere.
- **The towel.** From roughly Day 6, a towel appears on the third monument along and is never removed. If the player examines it on a later visit, it has been joined by a second towel and a sock.
- **The door latch.** Examining the center unit's broken latch at catapult row and then speaking to Rosťa in the same visit gets his second canon line unprompted, delivered exactly as flatly as the first.
- **He counts the player's launch.** The number in his greeting is live — it reflects actual units in the river that day, including the party's, and a player who watches all three staggered launches will hear the number climb across a single taping.

## 🗣️ Voice Direction

### Accent
Local Central European English, unhurried and worn smooth — a working voice, not a performing one. Every sentence lands at the same volume, including the ones about the smell.

### Tone
- **Reporting the count**: the exact register of a man reading his own handwriting back to himself; no pride, no complaint
- **Answering a bro's question**: flat, complete, and finished the moment the answer is finished — no follow-up, no upsell, no invitation to keep talking
- **Mid-retrieval**: talking around work, breath in the wrong places because his arms are busy; the work never pauses for the sentence
- **The callback line**: identical to the greeting; he does not warm up across the game and must not be voiced as if he does

### Key Delivery Notes
- "I keep a chart" is never a punchline on his end — it is offered as useful context, sincerely, and any hint of a wink in the read breaks the character
- The two canon barks ("Three today. Personal best is five. I keep a chart." / "You get used to the smell. You do not get used to the door latch.") ship close to verbatim and are delivered at the same energy as each other
- Never raise, never sigh, never trail off — he is not tired, he is working
- Voice acting priority: lock the flat working baseline first; every other line calibrates against the chart line

## 📝 Development Notes

- **The chart count is live.** It reflects actual units in the river that day, the party's included. This is a small state read, not a static string, and it is the character's single load-bearing implementation detail.
- **Boat hook in every pose.** `rosta_retrieval_man.png` (256×192) ships three poses — `idle_at_station`, `retrieval_action`, `monument_placement` — and the hook is in-frame in all three, per the Bohemian Riviera sprite mapping.
- **Hook under the vent flange, not the handle.** Animation note, not flavor text: the retrieval animation must show the correct hold, because the incorrect one would make him bad at his job and he is not.
- **He is absent from the night party zone.** This is deliberate — the unstaffed retrieval station is the location's signal that the monuments are seating now. Do not add a night-rave Rosťa variant in a later pass.
- **Never explains the monuments.** He answers what he is asked, with a fact, and stops. Any later pass that gives him a line acknowledging what the bros think the monuments are breaks him.
- **He is competent, not comic relief.** The satire is entirely the bros' upgrade of a riverbank worker into a curator; Rosťa himself is skilled, paid, unbothered, and correct about everything he says.
- **Eleven seasons is canon** (Bohemian Riviera NPC JSON, `bohemian_riviera.md`) against the show's twenty-two — he did not start with the show and this gap is not an error to reconcile.
