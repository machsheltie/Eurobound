# Venustempel (Venus Temple)

*Where Victorian curiosity meets modern awkwardness — the world's first sex museum, still kicking since 1985*

## Location Identity
- **Location ID**: shamsterdam_roodelicht_venustempel_01
- **Display Name**: Venustempel
- **English Translation**: "Venus Temple"
- **Satirical Subtitle**: "Giggling Since 1985"
- **World Location**: Shamsterdam
- **Area Zone**: Roodelicht Row, directly on the main tourist corridor
- **Location Type**: Historical Sex Museum / Mechanical Curiosity Collection / Group Photo Disaster Zone

## Access & Requirements
### Entry Conditions
- **Time Restrictions**: 10 AM - 11 PM (extended tourist hours)
- **Stat Requirements**: None (maturity stat would help, not required)
- **Item Requirements**: €5 entry fee (suspiciously cheap, explained by what's inside)
- **Previous Location Requirements**: None (often stumbled into by accident)
- **Special Conditions**: "18+" sign at door is more suggestion than enforcement

### Exit Conditions
- **Normal Exit**: Through cramped gift shop (unavoidable, naturally)
- **Completion Requirements**: No completion (survive the giggling figure, technically "complete")
- **Failure States**: Cannot be banned (they've seen worse reactions than yours)
- **Progressive Unlocks**: None (experience is consistent: enter curious, leave confused)

## Satirical Design
### Theme & Vibe
**Core Concept**: The world's first sex museum that's somehow both groundbreaking (1985!) and utterly dated (1985...). It exists in a strange space between genuine historical interest and tourist trap kitsch. The Victorian peep shows are legitimately fascinating, the John Lennon sketches are genuinely surprising, but it's all wrapped in a presentation that feels like a haunted house designed by someone's uncomfortable uncle. Tourists come expecting titillation, receive awkwardness, leave with photos they'll never show anyone.

### Cultural Targets
- **Primary Satirical Focus**: The gap between "edgy museum experience" expectations and "mechanical mannequin in a cramped canal house" reality
- **Secondary Targets**: Tourist photo-op culture, the commodification of "taboo," group dynamics around awkward content
- **Cultural Specificity**: Amsterdam's unique position as "city where this museum could exist," Dutch directness about sexuality
- **Humor Approach**: The comedy of groups trying to act cool about content nobody's actually cool about
- **Sensitivity Considerations**: Targets tourist behavior and museum kitsch, not human sexuality or legitimate sex education

### Seedy Underbelly Elements
- **Primary Vice**: Charging €5 for an experience people can't adequately describe later
- **City-Specific Comedy Operations**: Exploiting Amsterdam's reputation for content that's mostly Victorian machinery
- **Buffoon Exploitation**: Bros expect shocking, receive "is that John Lennon's signature on that?"
- **Comedy of Errors**: Group photos that will never be posted, reactions that can't be controlled
- **Consequence System**: Photos in inventory marked as "Unexplainable" — can't show to certain NPCs

### Buffoon Delusion Scenarios
**What bros THINK is happening**: Edgy, boundary-pushing museum experience befitting Amsterdam's reputation

**What is ACTUALLY happening**: Looking at 150-year-old flip-card machines and a mannequin that lifts its skirt on a timer

**How impaired are they**: Usually sober (it's daytime, it's €5, it's "cultural")

**Obvious signs they ignore**:
- €5 entry fee (that's suspiciously cheap for Amsterdam)
- The giggling mannequin is from 1985
- John Lennon sketches are the most interesting thing here
- Every photo op is designed for Instagram, none will be posted
- The gift shop sells things your grandmother would find quaint
- Multiple school groups have come through today (it's educational!)

**How locals play along**: Staff are completely immune to awkwardness, treat this as normal job

## Gameplay Mechanics
### Museum Navigation
- **Flow**: Ground floor → First floor → Second floor → Gift shop (no alternate routes)
- **Pacing**: Can't skip exhibits in narrow canal house, everyone sees everything
- **Interaction**: Most exhibits have examine option, some have photo option, one has flee option (The Giggling Figure, first encounter)

### The Giggling Figure Encounter
- **Trigger**: Motion sensor, automatic on approach
- **First Encounter**: Jump scare, -Composure temporarily, surprised dialogue
- **Second Encounter**: Photo opportunity, Chadwick INSISTS on filming
- **Mechanical Sound**: Clicking and whirring before activation (learn to anticipate)
- **Staff Reaction**: Completely deadpan, seen it 10,000 times

### Peep Show Devices (Interactive)
- **Cost**: €0.50 per viewing (coins at reception)
- **Content**: Victorian flip-card animations, surprisingly tame by modern standards
- **Effect**: +Vintage Knowledge status, +slight confusion about past generations
- **Bros' Reaction**: "People paid money for THIS? ...Put in another coin."

### The Photo Consequences System
- **Photos Taken**: Automatically collected in "Venus Temple Album"

- **Effect**: +Trivial Knowledge, unlocks dialogue option "Did you know John Lennon drew—"
- **Historical Accuracy**: Yes, he really did draw these (Google it later, the game's not lying)

### Bravado Interactions
- **Gain Bravado**: Maintain composure at Giggling Figure, discuss exhibits with academic detachment, correctly identify John Lennon piece
- **Lose Bravado**: Visible jump scare reaction, nervous laughter in group setting, ask staff "is this it?", photos leaked
