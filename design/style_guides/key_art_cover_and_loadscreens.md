## Shared doctrine (all covers)

**The poster is sincere.** Every cover is composed as though the artist believed these three men are heroes. Nothing is staged as a joke — no raised eyebrow, no winking prop placement, no arrow pointing at anything. The comedy is entirely in what the sincere composition includes and what the bros are doing inside it.

**They pose like they've seen the poster.** All three know they're being photographed and are performing for it. Chins up, chests out, weight on the front foot. They believe this is the one thing they're all genuinely good at.

**Never sexy, never cool, never competent.** Pilsner's silhouette is load-bearing: navy tee riding up over the gut, cargo shorts, white crew socks, black flip-flops. Chadwick's collar is popped and his hat is backward. Bradley's jersey is a size too small over a size too large torso. Camera at chest height — the height of a friend taking your picture. No rim light sculpts a jaw.

**Three figures, nothing else alive.** No mascot, no pet, no animal, no fourth party member. Background NPCs appear only where the concept requires a witness, and witnesses are mid-task, unimpressed, turned slightly away.

**No real place names, no real landmarks.** The world is built only from canon parody locations: Publandia, Debaucheryville, Sinfonia, Shamsterdam, Euromemeia, Doner Lane, Kebab Alley, Clocktower Plaza, the Astronomical Cock-Up Square clocktower, the Bassline Opera House, The Blarney Trap, The Hoisted Pint, The Wobbly Windmill Brewery, The Tolerance Hostel. Nothing in any cover is traceable to a real street.

**Real products keep their real names but carry no weight.** The Sidekick, the N-Gage, the pager, the Livestrong band are texture at 3–4 mm tall. No cover is built around a logo that isn't ours.

**The world is gorgeous; they are the anachronism.** HD-2D painterly diorama, tilt-shift, real light, soft focus at the edges. The bros are in crisp focus and belong to a different decade than the light falling on them.

**The bros never look at each other.** They look at the camera, the horizon, or their own phone. This trip is three men performing separately in the same photograph.

## The wordmark [PROPOSED]

One design for all covers and load screens. Heavy collegiate slab, all caps, tight kerning, slight forward lean. Chrome-bevel face with a dawn gradient running pink at the top to teal at the bottom — the exact colour of a sky at 05:40. Thin gold rim light along the upper edges only. The **final O is replaced by a bottle cap**, face-on: crimped edge, one prong bent, a dull highlight on the crown. It reads as a halo at a glance and as a bottle cap at a second glance, and those two readings never resolve. Set on a soft dark plate so it survives on any background. Played completely straight: this is what a 2003 game logo looked like, and the designer of it was not smiling.

## Cover 1 — "LAST CALL AT THE EDGE OF EUROMEMEIA"

**Format:** full-bleed panoramic, characters small in the lower third, sky doing most of the work. Classic JRPG box.

**Composition.** Chest-height camera, behind the three of them, wide. They stand on the parapet of The Tolerance Hostel's roof terrace — a satellite dish, a string of dead fairy lights, a sun-bleached plastic chair on its side, a laundry line. They occupy the bottom third, shoulders touching, arms around each other. Above them, two-thirds of sky, and on the horizon the four cities stacked in a single painterly composite: the clocktower left, the windmill and the leaning canal gable right, the opera dome centred and half-lit.

**The bros**
- **Pilsner** (centre): one black flip-flop up on the parapet, arm thrown out toward the horizon in a conqueror's gesture, the other arm around Chadwick. Frosted tips lifting in the wind, puka shells catching the light, Sidekick on his belt. His tee has ridden up. He is mid-yell — a man addressing a crowd that isn't there.
- **Chadwick** (left): ring-light mace held aloft like a torch, phone raised in the other hand filming the horizon, not the moment. Backward trucker hat, popped collar. He is looking at his phone screen, not the sunrise, and grinning.
- **Bradley** (right): double bicep flex, elbows out, jersey riding up, broken pool cue tucked under one arm like a standard. Livestrong band, pager. His face is doing the thing he does in the mirror: absolute conviction, zero result.

**The setting.** Dawn, 05:40. The hostel roof is cheap and real: tar paper, a vent stack, a shopping trolley on a lower roof. The four city silhouettes are warm and soft and slightly out of focus, and every window in them is dark except the ones that shouldn't be.

**Palette.** Peach and rose at the horizon, cooling to teal-navy at the top of frame. Warm key from behind, so the bros are rim-lit but their faces stay half-lit and ordinary. The wordmark's chrome picks up the same peach and teal. One acid-green accent, bottom left, from a kebab shutter's neon.

**The joke it lands.** The triumphant dawn is the end of the night, not the beginning of one. The light is beautiful because they never went to bed, and the only thing open in frame is a kebab stand whose shutter is coming up in the bottom-left corner, neon still on. They're saluting the sunrise like a beginning. It's a closing time.

**Why it lands for the audience.** Every woman who has watched a man toast himself at 5 a.m. for surviving a night she wouldn't have started recognizes the salute. He thinks it's the opening shot of the movie. She's the one who knows it's the closing credits.

**Thumbnail (Steam capsule).** The three silhouettes plus the gut-and-flip-flop shape read at 231×87 as "three guys, sky, logo." The sunrise gradient does the heavy lifting at every size. The gag is a full-size reward, not a thumbnail requirement — the correct way round for a box.

## Cover 2 — "THE ONLY PHOTO THEY'LL KEEP"

**Format:** diegetic. The cover *is* a photograph, cropped with the phone's own frame edges visible — a selfie taken at arm's length, slightly off-level, thumb in the corner.

**Composition.** Chadwick's arm enters from the bottom right, holding the phone. The three faces are jammed into the top two-thirds, the way a group selfie always is: Pilsner centre with his chin lifted to hide the jaw, Chadwick's hat brim cutting the top edge, Bradley leaning in from the left so only half his face makes it. Behind and below them, the actual location is still happening and mostly out of frame.

**What's in the frame that shouldn't be**
- The bottom left corner: a canal, a bicycle in it, a traffic cone standing on the bicycle.
- The bottom right, behind Chadwick's shoulder: a taxi door closing on someone.
- Extreme left edge, cut off at the eyelid: the face of a bouncer, arms folded, looking directly down the lens.
- Directly behind Pilsner's head, in soft focus, the leaning canal gable of The Hoisted Pint, lit up and gorgeous.

**The bros.** All three grinning. Genuinely, uncomplicatedly happy — this is the best moment of the trip and it's this one, the photo. Pilsner's thumb is up. Bradley's thumb is up. Chadwick's thumb is up. Nobody is looking at the same thing.

**Phone chrome.** Thin UI edges, played straight and small: a battery at 3%, a notification stack of 47, and one message preview from "Mom" reading "Are you eating." Unread. The time reads 05:41.

**Palette.** Warm saturated night: sodium orange, canal black-green, one magenta from a club sign behind. Faces the warmest thing in the shot, because the phone flash fired and flattened them — which is exactly why they love it.

**The joke it lands.** This is the picture that ends up on the fridge. Everything that actually happened is at the edges, cropped out, unfocused, or behind them — and they will never look at the edges. They'll look at themselves and remember it as a great trip, and they'll be right, and it will be worse than they think.

**Why it lands for the audience.** The audience has seen this exact photo on an ex's socials: three flushed thumbs-up, and in the corners the night actually happening to them. The men post it as proof of a legendary trip. She reads the edges like a police report.

**Thumbnail.** Three lit faces and a bright phone-flash warmth — reads instantly as a photograph, which no other game's capsule does. The wordmark sits top-left on a dark strip so it never fights the faces. The edges are a full-size reward. Per Aion's note: push the bouncer's eye and the closing taxi door far enough into frame to register as a "wrongness" cue even at capsule size.

## Cover 3 — "THE LAST STAND"

**Format.** Full-bleed action-movie poster. Characters large in the lower two-thirds, painted with total sincerity as heroes making a last stand.

**Composition.** Wide, chest-height camera, frozen mid-shot. The three bros stand back-to-back in a tight triangle at centre-frame — the classic surrounded-heroes layout from a thousand action posters. Weapons drawn: Pilsner grips a beer paddle two-handed like a war hammer; Bradley levels a broken pool cue like a lance; Chadwick rests his ring-light mace on one shoulder. Stances wide, chins up, faces set in absolute conviction. They are fully committed to a battle they believe is happening.

**What surrounds them.** The "army" is a patient, well-spaced ring of Euromemeia's service industry standing at a comfortable distance in a loose circle: two bouncers with arms folded, a barmaid with a mop, a paramedic leaning on the open door of an ambulance, a taxi driver beside his idling cab, a kebab-shop owner holding his shutter key, a street cleaner with a broom, a police officer with a clipboard. Nobody is attacking. Nobody is even moving. They are waiting, in the posture of people who have seen this exact scene before and know exactly how it ends. The only thing anyone in the ring is holding toward the bros is paperwork: receipts, incident forms, laminated notices, a bill.

**The bros**
- **Pilsner** (centre-front, facing camera): navy tee ridden up over the gut, cargo shorts, white crew socks, black flip-flops planted wide. Beer paddle held two-handed. Chin up, eyes locked on a middle distance where he believes the enemy is gathering. Frosted tips, puka shell, Sidekick on the belt. He is a war-chief. The tee says otherwise.
- **Bradley** (back-left, three-quarter): jersey stretched, baggy jeans, Livestrong band, pager clipped. Broken pool cue levelled, elbows out, a stance he learned from a movie. Pure aggression aimed at nothing.
- **Chadwick** (back-right, three-quarter): backward trucker hat, popped collar, ring-light mace on one shoulder, phone in the other hand filming the standoff. Not watching the "enemy" — checking his footage. Grinning. He believes the trailer is going viral.

**The setting.** A wet cobbled square at 03:00, warm sodium lamps and one green-white neon strip. Parody signage only: the leaning canal gable of The Hoisted Pint in the mid-ground, the Astronomical Cock-Up Square clocktower in soft focus far behind, a shuttered Kebab Alley to the right. The ground around the bros is littered with the aftermath — a tipped-over street food cart, one cracked window, a spilled pint, a bicycle on its side. Nothing on fire, nothing dramatic, everything small, mundane and entirely self-inflicted.

**Palette.** Warm sodium amber and deep slate-blue night, one acid-green neon accent. The bros are lit flatly from above by a single streetlamp — unflattering the way a real streetlamp is unflattering. The service ring stands slightly closer to the light than the bros do: the world is comfortable; the bros are in the cold spot.

**The joke it lands.** They are the last stand. The ring around them is not an army — it is everyone the city sent to deal with them, and nobody in it is going to fight. They are going to be billed.

**Why it lands for the audience.** This is the 3 a.m. tableau every woman in the audience has watched from a doorway: the man squaring up to destiny while destiny holds a mop and an incident form. He's doing Braveheart. The barmaid is doing payroll.

**Thumbnail read.** Three figures in a triangle, one warm-lit ring around them, wordmark above. At 231×87 it reads as "three bros, surrounded, action poster" — and the triangle-plus-still-ring silhouette reads as *something is wrong with this action poster* at every size, which is the joke doing its job before anyone zooms in.

# PART 3 — LOAD-SCREEN CONCEPTS

*Three concepts, kept from four submitted (Mistral L1–L3, Aion "The Walk of Shame"). Aion's Walk of Shame is folded in as a frame variant of L1 "3% Battery" rather than a fourth screen: both are morning-after screens built on the same engine (bros in full canon looks, real load bar, tip pool, cycling city dioramas), and shipping both as separate rotations would make the morning-after joke wear out twice as fast. The full Walk of Shame concept survives in `drafts/aion_loadscreen_concepts.md` if the author wants it as a fourth rotation.*

## Shared doctrine (all load screens)

**A load screen is not a cover.** It repeats hundreds of times and appears at the worst moments, so every load screen must have a **variation system** — tip pools, flipping board rows, cycling city dioramas, random late clock times — so the 400th sighting still has something new in it. A single-payoff image is a cover; a load screen is a small machine.

**The sincerity rule extends to the UI.** Nothing is staged as funny — no winking icon, no arrow, no "loading... probably" quip in the bar itself. The comedy lives in what the sincere machine contains. The UI may be diegetic — that is where a load screen can be funnier than a cover — but the diegesis is played straight.

**The bros are sincere doofuses even asleep.** Pilsner's look-B silhouette is load-bearing (gut, ridden-up navy tee, cargo shorts, crew socks, flip-flops); Chadwick: backward trucker hat, popped collar, chain wallet, N-Gage; Bradley: Livestrong band, jersey, baggy jeans, pager, broken pool cue. Chest-height camera, never sexy. No mascot, no animal, no fourth figure.

**Signage uses parody names only** (same canon list as Part 2). No real city, no real landmark, no recognizable silhouette of one.

**Wordmark** is the proposed chrome collegiate slab with bottle-cap O from Part 2 [PROPOSED — placement only, not a claim on the design], present on every load.

**Motion is bounded and optional.** Idle loops are 2–4 seconds, subtle, and every motion effect ships with a reduced-motion toggle, per the intoxication law's accessibility rule. The loading bar maps 1:1 to real load progress — never fake. If a load is instant, the bar flashes and the payoff beat still plays.

**Tips are real information with teeth, never lessons.** Info first, knife second. No moralizing, no PSA, no finger-wagging.

**Do not reuse the cover compositions.** No dawn roof, no selfie frame, no triangle stand.

## Load Screen 1 — "3% BATTERY" (the morning-after phone)

**Composition.** Full-bleed painterly diorama of a budget hostel room in Publandia at 14:37: grey daylight through a grimy window, two sleeping bags, a radiator, a puddle. The three bros are asleep on the floor in their full canon looks, in the poses sleep actually puts people in. Dominating the right two-thirds of frame, foreground, is Chadwick's N-Gage lying face-up on the floorboards — its screen is the load UI, glowing cyan against the dark room. The wordmark sits at the top of the phone screen, chrome slab on dark glass, bottle-cap O catching the room light.

**The bros (asleep, sincere even now)**
- **Pilsner**, on his back, one flip-flop still on, the other kicked off across the room; navy tee ridden up over the gut; Sidekick on his belt blinking. His mouth is open mid-snore — he is telling a story in his sleep.
- **Chadwick**, curled around the phone, one arm still holding it, backward hat over his eyes, ring-light mace beside him like a fallen standard. His thumb hovers over the screen.
- **Bradley**, asleep sitting up against the wall, slid halfway down into a wall-sit, pool cue across his lap, pager still clipped. He chose this. In his dream he's winning.

**The phone screen (the load UI).** Lock screen: time **14:37**, battery **3%**, 47 unread. The loading bar is a pint glass filling with foam as the game loads. Notifications pop in at the top of the screen one at a time — these are the tips — and every few seconds **Chadwick's sleeping thumb twitches and swipes the notification away, unread.**

**Background/setting.** Doner Lane is visible through the window, daylight, a shutter half-up, one pedestrian. Signage: Doner Lane only.

**Palette.** Dark navy-brown room, one amber lamp, cold cyan phone glow. The bros are lit by the phone — the only light in the room is the load screen.

**What moves.** Breathing (subtle chest rise). The pint-glass load bar fills with real progress; foam wobbles. Battery ticks 3% → 2% during the load. Chadwick's thumb twitch-swipes one notification per few seconds. Pilsner's snore is a 4-second loop. At 100%, the screen locks (a small padlock icon) — load done.

**Where the tip sits.** As notification banners on the phone screen, swiped away unread by the sleeping bro. The game hands you the tip and the bro throws it out without reading it.

**The one joke it lands.** You are loading the game on a device that is about to die, in a room where the only thing still running is the bros' group chat — and they are asleep. Every load is a hangover; the battery and the bros are at the same percentage.

**Why it lands for the audience.** She has stood in this room — or one like it — at 2 p.m., watching a grown man sleep like a crash-site survivor while his phone dies on the floor under forty unread messages. The phone is the only thing in the room with a plan for the day, and it's at 3%.

**Repetition system.** Tip-notification pool cycles randomly; sender names cycle (Mom, Tony420, The Group Chat, Unknown Number, ICQ: 88412093); the clock reads a different late-afternoon time per load (14:37 / 15:02 / 16:48); occasionally the battery starts at 2%. Sleeping poses are constant — that's what makes the variants land.

**Frame variant — THE WALK OF SHAME (Aion).** Same machine, exterior frame: the bros trudge a canal path at 07:52 in single file, last night's clothes, documentary side-on framing. Pilsner leads with a döner bag under one arm; Chadwick stops every few seconds to review footage, then hustles; Bradley drags three steps back, pool cue used as a walking stick, dropping one more step behind each loop so the column stretches over long loads. Diorama cycles by city (Doner Lane service alley / Clocktower Plaza outer ring / a Sinfonia footbridge with the Bassline Opera House dome behind / The Wobbly Windmill Brewery exterior, shutters half-up). Street cleaners work around them; a delivery cyclist overtakes them while looking at them. Same tip pool in a plain dialogue box above the same real bar. The save-and-restore ritual as the morning after: every load *is* the walk of shame, taken again.

**Glance read (<2 s).** Dark room, glowing phone, three lumps on the floor — or, in the variant, three figures single-file in morning light. Reads instantly as "the morning after."

## Load Screen 2 — "DELAYED — REASON: CREW" (the departure board)

**Composition.** A Euromemeia rail hub platform at night. The upper two-thirds of frame is a split-flap departure board, black with amber letters. The header row of the board *is* the wordmark: the flaps flip to spell **EUROBOUND** in the same chrome-on-dark treatment, rendered in amber flap text — the logo itself is a departure. The lower third is the wet platform, where the three bros stand under the board, shoulder to shoulder, thumbs up, looking up at it, posing for a holiday photo that the board is not taking.

**The bros**
- **Pilsner**, centre, thumb up, arm out along the board like a tour guide presenting a monument. Tee ridden up. He believes the board is showing *his* itinerary.
- **Chadwick**, left, filming the board (not the moment) on the N-Gage, hat backward, collar popped, grinning at the screen.
- **Bradley**, right, thumbs up with both hands, jersey stretched, pager on the belt. Absolute conviction, zero result.

**The board rows (the load UI).** Rows flip as the game loads, one row per ~25%:
- `PUBLANDIA ....... DELAYED 47 MIN`
- `DEBAUCHERYVILLE .. DELAYED - REASON: CREW`
- `SINFONIA ........ DELAYED - REASON: CREW`
- `SHAMSTERDAM ..... CANCELLED - REASON: CREW`
- `LAST CALL ....... ON TIME - PLATFORM 9`

The delay minutes count up while loading. Occasionally a row flips to `ARRIVED` for half a second, then back to `DELAYED`.

**Background/setting.** Wet platform, steel blue night, one fluorescent tube humming, the edge of a train that is not the one they're on. No real station, no real landmark; signage is parody only.

**Palette.** Black board, amber flap text, steel blue platform, cold white tube light. The bros are lit flat from above — the least flattering light in Euromemeia, and they are posing for it.

**What moves.** Split-flap letters animate with load progress (each row flip = a chunk of progress). Delay minutes tick up. Chadwick's N-Gage screen glows on his face. The fluorescent tube flickers once per loop. At 100%, the LAST CALL row flips to `BOARDING` and holds.

**Where the tip sits.** A scrolling ticker strip along the very bottom edge of the board, in the same amber flap font — tips read like station announcements.

**The one joke it lands.** The only thing in Euromemeia that runs on time is last call, and every train the bros take is delayed by the crew — the board files the indictment in the bros' own handwriting, and they're posing proudly underneath it.

**Why it lands for the audience.** Anyone who has ever waited on a platform for a man who swore the itinerary was handled knows this board. The whole continent has rescheduled around them; they're taking a picture of it for the group chat.

**Repetition system.** Row order and delay numbers randomize per load; extra rows cycle in from a pool (`KEBAB ALLEY .... OPEN`, `CLOCKTOWER PLAZA .. MEET AT MIDNIGHT`, `THE FLESH MARKET .. NO REFUNDS`, `BASSLINE OPERA ... BLACK TIE`, `THE BLARNEY TRAP .. 21+`, `DONER LANE ...... OPEN ALL NIGHT`, `WOBBLY WINDMILL .. TOURS AT NOON`, `TOLERANCE HOSTEL . CHECK-OUT WAS 10`). Tip ticker pool cycles. Poses constant.

**Glance read (<2 s).** Amber board on black, three thumbs. Reads as "transit, delayed, these guys" instantly.

## Load Screen 3 — "BEER GOGGLES BOOT" (the game loads drunk)

**Composition.** A painterly HD-2D diorama of Clocktower Plaza, Debaucheryville, midday — the clocktower (parody silhouette), the plaza, a Kebab Alley shutter. The three bros stand mid-pose in the square. The whole frame is rendered **through the Beer Goggles overlay** — the literal render layer already canon for tier 2 (`design/Quests/debaucheryville/nothingisgettingthrough.md`): rose tint, drifting hearts and sparkles, soft bloom, everyone beautiful. The wordmark floats top-centre, frosted and slightly blurred, as if seen through the same layer. At 100% load, the overlay **snaps off in one frame**, and the same scene is revealed flat, grey and unflattering.

**The bros (in the goggles, and after)**
- **Pilsner**, arm spread addressing the square, chin up, tee ridden up — through the goggles he is a conqueror; after the snap, he is a man shouting at a clocktower at noon.
- **Chadwick**, filming the crowd on the N-Gage, ring light on — through the goggles the crowd is his audience; after, it is three locals walking away.
- **Bradley**, double-bicep, pool cue over the shoulder — through the goggles he is the statue; after, the pose is just a pose.

**Background/setting.** One diorama per city, cycling across loads: Clocktower Plaza (Debaucheryville), Doner Lane (Publandia), Bassline Opera House forecourt (Sinfonia), The Wobbly Windmill Brewery exterior (Shamsterdam). Parody signage only.

**Palette.** While loading: hot rose, gold, pink bloom. After the snap: flat slate, grey daylight, sodium accents. The snap between the two palettes is the visual punchline.

**What moves.** The overlay pulses gently (bloom in/out, hearts drift upward, 3-second loop). The load bar is a stein filling, also under the overlay. At 100%: one-frame snap to clean render, logo sharpens, a single heart pops and dies. Reduced-motion mode: the overlay is a static tint only, and the snap is a crossfade — the joke survives without the motion.

**Where the tip sits.** A JRPG-style dialogue box along the bottom, plain UI, above the overlay — the one thing in frame that is *not* drunk. Reads clearly in both palettes.

**The one joke it lands.** The game itself can't load straight: it boots through Beer Goggles and sobers up exactly at 100%. The bros would do the reverse — they'd start sharp and end blurred — which is the whole trip in one load screen.

**Why it lands for the audience.** She knows exactly how the night got prettier from inside his head, because she watched him get worse-looking in real time while he got more confident. The screen shows his 11 p.m. eyes, then snaps to what she was actually looking at.

**Repetition system.** City diorama cycles (4); ~1 in 4 loads is a **clean load** — no overlay at all, grey and flat from frame one — so the goggled loads keep their snap. Tip pool cycles. Overlay intensity varies slightly per load. Poses constant. Production dependency: the clean/goggled distinction must survive very short loads — if a load is instant, the overlay beat must still read (minimum hold on the goggled frame), or the variation is invisible.

**Glance read (<2 s).** Pink haze + three silhouettes + logo. Instantly reads as "loading, drunk" — and the clean loads read as "loading, sober," which is funnier in context.
