## 🎭 Core Concept

### What He's Actually Doing
Running an off-licence and corner shop that stays open from 10 AM to 5 AM, stocking whatever a human being might conceivably want at three in the morning. He sells legitimate product at a 25-40% night markup and "alternatives" at a discount, and he prices the discount to exactly match the risk. Twenty-three years of doing this has given him a working actuarial model of human desperation. He is not hiding it. There is a laminated "NO REFUNDS" sign at eye level and a hand-written card on the door reading "Card machine broken (always)." He is the most honest man on the street and nobody notices, because honesty at 3 AM sounds like small talk.

### What Bros Believe
That they have discovered a hidden local gem where the drinks are cheap because Europeans just do things differently. That MurphyBoost™ is an authentic Irish energy brand they have never heard of because it hasn't broken into the American market yet. That the old fella behind the glass likes them. That 3 Sovs for a tall can is a *deal*, and the fact that Murphy said "strong though, very strong" while ringing it up was a compliment about their tolerance.

### The Comedy Gap
Murphy tells them, in plain language, exactly what will happen. He tells them the wine has been on that shelf since 2008. He tells them the sandwich is from "earlier — earlier today, probably." He tells them to keep water nearby. They hear all of it, thank him warmly, and buy three EXTREME. The next morning they come back grey-faced looking for a cure and buy the "Headache Cure" that gives them "Drowsy," and then buy a MurphyBoost to fix the Drowsy, and Murphy rings up both without comment because business is business. The bros will call this place "our spot" by day four. They will never once connect the shop to the feeling.

### The Hidden Weight
Murphy's father was a publican. His grandfather was a publican. Rory broke the line — he does not serve the night, he services the aftermath. He has watched this street gentrify, de-gentrify, and re-gentrify three times. Coffee shops opened and closed. A yoga studio lasted eleven months. Murphy's remained, because the one constant Rory ever identified is that human beings make poor decisions at 3 AM and would like a snack about it. He is not bitter and he is not proud. He keeps the lights on and the refrigerators running, and manages one of those two most nights.

### Clothing
- **The apron**: Canvas, once green, now a stain archaeology site 23 years deep. He has never explained any individual stain and has never been asked twice.
- **Shirt**: Collared, sleeves rolled to the elbow year-round regardless of season, because the shop temperature is set entirely by the refrigerators.
- **Accessories**: A biro behind the ear that he never uses; a set of keys on a length of string; reading glasses that live on the register and are put on only to read a price tag he already knows.
- **Footwear**: Soft-soled shoes, flattened at the heel from being stepped into rather than put on. He does not walk far. He walks the same nine feet.

### Distinguishing Features
- Watches the door from the moment it sticks until the moment it closes behind you. Not suspicious — attentive. Same expression either way.
- The "hmm." A single closed-mouth note delivered when he sees what you've put on the counter. He will not elaborate. It is the entire review.
- Folds his hands on the counter while you decide, and does not fill the silence.
- Reaches for the 1987 register by muscle memory without looking at the keys.
- A very slight forward lean — only ever deployed at sober customers. It is the whole warning system.

### Sprite Notes
Restraint is the direction. Murphy never emotes above a 3/10 — the shop does the screaming, with its flickering fluorescent tube, its uneven refrigerator hum, and its neon pop. Reference the sprite sheet's eight states (Counter Idle, Greeting Nod, Subtle Warning Lean, Ringing Up, Knowing Hmm, Frequent Sufferer Warmth, Restocking, Neither Confirms Nor Denies). The Subtle Warning Lean must be readable as a distinct silhouette at mobile scale — that pose is a game mechanic, not a flourish. Frequent Sufferer Warmth should be genuinely warm and mildly alarming at the same time: the smile of a man who has watched you suffer five times and now considers you family.

## 🧠 Personality

### Archetype
**The Amoral Utility** — Murphy has arrived, through decades of observation, at a position that looks like cynicism and is actually just refusal to lie. He does not think people are stupid; he thinks people are predictable, which is a different and more forgiving thing. He will not talk you out of a purchase, because he has no standing to run your life, and he will not talk you into one either, because you were always going to buy it. He occupies the exact middle: the opportunity. What you do with the opportunity is your business, God's business, and your stomach's business, in ascending order of relevance.

### Personality Traits
| Trait | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| Non-judgmental to a fault | Will sell mystery meat at 4 AM to a man in one shoe without a change of expression |
| Compulsively honest | Every warning he gives is accurate; he simply gives them at conversational volume |
| Economically ruthless | Prices the night markup at exactly what desperation will bear, and knows the number |
| Institutionally patient | Twenty-three years of not being surprised by anything |
| Quietly loyal | Regulars get the back room, the discount, and the truth. Loyalty is earned through suffering |

### Internal Monologue
> "Three of them. American, loud, one's already holding the EXTREME. He'll have three. He always has three — not him specifically, but him. I'll say the line about the water. He won't hear the line about the water. He'll be back at ten past eleven tomorrow morning looking like a bad photocopy and he'll buy the Headache Cure, and then he'll buy a MurphyBoost to fix the Headache Cure, and I'll ring up both and say nothing, because nobody ever asked me for an opinion. 15 Sovs. Cash. The machine's broken."

### Motivation
Keep the lights on. Keep the refrigerators running. Outlast whatever the street becomes next. He is not building an empire and he is not saving anyone. He identified a permanent human constant, positioned himself directly beside it, and has been standing there ever since. That is not ambition. That is a job.

### Territory / Social Dynamics
- Murphy does not compete for customers. The street's other traders keep hours; Murphy keeps the *gap*. There is no rivalry because there is no overlap.
- He is the resupply point for every all-night location in the district. Everyone knows this. Nobody thanks him for it.
- Regulars are handled by a strict and unspoken ladder: browse, buy, suffer, suffer again, get the discount, get the door behind the counter. Nobody skips a rung and nobody is told the ladder exists.

### Seamus O'Brien — Full Resolution
The cross-location note in `murphys_lawless.md` ("The Blarney Trap: Seamus knows Murphy — old Dublin connection") resolves explicitly to **Seamus O'Brien**, owner and head barman of The Blarney Trap, the stamp-and-passport giver. There is no other Seamus in this relationship and no other Seamus in Publandia. If the player says "Seamus sent me" at Murphy's counter, Murphy's response is:

> "Seamus O'Brien. Right. He'd know I'd still be open." *hmm* "Whatever he told you about me is either true or he was being polite. Those don't overlap much."

**Murphy on Seamus O'Brien, if asked directly:**
> "Seamus O'Brien pulls a pint in a hundred and nineteen and a half seconds and makes you feel like you were there for the whole of it. He sells the night. I sell what's left of it. We've an understanding going back a long way and neither of us has ever put a word on it."

## 🔄 Recurring Appearances

### Within Publandia
- **Never leaves the shop.** Murphy has no overworld sprite and appears in no street scene. This is deliberate: the shop is the character's boundary.
- **Named at The Blarney Trap**: Seamus O'Brien mentions "Murphy's, off the Craic Tax" at closing time to any player still standing.
- **Named at The Bunk & Blunder**: Colm O'Brien's reception spiel lists Murphy's as the only thing open after two, delivered in the same monotone as the fire exits.
- **Named at The State of Ya**: Nuala Brennan's response to a player carrying a Murphy's bag is a single raised eyebrow and "Ah. You went to Murphy's first."

### Progression States
| Stage | Trigger | Murphy's Behaviour |
|-------|---------|--------------------|
| Stranger | First entry | Greeting Nod, full warning system if sober |

### Evolution Through Game
Murphy's dialogue does not soften so much as *shorten*. Early lines are complete sentences with explanations attached. By Trusted status he communicates in fragments — "Sealed stuff. Back left. Go on." The warmth is in the brevity. A player who never earns the ladder never notices there was one.

Murphy gates nothing on the critical path. The only currency Murphy accepts for trust is suffering, and he does not advertise the exchange rate.

## 🗣️ Voice Direction

### Accent
Dublin, working, unhurried. Not a performance accent — Murphy is the flattest voice in Publandia, sitting deliberately between Seamus O'Brien's warmth and Fergus O'Malley's fatalism. He is neither charming nor haunted. He is *at work.*

### Tone
- **Standard transaction**: Conversational, low volume, zero salesmanship. He has never once tried to sell anything.
- **Warnings**: Identical to standard transaction. This is the joke and it must not be signposted. The warning is not delivered as a warning.
- **After-effect commentary**: A half-degree warmer. Still nothing approaching sympathy.
- **Frequent Sufferer / Trusted**: Shorter sentences, longer pauses, and the only real warmth in the character.
- **Philosophy lines**: Delivered while doing something else — restocking, wiping the counter. Never delivered *at* the player.

### Key Delivery Notes
- The "hmm" is a closed-mouth single note, never followed by explanation. If a take has him elaborating after the hmm, the take is wrong.
- "Very strong" in the MurphyBoost line lands flat, not emphatic. The bros supply the emphasis by mishearing it.
- Never raise the voice. Murphy's ceiling is a 3/10 and the shop provides all remaining volume.
- The Seamus O'Brien line should be the warmest reading in the file, and should still be under-played.
- No line of Murphy's may ever indicate he finds any of this funny. He does not. He finds it *usual.*
