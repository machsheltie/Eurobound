# NPC Profile: Mickey "The Mick" Finnegan

*"A good scam is a story with a sad ending. But the story is worth paying for."*

## 📋 NPC Identity
- **NPC ID**: `publandia_npc_mickey_finnegan_01`
- **Display Name**: Mickey
- **Internal Name**: Mickey "The Mick" Finnegan — "The Proprietor"
- **Role**: Scammer / Vendor / Mini-Game Operator / Stamp Giver / Moral-Choice Broker
- **Associated Quest**: `publandia_sidequest_wise_fool_01` (the Wise Fool track — the only Brewery Passport stamp in the game earned by losing)
- **Primary Location**: Shenanigans, a side alley off The Craic Tax, Publandia
- **Secondary Appearances**: Referenced at The Blarney Trap (Seamus stocks Fool's Gold post-stamp); named as a professional acquaintance in the Paddy's Book dialogue tree; appears in the Publandia epilogue montage running the wheel for a fresh coach party
- **Family**: Third-generation. Grandfather ran games on O'Connell Street; father worked the ferries. Mickey does not name them — "You don't put family in the patter."

---

## 🎭 Core Concept

### What He's Actually Doing

Operating seven rigged games out of a cramped pub down a side alley, with a five-person shill network, and taking roughly 150 Sovs off every American tourist group the touts steer his way. The wheel is weighted to "TRY AGAIN" 95% of the time. The rings are half a centimetre too small to clear a bottle neck. The darts are subtly bent. The dice hit doubles at 8% instead of 16.7%. The queen is palmed before you ever point at a card.

He is also entirely serious when he says he's an entertainer. The drinks are legitimately good and legitimately priced. Nobody is ever cheated on the beer — Mickey considers that sacred. What he sells is the *experience of hope followed by loss*, retailed in 5 Sovs and 10 Sovs increments, and he believes this is a more honest product than most of what's on offer in The Craic Tax. He is, on the evidence, not entirely wrong, and he would very much like to discuss it with you.

### What Bros Believe

That Shenanigans is a hidden local pub they discovered by accident (they were guided there by a tout), that the games are pub games (they are commercial extraction devices), that the man who won three times at the wheel is a lucky punter (he is Darren, and he is on payroll), and that they are one spin away. Lord Pilsner has drafted a theory about the wheel's deceleration. Chadwick is going to expose the scam, right after one more try.

### The Comedy Gap

The pub is called **Shenanigans**. The Irish alias painted inside the door translates to *Trickery*. The window promises "EVERYONE Wins!" and there is a legally protective sign, very small, reading "For entertainment purposes only." The same man wins loudly at the wheel every eleven minutes. None of this registers. The bros lose 20 Sovs and blame variance, lose 50 Sovs and invoke the law of averages, lose 100 Sovs and briefly suspect the truth, and then come back the following night — which is the exact behaviour Mickey has spent his life cultivating and the exact behaviour he rewards with the stamp.

The deepest joke: the stamp is not a consolation prize. Mickey means it. Coming back after being taken requires something he genuinely admires, and he has built an award for it.

| Build | Compact, light on his feet, moves through a crowded room without touching anyone |
| Height | Short — reads as unthreatening, which is load-bearing |

### Clothing
- **The rumpled suit**: Brown, good cut, deliberately un-pressed. A sharp suit would trigger suspicion; a rumpled one reads as a man who owns a pub and is having a nice night. The rumpling is maintained.
- **Waistcoat with Lucky Gold pocket accents**: Where the float lives. He never reaches for it in front of a mark mid-game.
- **Accessories**: Grandfather's pocket watch, from the O'Connell Street days — charm only, the works have been dead since the eighties. The faded-gold stamp and ink pad, kept in the inside breast pocket and handled like a sacrament.
- **Footwear**: Soft-soled, silent. He arrives beside you.

### Distinguishing Features
- **The twinkle**: Warmth over calculation, with the calculation clearly visible if you look. He does not hide it. It has never once cost him a mark.
- **The half-second "gotcha"**: During scam encouragement, a flicker of the con showing through, 0.3 seconds maximum. Players paying close attention will catch it. Players who miss it are never punished for missing it.
- **Hands**: Always empty and always visible when he's talking to you. This is a discipline. Eamon does the sleight; Mickey does the *believing*.
- **The lean-in**: For the shill offer and the Wise Fool conversation, he closes the distance and drops his volume. Both times, he means what he says.
- **The laugh**: Head back, genuine, and never at your expense in a way you can prove.

## 🧠 Personality

### Archetype

**The Affectionate Con Artist** — Mickey does not despise his marks and does not pretend to. He likes them. He finds them interesting. He has structured a business that requires him to take their money and he has decided, sincerely, that the correct way to do that is to give them a genuinely good night in exchange. He is not a thief with a conscience; he is a craftsman with a stable moral framework that happens to be indefensible. He will explain it to you cheerfully and at length and you will find it difficult to argue with.

### Personality Traits
| Trait | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| Genuinely warm | The affection for marks is real. This is why the con works and why it isn't cruel. |
| Craft pride | Considers scamming an art form with rules, technique, and standards. Sloppy scamming offends him. |
| Honest about the dishonest | Will confirm the games are rigged the moment you've earned the right to ask, and never before. |
| Beer is sacred | Refuses to water, short-measure, or mark up drinks. "You can't scam someone on the beer." |
| Reads people fast | Sizes a mark's wallet, tolerance, and personality within four seconds of the door opening. |
| Respects the returner | Anger bores him. Cleverness he tolerates. Coming back after losing is the only thing that genuinely moves him. |

### Internal Monologue

> "Four of them. The loud one's got the money and the theory — he'll spend forty on the wheel before he touches a card. The one with the camera wants a story more than he wants a win; give him near-misses, he'll pay for near-misses. The quiet one holds the coats and won't play at all, and honestly? Good. Smart. And the fourth one is going to get angry around the hundred mark and I'll buy him a drink and he'll take it. Darren wins at eleven past. Gráinne moves in at half past. The beer's real, the craic's real, and the wheel is a lie I've maintained since 1994. If one of them comes back tomorrow night, I'll stamp his book and mean it."

### Motivation

He was offered legitimate pub work and found it boring. That is the honest answer and he gives it. Underneath: Mickey is the third link in a chain his grandfather started on O'Connell Street, and he does not intend to be the one who breaks it by going straight. He wants every night to be a story. He wants the marks to be characters rather than victims, which requires giving them a good enough evening that they're not victims. And he wants — this is the part he doesn't lead with — to find the occasional person who loses everything, understands exactly what happened, and walks back through his door anyway. Those people are the reason the stamp exists.

**The tour**:
> "First time? Let me show you around. We've got games, drinks, and more games. You look lucky."

**The size-up (delivered as flattery)**:
> "That face! You've got winner written all over you. I can tell these things."

**If the player arrives holding the Bottle Opener Keychain**:
> "Ah — the brass shamrock. Seamus sent you, did he? Course he did. He sends me all the good ones and keeps the money. Sit down, friend of a friend. First drink's proper price, same as everyone. I'll not insult you with a freebie."

**If the player says "Seamus sent me" without the opener**:
> "Everyone says that. Paddy's lot say it at a door in an alley three streets over and they mean it less than you do. *smiles* Come in anyway. The wheel doesn't check credentials."

**If the player asks whether the games are rigged, on night one**:
> "Rigged! *delighted* That's a serious word for a spin wheel. It's five Sovs. What would I even be rigging, the joy?"

### The Wise Fool Moment (3+ scams lost AND returned)

*Mickey crosses the room himself. The music does not stop but it gets further away. Portrait Wise Fool, used once.*

**Mickey**:
> "You know the games are rigged. You've always known."

*Player options: "I figured it out" (lie) / "I suspected" (partial truth) / "I just kept hoping" (full truth)*

**Mickey** (to any answer, without judging which):
> "And yet you came back. That takes something. Not intelligence. Something better."

> "You know, most people get angry. Storm off. Blame me. You came back."

> "That takes something special. Not brains — I'm not insulting you — but something better. Character."

> "You're a good mark. The best kind. Let me buy you a drink. A real one."

> "The fools who learn are the best fools. Here's to you." *raises glass*

> "And here—" *stamps passport* "—you've earned this. Not for winning. For losing with grace."

> "You've earned the stamp. Not for winning. For understanding what winning actually is."

**On the stamp design itself**:
> "Jester's cap round the edge. Clover with one leaf gone soft — that's luck that doesn't work, which is the only kind anybody's ever actually had. And the year. Your year. Wise Fool since tonight."

### The Shill Offer (Moral Choice)

**The pitch**:
> "You know the games now. Want to be on the winning side? 50 Sovs per night to play 'winner' for fresh marks."

**If accepted**:
> "Good man. Darren'll show you the timing — it's all timing. You win at eleven past the hour and you sound surprised about it. And listen: you'll be fine. It's the sounding surprised that gets people."

**If declined ("Moral Fool")**:
> "No? *nods, entirely unbothered* Fair play to you. Genuinely. I'd think less of me if I were you too. Drink's still on the house, don't be daft."

**If the player asks for more money**:
> "*laughs, head back, delighted* Seventy-five and you're robbing me. Which — yes. Fine. Yes. You've been paying attention."

## 📅 Schedule & Location

### Primary Schedule
| Time | Location | Activity |
|------|----------|----------|
| 16:00-18:00 | The Entry / Main Bar | Doors open. Slow trade. Mickey personally greets, sizes up, and remembers every face that comes through. |
| 18:00-21:00 | Main Bar → Games Corner | The softening hours. Good drinks, good music, "just for fun" bar games that are actually skill assessments. |
| 21:00-00:00 | Games Corner | Peak extraction. Mickey works the wheel and the darts personally; Eamon runs cards and cups; Darren wins on the hour and at eleven past. |
| 00:00-02:00 | Back Tables / Recovery | The consolation shift. Drinks bought, sympathy dispensed, "one more try" offered exactly once per mark. |
| 02:00-03:00 | The Snug | High-value marks and long cons. Also where the Wise Fool conversation happens if the player has qualified. |
| 03:00-16:00 | Closed | Mickey is not on the premises. Nobody knows where he lives and nobody has ever thought to ask. |

### Positioning Notes
- Mickey has no fixed post. He circulates. Wherever the player is losing, Mickey arrives within about fifteen seconds, which should feel warm on the first visit and slightly uncanny on the fourth.
- He is never behind the bar and never at the card table. Distance from the mechanism is deliberate — Eamon's hands do the crime, Mickey's face does the business.
- During any active mini-game he stands to the player's off-hand side, at the edge of the frame, and reacts to outcomes a quarter-second before the player does.
- Post-stamp, his default position shifts to the Snug doorway, which is the visual signal that the player's status in the building has changed.
- He physically crosses the room only twice in the entire game: for the Wise Fool conversation, and for the Angry Tourist scene. Both should feel like events.

### Hidden Details / Easter Eggs
- **The lucky penny jar** at the entrance is rigged. Examining it says so, in the examine text, in plain language, on the first visit. Nobody reads examine text on the first visit.
- **The prize shelf** in the Games Corner has a fine layer of dust on every item except one, which has been handled constantly. It is the only prize ever awarded, and it has been awarded to Darren four hundred times.

- **The framed photo behind the bar**: a young Mickey and a young Seamus O'Brien holding darts trophies. Both men refuse to discuss it, in separate buildings, using different denials.

### Territory / Social Dynamics
- **The shill network is choreographed, not improvised.** Darren wins on the hour and at eleven past. Gráinne moves in at the half hour. Eamon never leaves the table. Watching the room long enough from the Snug reveals the pattern, which is the entire point of granting Snug access post-stamp.
- **Locals do not come here.** Every human being in Shenanigans is either staff or a tourist who was steered in. Mickey does not consider this a moral problem; he considers it market segmentation.
- **The touts of The Craic Tax** are not on payroll and are not exactly not on payroll. Mickey buys them drinks. Groups arrive.
- **Hierarchy**: Mickey decides, and nobody else in the building makes a decision about anything. Staff are paid well, treated well, and have no autonomy. This is presented as generosity and functions as control.
- **The one rule**: nobody scams anyone on the beer. Staff who short-measure are gone the same night. Mickey has fired two people in thirty years and both times it was for this.

## 🔄 Recurring Appearances

### Within Publandia
- **The Blarney Trap**: Once the player earns the Shenanigans stamp, The Fool's Gold appears on Seamus's back bar at 8 Sovs. Seamus pours it with a knowing smirk and says nothing. If asked, Seamus: "Mickey's beer. It's good. That's the annoying part."
- **The Craic Tax streets**: Touts adjust their pitch for players carrying the Shenanigans stamp — they stop offering "hidden local pubs" and start offering things that are actually hidden, which is a small, real reward for having been thoroughly taken.
- **Paddy's Book**: Mentioning Mickey to Paddy Malone gets a professional, faintly dismissive read: "Mickey takes fivers off tourists and calls it art. I take everything and call it arithmetic. We're not the same trade."
- **Shenanigans, post-stamp**: Mickey's default position moves to the Snug doorway. The Fresh Mark group arrives nightly and can be watched from the Snug, losing exactly as the player did, in the same order.

### In Other Cities
| City | Location | Role/Context | Callback Dialogue |
|------|----------|--------------|-------------------|
| Debaucheryville | Three Crown Keg Haus | Mickey does not appear, but the Shenanigans stamp in the passport is recognised by the cellar staff as a mark of a specific kind of durability. | "You have the wilted clover. ### Evolution Through Game
- **Pre-stamp**: Every Mickey line is patter. Warm, generous-sounding, and entirely transactional. The player is a revenue stream and the writing should never quite let them forget it while never quite letting them notice.
- **The Wise Fool conversation**: The single tonal pivot. The mask comes off, once, deliberately, and does not go back on for this player.
- **Post-stamp**: Mickey is genuinely, unguardedly friendly. He gives real advice, reveals one rig per visit, and warns the player about Paddy Malone's loan book — a warning he gives to nobody else. The scam continues around the player at full volume and the player is now inside it.
- **If the player accepts shill work**: Mickey treats them exactly the same as before, which is the point. The change is entirely on the player's side of the screen.
- **If the player declines shill work**: Mickey respects them more and says so once, in one sentence, and never brings it up again.
- **What never changes**: Mickey never apologises, never returns money, and never stops running the games. There is no redemption arc and none is offered.

## 🎯 Quest Integration

### Primary Quest: The Wise Fool
- **Quest**: The Wise Fool
- **Quest ID**: `publandia_sidequest_wise_fool_01`
- **Role**: Quest Giver, Mini-Game Operator, Stamp Giver, Moral-Choice Broker
- **Trigger**: First loss at any Shenanigans game (auto-starts, silently — no quest marker, no notification)
- **Stages**:
  1. **Fresh Mark** — lose at any game. Staff sympathetic. Shill "wins" nearby to encourage a retry.
  2. **Repeat Mark** — lose 3+ times across any games. Staff recognise you: "Back for another go?"
  3. **Persistent Mark** — lose at 3 *different* scams AND leave the building AND return. Mickey approaches personally.
  4. **The Wise Fool conversation** — three dialogue options, none of which changes the outcome, all of which change how Mickey looks at you.
  5. **Stamp awarded** — Bottle Opener Keychain must be in inventory.
- **Outcome**: "Wise Fool" status, Shenanigans stamp (2 of 4 Publandia), The Fool's Gold unlocked, Snug observation access, one rig revealed per visit, and the shill offer opens as a standing moral choice.

### Quest Gate Function

Mickey gates the second of Publandia's four Brewery Passport stamps, and therefore gates the **Publandia Legendary Hangover** (all four stamps required) and contributes to **Continental Beer Master** (18 stamps). Critically, this stamp **cannot be earned by playing well**. A player who wins is impossible; a player who quits after one loss is ineligible; a player who never returns after being taken is permanently locked out until they come back. The gate is emotional, not mechanical, and it is the only one like it in the game.

Hard requirement: the Bottle Opener Keychain from Seamus O'Brien at The Blarney Trap must be in inventory at the moment of stamping. Without it, Mickey delivers the Wise Fool speech in full, reaches for the stamp, finds no book, and says: "You've earned it and you've nowhere to put it. Go and see Seamus. Fifteen Sovs. Then come back and I'll do this properly — I'm not doing the speech twice, mind."

## 🗣️ Voice Direction

### Accent

Dublin, inner-city, quick and musical. Mickey talks faster than Seamus and uses more words. The accent is warm and completely unexaggerated — the character's charm is a professional tool, so the actor should sound like a genuinely charming person rather than someone performing charm. Irish-language flourishes are rare; Mickey's register is patter, not heritage.

### Tone
- **The Welcome**: Bright, fast, generous. Sells nothing directly. Every sentence is an invitation.
- **During Scams**: Encouraging, physically close, always a beat ahead of the outcome. Never pushy — the pressure comes from the room, not from him.
- **After Losses**: Softer, slower, and 90% sincere. The remaining 10% is a business decision the actor should know about and never play.
- **The Wise Fool Moment**: Completely different. Slow. Quiet. No twinkle. This is the only place in the entire performance where Mickey is not working.
- **The Shill Offer**: Conspiratorial, warm, and genuinely indifferent to the answer.

### Key Delivery Notes
- "That's the whole game. People make it complicated." — flat, almost bored. The boredom is the con.
- "Rigged! That's a serious word for a spin wheel." — delighted, never defensive. He is enjoying the accusation.
- The Wise Fool speech must not sound like a con. If a single line reads as manipulation, the track's entire emotional payoff collapses. Direct it as though Mickey is slightly embarrassed to be sincere.
- "It says you're human. Welcome to the club." — no warmth added. Say it plainly and let it land.
- The laugh at "Seventy-five and you're robbing me" is the biggest laugh Mickey gets in the game. Let it run.
- He never raises his voice, never threatens, and never once tells the player they were stupid. Not in any branch, including the angry one.

## 📝 Development Notes

- **The Seamus referral beat is a first-class interaction, not flavour.** Arriving with the Bottle Opener Keychain changes Mickey's opening line and warms the entire Wise Fool track. Wire the inventory check on entry, not on dialogue.
- **The stamp cannot be earned without losing.** Verify at implementation that no combination of mini-game outcomes can produce a net-positive player. If a build ever lets someone win at Shenanigans, the location's premise is broken.
- `publandia_sidequest_wise_fool_01` is a **newly coined quest ID** — no prior ID for the Wise Fool track existed in the design corpus at time of writing. Reconcile with the Quests directory before it hardens.
- The Wise Fool conversation's three player options are cosmetic by design. Do not add a branch where "I figured it out" produces a better outcome; the equality of the options is the joke and the point.
- **Portrait Wise Fool must be hard-gated** in code, not by convention. One reuse anywhere else and the beat is dead.
- The shill offer should remain available permanently after the stamp. Players who decline and later reconsider get no commentary from Mickey, which is more interesting than either praise or judgement.
- Near-miss tuning is the highest-risk balance work in the location. "So close" must be felt on roughly one throw in three, or the games read as unfair rather than tantalising — and unfair games get abandoned before the mark ever returns, which kills the stamp.
- Easter egg: the darts trophy photo behind the Shenanigans bar is the same event as the fourteenth framed regular at The Blarney Trap. Two men, two buildings, two different denials, no explanation ever given.
- Voice acting priority: **second in Publandia, after Seamus.** Mickey carries more lines than any other Publandia NPC and the Wise Fool speech is the city's single most important read.

## 🚫 Warnings & Restrictions

### ⚠️ CRYPTO CHECK
- [x] **Crypto content conforms to `design/economy/economy_system.md`** — Mickey has no crypto content. Shenanigans is a cash Sov economy: 1 Sov to 10 Sovs per play, 8 Sovs a pint, 50 Sovs a night for shill work. Nothing in the location touches SOVS or the darknet shitcoin pool, and nothing has been added.
- [x] **NO automatic crypto assumptions** — Mickey is a physical-games operator running weighted wheels and bent darts. Digital assets would be a different craft and he'd be openly contemptuous of it.
- [x] **Coins, kiosks, and certificates come from the master pool** — none invented here; none used here.
- [x] **The bros are never scam-aware** — Lord Pilsner reaches the edge of realisation ("that guy who won earlier... he's STILL here...") and then spins again. Chadwick reframes being robbed as compelling content. Neither ever concludes he was foolish; both conclude they were unlucky and interesting.
- [x] **Nothing winks** — Mickey's honesty inside the fiction is a character trait aimed at other characters. No line, tooltip, or examine text signals to the player that this is satire.
- [x] **Focus on city-specific cultural exploitation instead** — Dublin's side-alley tourist economy, carnival game psychology, and the tout-to-trap pipeline.

### Satirical Target Verification
- [x] Targets tourist/frat bro behavior, NOT local culture or ethnicity — the joke is the mark's willingness, in a pub literally named Trickery, not any trait of Irish people.
- [x] NPC is savvy entrepreneur, not villain or stereotype — Mickey is skilled, principled within his own frame, generous to his staff, and never cruel to a loser.
- [x] Humor is affectionate mockery, not mean-spirited — the location's thesis is that losing gracefully is admirable, and it awards its highest honour for exactly that.
