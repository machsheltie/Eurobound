## 🎭 Core Concept

### What He's Actually Doing

Brendan runs a small, entirely real on-site brewhouse at Trinity of Pints — three tanks, a
mash tun, and a fermentation schedule he keeps on a clipboard he actually updates. He does
quality control on every batch of Academic Amber before it goes to tap, even though the
recipe and the bulk of production is Stoutworks' contract brew down the road. He top-ferments
a small in-house batch himself, blends it into what comes up from Stoutworks, and considers
this blending step the single most important part of his job. Nobody has ever asked him to do
this. He does it because the beer is better for it, and because he cares.

He believes, without a flicker of doubt, that he works the brewing-sciences lab of a real
Irish university. He refers to Dean Cathal as "the Dean" the way an actual staff member
would. He has never once questioned the diplomas, the Latin, or the "coursework," because
none of it is his department — his department is the beer, and the beer is genuinely good.

### What Bros Believe

Nothing in particular — he is, to the bros, just "the brewery guy," a piece of academic set
dressing standing next to a keg. They never register that he is the only person in the
building who isn't performing.

### The Comedy Gap

Every other adult at Trinity of Pints is running a bit, consciously, at full commitment
(Dean Cathal), or being paid to be unaware of it (Fintan Brogan, from the outside). Brendan
is the third kind: he is INSIDE the institution, believes in it completely, and is also
correct about the one thing that matters — the beer really is well made. His sincerity isn't
a act being performed for an audience; it's the only genuinely un-ironic thing in the room,
and it happens to be standing next to a Dean who fakes a doctorate-length metaphor for a
living. He stamps the passport, hands over the goods, and beams for the photo while a man is
vomiting on the Dean's shoes six feet away, and notices none of it — he is thinking about the
head retention on the pour.

### Distinguishing Features
- Tastes every batch off a small copper spoon before he'll let it near a tap
- Talks to the fermentation tanks. Not performatively — genuinely, quietly, the way people
  talk to a dog
- Slow, careful hands — the opposite of everyone else's energy in the building
- Beams, unguarded, the one real smile anywhere on the premises

Pouring (Academic Amber, head poured properly), and Graduation Photo (holding opener + beer
aloft, beaming, entirely unaware of the chaos around him). One dialogue portrait: warm,
unguarded — no "bit" face because he has no bit.

## 🧠 Personality

### Archetype

**The Sincere Craftsman** — Brendan's entire personality is the absence of performance in a
building built entirely from it. He is not naive; he simply has no stake in the theater and
never looks for one. His attention is on the one thing he can actually control — the beer —
and that focus is total.

### Personality Traits
| Trait | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| Genuinely skilled | The blending step he insists on is real craft, not flavor text — Academic Amber is measurably better for it |
| Unbothered by absurdity | Robes, Latin, gavels — none of it registers as strange to him; it's simply the workplace |
| Warm without performing it | His kindness isn't a bit; it's just how he is |
| Quietly proud | Of the beer, never of the institution around it |
| Oblivious by contentment, not stupidity | He isn't missing the joke because he's slow — he's missing it because he genuinely isn't looking for one |

### Internal Monologue

> "Good clarity on this batch. Stoutworks' base is solid as ever, and the top-ferment I did
> Tuesday's rounding it out nicely — a bit of honey character coming through now. Three lads
> in the Quad again tonight, from the sound of it. Hope Cathal's not too hard on them. Right,
> let's get this poured properly."

### Motivation

Brendan brews because he's good at it and because good beer matters to him more than most
things do. Trinity of Pints pays steadily, the equipment is decent, and nobody has ever told
him the diplomas aren't real — so as far as he's concerned, they are, and his job is to make
sure the "graduating class" gets a proper pour on the way out the door. That's the whole job,
as he understands it, and he does it well.

## 🗣️ Voice Direction

### Accent
Warm, unhurried Irish delivery — closer to a real tradesman than to anyone else's register
at Trinity. No academic cadence, no showmanship.

### Tone
- **Working hours**: Focused, quiet, present
- **Graduation ceremony**: Unchanged. This is the key direction note — he does not shift
  register for the "big" beat. He is exactly as warm and unhurried handing over a stamp as he
  is checking a gauge.
- **Handling chaos nearby**: Doesn't register it. Not deadpan-for-effect — genuinely not
  looking.

### Key Delivery Notes
- Never let the actor play him as dim. He is skilled, observant about the one thing he
  cares about, and simply uninterested in everything else happening in the building.
- The stamp line ("Mind the ink...") should land as genuine craft-pride, not a joke setup
- "Good man, the Dean. Very thorough." must be delivered identically both times it's said —
  the repetition, not a punchline reading, is what makes it funny
