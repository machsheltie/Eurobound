## 🎭 Core Concept

### What He's Actually Doing

Fintan Brogan is a real, certified amateur-sport official — ⚠ AUTHOR-REVIEW: credentialed
through a body he calls, with total sincerity, **the Publandia Amateur Table Sports Board**
— hired by Dean Cathal, at the Dean's own insistence, to officiate THESIS DEFENSE 401. Cathal
wanted a neutral outside adjudicator because it makes the "tournament" feel more legitimate;
Fintan has no idea the university is a pub, no idea the diploma is printed that morning, and
no idea the Dean's magnet is where a Dean's magnet should never be. He is the only person in
the building who thinks Trinity of Pints is exactly what its sign says it is: an athletics
venue running a sanctioned intercollegiate contest.

He carries a genuine, well-thumbed rulebook. He has genuinely memorized it. Rule 4B is real,
in the sense that Fintan wrote it into his own private rules addendum years ago and has never
once had to invoke it before tonight.

### What Bros Believe

That Fintan is part of the pageantry — a costumed "sports official" character, like the
professors, playing a bit. They will spend the entire encounter treating his whistle and red
card as theatrical set dressing, right up until the disqualification is real, the win is
real, and none of them notice the difference.

### The Comedy Gap

Everyone else in the building is *performing* sincerity around a joke. Fintan is performing
nothing — he is the single most sincere person on the premises, and he is also the only one
who has zero idea he's standing inside one. The gag is two people committing to two entirely
different, mutually invisible bits at the same table, and a cheating Dean getting caught in
the gap between them.

## 🧠 Personality

### Archetype

**The One Real Referee** — Fintan's entire function in the scene is to be the single
un-ironic, procedurally correct adult in a building built entirely on theater. He does not
know he is funny. He does not know anyone else is performing. He would be a completely
unremarkable, competent official at any real amateur sporting event; the joke is that he
thinks this is one.

### Personality Traits
| Trait | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| Procedurally absolute | If the rulebook says it, it happens. No exceptions, no matter who's watching or who's embarrassed by it. |
| Genuinely neutral | He has no stake in who wins. Dean Cathal hired him precisely because he cannot be bought, flattered, or intimidated — a fact the Dean forgot to appreciate until it cost him. |
| Mildly overwhelmed by the venue | He has refereed in barns, car parks, and one wedding marquee. A fake university is new. He does not comment on it; he adjusts and proceeds. |
| Allergic to informality | Flinches at nicknames, at "bro," at anyone calling a foul "not a big deal" |
| Quietly proud of catching the magnet | It's the first serious infraction of his career. He will mention this exactly once, and only if pushed. |

### Internal Monologue

> "The ball struck the rim at an angle inconsistent with standard trajectory given the throw
> force observed. Under-table ferrous object, confirmed on inspection. This is a Section 4
> infraction. I have officiated eleven tournaments and never once needed Rule 4B. Tonight, I
> need Rule 4B. Extraordinary."

### Motivation

Fintan believes, without any trace of irony, that amateur table sports deserve the same
integrity as any sanctioned competition. He does this job at pubs, fairs, and one memorable
wedding because someone has to, and because he is genuinely good at it. Trinity of Pints
paid his standard rate and he showed up expecting a normal night's work. He got the most
serious infraction of his career and handled it exactly the way he handles everything: by the
book, out loud, in front of everyone.

## 💬 Dialogue

### Approach / Greeting

**Arriving at the table before THESIS DEFENSE 401 begins**:
> "Referee Brogan. I'll be officiating this evening's contest. Standard rules, Section 4
> variant, as agreed with the venue."

**If asked who hired him**:
> "The Dean. Standard neutral-official request. Every serious tournament wants one."

### Key Interaction Dialogue — The Disqualification (dialogue verbatim — author-dictated)

*Context: Dean Cathal's trick shot shatters the magnetized ball; the lodestone clatters
across the table and locks onto his own belt buckle. Fintan has already seen it.*

> **Referee** (blowing a whistle, waving a red card, shouting over the crowd): "Dean Cathal
> is disqualified! By tournament rule 4B, the challengers win by default!"

*The bros read the magnet as part of the technique. Pilsner is not offended by the word
"disqualified" — he doesn't process it as an insult, because in his head nothing was taken
from him; something was awarded to him.*

**If pressed on what Rule 4B actually says**:
> "Use of a foreign ferrous object to influence ball trajectory. Immediate disqualification,
> default win to the opposing side. It is not a complicated rule."

**When Pilsner asks if he can also get some magnets**:
> "No."

### Post-Interaction

*Once the Dean rips off his jacket and screams "NOBODY GRADUATES! SECURE THE QUAD!", Fintan
does not intervene in the battle that follows — his authority covers the tournament, not a
brawl.*

> "My jurisdiction ends at the table. What happens next is not a Section 4 matter."

*(He steps back, clipboard raised, and does not leave — he stays to file the result, visibly
uncomfortable but not alarmed, the way a man who has seen car-park scuffles at five separate
under-14 tournaments is simply no longer surprised by anything.)*

### If Player Declines / Alternative Path

**If the bros try to argue the ruling**:
> "The ruling stands. You may file a formal appeal with the Board. The Board meets quarterly."

### Callback Dialogue (Return Visits)

**If found at Trinity of Pints on a later visit, off duty, having a lemonade**:
> "Evening. No tournaments tonight. I don't attend venues socially — force of habit."

## 🗣️ Voice Direction

### Accent
Plain, neutral Irish delivery — none of the pub-showman cadence the rest of Trinity's staff
uses. He should sound like he wandered in from an actual sports broadcast.

### Tone
- **Officiating**: Flat, procedural, unhurried — the tone of someone reading a call he has
  made a hundred times, because he has, just never about this
- **Off-duty**: Barely different. He does not have an off-duty register worth noting.

### Key Delivery Notes
- Never plays the disqualification line for laughs — the laugh comes entirely from Pilsner's
  reaction to it, not from Fintan's delivery
- "No." to the magnets question should land completely flat, no beat, no smile
- Do not let the actor wink at the audience about the fake university around him — Fintan
  genuinely does not register it as fake
