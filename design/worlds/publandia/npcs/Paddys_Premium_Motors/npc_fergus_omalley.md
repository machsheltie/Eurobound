## 🎭 Core Concept

### What He's Actually Doing
Running a car rental business at the edge of Publandia, directly adjacent to The Cloverleaf—Publandia's most inescapable roundabout. He's been warning tourists for 40 years. None of them listen. He warns them anyway.

### What Bros Believe
A colorful local character with a flair for dramatic storytelling. His warnings about "The Cloverleaf" are clearly exaggerated Irish folklore meant to add atmosphere to their authentic European road trip.

### The Comedy Gap
Fergus has a wall full of Polaroids of abandoned rental cars. A counter showing "DAYS SINCE LAST CLOVERLEAF INCIDENT" that has been stuck on 0 since 1984. A map with "HERE BE DRAGONS" written over the roundabout. The bros see all of this and think: "What a quirky character."

### The Hidden Tragedy
In 1992, Fergus's wife Mary went for "a quick drive to see the cliffs." She entered The Cloverleaf. The car came back eventually. She didn't. Fergus still wears his wedding ring. He tells people she's "still circling." He might not be joking.

## 🧠 Personality

### Archetype
**The Cassandra** - Fergus sees the future. He knows what will happen. He warns everyone. No one believes him. He warns them anyway because someone has to try.

### Personality Traits
| Trait | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| Resigned fatalism | Knows warnings won't be heeded, gives them anyway |
| Dark humor | Copes through gallows comedy |
| Caring despite defeat | Genuinely wants to save people |
| Thousand-yard stare | Has seen too many enter, too few return |
| Professional persistence | Still runs the business, still maintains hope |

### Internal Monologue
> "Here come three more. Americans, by the look of them. They'll want a car. They'll ignore the warnings. They'll enter The Cloverleaf. And I'll be here when they come back. If they come back. They always come back eventually. The roundabout spits most of them out. Eventually."

### Motivation
Once: Make an honest living renting cars.
Now: Bear witness. Warn those he can. Maintain the Board of Shame. Wait for Mary.

### Hidden Details / Easter Eggs
- **The photo of Mary**: silver frame on the desk. Examinable. Turning it over — a second interaction — reveals the back: "MARY - CLOVERLEAF '92". Fergus says nothing when the player does this. He watches, and the `touch_photo` animation fires.
- **The Mapquest printouts**: the wastepaper basket beside the desk contains crumpled Mapquest printouts. Not from this decade. Not from any recent decade. Examine text notes the dates.
- **Returned GPS units**: a shelf behind the counter holds a row of rental GPS devices with "RECALCULATING" burned permanently into the screens. Fergus does not throw them out. He does not explain why he does not throw them out.
- **The reserved spot**: the Board of Shame has one empty pin position with a handwritten note: "Reserved for next." If the player examines it before renting, it is empty. If they examine it after surviving the Cloverleaf, there is a Polaroid of their own rental car in it.
- **The insurance fine print**: the premium insurance document is fully readable if the player chooses to read it. Page 47 genuinely contains the circular-traffic exclusion. Nobody reads it. Reading it is an achievement.
- **The counter's small print**: "Record: 0 (achieved every day since 1984)."
- **The wandering sheep**: 1-2 ambient sheep cross the gravel lot. Approaching one produces "*baaaaah*" and the subtext line "(The sheep knows what's coming)."

- **Post-credits documentary** features Fergus interview

### Territory / Social Dynamics
- Paddy's Premium Motors is the last building before the country roads, and Fergus is the last human being a tourist speaks to before entering The Cloverleaf. Every NPC in the roundabout community passed through this counter first. That gives him a strange, unearned status inside a society he has never visited.
- The Cloverleaf residents do not resent him. Several are fond of him. Orla Brennan sends Christmas cards from inside a circling tour bus, which Fergus keeps and does not display.
- He has no staff, no competitors and no successor. Nobody else in Publandia rents cars at the outskirts. When he stops, the warnings stop.
- The tow truck driver is the only person he speaks to professionally on a regular basis, and their conversations are almost entirely logistics. Neither has ever raised the obvious subject.
- Fergus does not go into the roundabout. Not once, in forty years, not to look for Mary, not to recover a car. This is the one boundary he holds absolutely and he has never explained it.

### Evolution Through Game
- **Pre-rental**: pure Cassandra. Warnings delivered flat and futile, thousand-yard stare at maximum.
- **The transaction**: mechanically helpful, emotionally absent. He sells the GPS he knows will fail and the insurance he knows excludes the incident, and he tells the player both facts out loud while doing it. He is not a con man; he is a man who has stopped expecting disclosure to change anything.

- **Post-quest, first return**: the single largest tonal shift in the character. Genuine surprise. The `brief_hope` sprite state exists for this moment and this moment only at first.
- **Return visits 2-4+**: warms fractionally each time, tops out at "almost smiles." Never crosses into friendliness.
- **Post-credits**: the only time he speaks about the forty years as a completed thing rather than an ongoing one.

## 🎯 Quest Integration

### Primary Quest
- **Quest**: The Cloverleaf of Confusion
- **Role**: Quest Giver / Vendor / Warning Giver — he triggers the quest by completing a transaction, not by asking for anything
- **Trigger**: Complete rental transaction
- **Outcome**: Sets `cloverleaf_quest_active`; player gains vehicle access and enters The Cloverleaf; on return, `fergus_respect_earned` unlocks the 10% survivor discount and the "Roundabout Survivor" title

### Quest Gate Function

Yes, and it is the hardest gate in Publandia's sidequest layer: **The Cloverleaf of Confusion cannot begin without renting a vehicle from Fergus.** There is no alternative vehicle source, no walk-in route, and no way to reach the roundabout on foot that the design acknowledges. A player who declines every rental simply never sees one of the game's signature set-pieces.

He does not gate on money in any meaningful way — the cheapest rental is 45 Sovs and he'll rent to anyone who has it — and he never refuses service. He warns and he sells. Both, every time, in that order.

## 🗣️ Voice Direction

### Accent
Irish (Munster region), not exaggerated. Worn, weary, but not without warmth.

### Tone
- Resigned but not defeated
- Dark humor as coping mechanism
- Genuine care underneath the fatalism
- Brief moments of hope (when survivors return)

### Key Delivery Notes
- "That's what they all say" should land like a prophecy
- Wife references should have subtle emotional weight
- Warnings should feel futile but sincere
- Post-quest dialogue should show genuine relief
- The pause before every warning is scripted, not performed — he knows the warning will fail before he gives it, and the pause is him deciding to give it anyway. Do not fill the pause with breath or business.
- "...Mapquest." is one word and a full stop. No inflection, no incredulity, no comic lift. The flatness is the joke.
- He never sounds like he is performing Irishness. If a take drifts toward tourist-brochure lilt, the take is wrong — the location file's satirical target is tourist arrogance, and the accent must never become the punchline.
