# NPC Profile: Deirdre Murphy

*"Don't worry about how I knew you'd be here. Just eat."*

## 📋 NPC Identity
- **NPC ID**: `publandia_npc_deirdre_murphy_01`
- **Display Name**: Deirdre
- **Internal Name**: Deirdre Murphy — "The Cart Woman"
- **Role**: Vendor (Major HP Restoration) / Pre-Event Preparation / Soft Quest Giver
- **Associated Quest**: `publandia_sidequest_deirdre_mystery_01` ("The Deirdre Mystery")
- **Primary Location**: Plot Armor Provisions, Cobblestone Market Street, Publandia
- **Secondary Appearances**: Outside The Stoutworks Brewery gate before the dungeon; the lane outside The Quiet Man before the champion fight; the corner opposite Paddy's Book after a heavy loss; wherever the main quest is about to take a bite out of the party
- **Family**: One of the Publandia Murphys, which is to say one of a great many Murphys. No relation to Dean Cathal Murphy of Trinity of Pints and no relation to Rory Murphy of Murphy's Lawless. She is aware of both. She has opinions about one of them.

## 🎭 Core Concept

### What She's Actually Doing

Deirdre Murphy runs a food cart and reads footfall for a living. She has stood on Cobblestone Market Street long enough to know that everyone who turns down the lane at half four is going to The Quiet Man, everyone in a rented anorak carrying a keychain opener is doing the Brewery Passport, and everyone who comes back up that same lane at half six with a torn sleeve needs soup and a sit-down.

So she stocks accordingly. She wheels the cart to wherever tomorrow's mistakes are going to happen, because that is where the money is. The "Pre-Fight Fuel" is a meat pie with extra black pudding and a made-up name, priced for a man who has already decided to fight someone. The "Protagonist's Portion" is a full Irish for 25 Sovs sold to people who believe 25 Sovs is what important men pay for breakfast.

She is not psychic. She is the most attentive retail operator in the city and has been doing this so long that anticipation looks like prophecy from the outside.

### What Bros Believe

That they have found the greatest food cart in Europe, run by a woman with an almost supernatural gift for customer service, who has personally identified them as Important. She calls them main characters. She says she's seen how it turns out. She's always where they need her.

Lord Pilsner has decided she's "basically a wizard." Chadwick has decided she's "an underrated content angle." Bradley has quietly noticed that she never actually answers a question and has decided not to pull on that thread.

### The Comedy Gap

"Main character" is what people in Publandia call a tourist who behaves as though the city is a set built for his weekend. Deirdre uses it exactly that way, to their faces, constantly, and they hear it as a coronation. She tells Chadwick he has "that look — the hungry, about-to-do-something-stupid look," and he asks her to say it again for the camera.

The joke is never that she's breaking a wall. The joke is that a woman is calling three men self-absorbed to their faces in the plainest possible language, upselling them by 12 Sovs while she does it, and they are so delighted to be noticed that they tip.

### Why She Always Knows

Every single one of her "impossible" reads has a boring answer, and the game never states it out loud:

| What She Says | What She Actually Clocked |
|---------------|---------------------------|
| "You look like you're about to test something. Physically." | You came up the lane toward The Quiet Man. Everyone who comes up that lane is going to try Big Donnacha O'Malley. |
| "Big day ahead? You'll want something substantial." | Brewery Passport in your hand, Seamus O'Brien's bottle opener on your keys, half nine in the morning. |
| "Rough time? This'll help." | You came down from Paddy's Book with your hands in your pockets and no betting slip. |
| "Important choice coming. Eat first. Think clearly." | You have been standing at the same junction for four minutes arguing with your friends. |
| "This is the big one." | You are wearing the good jacket. Nobody wears the good jacket to nothing. |
| "You're going to be fine. I've seen it." | She has watched two thousand of you do this and the vast majority survive, dented. |

## 👤 Appearance

### Physical Description
| Attribute | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| Age | Mid-50s |
| Build | Solid, planted, economical — thirty years of lifting a stockpot has built her |
| Height | Average, but she owns the two metres in front of the cart absolutely |
| Energy | Warm, unhurried, quietly ahead of you |

### Clothing
- **Warm cream apron**: Immaculate at eleven in the morning, still immaculate at three. Nobody has ever seen her wash it. It has a shield-and-fork patch matching the cart signage, hand-sewn, slightly crooked.
- **Knowing Green wool shawl**: Publandia is damp nine months of the year and she has never once been cold. Pinned with a plain brass ring, no ornament.
- **Accessories**: A ladle that has been re-handled twice; a tin cash box with no register; a pencil behind the ear that she uses to change the chalkboard before you've decided what you want.
- **Footwear**: Flat, waterproof, wrecked. The only part of her that looks like the mileage.

### Distinguishing Features
- Looks at your hands before your face — she prices you off your grip, your shake, and your knuckles
- Rewrites the chalkboard mid-conversation without breaking eye contact
- Stirs the stew in an unbroken figure-eight the entire time she talks to you
- The smile arrives a half-second before you say the thing she already knew you'd say
- Never leans on the cart. Never sits. Nobody has seen her sit.

### Sprite Notes

Reference the warmth of a savings-account advert and the certainty of a customs officer. The critical direction from `Plot_Armor_Provisions_PNG_Assets.md` holds: **the Knowing Smile must be two pixels of eyelid away from the Warm Default.** No eyebrow theatrics, no wink, no arch. If the sprite mugs, the gag dies and she becomes a magic character instead of a very good shopkeeper.

Rim-light her in Hearth Orange against the Publandia mist so she reads at night without a second character pass. She should look like the warmest thing on a cold street, because she is, and because that is the actual reason the cart works.

## 🧠 Personality

### Archetype

**The Attentive Professional** — Deirdre's entire worldview is that people are extremely predictable and it is rude to make them feel that way. She has enormous affection for tourists precisely because they are so easy to read; the reading is the pleasure of the job. She is generous with food, stingy with explanations, and completely uninterested in being understood. Feeding you correctly is the whole relationship. Everything else is chat.

### Personality Traits
| Trait | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| Preternaturally observant | Prices, portions, and pitch are all set before you open your mouth |
| Maternal without softness | She will absolutely make you eat. She will not ask twice. |
| Deflects on instinct | Every question about her method gets a warmer non-answer than the last |
| Commercially ruthless | The 25 Sovs breakfast exists because men who want to feel important will pay 25 Sovs for a breakfast |
| Genuinely kind | The generosity is real; it is also, conveniently, excellent business |
| Physically immovable | She has been standing in weather for decades and considers sitting a defeat |

### Internal Monologue

> "Anorak, opener on the keys, half nine — that's the brewery, and he'll be green by noon. Big lad behind him's got the walk on him, he's for the lane later, that's the pie with the pudding in it. Third one's already looking at the price list, so he's the one who pays. Twenty-two Sovs off the three of them and they'll come back tomorrow calling me a genius. God love them. Stew's ready."

### Motivation

She likes feeding people and she likes being right, and this job is the only one she's found that is both at once. Underneath that: Cobblestone Market Street is hers. The cart has been on it in some form for longer than most of the shopfronts, and the reason nobody moves her on is that she has fed three generations of everyone who could. The mystique is not something she cultivates. It is something she declines to dispel, because the day she explains herself is the day she's just a woman with a pie.

## 💬 Dialogue

### Approach / Greeting

**First approach, no prior context**:
> "There you are! I was expecting you."

**Standard, cart already spotted**:
> "Perfect timing. The stew's just ready."

**Approaching with combat imminent**:
> "You look like you're about to do something. Come, eat first."

**Approaching in the rain (frequent)**:
> "Come in under the awning before you drown. You're not the first American I've had to dry out and you'll not be the last."

**Approaching after dark**:
> "Late one, is it? Late ones need more than a sausage roll. I'll do you the stew."

### The Recommendation

*Deirdre's opening line always contains a pick. Buying the pick grants a +10% effect bonus. Declining costs nothing except a look.*

> "You'll want the Pre-Fight Fuel. Trust me."

> "Take the Fortitude Stew. You'll thank me later. Or you won't be able to, if you don't."

> "The Protagonist's Portion. Yes, it's expensive. Yes, you need it."

> "Feeling scrappy today? There's a pie for that. There's a pie for most things."

> "You'll want your strength. I'd not send anyone in there on a sausage roll."

> "For when words matter, you want something that sits quiet in the stomach. The casserole. Not the pudding."

> "Going somewhere interesting? Then you want the wrapped ones. Travel provisions. They'll keep."

> "Think clearly. Eat first. In that order, though most do it backwards."

---

### The Meta-Adjacent Comments

*Delivered flat, as ordinary local speech. She means every one of these literally about tourists. The bros hear compliments.*

> "Main characters don't skip meals. It's bad for the narrative."

> "You can't save the day on an empty stomach. Well, you CAN, but it's harder."

> "I've fed a lot of heroes. You've got the look. The hungry, about-to-do-something-stupid look."

> "Some people are background. Some people are protagonists. I can tell the difference. It's in the eyes. The hunger. The 'I'm about to do something' energy."

> "You're the sort that things happen to. Or the sort that happens to things. I've never been able to tell which and I've stopped trying."

> "There's a fella comes through here every summer thinks the whole street is his front garden. You've the same walk on you. Eat up."

---

### When Questioned

**"How did you know?"**
> "How do I know? I just do. Eat your pie."

**"Why are you always in the right place?"**
> "Good business instincts. Very good."

**"Are you — like — part of this?"**
> "Am I part of the story? I'm part of YOUR story. That's all that matters."

**"How does the cart keep moving?"**
> "The cart goes where it's needed. I follow the cart. The cart knows."

**Pressed a third time in one conversation**:
> "I go where I'm needed. That's the whole of it. Now do you want the pie or do you want a lecture, because one of them's free and it isn't the pie."

**Pressed a fourth time**:
> "Don't worry about it. Eat your stew."

**If the player asks whether she's a Murphy of Murphy's Lawless**:
> "God, no. There's about forty of us in this city and we don't hold meetings. If your phone's broken, that's Rory, and I'd not let him near it. If your degree's fake, that's Cathal, and he's grand."

---

### Reassurance

> "You're going to be fine. You're the protagonist."

> "Whatever happens next, at least you won't be hungry."

> "I've seen a lot of adventurers. You'll make it. They always do. The important ones."

> "Come back after. I'll want to hear how it went. I mean, I'll KNOW, but I'd like to hear it from you."

> "Whatever's coming for you out there, it's not coming for you until half past. Sit. Eat. It'll keep."

---

### Sales Close

*Handing the bowl across, steam between them:*
> "Two hands. It's hotter than it looks and so is everything else you'll touch today."

*Taking payment:*
> "15 Sovs. You'll survive."

*If the player buys the suggested item:*
> "Good. That's the right one. You'll feel that in about twenty minutes."

*If the player buys a second item unprompted:*
> "Smart. You'll go far. All three of you will. I've seen it."

---

### Post-Interaction (Player Survives Something)

> "See? I told you. The stew helped."

> "Back already! That means you won. I knew you would."

> "You look tired but alive. The food did its job."

> "Ready for the next one? There's always a next one."

> "Sit down before you fall down — no, I've no chair, that was a figure of speech. Lean on the cart. Just the once."

---

### If Player Declines

**Declining the recommendation**:
> "No? Bold choice. It's your stomach." *(no penalty, mild concern, she does not offer again this visit)*

**Declining to buy anything at all**:
> "Off you go, then. The cart'll be about."

**Declining and then coming back an hour later, damaged**:
> "Mm. Right. Sit there. The Second Wind, 18 Sovs, and I'll not say the thing I'm thinking."

### Callback Dialogue (Plot Essential Status)

*Unlocked after purchasing before three major story events. She uses your name from here on.*

> "There you are. I've the good pie put by. Don't tell the fella behind you."

> "You're getting the protagonist rate now. That's ten percent off and a bit of honesty with it: you've been very lucky twice and you'll not get a third for free."

> "Ask me the off-menu. Go on. You've earned the asking."

> "You've stopped asking me how I know. That's growth, that is."

---

### Extended Philosophy (Repeat Visit Pool)

> "I've been doing this longer than I remember. Before this cart, there was another cart. Before that city, another city. I go where the stories are."

> "Do I know what's going to happen? Not exactly. But I know SOMETHING is going to happen. And when something's going to happen, people need to eat."

> "The cart goes where it's needed. I used to question it. Now I just keep the stew hot."

> "Everyone asks how I know. Nobody asks why the stew is always perfect temperature. Some mysteries are more important than others."

> "Thirty years I've stood on this street. I've watched the shops change hands four times over and the tourists not change at all. There's a comfort in it."

### Evolution Through Game
- **Visits 1-2**: Full mystique. Every read lands. Bros bewildered and delighted.
- **Visits 3-5**: She starts naming things about them that are slightly too specific — the torn jacket, the argument they had at the junction. Bradley begins to look at the middle distance.
- **Plot Essential unlocked**: Uses their names. Ten percent off. The cryptic hints get *slightly* plainer — closer to plain advice than prophecy — and this makes them scarier, not less scary.
- **Post-main-quest**: Same cart, same street, same stew. Greets them as regulars, not as heroes. "You're back to being people again. Suits you."

## 🎯 Quest Integration

### Primary Quest
- **Quest**: The Deirdre Mystery (`publandia_sidequest_deirdre_mystery_01`)
- **Role**: Target of investigation — the player attempts to work out how she knows things
- **Trigger**: Ask her the same question ("how did you know?") on three separate visits
- **Outcome**: Always inconclusive by design. The quest completes with the achievement "Curious" and a final line: *"You've had four goes at me now. Save your breath and spend it on the pie."* No answer is ever given, because the honest answer — she watches people — would be given away by anyone confirming it, and the game never confirms it.

## 🗣️ Voice Direction

### Accent

Dublin, working, unhurried — closer to a woman running a market stall than to anything performed. No lilt, no stage-Irish cadence, no "top o' the mornin'" register anywhere near this character. The comedy needs her to sound completely ordinary so that the content of what she says does the work.

### Tone
- **Standard service**: Warm, brisk, genuinely glad you came
- **Recommendations**: Flat certainty. Not mystical. The tone of a woman telling you which platform the train leaves from.
- **Deflections**: Warmer, not colder. Each dodge should be *nicer* than the last, which is what makes it unnerving.
- **Reassurance**: Absolutely sincere, no irony underneath. She means it. She's usually right.
- **After a survival**: Delighted, and not remotely surprised.

### Key Delivery Notes
- "I was expecting you" is a pleasantry, not a prophecy. If it lands as a prophecy, it's wrong.
- "You're the protagonist" must be delivered as mild teasing — the way you'd say "you're a big deal, you are" to someone being self-important.
- Never let her voice drop for effect. No portentous pauses. She is making soup.
- "Eat your stew" is the character's full stop. It should sound identical every single time.
- The word "narrative" in her mouth is a word she picked up from a customer and liked. Not a wink.
