### The Nora Barnacle Connection
The café is named after Nora Barnacle, James Joyce's wife and muse. She was a Galway woman who famously eloped with Joyce, inspired much of his work, and was known for her earthy wit and directness. The café owner (also named Nora) claims distant relation. This is unverified.

## Satirical Design
### Theme & Vibe
**Core Concept**: Café NORA is a satirical collision between Dublin's genuine literary heritage and modern digital narcissism. It's named after Joyce's muse but primarily used for uploading selfies and chatting on ICQ. The pretension is thick — quotes on walls, literary ambiance, "Ulysses Blend" coffee — but the reality is tourists checking Instagram and bros messaging the Sauce Apostates.

The joke operates on the gap between what people THINK they're doing (participating in literary Dublin) and what they're ACTUALLY doing (the same thing they'd do at any Starbucks, but with more guilt).

### The Nora Philosophy
Nora Fitzgerald (the owner) may or may not be related to Nora Barnacle. She's heard every Joyce joke, endured every tourist asking "what's Ulysses about," and watched countless people photograph her bookshelves without buying anything. She provides the Wi-Fi, the coffee, and the aesthetic. What you do with it is your business. She just wishes someone would actually READ something.

### Cultural Targets
- **Primary Satirical Focus**: Literary tourism as performance, digital narcissism in cultural spaces
- **Secondary Targets**: Internet café culture, "digital nomad" pretension, selfie obsession
- **Cultural Specificity**: Joyce worship without Joyce reading, Dublin literary brand
- **Humor Approach**: High-culture aesthetic, low-culture usage
- **Sensitivity Considerations**: Satirizes tourist behavior, not Irish literature itself

### Seedy Underbelly Elements
- **Primary Vice**: Pretension without substance
- **City-Specific Comedy Operations**: Exploiting literary heritage for Wi-Fi fees
- **Buffoon Exploitation**: Bros who think coffee shop = culture absorbed
- **Comedy of Errors**: Literary posing, selfie obsession, ICQ addiction
- **Consequence System**: Bravado buffs from selfies, rumor pings for quests

### Buffoon Delusion Scenarios
**What bros THINK is happening**: Absorbing Dublin's literary culture, being intellectual

**What is ACTUALLY happening**: Using Wi-Fi to upload selfies and chat with scammers

**How impaired are they**: Usually sober (it's a café), but hungover often

**The café journey**:
1. "A LITERARY café! This is SO PUBLANDIA!"
2. "I should take a photo with the Joyce quotes. For CULTURE."
3. "Let me just upload this real quick..."
4. "Oh, ICQ notification from the Sauce Apostates!"
5. *3 hours later, still on computer*
6. "I feel so... CONNECTED to Irish literature now."

**The transformation**:
- Enter seeking culture
- Photograph the bookshelves
- Never touch a book
- Upload 47 selfies
- Chat on ICQ for hours
- Leave feeling literary
- Have read nothing

**How locals interact**: Actual writers work here silently, judging everyone

## NPCs & Characters
### The Proprietor

**Nora Fitzgerald**
- **Role**: Owner, barista, literary guardian, Wi-Fi password keeper
- **Appearance**: 40s Irish woman, practical elegance, perpetual slight disappointment
- **Background**: Literature degree, opened café to "promote culture," now provides Wi-Fi
- **Demeanor**: Warm but weary, hopeful but realistic, watches people photograph books
- **The Nora Method**: Provide the aesthetic, accept the reality, occasionally recommend a book
- **Philosophy**: "Joyce wrote about ordinary people doing ordinary things. I suppose selfies are ordinary now. He'd probably understand. He'd definitely judge."

**Nora's Dialogue Categories**:

*The Welcome:*
- "Welcome to Café NORA. Named for Nora Barnacle, Joyce's wife and muse. Coffee?"
- "Wi-Fi password changes hourly. Current one's on the board. It's a Joyce reference. Always is."
- "Computer time? 3 Sovs for thirty minutes. The terminals in the back have the best light for... whatever you're doing."

*Coffee Orders:*
- "Ulysses Blend? Bold choice. Like the book. Have you read it? ...no one has."
- "Finnegans Wake? Three shots. You'll either achieve enlightenment or heart palpitations. Possibly both."
- "Nora's House Blend. My own recipe. My grandmother's, actually. She never met Joyce. Neither did I. Probably."

*Observations:*
- "You've been photographing the bookshelf for ten minutes. Feel free to actually take one down."
- "The Joyce quotes on the wall? Mostly accurate. I made up one to see if anyone notices. No one has."
- "Yes, that's a real photo of Nora Barnacle. No, I'm not definitely related. But I'm not definitely NOT related."

*Literary Moments:*
- "Someone actually read a book today. First time this week. I marked it on my calendar."
- "You want to know what Ulysses is about? It's about one day in the city. June 16, 1904. Everything and nothing happens. It's very long. Start with Dubliners."
- "Joyce and Nora's first date was June 16th. That's why Bloomsday. Now you know more than most tourists."

*On the Digital Reality:*
- "I opened this café to promote literature. Now I provide Wi-Fi for selfies. Joyce would appreciate the irony. He loved irony."
- "ICQ chats? In my literary café? ...yes, the terminals are that way."
- "The 'digital nomads' never buy books. But they buy a LOT of coffee. Literature survives on espresso now."
