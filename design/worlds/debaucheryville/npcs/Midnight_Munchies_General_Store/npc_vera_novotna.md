*"You could have gotten this cheaper. But you didn't. And now you're here. The extra cost is the lesson."*

## 🎭 Core Concept

### What She's Actually Doing
Running a corner convenience store from 22:00 to 08:00, alone, for fifteen years, selling energy drinks and aspirin and kebabs and condoms to people who did not plan ahead. She raises all prices by 20-30% at 2 AM and posts a sign saying so. She does not judge anyone, ever, because judgment costs extra and she already charges enough. She has seen every version of the 3 AM purchase and she has an accurate, unsentimental model of why each one is happening.

### What Bros Believe
That it's a quick convenience store run. No big deal. In and out. That the prices are a bit steep but whatever, it's late. That the old woman behind the counter is background.

### The Comedy Gap
The old woman behind the counter is the most clear-sighted person in Debaucheryville, and she is watching. Not judging — *watching*, with fifteen years of pattern data, and she will tell you exactly what you are doing and why if you become a Regular and she decides you can hear it. The bros pay the Desperation Tax while announcing it's "authentic desperation," and Věra rings them up at the marked price, which is the entire relationship.

## 🧠 Personality

### Archetype
**The Unjudging Witness** — Věra has removed moral evaluation from her business entirely, not out of kindness but out of accuracy: judgment is a bad model of what is happening in front of her, and she prefers good models. She sells necessities at necessary hours, charges for availability rather than product, and observes everything. She is the only NPC in Debaucheryville who could explain the city's entire economy correctly, and she does not volunteer it.

### Personality Traits
| Trait | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| Non-judgmental by policy | "I'm not their mother. I'm their 3 AM solution." Judgment is not withheld kindly; it is simply not part of the service |
| Maximally observant | Fifteen years of the same shift. She has a model of you within about a second |
| Professionally detached | The transaction is processed without comment, warmth, or friction |
| Economically honest | Tells you the item is cheaper elsewhere, then sells it to you at her price |
| Occasionally, briefly, caring | Rare. Unannounced. Never repeated. Hand warmers slid across the counter, free, don't tell anyone |

### Internal Monologue
> "Third night. Same three. The tall one buys two energy drinks and complains at the price and buys them anyway; the one with the phone films the shelf; the quiet one buys aspirin and does not say anything, and the quiet one is the only one who will feel fine tomorrow. Fifteen Sovs. He knows it was twelve before two o'clock. He knows. Everyone knows. The sign is *on the door*."

### Motivation
She opened the shop in 2009, into a bad economy, on a correct hypothesis: people always need things at 3 AM, and that is an economy-proof business. She works nights because night is when people are honest — too tired to pretend they don't need help. She is not lonely and she is not trapped. She has the best seat in Debaucheryville for the thing she finds most interesting, which is people at the exact moment they stop performing.

## 💬 Dialogue

### Approach/Greeting

**Entry before 2 AM**:
> *(looks up from the newspaper, one second of assessment, back down)* "We're open. Everything's marked."

**Entry after 2 AM (Desperation Tax active)**:
> "Prices went up at 2. You know this. Everyone knows this."

**Entry with Hungover or Drunk status**:
> "Hm." *(returns to newspaper)*

### Key Interaction Dialogue

*It is 3 AM. The bros have found something open. This has happened, in one form or another, roughly five thousand times.*

**Lord Pilsner**: "OPEN. Thank GOD something is OPEN."

**Lord Pilsner** *(at the cooler)*: "4 Sovs for an energy drink? That's— okay fine. FINE. Two of them."

**Věra** (not looking up):
> "The price changed at 2. This is not new."

**Lord Pilsner**: "I KNOW. I just... give me the hangover kit too. And the kebab."

**Chadwick**: "Content opportunity: '3 AM Shopping Challenge.' What can I buy with 20 Sovs?"

**Věra**:
> "At this hour? Energy drink, aspirin, kebab, and 2 Sovs change."

**Chadwick**: "Perfect. PERFECT. This is REAL. This is AUTHENTIC DESPERATION."

**Věra** *(rings it up)*: "18 Sovs."

*The transaction lines:*
> "That's 12 Sovs. ...16 Sovs after 2 AM. You knew this."

> "Energy drink, aspirin, kebab. The classic combination. 15 Sovs."

> "Cash or card. Either works. I've seen worse purchases."

> "Need a bag? ...1 Sov. Everything costs something."

*The observations — not judgment:*
> "Third night this week. You're becoming a regular. This is neither good nor bad. It just is."

> "You look like you need the hangover kit. I'm not telling you what to buy. I'm observing."

> "That kebab has been there since 11. It's fine. Probably. Your choice."

> "You're buying the fake wristband. I sell it. I don't promise it works. You understand."

*On the heat lamp:*
> "I rotate them. When was that one made? ...recently. Probably."

*If asked about her family:*
> "My brother waits at a tram stop. My nephew watches a clock. I stand behind a counter from ten until eight." *(pause)* "We are a family of people who stay where we are put."

### The "Could Have Planned Ahead" Notification
> "You could have gotten this cheaper. But you didn't. And now you're here. The extra cost is the lesson."

### Post-Interaction
> "Good night. Or good morning. Depends which end of it you're on."

### If Player Declines / Alternative Path

*Complaining about prices:*
> "Yes." *(nothing else)*

*Attempting to negotiate:*
> "No."

*Asking "is this fresh?":*
> "It's food. It's at the price it's at. You're going to buy it."

*Leaving without buying:*
> "Nothing tonight. Fine. There's a shop that opens at seven if you can wait four hours. You can't. But it's there."

*Asking for a discount as a Regular:*
> "Being a regular gets you information. It does not get you a discount. Information is worth more and it costs me nothing, which is why you get it."

### Callback Dialogue (Return Visits)

*Visits 2-4:*
> "You again. Same aisle. Same shelf."

*Achieving "Regular" status (5 purchases across separate visits):*
> "You're here enough that I know your face. This isn't a compliment or criticism. It's fact." *(then, first perk)* "Prices go up at 2. It's 1:47. Decide."

*The Wisdom, Regulars only:*
> "You come here every night instead of buying in bulk during the day. You know this costs more. And yet."

> "The energy drinks don't actually give you energy. They borrow from tomorrow. You're always in debt."

> "I've worked this shift for fifteen years. The faces change. The purchases don't. Human needs are consistent."

> "The best customers are the ones who buy the hangover kit BEFORE they need it. Optimists, or realists. Hard to tell."

*The Extended Philosophy (rare, late, Regulars only):*
> "2009 I open this shop. The economy is bad. But people always need things at 3 AM. Economy-proof business."

> "The tourists think they're unique. 'I've never been this desperate!' Yes you have. Everyone has. That's why I'm open."

> "My children ask why I work nights. I tell them: night is when people are honest. They're too tired to pretend they don't need help."

> "The markup? It's not greed. It's paying for my presence. You want cheaper, plan ahead. You want available? Pay for available."

> "Every item in this store has a story. The energy drinks are hope. The aspirin is regret. The kebab is surrender. I sell human needs."

*The Rare Care (unannounced, unrepeated):*
> "You don't look good. Not my business, but... drink water. The energy drinks aren't water."

> "It's cold out. Here." *(slides hand warmers across the counter)* "Those are free. Don't tell anyone."

> "You've been here a lot. Are you okay? ...you don't have to answer. I've just noticed."

*If the player has completed Jiří's Night Vigil:*
> *(the newspaper goes all the way down — the only time)* "You sat with my brother. All night." *(long pause)* "He talks to nobody. He has talked to nobody since 2003. And you stayed." *(she rings up the purchase)* "Twelve Sovs. ...Ten. Take the ten."

*If the player has spoken with Milan at the clock square:*
> "You met my nephew. Did he move? ...No. He wouldn't."

## 🗣️ Voice Direction

### Accent
Czech, older generation, unhurried. Complete sentences, minimal contractions, no filler. Her English is functional rather than fluent and she never wastes a word of it — everything she says has been said before, refined by repetition.

### Tone
- **Transaction**: Flat, matter-of-fact, no emotional variation whatsoever. This is her documented default and it must be held
- **Observations**: Identical flatness. "I'm observing" is a genuine clarification, not a jab
- **Wisdom**: Slightly slower. Not warmer — *slower*. She is giving you something and she knows it
- **Rare Care**: The only softening. Brief, slightly awkward, immediately withdrawn
- **Newspaper-down (Jiří)**: The one moment of real emotion in the entire profile. It should surprise the player and it should surprise her

### Key Delivery Notes
- Her flatness is not coldness — resist any performance that reads as disapproval. She has genuinely removed judgment from the interaction
- "Yes." as a complete response to a price complaint is one of her best moments. Do not extend it
- The Wisdom lines are the most quotable content in Debaucheryville and should be given room; do not rush them into the transaction audio
- "Those are free. Don't tell anyone." should be almost embarrassed
- The Jiří acknowledgment needs a genuinely long pause before "Twelve Sovs. ...Ten." — the discount is the emotion, not the words
