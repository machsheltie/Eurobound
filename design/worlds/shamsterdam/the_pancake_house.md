# The Pancake House

*Where American expectations meet Dutch reality — 18 Sovs for something that's basically a crepe, and no, we don't have IHOP syrup*

## Satirical Design
### Theme & Vibe
**Core Concept**: The collision between American pancake expectations (thick, fluffy, stacked, drowned in syrup) and Dutch pancake reality (thin, plate-sized, savory options, portioned syrup). The Pancake House exists to serve tourists what they asked for while giving them what they didn't know they were asking for. Every disappointed American saying "but I wanted PANCAKES" while looking at a crepe the size of a hubcap is the business model.

### Cultural Targets
- **Primary Satirical Focus**: American food expectations abroad, the assumption that familiar words mean familiar food
- **Secondary Targets**: Tourist trap pricing on "authentic" food, canal-side premium markup, the Dutch sweet-savory confusion
- **Cultural Specificity**: Dutch pannenkoeken are genuinely different from American pancakes — this is not a scam, it's a cultural mismatch
- **Humor Approach**: The bros expecting IHOP and receiving something closer to a savory crepe with bacon
- **Sensitivity Considerations**: Targets expectation mismatch and tourist behavior, not Dutch cuisine (which is legitimately delicious)

### Seedy Underbelly Elements
- **Primary Vice**: Charging 18 Sovs for what costs 3 Sovs to make because it's on a canal
- **City-Specific Comedy Operations**: "Authentic Dutch Experience" at "Authentic Tourist Prices"
- **Buffoon Exploitation**: Bros order "with bacon" expecting American bacon strips, receive Dutch bacon
- **Comedy of Errors**: Every modification attempt creates new confusion ("more syrup" = 3 Sovs extra)
- **Consequence System**: Ordering wrong results in food that's technically correct but disappointing

### Buffoon Delusion Scenarios
**What bros THINK is happening**: Having classic pancake breakfast, carb-loading for the day

**What is ACTUALLY happening**: Paying 18 Sovs for a thin pancake with toppings they didn't understand

**How impaired are they**: Usually hungover (this is breakfast after Shamsterdam night), craving comfort food

**Obvious signs they ignore**:
- Menu photos show THIN pancakes
- No "stack" options visible
- Prices are per SINGLE pancake
- "Maple-flavored syrup" is not maple syrup
- The Dutch word "pannenkoek" (singular) is everywhere
- Other tourists already looking confused at their plates

**How locals play along**: Staff are used to this, have mastered the art of "that's how we make them" with a smile

### The Syrup Situation (Mini-System)
- **Expectation**: Bottle of maple syrup, unlimited
- **Reality**: Small pitcher of "stroop" (Dutch syrup, thicker, less maple)
- **More Syrup Request**: 3 Sovs extra, same small pitcher
- **American Syrup Request**: "We have maple-FLAVORED syrup" (corn syrup adjacent)
- **Bros' Reaction**: Escalating disbelief, eventual acceptance
- **Cultural Learning Moment**: Dutch stroop is actually excellent, just different

### The Portion Revelation
- **Trigger**: First pancake arrives
- **Visual**: Single pancake, plate-sized, thin, with arranged toppings
- **Bros' Expected Reaction**: Stack of fluffy circles
- **Bros' Actual Reaction**: "Is this... is this all of it?"
- **Staff Response**: "This is one Dutch pancake. Traditional size. Very filling." (it is filling)
- **Gameplay Effect**: "Expectation Adjustment" status — slightly wiser about European food terms

### The Bill Breakdown
- **Base Order**: Pancake + toppings shown
- **Hidden Additions**: Terrace heater fee (if applicable), bread basket (automatic, 4 Sovs), service charge
- **The Bread Basket Trap**: Arrives automatically, charged automatically, 4 Sovs
- **Revelation Moment**: Bill arrives, math happens, regret follows
- **Bravado Check**: Can contest charges (success = small discount, failure = awkward)

### The Bacon Specificity Problem
- **American Bacon**: Crispy strips, familiar
- **Dutch Bacon (Spek)**: Cubed, different texture, different flavor
- **Ordering "Bacon Pancake"**: Receive spek, experience confusion
- **Clarification Dialogue**: "Spek is Dutch bacon. Not strips. Cubes. Very traditional."
- **Effect**: Food is good, but not what was expected

### Bravado Interactions
- **Gain Bravado**: Accept Dutch pancake gracefully, learn "lekker" and use it correctly, finish plate without complaint
- **Lose Bravado**: Ask "where's the rest?", request "real" maple syrup, try to send back perfectly good food

## System Integration
### "Expectation Adjustment" Status Effect
- **Trigger**: Surviving Pancake House ordering
- **Effect**: Better understanding of European food terms going forward
- **Duration**: Permanent subtle buff
- **Practical Effect**: Less confusion at future restaurants, minor menu reading bonus

### Food Photography Consequences
- **Photo Taken**: Added to album, shareable
- **Caption Options**: "Dutch pancakes!" (neutral), "Basically a crepe" (honest), "Amazing!" (lying)
- **Social Response**: Varies by caption, honest caption gets -Bravado but +Authenticity

### Cross-Location Dependencies
- **The Tolerance Hostel**: Kitchen discussion "Did you go to the pancake place? Did they explain before you ordered?"
- **FEBO Automat**: Bros may compare value "At FEBO you KNOW what you're getting for 3 Sovs"
- **Café de Schaamte**: Espresso after pancakes, processing what happened
- **De Groene Gids**: Pre-pancake visit may affect appetite appreciation (munchies help)

### The Dutch Food Learning Curve
Pancake House contributes to larger system:
- **First Dutch Food Experience**: Confusion
- **Second**: Adjustment
- **Third**: Acceptance
- **Fourth**: "Actually it's pretty good"
- **Fifth**: Smugly explaining Dutch food to other tourists

### Time-Based Mechanics
- **Breakfast Rush (8-11 AM)**: Maximum expectation collision, American tourists dominant
- **Lunch Calm (11 AM-2 PM)**: Dutch families, more savory orders, less confusion
- **Afternoon Snack (2-5 PM)**: Sweet pancakes popular, more aligned with expectations
- **Dinner Service (5-10 PM)**: Full meals, combination orders, complex bills
