# NPC Profile: Gunter

*"My cart is clean. What happens at the fountain is between you and the fountain."*

## 📋 NPC Identity
- **NPC ID**: `shamsterdam_npc_gunter_01`
- **Display Name**: Gunter
- **Internal Name**: Gunter — owner and sole operator, **Gunter's Deep Dips**, the dipping-stick cart on the Venustempel fountain plaza. No surname is ever given on screen; the cart name is the brand and the brand is the whole identity. Twelve years on this pitch. He followed the fountain when the museum moved off Roodelicht Row in 2011; he traded outside the old site before that.
- **Role**: Vendor (street cart) / Mechanic Delivery Point (Sharing the Love cure item) / Queue Processing Machine
- **Associated Quest**: None. He is a permanent vendor and a mechanic delivery point. He gives nothing, asks nothing, and sells at a posted price all day.
- **Primary Location**: Venustempel fountain plaza, Museum Mile, Shamsterdam — four metres from **De Grote Fontein**, the four-metre working chocolate phallus fountain, on the corner where the ticket queue bends
- **Secondary Appearances**: None. One cart, one pitch, one permit.

## 🎭 Core Concept

### What He's Actually Doing

Running an extremely well-managed food cart beside a giant chocolate phallus fountain. De Grote Fontein is the Venustempel's outdoor centrepiece: four metres of cast bronze with warm couverture pumped up through the core and running down into a catch basin, continuously, from opening to close. It is the most photographed object in Museum Mile. Gunter sells things on wooden skewers so that tourists have something to dip into it.

That is the whole business and it is a very good business. Strawberries, marshmallows, rice krispie squares, banana, pineapple, all five SOVS. A bare stick for one SOV, which is his best margin by an enormous distance. A hand wipe for one SOV, which almost nobody buys. He moves about six hundred skewers on a Saturday, single-handed, without visible effort, and he has done it for twelve years.

His cart is spotless. His gloves are changed constantly. His tongs sit in sanitiser. His municipal hygiene card is laminated and taped to the side of the cart at eye level and it shows a straight **A**. He will point at it.

The fountain is not his. The fountain is a shared public chocolate cascade that several hundred strangers put their hands, sticks, phones and faces into every day. He knows exactly what the fountain is. He has zero stake in it, no authority over it, and no opinion he is willing to volunteer. He has never eaten from it.

### The Signage Does All The Work

The single most important thing about this character: **the menu board and the warning sign are relentlessly, gleefully filthy, and Gunter is completely matter-of-fact and never once acknowledges any of it.**

He does not leer. He does not smirk. He does not wink. He does not do a bit. He hand-lettered the board because it sells and he has not thought about it since. When a customer reads a line off the board out loud and laughs, Gunter says the price. That contrast is the joke. **If Gunter ever appears to be enjoying his own signage, the character has failed and the location fails with him.**

### What Bros Believe

**Lord Pilsner** has declared the fountain the single greatest thing Europe has produced, out loud, twice, and has committed to double-dipping on principle because "you cannot half-commit to a fountain." **Chadwick** is filming it from four angles and will at some point dip his phone case, then continue filming with a chocolate-coated phone case, then explain that the chocolate is part of it now. **Bradley** refuses to dip. Bradley has standards. Bradley eats his fruit dry off the skewer like a man at a wake, and still racks up Sharing the Love stacks, because Pilsner keeps handing him things.

All three believe Gunter is part of the attraction — that the board is a performance and he is performing it. He is not. He is a small business with a permit and a laminated A.

### The Comedy Gap

He is clean and the fountain is not, and nobody makes the connection. Gunter's entire operation is a monument to food hygiene parked four metres from a communal vat of double-dipped chocolate, and the two facts sit side by side all day without ever touching. He will answer any direct question about either of them honestly and completely, and it will change nothing, because nobody is listening — they are already walking toward the fountain with a skewer in their hand.

He is not warning anyone. He is not a victim of anything. He is not the punchline. He has excellent margins and a hygiene score of A and he finds these men tedious rather than offensive. There have been ten thousand of them.

### The Bare Skewer

One SOV. Nothing on it. It exists because people bring their own food, or bring nothing, and either way they need a skewer. It costs him about four cents. He sells eighty of them on a good day. He has never once explained why the empty item is on the board, and no bro has ever asked, because the bare skewer is the funniest thing on the menu and it is also the most profitable, and both of those are true at the same time, and he is the only person on the plaza who knows it.

## 👤 Appearance

### Physical Description
| Attribute | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| Age | Mid-to-late forties |
| Build | Big and slow-moving. Economical rather than heavy. **Forearms of a man who has hand-loaded four hundred skewers a day for twelve years** — the single most striking thing about him and the only part of the design that should read as "athletic." |
| Height | Tall, and broader than the cart wants him to be |
| Energy | Efficient, weatherproofed, faintly bored |

### Clothing
- **Apron over a fleece**: The fleece is the real garment; the apron is the job. Green-and-white to match the cart. Both are clean at 09:30 and clean at 22:30, which is a choice he makes hourly.
- **Cap**: Food-handling rules. Not a style decision and should not be drawn as one. There is nothing decorative anywhere on him.
- **Nitrile gloves**: Changed constantly, visibly, mid-conversation, without comment. The glove box is mounted on the cart within reach and it is never far from empty.
- **Reading glasses on a cord**: Used exclusively for the card reader. He puts them on to read a payment total and takes them off immediately. He does not read anything else at work — including, notably, his own board.
- **Footwear**: Standing shoes. Thick soled, waterproof, replaced twice a year. Thirteen hours on stone.

### Distinguishing Features
- **Moves without wasted motion.** Every action is the shortest version of itself. Load, present, take payment, next. There is no flourish anywhere in the animation set and no big man's heaviness either — he is slow because slow is efficient at this volume.
- **Looks up exactly once per customer**, at the moment of payment, and holds it for as long as the transaction takes and not one frame longer.
- **The point at the hygiene card.** A single economical gesture at the laminated municipal card taped to the cart. He does it when hygiene comes up and he does it without expression.
- **Never looks at the fountain.** Not once, in any frame, in any state. It is four metres away and behind his working shoulder and he has not looked at it in about nine years.
- **Never looks at his own board.** He knows what it says. He wrote it. He has not read it since he laminated it.

### Sprite Notes

Sheet `npc_venustempel_plaza.png`. Gunter anchors the plaza cast. Required frames: Idle (loading skewers, hands down, eyes down); Loading 1–3 (strawberry onto skewer, single motion, repeated); Presenting (skewer handed across, arm extended, eyes up — **this is the one frame where he makes eye contact**); Card Reader (glasses on, terminal turned, glasses off — three frames); Glove Change (two frames, fires mid-idle at random); Point At Card (held frame, one arm, flat face); Restocking (crouched at the under-cart store, back to the plaza, used during the Gerda closure window).

**Hard constraints for the artist:**
- **He never looks at the fountain in any frame.** Not a glance, not a background eyeline. If he looks at it even once, he becomes a commentator and the joke dies.
- **No smirk, no raised eyebrow, no knowing beat, ever.** Every reaction sprite on this plaza belongs to a tourist. Gunter's expression range is narrower than the sprite sheet's resolution can express and that is correct.
- **He is never drawn in the same frame as a customer laughing at the board, reacting.** If a laughing-tourist frame and Gunter are composited together, Gunter's face is the same face as the idle.
- The forearms are the character design. Draw the forearms.
- The cart is a co-star: green-and-white paint, spotless, awning, a radio, the laminated A card taped at eye level, **the hand-lettered menu board**, and **the bolted warning sign**. The board and the sign must be legible at native resolution — they are the funniest assets in the location and they carry the entire joke.

## 🧠 Personality

### Archetype

**The Competent Operator** — Gunter runs a good business in a strange place and does not consider the strangeness his problem. He is not stoic, not repressed, not secretly appalled. He is *at work*. Everything he is famous for — the flatness, the three-word sentences, the total refusal to editorialise about a giant chocolate phallus he has personally written five double entendres about — is not a defence mechanism. It is throughput. He has a queue.

### Personality Traits
| Trait | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| Efficient past the point of comedy | Three-word sentences, stated prices, a rhythm that does not break for anybody. Groups of loud men do not slow him down; he processes a stag party faster than it can be funny at him. |
| Deadpan without effort | He is not withholding a reaction. There is no reaction being withheld. This is the load-bearing distinction in the whole character. |
| Has heard every joke | Every one. Twelve years, six hundred skewers a Saturday. There is no line a customer can deliver at this cart that has not been delivered at this cart. He does not find this tiring; he finds it uninformative. |
| Scrupulous about his own operation | Gloves, tongs, temperatures, the A on the card. This is genuine professional pride and the only subject on which he has any warmth. |
| Completely indifferent to the fountain | It is not his fountain. He has no stake, no authority, and no opinion he will volunteer. He will answer questions about it accurately and then move on. |
| Unbothered by ten thousand identical men | Not offended, not amused, not wounded. Bored. The bros are the fourteenth iteration of the bros he processed this morning. |

### Internal Monologue

> "The loud one will read the marshmallow line out. They always read the marshmallow line out. Then he will look at his friends and then he will look at me, and I will say five. The one with the phone will buy nothing for four minutes and then buy something when he realises he is holding a phone and no skewer. The tall quiet one will take strawberries and he will not dip them, and the loud one will hand him things anyway, and he will hold them, because he does not know how to refuse a thing that is already in his hand. Five, five, five. Fifteen. Card. Wipe is one — no. Fine. Next. Two more groups behind them and then the tour bus at two. I need more skewers up from the box before the bus. The pineapple is going faster than the banana again, that is four weeks now, I will order differently."

### Motivation

The pitch. It is the best fountain-adjacent trading position in the country and he got it twelve years ago by being outside the old museum site when the fountain was there, and by moving the day the fountain moved. It is a licensed pitch, it is his, it prints money between one and five in the afternoon, and it takes one person to run. He has no ambition beyond keeping it, which is not a small ambition — the plaza is public, the permit is annual, and the municipality reviews pitches. Hence the A. The A is not a joke about hygiene. The A is why he still has the corner.

## 🪧 The Signage

Two laminated objects on the cart. Between them they are the crudest text in this document, in this location, and arguably in Museum Mile. Gunter wrote both, hand-lettered both, laminated both, mounted both, and has not looked at either since.

### The Menu Board

*Hand-lettered, laminated, mounted on the cart facing the queue at reading height. Reproduce verbatim in-game — this is authored copy and must not be paraphrased, softened, or trimmed.*

> **"Get them while they're hot and heavy- 5 SOVS"**
>
> **Organic Strawberries**
> *(Plump, firm, and begging to be absolutely smothered.)*
>
> **Pineapple Chunks**
> *(For a sweeter finish. You know why. We don't need to explain it to you.)*
>
> **Banana Slices**
> *(Just the tips. Yes, it's redundant. No, we don't judge.)*
>
> **Jumbo Marshmallows**
> *(Soft, sticky, and expands nicely in the mouth.)*
>
> **Rice Krispie Squares**
> *(Firm, rigid, and heavily textured for maximum friction against the flow.)*

### The Warning Sign To Patrons

*Bolted beside the board. Also verbatim.*

> **"Please maintain a firm, two-handed grip on your skewer at all times. Do NOT double-dip, do NOT stroke the fountain base, and absolutely NO open-mouth catching directly from the tip of the statue. We've had incidents."**

### "We've Had Incidents"

**Never explain it.** Not in dialogue, not in examine text, not in a codex entry, not in an ambient line from another NPC, not in the epilogue. No character in this game knows what the incidents were. The sign implies an entire history and the history does not exist and must never be written.

If the player asks Gunter directly, he gives a non-answer and takes the next order. Two of these are written into the dialogue below and neither reveals anything. Any future writer adding a third must hold the same line: the non-answer must not narrow the space. "It was a long time ago" is acceptable. "It was a school group" is not, because it is information.

### The Contradiction Nobody Mentions

The warning sign forbids double-dipping. **Everybody double-dips.** Universally, constantly, in full view of the sign, often while quoting the sign. The sign is ignored by every single person who reads it.

That contradiction is the germ mechanic's entire setup, and **Gunter never comments on it.** Not once. He does not enforce the sign, reference the sign, point at the sign, or acknowledge that anyone has ever violated the sign. He bolted it to the cart and moved on.

## 💬 Dialogue

### Approach/Greeting

**Standard, any customer, any time** *(he is loading skewers; he does not look up)*:
> "Board's there."

**If the player stands still for four seconds without ordering**:
> "Five a skewer. Strawberry, pineapple, banana, marshmallow, krispie. Stick on its own is one."
> *(Delivered in one breath, at speed, from memory, ninety times a day. Note what is missing: he recites the prices and the item names and none of the board's descriptions. He has never once read his own copy out loud.)*

**Peak hours, queue behind the player**:
> "Yes."
> *(That is the greeting. It means "order.")*

**First customer of the morning, 09:30, museum not yet open**:
> "Early." *(He keeps loading.)* "Doors are ten. Fountain's running already."

### Key Interaction Dialogue — The Board

*This is the location's signature exchange and it should be available from the very first interaction. The customer does the joke. Gunter does the price. The gap between those two things is the entire location.*

**Player reads the marshmallow line off the board, out loud**: "'Soft, sticky, and expands nicely in the mouth.'"

**Gunter** *(does not look up; does not pause loading; no change in tone whatsoever)*:
> "Five."

**Player**: "No, I'm — did you *write* that?"

**Gunter**:
> "Yes."

**Player**: "Why?"

**Gunter** *(the shortest possible true answer, delivered as a fact about inventory management)*:
> "They sell."

**Player reads the Rice Krispie line out to their friends, at volume, with a hand gesture**:
> *(Gunter waits. Fully. Without expression, without impatience, without giving them a single thing to work with. When they run out:)*
> "Five."

**Player asks whether he thinks the board is funny**:
> "I don't read it." *(Beat. He loads another skewer.)* "I wrote it in 2013."

**Player asks who does his signage**:
> "Me." *(He turns the card reader around without being asked to.)* "Two evenings. Laminator's from the museum shop."

**Player points at the banana line specifically**:
> "Five." *(No beat, no acknowledgement, no glance at the board.)* "Same as everything."

### Key Interaction Dialogue — The Warning Sign

**Player reads the warning sign and asks about it**:
> "It's a sign." *(He does not look at it.)* "Do you want a skewer."

**Player**: "What incidents?"

**Gunter** *(does not look up; the tone does not shift a millimetre)*:
> "Before your time."
> *(He is already loading the next skewer. That is the whole answer and there is not a second half.)*

**Player**: "No, seriously — what *incidents*?"

**Gunter** *(a beat, exactly one, which is not reluctance and is not a tease — it is a man deciding whether the queue can afford this)*:
> "Ask the museum." *(Beat.)* "Ruud's on the door."
> *(Ruud does not know either. Nobody knows. There is nothing to find.)*

**Player asks a third time, on a later visit**:
> "Five for a skewer."
> *(That is the response. It is not a deflection he is performing; it is the next transaction. He has moved on and the player has not.)*

**Player asks whether anyone actually follows the sign**:
> *(Nothing. He does not answer this one, because answering it would require him to have noticed. He hands over the skewer.)*
> "Five. Card or cash."

**Player asks why he bothered bolting up a sign nobody reads**:
> "Municipality likes signs." *(Beat.)* "It's bolted. It's been bolted twelve years."

### Key Interaction Dialogue — The Fountain

*The player asks any direct question about the fountain. He answers completely. It changes nothing.*

**Player**: "Is that thing clean?"

**Gunter** *(does not look up; one gloved hand indicates the laminated card taped to the cart)*:
> "My cart is clean. A. Every inspection."
> *(Beat. Same tone, no shift whatsoever.)*
> "What happens at the fountain is between you and the fountain."

**Player**: "But is it clean?"

**Gunter**:
> "Several hundred people a day put a stick in it." *(He loads another skewer.)* "Some of them put the same stick in twice."

**Player**: "Do you eat from it?"

**Gunter**:
> "No."

**Player**: "Why not?"

**Gunter** *(looks up. One second. Goes back to the skewers.)*:
> "I have seen the fountain."

**Player asks whether he likes working next to it**:
> "It's a good pitch." *(Beat.)* "Best in the country, probably."

**Player asks what the fountain is, in a leading way, hoping he will say something**:
> "It's a chocolate fountain. Four metres. Bronze underneath, couverture over." *(Beat.)* "It's shaped how it's shaped. Do you want a skewer or not."

**Player asks whether the museum pays him**:
> "No. I pay the municipality." *(He turns the card reader around.)* "Different building entirely."

### Key Interaction Dialogue — The Hygiene Card

**Player examines or asks about the laminated card**:
> *(Point At Card animation. One arm. Held. Flat face.)*
> "A. Twelve years." *(Beat.)* "Gloves are in the box. Tongs are in sanitiser. You can look."

**Player asks how his cart can be an A when the fountain is what it is**:
> "The fountain isn't my cart."
> *(That is the entire answer. He does not extend it, defend it, or enjoy it. It is a jurisdictional fact and he states it as one.)*

### Key Interaction Dialogue — The Bare Skewer

**Player selects the bare wooden skewer (1 SOV)**:
> "One."

**Player**: "There's nothing on it."

**Gunter**:

> "No." *(He is already looking at the next customer.)* "Next."

*Elapsed time: eleven seconds. He did not land a single line.*

### Positioning Notes
- Four metres from De Grote Fontein, on the corner where the ticket queue bends — he is in the path of everyone entering *and* everyone leaving, which is why the pitch is worth what it is worth
- He faces the queue, not the fountain. The fountain is behind his working shoulder, permanently out of his eyeline, and the sprite must respect this
- The plaza is public and never closes; the museum does. His hours are set by the museum's, not the plaza's
- The awning is angled against the prevailing wind off the canal, not for shade. Twelve years of data went into that angle
- **The menu board faces the queue at reading height. The warning sign is bolted beside it.** Both are readable from the back of a six-person queue, which is deliberate and is the only marketing decision he has ever made. The laminated hygiene card faces the *side*, at the point where customers stand to pay
- The radio plays Dutch talk radio at low volume. He does not listen to it. It has been on the same station for twelve years and he could not name the programme.

### Hidden Details / Easter Eggs
- **The two documents.** Examine the hygiene card. It is a real municipal food-hygiene certificate, current, in Dutch, with Gerda Vonk's signature at the bottom — **the same signature that appears on the fountain's standing violation notice bolted to the plinth four metres away.** The two documents are never mentioned in the same breath by anyone. The player has to notice.
- **The board's date.** Examine the menu board closely. Bottom right corner, small, in the same hand: *2013*. It has been laminated and remounted twice and never rewritten.
- **The bare-skewer counter.** The game silently tracks bare-skewer purchases across the save. It surfaces nowhere except the epilogue, where the number is stated without comment.
- **The radio.** Examine it. Dutch talk radio, low volume, mid-sentence, about municipal parking policy. Gunter has not heard a word of it in twelve years. It is the only object on the cart he has no opinion about.
- **The glove box.** Examine it during a peak hour and the count is visibly lower than it was an hour ago. He goes through about forty pairs on a Saturday.
- **The pineapple.** Ask him what sells best and he will say pineapple, correctly, and then say "four weeks now," which is the only piece of unprompted analysis he offers in the entire game.
- **The unbought wipes.** Examine the wipe box across multiple visits. It never goes down. It is restocked anyway, every morning, in full.
- **The bolts.** Examine the warning sign's fixings. Four bolts, over-specified, weathered, clearly original. Whatever the incidents were, he did not want the sign coming off.

## 🔄 Recurring Appearances

### Within Shamsterdam
- **First visit**: The board. The player reads it — the game should give them time to. The price recitation, which pointedly contains none of the board's copy. First flat wipe offer. First decline.
- **The board-read beat, any visit**: A customer reads a line out loud and laughs; Gunter says the price. Available from the first interaction, never removed, and it should fire with ambient NPCs even when the player is not the one reading.
- **The warning-sign beat, any visit**: "What incidents?" → a non-answer. Repeatable, and the answer narrows nothing on any repetition.
- **Any fountain question, any visit**: The signature exchange. "My cart is clean. What happens at the fountain is between you and the fountain."
- **The "I have seen the fountain" beat**: Requires the player to ask *why* he doesn't eat from it — a second-level question almost nobody asks. It is the single best line he has and it is gated behind curiosity.
- **Peak-hour set piece (13:00–17:00)**: The queue-processing scene. Loud men enter with material — usually the board's own copy — and he closes them out in eleven seconds. Replayable, and it lands harder the second time.
- **Post-Shamsterdam-Stomach return**: He sells the player exactly the same thing at exactly the same price in exactly the same tone. The lack of acknowledgement is the point.
- **Gerda's inspection window (quarterly)**: The fountain roped, the plaza confused, tourists milling, and Gunter crouched under the cart with his back to all of it, restocking. Best ambient shot in Museum Mile.
- **Late-game quiet hour (22:00)**: Break-down. The six-hundred line. The pineapple line. The twelve-years line. This is the only place he uses more than nine words at a stretch.

### Evolution Through Game
Gunter does not change. His total constancy is the instrument that measures the player's education, and he must never be seen to have taught it.

## 🎯 Quest Integration

### Primary Quest
**None.** Gunter is a permanent vendor and a mechanic delivery point. He gives no quests, gates no quests, and appears in no quest chain as a required step.

### Mechanic Delivery: Sharing the Love

Gunter is the delivery point for the cheapest cure in the **Sharing the Love** system. He does not own, explain, warn about, or reference the mechanic in any way. He sells skewers and he sells wipes. **His warning sign forbids exactly the actions that generate the most stacks, and he has never enforced or referenced it.**

**The system, referenced as authored — do not redesign here:**

| Action at the fountain | Stacks gained |
|------------------------|---------------|
| Dip | **+1** |
| Re-dip the same skewer (double-dip) | **+2** |
| Dip something already bitten | **+3** |
| Dip a bare hand | **+3** |
| Retrieve something from a collection bowl | **+3** |
| Lean into the cascade / open-mouth catching from the tip | **+5** |
| Share a skewer (applies to the recipient) | **+2** |

**Sharing the Love** is a stacking player-facing status, tiers 1–5+. **Reaching tier 5 triggers Shamsterdam Stomach, a day-long debuff.**

| Cure | Source | Removes |
|------|--------|---------|
| Hand wipe, 1 SOV | **Gunter's Deep Dips** | 1 stack |
| "Handreiniger" sanitiser, 4 Sovs | Venustempel gift shop (Lotte) | 2 stacks |
| A night's sleep | The Tolerance Hostel | 2 stacks |
| Febo Automat croquette | Febo Automat | 1 stack |

**His role in it, precisely:** he offers the wipe once, flatly, at the point of sale, in exactly the same tone he states the price. Nobody takes it. He restocks them anyway. He does **not** explain the mechanic, warn the player, connect symptoms to causes, escalate the offer as stacks accumulate, or react to a player carrying tier 5. If he ever appears to know what the wipe is for, the mechanic becomes a tutorial and the joke dies.

None. Gunter gates nothing, blocks nothing, and refuses nobody. Every item on his board is available from the first interaction at a fixed price with no requirements. ## 🗣️ Voice Direction

### Accent
Dutch, native, working Amsterdam register, fluent functional English. **Dutch-inflected, never phonetically written out** — no dropped articles for comic effect, no spelled-out accent, no "ja" sprinkled in. His English is completely competent and completely uninterested in being charming. He compresses sentences because he has a queue, not because he is struggling. A low, unhurried voice; a big man who has never needed to raise it.

### Tone
- **Order taking**: Flat, fast, factual. Prices are stated the way a clock states the time.
- **The price recitation**: One breath, at speed, ninety times a day. It should sound worn smooth, like a station announcement. **It contains none of the board's copy and he must never be heard to read the board aloud.**
- **Responding to a customer reading the board**: **This is the critical delivery note in the entire document.** The customer performs; Gunter says a number. Not a beat before it. Not a beat after it. No dryness added, no comic timing applied — comic timing implies awareness. He is answering a question that was not asked, because the only question a customer at a food cart can be asking is "how much."
- **"We've had incidents" deflections**: Nothing withheld. He is not protecting a secret, he is not being coy, and he is not enjoying the player's curiosity. He simply does not have this conversation, in the same way he does not have a conversation about the weather. Play it as scheduling, not mystery.
- **The wipe offer**: Identical to the price. **Second critical delivery note.** If "wipe is one" carries even a hint of significance, warning, or knowingness, the entire mechanic becomes a tutorial and the character becomes a moraliser.
- **Fountain questions**: Complete, accurate, entirely un-arch. He is answering a question about a piece of municipal street furniture, and that is genuinely how it sounds.
- **"I have seen the fountain"**: The one line with any weight, and it gets its weight from being three seconds slower than everything else. Not ominous. Not a joke. A fact, stated once, by someone who is not going to elaborate.
- **The closing hour**: Marginally slower and marginally longer. The only register shift in the character and it should be almost imperceptible.

### Key Delivery Notes
- **Never arch. Never knowing. Never a smile in the voice.** He is not in on anything. If Gunter ever sounds like he is commenting on his own board, the satire inverts and starts mocking the plaza instead of the tourists.
- **He is funny because he is efficient, not because he makes jokes.** He has written five jokes and says none of them. Every laugh in his dialogue comes from speed, brevity, or the total absence of the reaction the listener expected.
- **"They sell" is not a punchline.** It is a two-word statement of commercial fact and it must be delivered as one. A director who leans on it has broken the character.
- **"Correct" is a complete sentence** and is doing enormous work. Do not point it. Do not let it land. It is an answer.
- **Never let him be weary, martyred, or long-suffering.** He is not enduring these men; he is processing them. There is a large difference and it is the whole character.
- Never paternal, never protective, never warning. He would not stop anyone from doing anything and it has never occurred to him to try — including the three things his own sign explicitly forbids.
- The only warmth available anywhere in the performance is on the subject of his own cart — the gloves, the tongs, the A — and one half-beat of professional respect for Gerda. Let it in there and nowhere else.
