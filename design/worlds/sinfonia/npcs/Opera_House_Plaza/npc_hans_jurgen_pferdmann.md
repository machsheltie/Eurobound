# NPC Profile: Hans-Jürgen Pferdmann

*"Gustav is the better driver. He knows the routes. He knows when to stop for photos. I just... hold the reins and drink."*

## 📋 NPC Identity
- **NPC ID**: `sinfonia_npc_hans_jurgen_01`
- **Display Name**: Hans-Jürgen Pferdmann
- **Internal Name**: Hans-Jürgen Pferdmann, Fiakermeister. **"Fiakermeister" is a professional TITLE, never a surname** — it is the licensed rank of a Sinfonia carriage driver, printed on the board in the plaza. His surname is Pferdmann. Any file, line, or asset that treats "Fiakermeister" as a family name is wrong and must be corrected against this profile.
- **Role**: Quest Giver / Fiaker Driver / Emotional Core / Comic Tragedy
- **Associated Quest**: `sinfonia_sidequest_fiaker_fiasco_01` ("The Fiaker Fiasco")
- **Primary Location**: Opera House Plaza, Sinfonia (`sinfonia_imperial_opera_house_plaza_01`) — Fiaker queue, post 3
- **Secondary Appearances**: Stadtpark (Gustav's Refuge) — Phase 4 resolution; Café Sachertorte (may arrive mid-scene to claim responsibility); The Moderato Pub (Path C only, as a sad drunk); his own apartment (couchsurf unlock, Path A only)

---

## 🎭 Core Concept

### What He's Actually Doing
Hans-Jürgen is a fourth-generation licensed Fiakermeister who has been drinking on the job for roughly eleven years and who has, this afternoon, finally lost the horse. He is not a scammer. He charges the posted rate. He is genuinely, professionally competent at everything about this job except the part where he stays sober, and he has been coasting for over a decade on the fact that Gustav knows the routes, knows the stops, knows the tourist photo spots, and requires no actual steering.

His panic is real. His grief is real. The 80 Sovs discount ride he offers as payment is real and he will honour it. What he is actually asking three American strangers to do is retrieve the only functioning half of his business before the licensing board finds out.

### What Bros Believe
A noble quest. A traditional Viennese horseman, separated from his noble steed by cruel fate, calling upon three passing knights. Lord Pilsner has already decided this is "the REAL Sinfonia experience" and that they are being trusted with something sacred. Chadwick is filming it as cultural immersion content. Bradley has correctly identified the smell of schnapps and has decided not to make it an issue.

### The Comedy Gap
The bros treat a man's alcoholic collapse as a knightly summons. Every escalating disaster — 340 Sovs of cake, 2,000 Sovs of Magic Flute — they log as "quest progress." Hans-Jürgen, meanwhile, is transparently, catastrophically obvious: he has a flask in his hand while explaining that he only had a very small drink, in August, for the cold. He is not hiding anything. Nobody has to see through him. The bros simply do not look.

The other Fiaker drivers watch the entire chase without moving, because they have all been waiting for this day since roughly 2014.

### The Hidden Weight
Hans-Jürgen's wife left. His children do not call. The pocket watch in his waistcoat stopped years ago and he has never had it repaired. Sixteen years of six-day weeks, and the only relationship in his life that survived is with a horse who has now filed the equine equivalent of a resignation letter. He is not a tragic figure the bros are meant to rescue — he is a man who is going to have to reckon with something, and the player decides whether he gets the chance.

## 👤 Appearance

### Physical Description
| Attribute | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| Age | Mid-50s |
| Build | Stout, barrel-chested, slightly hunched from decades on a carriage bench |
| Height | Average, reads shorter because of the hunch |
| Energy | Jovial disaster; a warm man operating three drinks past competence |

### Clothing
- **Traditional Fiaker driver's jacket**: Formal black, correct, cut for the job, and the single most dignified thing about him. One brass button is missing and he has not noticed in four years.
- **Bowler hat**: Perpetually askew. It is never straight in any sprite, any state, any scene. It is straight in exactly one image in the entire game: his 2002 licensing photograph on the plaza board.
- **White shirt**: Stained. Coffee, schnapps, and something older.
- **Accessories**: Schnapps flask (barely concealed, and he does not really try); worn leather whip (carried as regalia, never used on Gustav, not once in sixteen years); pocket watch (stopped); a creased photograph of Gustav in the breast pocket.
- **Footwear**: Sturdy black boots, polished on the toes only, because that is the part passengers see.

### Distinguishing Features
- Red-cheeked in a way that is not weather.
- Bushy grey moustache, groomed daily — the grooming is the tell that he still has standards somewhere.
- Reaches for the breast pocket photo whenever Gustav is mentioned, then does not take it out.
- Switches into German the instant an emotion outruns his English: "Mein Pferd! Mein Gustav!"
- When standing still he sways, gently, on a two-second cycle, like a man on a boat only he is on.

### Sprite Notes
Warm, not grotesque. He must never read as a drunk to be mocked — he reads as a man everyone in the plaza likes and nobody has helped. The red face is broad-comic, but the eyes are not: give him a working, expressive gaze that lands the confession scene. Priority frames: the drunk idle sway with flask sip, the panic run (arms wild, hat bouncing, hat never falls off), the bear-hug relief, and the carriage-slump confession. The Stadtpark variant is the same man visibly worse — lederhosen askew, panic sweat, halter clutched in both hands like it might summon a horse.

## 🧠 Personality

### Archetype
**The Coasting Professional** — a genuinely skilled tradesman who long ago outsourced the difficult half of his profession to a horse and drank in the space that opened up. He is proud, generous, self-deceiving, and entirely without malice. He believes he loves Gustav, and he does. He has also never once asked what Gustav wanted, and the whole quest is the bill for that arriving.

### Personality Traits
| Trait | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| Alcoholic denial | Every drink is contextualised: tradition, the cold, the nerves, the occasion. Never "a drink." |
| Genuine affection for Gustav | Not sentimental cover — he sleeps badly when the horse is unwell and always has |
| Professional pride | Will not let anyone else touch his carriage; keeps the brasswork immaculate while wearing a stained shirt |
| Generous under panic | Offers discounts, rides, and his own money to strangers within ninety seconds of meeting them |
| Emotionally unguarded | Confesses everything to anyone, at volume, in a public square, immediately |

### Internal Monologue
> "It is fine. It is fine. He has gone for the fountain, he does this, he likes the fountain. He will come back and I will not say anything and we will do the four o'clock and nobody will— he has not gone for the fountain. Sixteen years and he has never once gone this far. He is not lost. He has *left*. Oh, Gustav. Oh, what have I— where is the flask."

### Motivation
Keep the licence, keep the carriage, keep the horse — in that stated order, which is a lie, because it is the horse and it has always been the horse. Underneath: he wants one thing in his life to still be there in the morning.

## 💬 Dialogue

### Approach/Greeting
**First contact, Opera House Plaza, day hours — he runs at the party, canon beats:**
> "BITTE! BITTE! You must help! My horse! Gustav! He has escaped!"

**Lord Pilsner**: "Your horse... escaped?"

> "I only looked away for... for a small moment. To have a small drink. A VERY small drink."

**Bradley**: "How small?"

> "The bottle was small. Very traditional. Sinfonian tradition. Schnapps for the cold. Even though it is August."

**The pitch:**
> "You! Americans! You are strong, yes? Fast? You help me catch my horse, I give you discount ride!"

### Key Interaction Dialogue — Why the Horse Left
*He does not understand that he is confessing. He thinks he is providing useful background.*

**Chadwick**: "And the horse just... left?"

> "Gustav has been... how you say... difficult. He has opinions now. About the tourists. About the work. About ME."

**Lord Pilsner**: "The horse has opinions?"

> "MANY opinions. Please. I will lose my license. My family has driven Fiakers for generations. I cannot be the one who lost the horse because of... schnapps tradition."

**If asked what Gustav actually does:**
> "Gustav is... the better driver. He knows the routes. He knows when to stop for photos. I just... hold the reins and drink."

### Quest Accept
> "You will help. You WILL help. I knew this. Americans are helpful people. This is known. Go — he went toward the Kaffeehaus. He likes the Kaffeehaus. He should not like the Kaffeehaus. This is part of the problem."

### At Café Sachertorte (Optional Arrival — Dialogue Option 3)
*If the party tells Herr Ober the horse is not theirs, Hans-Jürgen arrives, breathless, and takes it.*
> "It is my horse. Mine. Hans-Jürgen Pferdmann, Fiakermeister, licence four-one-two. Send the bill to me." *(and to the bros, quietly)* "You do not have to say it is yours. You are not from here. I am from here. That is different."

**Result:** Café debt halves to 170 Sovs; Hans-Jürgen's standing with the party rises permanently.

> **Economy rule:** this halving **is** the single 170 Sovs debt relief. It sets `debt_relief_claimed = true` and thereby **disables his 170 Sovs café insurance payout at Stadtpark** — the two are the same relief and may never both apply. His separate **500 Sovs Path A contribution** is a different pot and still stacks on top. Authority: `Design/Quests/Location Specific/Sinfonia/fiaker_fiasco.md` → "Damage Debt Resolution — Authoritative".

### Stadtpark — Arrival
> "GUSTAV! Gustav, bitte, please! Come back to Hans-Jürgen!"

### Stadtpark — The Realisation
*The single most important line he has. Delivered quiet, flat, sober-sounding for the first time in the quest.*
> "He is not running FROM something. He is running TO something. To... freedom."

> "Sixteen years. Sixteen years I work him. Never once I ask... is he happy?"

### Resolution — Path A (Gustav Returns)
> "You are right. Gustav is not just horse. Gustav is... partner."

> "I will do better, Gustav. No more American bachelor parties. I promise."

*The reform terms he actually commits to, spoken aloud so the player can hold him to them:*
> "Six hours. Not twelve. One day off in the week — a real day, not a day I say and then forget. And the rainbow candies. I will buy them. I do not know where. I will find out where."

### Resolution — Path B (Gustav Retires to the Park)
> "Sixteen years... perhaps he has earned this."

> "Go, old friend. You have earned your... retirement."

*Then, to no one, still watching the treeline:*
> "The insurance will cover the carriage. The insurance does not cover the mornings."

### Resolution — Path C (Gustav Escapes)
> "He is gone. Gustav is gone forever."

*If spoken to again in the park:*
> "Do not. Please. Do not say a kind thing to me right now. I will not survive a kind thing."

### If Player Declines / Alternative Path
*Refusing the quest at the plaza:*
> "No. No, of course. Why would you. You are on holiday. I am a man in a hat shouting about a horse." *(he turns back to the empty queue post)* "...Enjoy the opera."

*Path D — the party leaves the park without resolving:*
> "You are LEAVING? He is THERE! He is RIGHT THERE! You—" *(he does not finish it, and that is worse)*

### Post-Interaction
*Path A, plaza, every subsequent day:*
> "Ah! My Americans! Gustav — look, Gustav, it is them. He remembers. He is pretending he does not remember. This is his way."

*Path A, offering the promised payment:*
> "The discount ride. I said it and I meant it. Twenty minutes, the good route, the one with the statue nobody photographs because they do not know. Get in."

### Callback Dialogue (Return Visits)
*Path A, late game:*
> "Six hours. I am doing the six hours. It is less money. I sleep. I did not know that was a thing that could happen to me."

*Path B, plaza, late game — he still runs the stand, now working Klaus:*
> "Klaus is in the traces now. Steady. Honest. He was a monster as a yearling and now he is the best animal in this square." *(pause)* "I go to the park on Sundays. I bring the candies. Gustav takes them. He does not look at me. That is all right."

*A Fiaker colleague, in passing, to the party, roughly once per Sinfonia visit — never as a bit, always deadpan:*
> "Klaus? Ja, good horse. Steady. Honest." *(beat)* "Better than the other one."

*Path C, The Moderato Pub, late game:*
> "The licence is in a drawer. The carriage is in a barn. I am in a pub. Everything is where it belongs now."

*Path D, plaza, late game — hostile:*
> "Americans. Yes. I remember Americans." *(turns away; will not trade, will not talk, will not take the money if offered)*

### Positioning Notes
- He stands at post 3 because posts 1 and 2 get first refusal on fares, and he stopped fighting for them years ago.
- He faces the opera house, not the street — he is watching for the audience to come out, which is his best money.
- His licensing photograph is on the plaza's "FIAKER LICENSED OPERATORS" board and is examinable: sober, straight hat, dated 2002. This is the character's entire arc in one prop.
- During the chase he is always one location behind, arriving after the damage, which is thematically the whole man.

## 🗣️ Voice Direction

### Accent
Austrian German accent over English, with Germanic syntax inversions ("You help me catch my horse, I give you discount ride"). Authentic, warm, never a cartoon Bavarian. Consonants soften as the day goes on — this is a performable clock and should be used as one.

### Tone
- **Plaza pitch**: expansive, salesman-warm, a shade too loud
- **Panic**: pitch rising, sentences colliding, an audible hiccup landing on the worst possible word
- **The confession**: low, slow, and suddenly and completely sober-sounding — the slur drops away entirely for the "sixteen years" line and returns afterward
- **Path C**: flat. No colour at all.

### Key Delivery Notes
- "Mein Pferd! Mein Gustav!" is always full-throat and always German — never translated in-line.
- The realisation line ("He is not running FROM something...") must not be played for laughs, must not be scored comedically, and must have silence on both sides of it.
- He never sounds pathetic when asking for help. He sounds like a man who genuinely believes strangers help each other, because he would.
- Never let him sound like he is performing his drunkenness. He is not aware it is visible.

## 📝 Development Notes

- **CANON — surname**: Pferdmann. **"Fiakermeister" is a title and must never appear as a surname.** `stadtpark_gustavs_refuge.md` line 139 uses the correct construction ("Hans-Jürgen Pferdmann, Fiakermeister"); anything shorter that reads as a name is an error.
- **CANON — NPC ID**: `sinfonia_npc_hans_jurgen_01`. The variants `hans_jurgen_01` and `sinfonia_npc_hansjurgen_fiaker_01` found elsewhere in the tree are superseded and must be migrated to this ID.
- **CANON — tenure**: sixteen years with Gustav. The "15 years" value in `opera_house_plaza_npcs.json` (`relationship_gustav`) is stale and should be corrected to sixteen.
- The clothing description in `stadtpark_gustavs_refuge.md` says "lederhosen askew" while `opera_house_plaza.md` specifies the formal black Fiaker jacket. Resolution: the jacket is the working uniform; by Stadtpark, deep in the chase, he has shed the jacket and is in the traditional trousers underneath. Both are correct in sequence.
- He must never be written as the villain of the quest and never as a figure of contempt. The satire is aimed at tourist-industry conditions and at the bros' misreading, not at addiction.
- Property damage is never his fault mechanically, but he always offers to pay first. This is load-bearing for Path A's credibility.
- Do not let any NPC explain to the bros what they are actually looking at. The plaza knows. The plaza says nothing.
- **Klaus the horse** (see Connections) is governed by the author ruling at `Design/Quests/Location Specific/Sinfonia/fiaker_fiasco.md` → "Canon Note: Why the Horse Is Called Klaus", with its on-screen payoff at the Stadtpark post-game stall nameplate (`KING KLAUS`). Hans-Jürgen is the origin of that joke and must never explain it, and he must never be in a scene containing both Klaus and König Klaus.
