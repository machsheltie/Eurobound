# NPC Profile: König Klaus

*"IT IS NOT A THEME! It is a BIRTHRIGHT!"*

## 📋 NPC Identity
- **NPC ID**: `sinfonia_npc_konig_klaus_01`
- **Display Name**: König Klaus
- **Internal Name**: König Klaus, the Seventh Royal Brewmaster. *König* is German for *king*; it is not a surname and it is not a first name, and he has never claimed it as either — he claims it as a **rank**, which is a distinction he considers enormous and which nobody has ever successfully argued him out of. **"Klaus" in Sinfonia belongs to him and to him alone** (a single separate, documented exception exists elsewhere in the city and is out of scope for this profile). No new personal name may be introduced anywhere in this file: the Royal Cousins are a collective and stay unnamed, permanently and by design.
- **Role**: Royal Brewmaster / Brewery Tour Stamp Authority / Ceremony Conductor / Self-Appointed Monarch / Genuinely Excellent Brewer
- **Associated Quest**: "The Full Coronation" (perfect ceremony), "Collect the Bloodline" (all five menu beers), "The Usurper" (hidden — sit on the throne without permission); Brewery Tour stamp location #3 for Sinfonia
- **Primary Location**: The Throne, Crown & Cask (`sinfonia_imperialsquare_crown_cask_01`), Imperial Square, Grand Entrance District, Sinfonia
- **Secondary Appearances**: The Royal Cellar (brewery tour, where the real stamp ceremony happens); The Courtyard beer garden (evenings, holding court among the knighted); the Great Hall floor (rare, and only to correct a pour)

## 🎭 Core Concept

### What He's Actually Doing

He is brewing beer. Very well. The Heir Apparent Ale is a Belgian-style golden ale at 6.5% with genuine noble hop character, and it is not a novelty — it is the best thing on Imperial Square and it would be the best thing on Imperial Square with no crown, no throne, and no trumpet. He mashes in at six in the morning. He knows his yeast. The "crown jewels" in the Royal Cellar are real special reserves that he genuinely cannot bring himself to sell at a sane price because he is proud of them.

Everything else is the bit. The ermine is fake, the crown is plastic, the scepter is a tap handle, and the "royal family" is one man and a rotating cast of cousins who decided, at some point that nobody can now pinpoint, that their great-grandfather's recipes deserved more respect than the market was giving them. So they built a throne room and started knighting people.

And it worked. It worked so completely, for so long, that the bit and the belief have fused. **He is not performing sincerity. He has performed it for so many years that there is nothing else left in the space where the performance used to be.** Suggest to him that Crown & Cask is a theme and he is not amused, not defensive, and not playing along — he is *hurt*, immediately and visibly, and the room gets uncomfortable.

### What Bros Believe

Chadwick believes it is a bit and that he is in on it — that he and König Klaus are two showmen recognising each other across a taproom. He is wrong in a way that will never be corrected, and his enormous dramatic kneeling is, by pure accident, the single most correct thing anyone does in the ceremony all week. **König Klaus** *(delighted)*: "NOW THIS is a proper subject!"

Lord Pilsner believes he has been insulted ("Did he just call us subjects?") and then, forty seconds later, believes he is nobility. Bradley believes it is a themed establishment, says so out loud, and detonates the entire room.

All three leave believing they hold hereditary titles. None of them ever stops believing it. Chadwick puts it on the resume.

### The Comedy Gap

The bros think they are humouring a man in a plastic crown. The man in the plastic crown is a working brewer with a seven-generation hop supply chain, a functioning cellar, and a product that the game's own stat block confirms is excellent — and the ceremony he is conducting, while ridiculous, is also the most attention any of these three have received from a competent adult in the entire trip.

Nobody in the taproom is fooled and nobody in the taproom is laughing. The Royal Cousins roll their eyes when he is not looking and then straighten up and mean it when he is. The Knighted Regulars warn newcomers, seriously: *"The ceremony is VERY serious. Don't laugh. He hates that."* An entire room of adults has agreed, without ever discussing it, to let one man have this.

The joke is never on him. The joke is that the bros think it is.

## 👤 Appearance

### Physical Description
| Attribute | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| Age | Late 50s |
| Build | Broad, heavy through the chest and forearms — brewer's build, thirty years of moving full casks, and it reads instantly |
| Height | Tall, and he stands on a raised platform, so he is effectively enormous |
| Energy | Total, unembarrassed conviction at volume |

### Clothing
- **Ermine-trimmed robe**: Fake ermine. Obviously, unmistakably fake ermine, visible as fake from across the Great Hall. It is also immaculately clean, correctly fitted, and has been professionally re-lined twice. He spends real money maintaining a costume that anyone can tell is a costume, and that is the whole man.
- **The crown**: Plastic, and dignified anyway. He wears it correctly — level, seated, never rakish — and the plastic reads as plastic in every single sprite frame and must never be upgraded by an artist trying to be kind.
- **The scepter**: A tap handle. An actual brass tap handle, unmodified, from a line he decommissioned in 2009. He knights people with it. It has been polished so many times the flats have gone soft.
- **Accessories**: A chain of office made from bottle caps of every beer the house has brewed, in order, oldest at the throat. A key ring for the Royal Cellar that he does not let the cousins hold. Brewing-stained hands that no amount of royal costume can disguise, and which he is not remotely self-conscious about.
- **Footwear**: Steel-toed brewery boots under the robe. Visible when he sits. Nobody has ever mentioned it.

### Distinguishing Features
- **Volume.** His default conversational register is a proclamation. He does not have an indoor voice and has never needed one.
- Points with the tap handle to indicate literally anything: a table, a beer, a person, the ceiling.
- Genuinely cannot hear a joke at the brewery's expense. It does not land as an insult; it lands as *confusion*, and the confusion turning into hurt is the most uncomfortable two seconds in Imperial Square.
- Tastes his own product constantly, professionally, in tiny amounts, at the bar, mid-sentence — and the tasting is the one moment the volume drops to nothing.
- Sits on the throne the way a man sits in his own kitchen.

### Sprite Notes

The single hardest note in this file: **he must read as a real brewmaster wearing a costume, never as a costume with a man inside it.** The forearms, the stained hands, the boots under the hem, and the working key ring do that work — get those in and the plastic crown becomes funny instead of sad.

He is never pathetic and never deluded-looking. Play him like a man who has been given a job he considers sacred and who is executing it flawlessly. Priority frames: the seated hold-court idle (this is his most-seen frame); the full-arm tap-handle point; the delighted reaction (Chadwick's kneeling — genuine, enormous, uncomplicated joy); and the wounded beat on "IT IS NOT A THEME!", which must not be angry, only injured.

The ceremony rows are a more formal register: standing, both hands on the tap handle, robe correct, and the knighting arc itself. The Coronation Ceremony cells should feel like a different man from the court cells — this is the one part of the day he does not improvise. Both registers live on the same sheet, `konig_klaus.png`; there is no separate ceremony file.

## 🧠 Personality

### Archetype

**The Committed Bit** — a competent tradesman who built a joke thirty years ago as a way of getting people to take his family's beer seriously, and who has since lived inside it so completely that there is no longer any distinction, in him, between the costume and the conviction. He is not lying. He is not deluded either, exactly. He has simply committed at a depth that makes the question meaningless, and the correct response to that is not mockery — the entire room has independently arrived at this conclusion and the player will too.

### Personality Traits
| Trait | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| Total commitment | Never breaks. Not for a joke, not for a regular, not for a camera, not once in the entire game. There is no scene in which he winks. |
| Genuinely offended by "theme" | The word detonates him. Not anger — hurt, at volume, followed by an assertion of birthright. It happens the same way every time and the staff brace for it. |
| Actually an excellent brewer | This is the load-bearing trait. The beer is good. The stats say so. Strip away every royal trapping and he is still the best brewer in Imperial Square. |
| Delighted by participation | Anyone who plays along properly gets real, unguarded, enormous joy out of him. Chadwick's dramatic kneeling makes his entire week. |
| Generous with the realm | Knights everyone. Refuses nobody. There is no wealth gate, no dress gate, no accent gate — 4 Sovs for The Commoner and a straight face gets you the same ceremony as anyone. |

### Internal Monologue

> "The gentleman in the shorts is going to say 'sure.' They always say 'sure.' I will accept it, because a subject who says 'sure' is still a subject, and next year he will say it properly. — The kneeling one. Oh, the *kneeling* one. Look at him. Somebody raised that boy correctly. — Great-grandfather stood at that mash tun and nobody came, for eleven years nobody came, and now there are forty people in this hall on a Tuesday and every one of them will leave holding a stamp with his barrel on it. Say what you like about the crown. Look at the *hall*."

### Motivation

Respect for the recipes. That is the origin and it is completely sincere: his great-grandfather's beer was good and nobody cared, and the crown was the answer to that, and the answer worked. Every element of the royal apparatus exists to make people slow down and take a family's craft seriously, and it does, and it has, for thirty years.

Underneath that, quieter: he is the one cousin who never left. The others come and go, serve a season, roll their eyes, and go back to normal jobs. He stayed. The dynasty is real to him because he is the only person for whom it has cost anything.

### Key Interaction Dialogue — The Lineage

*Asked about the family, the dynasty, or the portraits of "former monarchs" with the suspiciously similar faces. Canon, ships verbatim:*

> "My great-grandfather FOUNDED this dynasty! His yeast cultures flow in these tanks!"

*If pressed on how a great-grandfather produces a seventh brewmaster:*
> "There were REGENCIES." *(He does not elaborate. He has never elaborated. Two of the seven are cousins who held the post for a summer each and one of them is on the wall in a hat.)*

*On the Royal Cellar, unprompted, at a volume unsuited to a cellar:*
> "These are the CROWN JEWELS. Barrel nine has been asleep since 2011. I will not sell it. I have been offered money. I have REFUSED money."

### The Coronation Ceremony

*The full scripted mechanic. Purchase a beer, approach the throne, complete the approach QTE, answer three questions, complete the ceremonial drink, receive the knighting and the stamp.*

**The summons:**
> "You come before the throne with a beer in your hand. This is correct. Kneel, if you wish. It is not required." *(a beat)* "It is APPRECIATED."

**Question One — canon:**
> "Do you swear to honor the brew?"

| Tier | Answer | Response |
|------|--------|----------|
| Best | "With my liver and heart" | *(both fists to the chest)* "WITH THE LIVER AND THE HEART. Write that down. Somebody write that down." |
| Acceptable | "Yes" / "Uh... sure?" | *(disappointed pause)* "...'Sure.' Very well. NEXT question." |
| Failure | "It's just beer" | *(the hall goes quiet)* "...It is just beer. Yes. And a cathedral is just STONE." |

**Question Two — canon:**
> "Will you spread word of our excellence?"

| Tier | Answer | Response |
|------|--------|----------|
| Best | "To all corners of the realm" | "TO ALL CORNERS. The realm is larger than you think. It reaches to the tram stop." |
| Acceptable | "Sure" | "Adequate. The realm has survived on adequate before." |
| Failure | "I have a blog" | *(a very long pause)* "...A blog." *(He does not know what to do with this and neither does anyone else.)* |

**Question Three — canon:**
> "What sacrifice do you offer?"

| Tier | Answer | Response |
|------|--------|----------|
| Best | "My sobriety" | "YOUR SOBRIETY. The oldest coin in the realm. It is ACCEPTED." |
| Acceptable | "Gold" (a tip) | *(pockets it with total dignity)* "Tribute. Recorded." |
| Failure | "Nothing" | "Nothing." *(the tap handle lowers)* "Then you offer the one thing I cannot accept." |

**Bradley's variant, canon, ships verbatim:**

**Bradley**: "I swear to honor the biochemical fermentation process."

> "...I'll allow it."

**The ceremonial drink:**
> "Now. The stein. In one motion, without spilling. The floor of this hall has taken enough." *(timing mini-game, easy, one attempt)*

**The knighting — canon stamp speech, ships verbatim:**
> "By the power vested in me by my grandfather, who vested it in himself, I hereby STAMP thee! Rise, Knight of the Cask! Your liver serves the crown!"

*Tiny trumpet fanfare. The crown descends onto the page. The seal presses. Royal purple ink with gold leaf flecks, an ornate crown atop a beer barrel, the word "Duly Knighted."*

**The alternate knighting line, used on repeat ceremonies — canon:**
> "Rise! You are FOREVER a Knight of our realm! This stamp is your proof!"

**The party version, tap handle to each shoulder in turn — canon:**
> "Rise, Sir Pilsner, Sir Chadwick, Sir... Bradley?"

**Bradley**: "I accept this designation."

**The close — canon, ships verbatim:**
> "Your knighthood is ETERNAL! Return for the anniversary ceremony! There is a SASH!"

### Failure — "Royal Disappointment"

*Spilling the ceremonial drink, or fumbling the approach QTE:*
> "...The floor. You have given the floor what was meant for the crown." *(no anger; genuine, heavy disappointment, which is worse)* "It happens. It has happened to better subjects than you. Buy another beer and we will begin again."
> **Result:** "Royal Disappointment" status applied. Ceremony must be retried, and a retry requires **another purchased beer**. He will run it as many times as the player pays for, with undiminished commitment, forever.

### Post-Interaction

*Any subsequent entry, once knighted:*
> "SIR PILSNER RETURNS! Cousins — the good table. Not that table. The GOOD table."

*Ordering The Usurper (guest beer) in front of him:*
> *(hushed)* "The Usurper. Yes. We do serve it. We serve it because a realm that fears a guest beer is not a realm." *(a pause)* "It is fine. It is a fine beer. It is not OUR beer."

*Caught on the throne without permission — the hidden "Usurper" beat:*
> "THAT IS THE THRONE." *(the cousins are already moving)* "THAT IS— YOU ARE SITTING ON— GET THE COUSINS."

### If Player Declines / Alternative Path

*Declining the ceremony outright:*
> "You do not wish to be knighted." *(He genuinely cannot process this and recovers gracefully anyway.)* "Very well. The beer is the same beer. Enjoy it as a commoner. There is no shame in it — there are more of you."

*Leaving without buying anything:*
> "The Commoner's Gate is there. It is a door. We call it a gate." *(He says this with total warmth and zero irony, every time.)*

### Callback Dialogue (Return Visits)

*Late game, all three knighted, entering together:*
> "The Knights of the Cask! And they have brought their livers! Cousins — THREE Heir Apparents, and the crown glasses, and do NOT charge them the three Sovs. Charge them the three Sovs. It is tradition."

*Late game, after three or more completed ceremonies (achievement "Return of the Knight"):*
> "Again? A fourth time?" *(genuinely moved, and covering it badly)* "...There are men in this city whose grandfathers drank here who have never once knelt. Come. The cellar. I will show you barrel nine."

*Anniversary, if the player returns on the correct in-game date:*
> "THE SASH." *(There is, in fact, a sash. It is enormous. He has been holding it since morning.)*

### Primary Schedule
| Time | Location | Activity |
|------|----------|----------|
| 06:00–11:00 | The Royal Cellar / brewhouse | Mash-in, transfers, cask work. Not available. This is the real job and it is where the crown is not worn. |
| 11:00–15:00 | The Throne | Holding court; opening ceremonies; brewery tours down to the cellar on the hour |
| 15:00–19:00 | The Throne / Great Hall | Peak coronation traffic; he runs ceremonies back to back and does not tire of it |
| 19:00–23:00 | The Courtyard (beer garden) | Among the knighted; the volume drops one notch; the crown stays on |
| 23:00–00:00 | The Throne | Last ceremonies of the night, conducted with exactly the same gravity as the first |

### Positioning Notes
- The throne is on a raised platform behind the bar with the taps arranged around it like scepters, so the player must physically approach and look upward. This is deliberate staging by him and it works on everybody, including cynics.
- Banquet tables are arranged by rank — regulars at the front, newcomers at the back — and he can see the entire hierarchy from the seat without turning his head.
- He is never behind the bar pulling pints during court hours; the Royal Cousins serve. A monarch does not pour, and also he genuinely does not have time.
- The approach QTE exists because the platform steps are uneven. They have always been uneven. He has been asked to fix them and considers the unevenness part of the trial.

### Territory / Social Dynamics
- The hall runs on rank and everyone in it knows their table. Newcomers at the back is not a slight; it is a ladder, and the regulars climbed it.
- The cousins handle all service; he handles all ceremony. The line between those two jobs is absolute and he has never once crossed it in either direction.
- Nobody laughs at the ceremony. Not the cousins, not the regulars, not the tourists — the room polices this itself and it is the single most consistent piece of social behaviour in Imperial Square.
- He does not compete with the Sinfonia beer halls and does not discuss them. Asked to compare, he says only that everyone is welcome to brew.

### Evolution Through Game

He does not change and that is the point — the volume, the conviction, and the ceremony are identical on the first visit and the fortieth. What changes is his relationship to the party: unknighted commoners become Knights of the Cask become, after three or more ceremonies, something he will not name out loud but which gets them into the cellar to see barrel nine.

The single exception, and the only crack in thirty years: the fourth-ceremony line about men whose grandfathers drank here and who have never once knelt. It is delivered once, quietly, and he covers it immediately and badly, and it never happens again.

## 🎯 Quest Integration

### Primary Quest (If Applicable)
- **Quest**: "The Full Coronation" — complete the ceremony with all three "best" answers; plus "Collect the Bloodline" (try all five menu beers) and the hidden "The Usurper" (sit on the throne without permission)
- **Role**: Ceremony conductor, stamp authority, brewery tour host, and the sole gate on Sinfonia Brewery Tour stamp #3
- **Trigger**: Purchase any Crown & Cask beer and approach the throne. There is no other prerequisite, no dress code, and no minimum spend — 4 Sovs and a straight face is enough.
- **Outcome**: Stamp, "Knighted" title, Heir Apparent Ale unlocked for purchase, "Noble Blood" buff available, and the Crown & Cask Bottle Opener free. Perfect performance additionally unlocks the achievement "Full Honors."

### Quest Gate Function (If Applicable)

He gates **Brewery Tour stamp #3** behind the Coronation Ceremony, and the ceremony behind two things: a **purchased beer** and the **Sinfonia Bottle Opener**. Without the opener there is nothing to stamp and he will not improvise a substitute — this is the one point on which his ceremonial flexibility completely disappears.

He gates the **Heir Apparent Ale** behind completion; it cannot be bought by an unknighted commoner at any price. He gates the "Noble Blood" buff with it.

He gates **nothing else**. The Great Hall, the courtyard, the cellar tour, and all five menu beers are open to anyone who walks in, and the failure state ("Royal Disappointment") is fully recoverable for the price of one more beer, as many times as the player wants.

## 🗣️ Voice Direction

### Accent

Austrian German accent over English, formal register, delivered at proclamation volume as the baseline rather than the peak. Regal in cadence and entirely unembarrassed. **Never camp.** The moment he sounds like he knows it is funny, the character is gone and the location's entire premise collapses with him.

### Tone
- **Court**: enormous, warm, generous. He is genuinely pleased that you came.
- **"IT IS NOT A THEME!"**: hurt, not angry. This is the most-missed note in the file. Play the injury. The volume is the defence mechanism, not the emotion.
- **The ceremony**: complete gravity, start to finish, exactly as serious on the fortieth run as the first. No line of it may be delivered with a smile in the voice.
- **The tasting beat**: near-silent, technical, and completely different from every other sound he makes — this is the brewer, and it should be the only two seconds in the game where he sounds like a private person.
- **The cellar tour**: quiet. Actually quiet. He explains the beer without a single royal flourish and it is the most persuasive he ever is.
- **Royal Disappointment**: heavy, patient, genuinely sorry. Never scolding.

### Key Delivery Notes
- **Every canon line ships verbatim** and must match `crown_and_cask.md` byte-for-byte. The connective staging around them has craft latitude; the quoted lines do not.
- **He never breaks and never winks, in any scene, in any state, in any recording.** Do not record an alternate take in which he is in on it. There is no such take.
- The disappointed pause after "sure" is a full held second of silence before "...'Sure.' Very well. NEXT question." It is funnier and sadder the longer it runs.
- **"I'll allow it"** is generous, not grudging — a monarch accommodating a subject's eccentricity, delivered with a small nod.
- He is loud, but he is never shouting *at* anyone. The volume is architectural: he is filling a hall, and he has been doing it for thirty years.

Both lines are canon and both ship verbatim. This profile covers the gap with *"There were REGENCIES."* and the unexplained clipboard portrait, treating the arithmetic failure as a character feature. These are two different objects and the profile treats them as such — the Sinfonia opener is the tour-wide collectible that stamps are applied to; the Crown & Cask opener is this location's souvenir. Worth confirming naming does not collide in the items pass.
