# NPC Profile: Herr Notenschreiber

*"A BILLION people can be WRONG."*

## 📋 NPC Identity
- **NPC ID**: `sinfonia_npc_herr_notenschreiber_01`
- **Display Name**: Herr Notenschreiber
- **Internal Name**: Herr Notenschreiber ("Mr. Note-Writer"). **CANON: "Notenschreiber" is a trade-name, not a surname, and he has no first name.** He has never given one, no NPC in Sinfonia knows one, and none is to be invented in any file, roster, or asset. Ask him directly and he answers: *"Notenschreiber. It is what I do. It has been sufficient for sixty years."* The joke is that this is a complete answer and he considers the matter closed.
- **Role**: Vendor / Music Authority / Reluctant Educator / Humming Judge / Quest Hint Source
- **Associated Quest**: `sinfonia_sidequest_the_lost_opus_01` ("The Lost Opus") — quest giver; also "Correct Interpretation," "The Humming Champion," "Forbidden Music"
- **Primary Location**: The Composer's Cart (`sinfonia_musikgasse_composers_cart_01`), MusikGasse / Street Performer District. 9 AM – 9 PM.
- **Secondary Appearances**: None — he does not leave the cart. The cart does not leave MusikGasse. Referenced by the tempo-theory arguers at The Moderato Pub and by the manuscript hints inside The Bassline Opera House.

## 🎭 Core Concept

### What He's Actually Doing
Herr Notenschreiber sells sheet music at fair prices from an antique cart that appears to be an avalanche and is in fact a catalogue he holds entirely in his head. Ask for anything and he produces it in under four seconds from a pile that looks structurally impossible. A Basic Score is 5 Sovs and is worth 5 Sovs. The Lost Manuscript is 50 Sovs and is worth considerably more than 50 Sovs. He has never once overcharged anybody, including tourists, including the tourists who deserve it.

He can identify any piece of music from three notes. This is not a gag stat — it is the load-bearing fact of the character, and it is why the humming mini-game works at all. He loves music with an intensity that is faintly alarming and he genuinely, sincerely wants to give it away.

And decades of tourists humming "Für Elise" badly at his cart have broken something in him. The gatekeeping — the sign that reads *"Please Do Not Hum. We Will Know If It's Wrong"*, the interrogation about major and minor keys before he will sell a 5 Sovs score, the visible physical pain — is a defence mechanism against cultural trauma. He is not a snob. He is a man who has been flinched at his own life's work eleven thousand times and has stopped being able to hear it coming without bracing.

### What Bros Believe
Chadwick has asked whether people still READ music, in the tone of someone asking whether people still use fax machines, and has decided the old man is running a novelty stall. Lord Pilsner has identified the Beethoven's Fifth opening as "Dun dun dun DUNNN," is proud of this, and considers it a shared cultural moment between himself and the vendor. Both believe the humming is friendly participation. Chadwick, specifically, believes that a song with a billion streams constitutes an argument.

Bradley, who took lessons until age 12 and paid attention, knows exactly what this cart is and says nothing about it until he has to.

### The Comedy Gap
Everything at this cart is fairly priced, accurately described, and expertly recommended by a man who wants nothing more than for somebody to want it. What arrives instead is three men producing sounds at him. The prices never move — the humming mini-game's discounts and penalties are the only price movement in the location, and they are earned in both directions, which means the "punishment" for humming badly is a 10% surcharge on a 5 Sovs sheet of paper and the reward for humming well is that an old man is briefly happy.

The satire targets cultural gatekeeping behaviour — the "correct interpretation" reflex, musical one-upmanship, the idea that knowing things about music makes you a better human being. It never targets classical music, music education, musicians, or Viennese musical heritage. Every single thing Herr Notenschreiber knows is true, and the game proves it by letting him be right every time.

### The Hidden Weight
He has been at this cart for sixty years. He knows what has happened to him: he can hear a stranger take a breath to hum and feel his shoulders go up before a note has left them. He does not want to be like this. He was not always like this. Once every few weeks somebody hums something correctly and unselfconsciously, and for about ninety seconds afterwards he is a completely different man, and then a tour group arrives.

## 👤 Appearance

### Physical Description
| Attribute | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| Age | Ancient — 80s, and disputes the number |
| Build | Small, spare, hunched over the cart; hands steady enough to rule a stave freehand |
| Height | Short, and shorter still behind the paper |
| Energy | Weary authority; a man permanently one bad note from a flinch |

### Clothing
- **Ink-stained work coat**: Decades of it. The stains are dated, in his head, by which manuscript he was annotating. He can tell you. He will tell you.
- **Spectacles thick as opera glasses**: Functional, necessary, and the reason for the perpetual squint. He is not squinting at you judgementally. He is squinting because he cannot see you. The effect is identical and he has stopped correcting the misunderstanding.
- **Accessories**: Quill and steel-nib pens in the breast pocket, always more than he needs; a pocket metronome he winds but does not carry; a folded page of manuscript paper in the coat, ruled and empty, which he has carried for years.
- **Footwear**: Old boots, cracked, unremarkable — he stands twelve hours a day and has stopped thinking about them.

### Distinguishing Features
- **The three-note identification.** Head tilts, eyes close, and the piece is named — composer, catalogue number, movement — before the third note has finished. Every time. It has never failed and it never will.
- Ink on every finger of the right hand and the side of the palm; the left hand is clean, because the left hand holds the paper.
- Winces before he hears the wrong note. He has learned to predict them. This is the saddest detail on the sheet and it should be played as a physical tic.
- Handles every manuscript, including a 5 Sovs Basic Score, with both hands.
- The metronome on the cart ticks at 60. He set it there in the first year and has never adjusted it.

### Sprite Notes
Ancient, ink-stained, squinting — and never a doddering old fool. He must read as the ranking authority on MusikGasse, because he is. The four expression sheets carry the entire character and the range between them is the whole joke: **neutral** is guarded and professional, **pain** is genuinely physical (chest, shoulders, closed eyes — not a comic mug), **approval** is small and controlled and precious, and **hope** is the one that matters — it is not joy, it is a man daring to think this one might be all right, and if it plays as broad delight the character is ruined. Priority frames: the three-note tilt, the both-handed manuscript pass, the pre-emptive wince, and the four-second stillness before "...Continue."

## 🧠 Personality

### Archetype
**The Wounded Enthusiast** — a genuine expert who loves his subject without limit, wants desperately to share it, and has been injured so consistently by the sharing that he now demands proof of worthiness before he will hand over a 5 Sovs sheet of paper. He is aware that this is what has happened to him. He does not know how to stop. Every discount in his shop is him trying.

### Personality Traits
| Trait | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| Encyclopaedic and Correct | Three notes. Composer, catalogue number, movement. Never wrong, never guesses, never bluffs. |
| Scrupulously Fair on Price | Not a scammer. Prices are the prices. The only movement is earned at the humming game, in both directions, and he applies the surcharge and the discount with the same face. |
| Physically Injured by Bad Music | The pain is real and involuntary. He clutches his chest at the street performers' wrong notes and is not performing. |
| Secretly Desperate to Give It Away | Ask for "something good" and he chooses for you, and his choice is always excellent, and he watches your face while you look at it. |
| Honest About Ignorance | Admitting you do not know earns his respect instantly and permanently. Pretending you do is the only unforgivable act at this cart. |

### Internal Monologue
> "He is going to hum. He has the face of a man about to hum. It will be 'Für Elise' and it will be the fourth bar wrong, it is always the fourth bar, why is it always the fourth bar — the piece is not difficult, it is a bagatelle, it is the least difficult thing Beethoven ever— he has stopped. He has not hummed. — Oh. Oh, that is Bach. That is the prelude and he has the *rhythm* right, the rhythm, which nobody has, which nobody— Do not scare him. Do not say anything clever. Do not correct the one thing. Let him finish."

### Motivation
He wants to hand somebody a piece of music and have them come back and tell him what it did to them. That is all. Sixty years, four thousand transactions, and it has happened perhaps two hundred times, and he remembers all two hundred, and he could name them in order.

## 💬 Dialogue

### Approach/Greeting
**First approach to the cart — canon, ships verbatim:**
> "Welcome to The Composer's Cart. Please tell me you know the difference between major and minor keys."

**Asking for Mozart — canon, ships verbatim:**
> "You want Mozart? EVERYONE wants Mozart. Fine. Which Mozart? There are HUNDREDS of pieces."

**Asked for his first name:**
> "Notenschreiber. It is what I do. It has been sufficient for sixty years."

### Key Interaction Dialogue — Browsing
*Canon beat, ships verbatim. Lord Pilsner points at random.*

**Lord Pilsner**: "What's this one?"

**Herr Notenschreiber**:
> "Beethoven's Fifth Symphony. You've heard it."

**Lord Pilsner**: "Have I?"

*He hums the famous four notes. Correctly. It is the only time in the game he hums first.*

**Lord Pilsner**: "OH! Dun dun dun DUNNN! That one!"

> "...Please leave the 'dun duns' to the professionals."

### Key Interaction Dialogue — The Humming Incident
*Canon beat, ships verbatim. Chadwick is absently humming a pop song.*

> "WHAT is THAT?"

**Chadwick**: "Just a song I like—"

> "That is NOT music. That is FOUR CHORDS repeating. Please. I beg you. Stop."

**Chadwick**: "It has like a billion streams—"

> "A BILLION people can be WRONG."

*This triggers the "Musically Offensive" failure state. See below.*

### Key Interaction Dialogue — Bradley's Redemption
*Canon, ships verbatim, and the single most important beat at this location. Bradley quietly hums Bach's Cello Suite No. 1. The sprite changes to `herr_notenschreiber_hope.png` on the second bar and does not change back for the rest of the scene.*

**Bradley**: *(quietly humming Bach's Cello Suite No. 1)*

**Herr Notenschreiber** *(stops mid-criticism)*:
> "...Continue."

**Bradley**: *(continues)*

> "Your friends are... unfortunate. But YOU. You may have 15% off."

**Lord Pilsner**: "BRADLEY!"

**Bradley**: "I paid attention during lessons."

*Bradley does not gloat, does not explain further, and does not become the party's music expert afterwards. He gets one moment, he takes it, and the subject is closed. Lord Pilsner attempts the same trick within the hour and it goes exactly as badly as it should.*

### Approval and Disappointment
*Canon, ships verbatim.*

**After good humming:**
> "...You have taste. This is unexpected. Refreshing, even."

**After bad humming:**
> "That wasn't music. That was... sound. Organized poorly."

### The Humming Mini-Game
*Triggered when the player hums, whistles, sings, or selects a "musical" dialogue option near the cart. Four steps, no exceptions: the player triggers a tune → he identifies it instantly → he judges the interpretation → consequences apply.*

| What You Hum | Vendor Reaction | Consequence | His Line |
|--------------|-----------------|-------------|----------|
| "Für Elise" (basic) | Heavy sigh | Nothing — he is used to it | "Yes. That one. Everyone finds that one." |
| "Für Elise" (wrong) | Visible pain (`_pain` sprite) | "Please stop. Please." | "The fourth bar. It is ALWAYS the fourth bar. Why." |
| Mozart (correct) | Nod | **5% discount** | "Correct. Unremarkable, but correct. Take the five percent." |
| Mozart (wrong) | "He's ROLLING in his grave" | **Price increase 10%** | "He is ROLLING in his grave. That is not a figure of speech. That is a description of what is happening." |
| Obscure piece (correct) | Genuine surprise (`_approval` sprite) | **15% discount + rare stock shown** | "...You have taste. This is unexpected. Refreshing, even." *(and he starts moving paper)* |
| Obscure piece (wrong) | "You ALMOST had it" | Respect for trying | "You ALMOST had it. You went for it. Nobody goes for it. Here — this is what you were reaching for." |
| Made-up tune | Confusion, then intrigue | **Quest trigger** | "What IS that? No. No, do not stop. Where is it FROM?" |
| Copyrighted modern song | RAGE | **10-minute ban** | "That is NOT music. That is FOUR CHORDS repeating. Please. I beg you. Stop." |

**"Musically Offensive" — the failure state:**
> "Go. Ten minutes. Walk to the end of MusikGasse and back and do not hum on the way." *(and, quieter, to nobody)* "A billion streams. A BILLION."

*The ban is exactly ten minutes, is announced with the timer visible, and expires without ceremony. He does not mention it afterwards, ever. Re-triggering it stacks nothing — it is always ten minutes, because he is not vindictive, he just needs the ten minutes.*

### The Three Special Interactions

**The Request** — *ask for a specific piece:*
> "Third pile. Under the folio. Beneath the Handel." *(four seconds; produces it)* "You did not think I knew where it was. Nobody does. It is not chaos. It is a system with one user."

*Ask for "something good":*
> "Something good." *(a long look, and he is not being difficult — he is choosing)* "...Here. Do not read it yet. Read it somewhere quiet. Come back and tell me."

*Ask for "something popular":*
> *(dies a little inside; complies without comment; hands over the Basic Score with both hands anyway)* "Yes. Fine. This one. It is popular. It is also good, which people forget is allowed."

**The Quiz** — *he tests customers unprompted, roughly one visit in four:*
> "Three notes. Tell me what it is." *(hums)*

| Answer | Result |
|--------|--------|
| Correct | Discount applied to the current purchase; "Passed the Test" achievement; a nod, which from him is a standing ovation |
| Wrong | The Lecture — educational, accurate, genuinely interesting, and eleven minutes long. No mechanical penalty. The penalty is the eleven minutes. |
| "I don't know" | **Respect.** Permanent. "Good. That is the correct answer to a question you cannot answer. Most people guess. Guessing is how we got here." |

**The Recommendation** — *based on purchase history, and unnervingly accurate to playstyle:*
> "You seem like a Beethoven player. Aggressive. Dramatic. Here."

> "You buy the Tempo Guides and never the Harmony Charts. You are playing alone even when you are not alone. ...That was not a criticism. Here is a Harmony Chart. Take it, do not pay me, and bring your friends next time."

### Patron of the Arts — Unlock
*Fires the moment the player's lifetime spend at the cart crosses 100 Sovs.*

> "One hundred Sovs. In this street. On paper." *(he stops, and actually looks at the player for the first time, and the squint is not judgement, it never was)* "...Come round the side of the cart. There are things I do not put out."

> "You may hum. Within reason. I will tell you what 'within reason' means as we go."

### Post-Interaction
*Patron path, every subsequent visit — he uses your name, and this is the actual reward:*
> "Ah. It is you. I have been keeping something back and I want you to see it before anyone breathes on it."

*Non-patron, every subsequent visit:*
> "You again. Have you practised?" *(he means it literally and he will check)*

### If Player Declines / Alternative Path
*Walking away without buying:*
> "Yes. Fine. It is only paper." *(the metronome ticks; he does not look up)* "It is not only paper."

*Refusing "The Lost Opus":*
> "Of course. It is a long way and it is an old man's errand and the thing may not exist." *(returns to ruling a stave)* "It exists."

### Callback Dialogue (Return Visits)
*Late game, patron path, after "The Lost Opus" completes:*
> "It was real. Sixty years of people telling me it was a story, and it was real, and it was in a cellar under a wine bar." *(he has not put it in the cart; it is in his coat)* "I am not selling this one."

*Late game, if the player has passed every humming challenge ("The Humming Champion"):*
> "You have never once hummed badly at me. Do you know how rare that is? I do. I have the number. It is four." *(pause)* "You are the fourth."

*Late game, if the player has only ever hummed badly:*
> "You have hummed at me eleven times. Eleven. I have priced you accordingly and I have sold you good music anyway, because I am not a monster, and because somewhere in eleven attempts there was one bar that was almost right and I have not stopped thinking about it."

### Patron of the Arts System
**Unlocked after 100 Sovs total spent.** Benefits, in full:
- Access to the premium manuscript inventory (the side compartment, which was always there)
- **10% permanent discount**, applied on top of any humming discount
- He remembers your name, and uses it, every visit, for the rest of the game
- You may request specific compositions and he will source them
- **You are allowed to hum.** Within reason. He defines "within reason" verbally, as you go, and it is a real running dialogue system rather than a flag.

### Hidden Details / Easter Eggs
- **Examine the sign** (*"Please Do Not Hum. We Will Know If It's Wrong."*): *"Hand-lettered. The 'We' has been painted over once and restored. There is no 'we'. There has not been a 'we' for a long time."*
- **Examine the metronome**: *"Ticking at 60. Set in the first year. Never adjusted. It is not a tempo — it is a second hand."*
- **Examine the blank manuscript page in his coat**: *"Ruled by hand. Five staves. Nothing on them. Carried for years."*
- **Examine the Lost Manuscript before purchase**: *"Unattributed. 1780s. The bass line does something in the fourth bar that no one was doing in the 1780s. The catalogue mark reads 'J.B.'"* — this is the **Johann Bassmeister** cross-link (`sinfonia_npc_johann_bassmeister_01`, Mozart's Last Rave). Herr Notenschreiber has no idea who J.B. was, has spent eleven years failing to find out, and will pay for information. Presenting Bassmeister lore acquired at the graveyard opens a dialogue branch he has been waiting his whole professional life for.
- **Knock over a manuscript pile** ("Paper Avalanche") and he does not shout. He kneels down and starts re-sorting, silently, and does not accept help, and the silence lasts until you leave.
- Hum Beethoven's Fifth at him and he finishes it for you, correctly, and then looks embarrassed, because he started it that one time with Lord Pilsner and he has not forgiven himself.

### Territory / Social Dynamics
- The cart is the ranking musical authority on MusikGasse and every performer on the street knows it, including the ones who resent it.
- He never approaches a performer to correct them. He waits. He suffers visibly. They come to him.
- The street's unwritten rule: you do not hum within thirty metres of the cart unless you mean it. Tourists do not know this rule. This is the entire location.
- Herr Notenschreiber does not compete with anyone, does not discuss other vendors, and does not have a rival. Asked to compare, he says the same thing every time: *"There is no other cart. There is no other cart because nobody else would do this."*

## 🎯 Quest Integration

### Primary Quest
- **Quest**: "The Lost Opus" (`sinfonia_sidequest_the_lost_opus_01`)
- **Role**: Quest Giver and sole beneficiary — he seeks one specific rare manuscript and has been looking for eleven years
- **Trigger**: Reaching Patron of the Arts (100 Sovs lifetime spend), OR triggering the made-up-tune humming outcome, which stops him mid-transaction and starts the conversation early
- **Outcome**: The manuscript is recovered. He does not stock it, does not price it, and does not sell it. It goes in his coat. He says "I am not selling this one" and nothing else about it for the rest of the game.

### Additional Quest Hooks
| Quest | His Role | Trigger | Outcome |
|-------|----------|---------|---------|
| "Correct Interpretation" | Teacher | Accept a performance commitment elsewhere in Sinfonia and come to him to learn the piece properly | He drills you. It is genuinely instructional, genuinely long, and the performance succeeds because of it. |
| "The Humming Champion" | Judge | Pass every humming challenge in the table above without a single failure | Achievement; the "four people in sixty years" callback; permanent maximum discount stacking |
| "Forbidden Music" | Supplier and obstacle | Late game; requires Patron status and the Forbidden Opus already purchased | He knows what the banned composition is, where it is, and why it was banned, and he argues with himself out loud for a full minute before telling you |

### Quest Gate Function
He gates the **premium manuscript inventory** behind 100 Sovs lifetime spend — a published, honest threshold with no hidden conditions. He gates "The Lost Opus" behind Patron status or the made-up-tune outcome. He gates nothing else: every standard item, every consumable, and the entire humming mini-game are available to a player who has never bought anything and hums badly.

## 🗣️ Voice Direction

### Accent
Austrian-accented English, elderly, precise, with the vocabulary of a lifetime specialist. He pronounces every composer, catalogue number, and Italian tempo marking correctly and does not slow down to do it. Never a cartoon, never a doddering mumble — the diction is exact even when the volume is low.

### Tone
- **Trading**: professional, guarded, faintly weary. Fair prices delivered in a flat register with no salesmanship whatsoever.
- **Identification**: instant, certain, and entirely without triumph. He is not showing off. He simply knows.
- **The pain**: involuntary. Weary sighs, small anguished noises, a genuine intake of breath. Never comic mugging — the discomfort is physical and the joke is that it is real.
- **"A BILLION people can be WRONG."**: full volume, and it is the only time in the location he raises his voice. It should be startling.
- **"...Continue."**: quiet. Four seconds of nothing first. Do not add warmth to it — the warmth arrives two lines later and it has to be earned by the silence.
- **The Recommendation**: this is the one place he is unguarded with a stranger, and the voice should drop the defensiveness entirely for the length of the recommendation and pick it straight back up afterward.

### Key Delivery Notes
- **All six canon lines ship verbatim** and must match `the_composers_cart.md` byte-for-byte: the major/minor greeting, the Mozart line, "...You have taste," "That wasn't music," "A BILLION people can be WRONG," and "Please leave the 'dun duns' to the professionals." The connective staging has craft latitude; those lines do not.
- **He is never wrong about music.** Not once, not as a gag, not as an act-three humbling. Every identification is correct, every recommendation is good, every price is fair. The character only works because his expertise is unimpeachable.
- **He is not a scammer and no line may imply otherwise.** The location file is explicit: prices remain fair, he is just disappointed. Any dialogue that reads as an upsell is wrong.
- **The gatekeeping is a wound, not a personality.** Every gatekeeping line should carry the sense of a man defending himself rather than a man asserting superiority. If it plays as snobbery the satire has inverted onto music education, which is the one thing this location must never do.
- **Bradley gets exactly one moment.** He does not become the music guy. He does not explain. He does not gloat. "I paid attention during lessons" is the last thing he says on the subject in the entire game.
- Never let him wink. He does not know he is in a satire, does not know the sign is funny, and has never once considered that "We Will Know If It's Wrong" might read as a threat.
