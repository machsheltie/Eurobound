*"I'm perfectly calm. Completely calm. Your bet?"*

## 🎭 Core Concept

### What They're Actually Doing
Graf von Zittrig comes four nights a week, sits at the lowest-minimum poker table in the room, brings exactly 200 Sovs, and goes home when it is gone or when it has doubled, whichever happens first. He has been doing this for nineteen years. He is a genuinely terrible poker player in one very specific and entirely public way: he adjusts his cufflinks when he is bluffing, he has always done it, everyone knows, and he cannot stop. He knows about it. He has tried tying his hands together under the table. It moved to his collar and then came back.

He is not being ruined. The 200 Sovs is an evening's entertainment he can comfortably afford, he never brings a second 200 Sovs, and in nineteen years he has never once asked the house for credit. The sweating, the dabbing, the visible torment — that is simply what it costs him to enjoy the thing he enjoys most, and he considers the price reasonable.

### What Bros Believe
The bros identify him instantly as prey and are, for once, essentially correct — and they take entirely the wrong lesson from it. Beating Graf von Zittrig at 5 Sovs hands is the first thing that goes right for them in Sinfonia, and Lord Pilsner reads it as confirmation of a talent he has always suspected he had. Chadwick begins referring to him as "my guy." By the end of the night, in their account, the Graf was a formidable aristocrat they broke down over a long, brutal session, rather than a fifty-eight-year-old man with a 200 Sovs limit who folds when anybody raises.

### The Comedy Gap
He is the one opponent in the building the bros can actually beat, and the win teaches them exactly the wrong thing at exactly the wrong moment, roughly ninety minutes before they meet the corner table. The Graf, meanwhile, has an absolutely lovely evening, loses his customary 200 Sovs to some enthusiastic visitors, thanks them sincerely, and comes back Thursday.

**Binding:** nobody in the room ever tells the player that the early win is why the later loss happens. No dealer aside, no narrator line, no achievement string. The player sees the sequence and is trusted to see it.

## 👤 Appearance

### Physical Description
| Attribute | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| Age | Late 50s |
| Build | Soft, well-fed, and permanently slightly damp; sits forward in the chair as though the table might be taken away |
| Height | Average |
| Energy | Excruciating, endearing, entirely un-hideable |

### Clothing
- **Excellent tuxedo, honestly earned**: Twenty-two years old, recut twice, and being sweated through in real time. The tailoring is better than anything the bros are wearing and none of them notices.
- **The cufflinks**: Heavy, old, family. They are the source of everything and he could simply not wear them and has never once considered it.
- **Accessories**: Two pocket squares — one for display, one for his forehead, and he uses the display one when the other is spent; a small silver flask of water, refilled by staff without being asked.
- **Footwear**: Formal, correct, and tapping.

### Distinguishing Features
- The cufflink adjustment. Left, then right, then left again. **It fires when he is bluffing and it fires at no other time.** He has done it an estimated four hundred thousand times.
- Dabbing at the temple with a pocket square between every single hand.
- Announces that he is calm, out loud, unprompted, to a table that has not raised the subject.
- A genuinely lovely laugh, which arrives only when he is losing, and which makes the whole table like him.

### Sprite Notes
Sweating through expensive clothes, and the clothes must be visibly expensive — he is not shabby, he is not a fool, he is a well-dressed man being publicly tormented by a card game he loves. The comedy is in the gap between the tailoring and the man inside it, never in cheapness. Priority frames: the cufflink adjustment (must be huge, unmissable, and identical every time), the temple dab, and the forward-perched idle. He should be immediately likeable at 64px. Reference energy: a man having the worst and best evening of his week, simultaneously, on purpose.

## 🧠 Personality

### Archetype
**The Delighted Sufferer** — a nobleman who discovered at thirty-nine that he loves poker and is dreadful at it, and who resolved that conflict by simply continuing. He is not addicted, not desperate, and not in trouble; he is a man with a hobby that costs him 200 Sovs a night and takes ten years off his life every time he holds a pair.

### Personality Traits
| Trait | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| Physically Incapable of Bluffing | The hands do it for him, every time, in public, unignorably |
| Announces His Own Calm | Says "I am perfectly calm" aloud, unprompted, several times an evening, and has never once been calm |
| Scrupulously Disciplined | 200 Sovs, no more, no credit, nineteen years, no exceptions — the one thing at this table he is genuinely excellent at |
| Warm to Strangers | Delighted by new players, welcomes them to the table, explains the house rules unasked and correctly |
| Un-self-conscious About Losing | Laughs when it goes badly, congratulates the winner specifically, and never sulks |

### Internal Monologue
> "Do not touch them. Do not touch them. You are a grown man and a Graf and you will simply not touch them. — He has raised. Oh, God, he has raised. — Do not touch— *(touches them)* — Ah. Well. Yes. There it is again. Fold, then, and no harm done, and there are four hours left and the champagne is very good tonight."

### Motivation
He loves the room. He loves the felt, the noise, the small society of people who are all there for the same silly reason, and the four nights a week when he is not at home being a Graf. The money is the entry fee to an evening among people who are pleased to see him, and he has calculated, correctly, that it is cheap at the price.

## 💬 Dialogue

### Approach/Greeting
**A new player sits down at his table:**
> "Ah — new blood, new blood! Sit, sit. The minimum here is the kindest in the room and the company is the best. Do you know the house rules? No? I shall tell you them, and I shall tell you them correctly, which is more than the placard does."

**Canon line, ships verbatim — delivered mid-hand, with the accompanying business:**
> "I'm perfectly calm. *(adjusts cufflinks for the 47th time)* Completely calm. Your bet? *(cufflinks again)*"

### Key Interaction Dialogue — At the Table
- **Bluffing** *(the hands are already moving)*: "It is a strong board. A very strong board. For somebody." *(left cuff, right cuff, left cuff)*
- **Holding a monster** *(hands perfectly still, voice an octave up)*: "I suppose I shall have a look at it. I suppose we shall all just... have a look."
- **Folding to any raise, which is every raise**: "No. No, no. Absolutely not. Take it, take it, you've earned it — you played that beautifully."
- **When somebody points at his cufflinks**: "Yes." *(a pause of great dignity)* "Yes, I am aware. My wife is also aware. Sinfonia is aware. Shall we continue?"

### To the Bros
*Lord Pilsner has just taken a 40 Sovs pot off him and is describing the achievement to the room.*

**Graf von Zittrig** *(genuinely, warmly, applauding with two fingers against his palm)*:
> "Marvellous. Genuinely — you read me exactly, sir, and you were right to. Do let me buy you something. What is it that you drink?"

**Lord Pilsner** *(to Chadwick, entirely certain)*: "He's rattled. I'm INSIDE his head."

**Graf von Zittrig** *(to the dealer, cheerfully, while dabbing his temple)*: "Deal on. The gentlemen are hot tonight and I am not, and that is how it goes."

### Post-Interaction — Out of Money
*The 200 Sovs is gone. It is 12:40 AM. He is not distressed in the slightest.*

> "And that is me. That is me, exactly on schedule." *(stands, pockets the damp square)* "A lovely table tonight. Thursday, then. Thursday, and I shall be worse."

### If Player Declines / Alternative Path
*Player leaves his table, or refuses the seat:*
> "Quite right, quite right — the faster tables are through there, and the money over there is serious. This corner of the room is for enjoying oneself." *(He is not being wry. He means every word.)*

### Callback Dialogue (Return Visits)
> "The gentlemen return! Sit, sit — I have a whole two hundred, untouched, and it is nine o'clock and anything is possible."

*Post-encounter, if the party returns after the corner table — the only time he lowers his voice all game:*
> "I saw. From this table, I saw." *(a small, admiring shake of the head, entirely without pity)* "He does that." *(and nothing further; no branch exists in which he elaborates)*

## 🗣️ Voice Direction

### Accent
Old-money Sinfonian, formal sentence construction, and pitched about a third higher than the man clearly intends. He speaks quickly when bluffing and slowly when strong, which is a second tell nobody has ever needed.

### Tone
- **Table talk**: warm, hospitable, faintly frantic
- **Bluffing**: fast, over-explanatory, rising — a man narrating his way out of a hand and failing
- **Losing**: genuinely delighted; the laugh is real and should be lovely
- **The two-short-line Aaron callback**: quiet, admiring, and completely without pity — the only low-volume lines he has

### Key Delivery Notes
- **The canon "perfectly calm" line ships verbatim**, with the cufflink business landing exactly where the location file puts it, both times.
- He is never played as pathetic. He is a well-liked man with excellent manners having a great evening at a fixed price, and the direction must protect that — the audience should like him more than it pities him, always.
- His congratulations to whoever beat him are sincere in every take. No irony, no gritted teeth.
- He never comments on the bros' delusions, never corrects them, and never once implies they are not as good as they think.

## 📝 Development Notes

- **The cufflink tell is canon and must be enormous.** It fires on bluffs only, is never suppressed, and is never confirmed by a `tell_indicator` UI element — the player learns it by watching, exactly as the location file intends.
- **The 200 Sovs ceiling is a hard character rule.** No credit branch, no second buy-in, no "one more" scene, ever. It is the discipline that keeps him a comedy character rather than a tragedy, and removing it changes what the room is satirising.
- **He is never humiliated by the text.** The bros' win off him is their misreading, not his failure, and no scene may frame him as pitiable. He is well-dressed, well-liked, and having a better night than anyone.
- **He never corrects a bro** and never remarks on their play except to praise it sincerely.
- His post-encounter Aaron callback is two short lines and stops. No pass may extend it into an explanation of the corner table.
- Animation priority: the cufflink cycle. Identical every time, at full amplitude, with no variation between bluffs — the joke depends on it being the same gesture forever.
