# NPC Profile: Johann Bassmeister

*"Mozart got the FAME. I got the BASS."*

## 📋 NPC Identity
- **NPC ID**: `sinfonia_npc_johann_bassmeister_01`
- **Display Name**: Johann Bassmeister
- **Internal Name**: Johann Bassmeister — "The Undead DJ, Ghost of Groove." Contemporary of Mozart, allegedly. Inventor of the bass drop, allegedly. Dead of excessive rhythm at 35, allegedly. Every part of this is club lore and the club has never been asked to produce paperwork for any of it.
- **Role**: Main Boss (three phases, dual resolution) / Post-Boss Vendor (appeased route only) / Lore Source / Couchsurf Morning Cameo
- **Associated Quest**: `sinfonia_sidequest_the_lost_composition_01` ("The Lost Composition") — the appeasement route; also the terminus of `sinfonia_sidequest_finding_bassmeisters_grave_01` and a required beat in `sinfonia_sidequest_dance_until_you_drop_01`
- **Primary Location**: The Conductor's Crypt (boss arena), Mozart's Last Rave (`sinfonia_cemetery_mozarts_last_rave_01`), beneath Komponistenfriedhof
- **Secondary Appearances**: His own headstone on the cemetery surface — "Johann Bassmeister, 1756-1791," the club entrance, knocked three times in rhythm; The Requiem Room (VIP, post-completion, appeased route — vendor stance and the Bone Throne selfie variant); The Dancefloor of the Damned (ambient passes between sets, appeased route)

## 🎭 Core Concept

### What He's Actually Doing

Johann Bassmeister is the headline act of a fifteen-year-old licensed nightclub — *Mozarts Letzte Raserei*, as the door plate under the headstone has it — and he goes on at approximately 2 AM every night the venue trades, which is every night the venue trades.

The set is a three-phase structure built around a converted pipe organ, timed to a house beat clock, with four supporting performers on the cardinal booths and a room full of skeletons who hit their marks. It is a genuinely excellent show. It has been refined over fifteen years. The health inspection certificate hanging by the kiosk upstairs covers the room he performs in.

Whether the performer is a ghost, a hallucination brought on by 18 Sovs of novelty cocktail, or an extremely committed professional in a costume is **never established by this game and must never be established by this game.** Every reading holds. Helga, who has worked twenty metres from him for twenty years, calls the skeletons "lovely coworkers" and has never elaborated. The theatre students who work the floor earn 15 Sovs/hour and are only ever seen off-shift, away from the room. Nothing in the Conductor's Crypt resolves anything. That is the design.

What is *not* ambiguous is the grievance. Whoever or whatever is up there behind the organ is genuinely, structurally furious about Mozart, has been for the entire run, and does not stop being furious when the music stops. The bitterness is the most consistent thing about him and the only thing anyone who has met him agrees on.

### What Bros Believe

That they have descended into a grave, in a cemetery, at 1 AM, and discovered an actual undead entity performing actual necromantic electronic dance music, and that they are possibly the only living Americans ever to have witnessed it.

**Lord Pilsner** has decided the story is tragic and is deeply moved by it: *"Wait, so this guy INVENTED bass drops and Mozart stole his thunder? That's... actually really sad."* **Chadwick** cannot decide whether this is the greatest night of his life or a documented supernatural incident, and has settled on both. **Bradley** has run the numbers — *"There is a 0% probability of actual reanimation. I am... 12% uncertain"* — and the twelve per cent is where he lives for the rest of the night.

None of them notice the gaffer-taped cable snake running across the floor to the organ.

### The Comedy Gap

The bros believe they have found a secret. The secret has a Yelp page with 847 reviews, 4.2 stars on TripAdvisor, fifteen years of filed tax notices pinned by the pass kiosk, a framed and current health inspection certificate, and a security guard who patrols past the open grave without turning his head, because he is aware that the grave is the door and has been aware for over a decade.

The 5 Sovs "Composer's Rest" Cemetery Visitor Pass is club cover. Everybody in the queue at 11:40 PM knows this. The bros bought three of them believing they were sneaking in.

And underneath all of that, the joke turns over once more and lands somewhere unexpectedly sincere: the show is *good*. Bassmeister is a tremendous performer. The lore is well-constructed. The bros are having an authentic, genuinely excellent night at a professionally-run venue, and the only thing they are wrong about is how hard it was to find.

### The Ambiguity Is Load-Bearing

No dialogue line, examine text, animation frame, sound cue, or item description in this profile — or anywhere else — confirms or denies what Bassmeister is. He never removes anything. Nobody ever refers to him by another name. He is never seen arriving or leaving. He is also never seen doing anything a very good performer could not do. Both readings must survive a full playthrough intact, including the couchsurf morning, including the vendor route, including the credits.

## 👤 Appearance

### Physical Description
| Attribute | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| Age | Died at 35 (1756–1791, per the headstone). Presents as a skeleton, which complicates the question and is meant to. |
| Build | Tall, narrow, theatrically angular; moves like a conductor who learned to DJ rather than a DJ who learned to conduct |
| Height | Tall — deliberately taller than any bro, and taller than the organ bench suggests he should be |
| Energy | Wounded showmanship, two centuries overdue |

### Clothing
- **18th-century formal wear**: Full period coat, waistcoat, breeches, lace at the throat. Period-credible enough to survive being looked at. This is not a costume-shop approximation and must not read as one — the entire character is the collision between this and the next item.
- **Modern DJ headphones**: Contemporary, over-ear, worn around the neck between drops and up on the skull during them. The single most important design element on the sprite.
- **Accessories**: A conductor's baton worn where a sword would go, used exactly once per fight as a fader; a ring of iron keys at the hip that nobody has ever asked about; lace cuffs that have been cut back at the wrist so they don't foul the decks — a working alteration, made by someone who does this every night.
- **Footwear**: Buckled period shoes. Scuffed at the toe on the left only, from two hundred and thirty years of the same bass-kick stance.

### Distinguishing Features
- **The count-in.** Before every attack he lifts two fingers and counts the party in. He is telegraphing on purpose. He wants a good fight, not a slaughter.
- Tilts his skull when a player lands a perfect on-beat hit — a small, involuntary, entirely professional flicker of respect that he immediately covers with a bigger gesture.
- Says "Mozart" the way other people say a name they have practised saying calmly.
- Between attacks he adjusts something on the organ that does not need adjusting.
- On the appeased route: the shoulders come down. Nothing else about him changes at all. Same skeleton, same coat, same headphones — the tension simply leaves.

### Sprite Notes
He must never read as a monster and never as a joke prop. The register is *headliner*: this is a performer with a two-hundred-and-thirty-year residency and total command of a room. The comedy lives in the collision — lace cuffs on a fader, buckled shoes on a bass kick — not in exaggeration.

Priority frames: the intro rise from behind the organ (the room's biggest moment); the two-finger count-in (used before every attack, needs real frames); the "Last Rave" 3.0-second held wind-up with the escalating charge overlay (this is the mechanic — the telegraph must be obviously, generously long); and the appeasement recognition frame, which is the only frame in the location where nothing is being performed.

**Absolute constraint, per the PNG asset spec:** no frame may resolve the ghost/hallucination/performer question. No visible harness, no seam, no wire, no zip — and equally no unambiguous supernatural tell. If an artist can look at a frame and answer the question, the frame is wrong.

## 🧠 Personality

### Archetype
**The Uncredited Headliner** — a magnificent performer with one wound, who has built an entire afterlife-shaped career around a credit he never got, and who is far more hurt than he is dangerous. He is theatrical because theatre is his job. He is bitter because the bitterness is real. He never once takes it out on the crowd — every attack in his set is telegraphed, counted in, and survivable, because ruining the show would be unprofessional and because, underneath everything, he wants them to have a good time and then remember his name.

### Personality Traits
| Trait | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| Theatrical by trade | Everything is delivered to the back of the room. Reverb is not an effect on his voice; it is how he talks. |
| Genuinely wounded | The Mozart material is not a bit. Ask him about it twice and the second answer is quieter than the first. |
| Scrupulously fair | Every attack telegraphs. The "Last Rave" wind-up is three full seconds because he built it that way. He has never actually hurt a customer and the room's safety record is spotless. |
| Professionally proud | He will not talk over the drop. He will not start Phase 2 early. He starts at 2 AM and he starts at 2 AM. |
| Starving for the credit | The appeasement works because it is the only thing anyone has ever offered him that he actually wanted, and it costs the party nothing but the trouble of looking. |

### Internal Monologue
> "They are counting. Good — they are counting. Two hundred and thirty years and the ones who count are always the ones who stay till five. — He wrote forty-one symphonies and I wrote one and mine is the one nobody can find. — If they get to the organ during the wind-up I will let them have it, and I will act astonished, and I will mean the astonishment, because somebody remembered where the counter-play was."

### Motivation
To be named. Not avenged, not worshipped, not feared — named. The whole three-phase set, the four supporting DJs, the converted organ, the fifteen years, the two hundred and thirty: all of it is an extremely elaborate way of standing in a room and saying *I made this* to people who might repeat it. The appeasement route works precisely because it hands him the one thing the entire structure was built to demand, and once he has it the structure is no longer necessary, which is why he becomes a shopkeeper without a trace of embarrassment.

### Positioning Notes
- The arena is the deepest room in the location and the only one with no second exit. Entering is a commitment and the descent telegraphs it — the bone decorations get more party-themed the whole way down, and then in the Conductor's Crypt they stop being party-themed entirely.
- He performs on the raised Boss Chamber Dais with the organ behind him and the Organ Pipe Bank as the back wall. The organ is an interactable object at all times, including before the fight, and examining it early is the single best hint the location gives.
- During the fight the room drops all ambient dancers and the disco ball to free draw calls — the crowd is gone, and the player is meant to notice that they are now alone with the headliner.
- On the appeased route his vendor counter is placed so the Bone Throne is in his sightline, which is why the selfie variant exists.

### Territory / Social Dynamics
- The four elemental DJs are explicitly **his orchestra** — same construction, four silhouettes, one lore. Defeating all four before descending changes nothing mechanically and changes his Phase 2 add-summon line, which is the only reward and is worth it.

### Evolution Through Game
**Combat route:** nothing evolves. He plays the same set every night, delivers the same intro verbatim, and never recognises the party. The Lost Composition remains in the organ pipe bank, findable and unusable, for the rest of the game — the single most quietly bleak object in Sinfonia, and the game never comments on it.

**Appeased route:** he stops being a boss and becomes a shopkeeper, permanently, and is genuinely good at it. The Bone Throne selfie gains its special variant. The couchsurf morning gains the breakfast. His callback line gets fractionally warmer every time the party clears a Sinfonia location, which is never remarked upon by anyone including him.

## 🗣️ Voice Direction

### Accent
Theatrical, period-inflected Sinfonian — a stage voice, not a horror voice. Per the location's audio design he carries a permanent **echo effect and dramatic reverb on every line**, including the vendor dialogue, including the breakfast. The reverb never drops, which is either a supernatural property or a very good radio mic, and the mix must never let anyone decide which.

### Tone
- **The intro**: to the back of the room. Enormous. He has done this ten thousand times and it has not gone stale, because it is the only ten thousand times anyone has heard him say his own name.
- **Phase dialogue**: showmanship over menace. He is running a set, not a slaughter. Every attack line is a cue, not a threat.
- **The Mozart material**: this is the one place the performance thins. The bitterness underneath is real and should be audibly costly to say.
- **The appeasement**: **no reverb reduction, no comedy scoring, no undercut, no wink.** This is the most sincere twelve seconds in Sinfonia and it must be played completely straight. The music does the work — layers dropping out to strings.
- **The vendor stance**: warm, brisk, and slightly too loud for a shop, because he does not have another volume.

### Key Delivery Notes
- Every canon line in this profile ships **verbatim** against `mozarts_last_rave.md`. The connective staging around them has craft latitude; the quoted lines do not.
- **"Feel the BASS... feel it in your BONES... because I don't have any FLESH." is DJ Grave-o's line, at the north booth.** It has never been Bassmeister's and must never be given to him in any pass, script, or marketing asset.
- Capitalisation in his dialogue is stress, not volume — he is not shouting, he is *landing*. The difference matters for a two-hour dungeon.
- He never mocks the party's dancing after Phase 1. Once the fight is real, he treats them as an audience worth playing to.
- Nothing in his delivery ever confirms or denies what he is. If a take resolves the ambiguity in either direction, it is the wrong take.

## 📝 Development Notes

- **THE CENTRAL AMBIGUITY IS BINDING.** Whether the undead of Mozart's Last Rave are real, hallucinated, or performed is deliberately never confirmed by `mozarts_last_rave.md` and must never be confirmed here or anywhere downstream. Helga calls the skeletons "lovely coworkers"; the bros' morning-after account is that they danced with ghosts; the stated reality is theatre students in costume at 15 Sovs/hour. All three coexist. No line, frame, sound cue, item description, or achievement may adjudicate.
- **"Feel the BASS..." belongs to DJ Grave-o.** Recorded here because it is the single most likely misattribution in this location.
- **The three phases are canon and load-bearing**: Phase 1 The Warmup (100–66%, 120 BPM steady), Phase 2 The Breakdown (66–33%, fluctuating 80–160 BPM), Phase 3 The Finale (33–0%, boss-controlled reactive). Do not re-tune without re-tuning `mozarts_last_rave.md` in the same pass.
- **The "Last Rave" wind-up is 3.0 seconds because that is exactly how long it takes to reach the organ.** This is the counter-play, it is the whole mechanic, and it must never be shortened for pacing.
- **The Lost Composition must be findable on a blind first run.** It is packed into a pipe in the organ pipe bank and is reachable by examining rather than attacking. If playtesting shows players missing it entirely, add a Ghost Groupie hint — do not add a quest marker.
- **The appeasement route is the better ending and the game never says so.** No achievement text, tooltip, or NPC line may signal that appeasement is "correct." Both achievements — "Appeased the Dead" and "Slayed the Dead" — are worded neutrally.
- **The combat route's silence is intentional.** He does not remember the party, ever. The Lost Composition stays in the pipe for the rest of the game. Nothing comments on this.
- **Sprite sheet contradiction — RESOLVED (author ruling: the PNG_Assets spec wins).** The old `mozarts_last_rave.md` PNG summary specced `bassmeister_boss.png` at 256x384 plus a separate `bassmeister_appeased.png` at 128x192. `PNG_Assets/Mozarts_Last_Rave_PNG_Assets.md` specs a single consolidated `bassmeister_boss.png` at **512x768** with sixteen 128x192 cells, including the four appeased/resolution frames, and does not list `bassmeister_appeased.png` among its eight required files. **The spec is correct.** This profile and the location file have both been corrected; commission the 512x768 sheet only.
- **Quest IDs coined this pass** in the established Sinfonia pattern and not yet present in any other file: `sinfonia_sidequest_the_lost_composition_01`, `sinfonia_sidequest_finding_bassmeisters_grave_01`, `sinfonia_sidequest_dance_until_you_drop_01`. The four mini-boss NPC IDs (`sinfonia_npc_dj_graveo_01` etc.) are likewise coined here and need profiles of their own.
- **No new first names introduced.** "Johann" is already established in Sinfonia. The Grave Guide, the Ghost Groupies, the security guard, and the theatre students remain unnamed by design.
- **No real composer's grave is depicted or mocked anywhere in this location.** Every tomb occupant, including Bassmeister, is fictional. Mozart is treated with irreverence — he is the subject of a two-hundred-and-thirty-year professional grudge — and never with disrespect. The joke is that Mozart would have loved this place.
