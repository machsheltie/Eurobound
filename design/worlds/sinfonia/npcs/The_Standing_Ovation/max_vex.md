🎭 Core Concept
What They're Actually Doing
Max Vex is one of Sinfonia nightlife's larger-than-life fixtures: an exceptionally good emcee, flirt, improvisational comic, and room reader who hosts a late-night drag revue.
Max is not a spokesperson for nonbinary people and has no interest in becoming one. They are an entertainer.
Onstage, their combination of confidence, sexual frankness, fast social perception, and complete comfort with themselves gives them enormous control over a room. They can identify an audience member's insecurity almost immediately and decide whether to caress it, puncture it, or spend the next four minutes playing with it.
The three American bros are unusually fertile material.
Offstage, Max remains recognizably Max. The volume changes; the person doesn't.
Their major comic weakness is poker.
Max is excellent at reading other people and absolutely terrible at preventing other people from reading Max. The emotional transparency that makes them magnetic onstage becomes a liability at a poker table.
Max knows the game. Max understands strategy. Max isn't stupid.
Max simply has tells you can see from space.
They keep playing because they genuinely love sitting around a poker table, drinking, bullshitting with strangers, hearing stories, flirting, arguing, and trying to read people.
Every once in a while, a sufficiently inexperienced player arrives who Max can read but who cannot read Max.
Max destroys them.
Those victories constitute virtually all of Max's evidence that Max is actually very good at poker.
What Bros Believe
The bros initially encounter Max in the environment where Max has maximum power.
Their first mistake is assuming that they understand what kind of person they're dealing with.
Their second is speaking.
Pilsner initially treats Max's attention as further evidence of his universal sexual magnetism.
Chadwick attempts to preserve respectable-married-man composure and accidentally reveals information Max hadn't even asked for.
Bradley encounters a more serious systems failure: Max's combination of attractiveness, gender presentation, confidence, flirtation and merciless teasing does not fit the sexual taxonomy Bradley has been operating since approximately 2002.
Bradley's body resolves the ambiguity before Bradley does.
The Comedy Gap
Max is not destabilized by the bros. The bros are destabilized by Max.
The comedy never depends upon nonbinary identity itself being strange, deceptive, threatening, or ridiculous. Max knows exactly who they are.
The three men who survived Y2K and refused all subsequent patches are the unstable variable.
Most importantly, Max does not exist to teach them anything.
No heartfelt identity discussion follows.
Nobody receives a lesson.
Bradley gets a boner.

🧠 Personality
Archetype
The Magnificent Instigator
Max likes people, particularly when people are interesting.
Unfortunately for interesting people, Max's preferred method of expressing affection is frequently to poke the thing they are desperately trying to conceal and see what happens.
They are socially fearless, sexually frank, fast, observant, shameless, theatrical and enormously difficult to embarrass.
Max doesn't need to dominate every interaction. They simply know that they can.

Internal Monologue
Oh, he's trying very hard not to look at me now. Well. We can't have that.

Motivation
Max wants a good room, a good story and an interesting night.
They enjoy performance because performance compresses human behavior: nervous people become more nervous, confident people become louder, horny people become idiots, and everybody eventually reveals something.
Max genuinely enjoys people.
Poker offers many of the same pleasures—strangers, alcohol, lies, bravado, money and hours of conversation—which is why Max keeps returning despite poker being one of the few social environments where being Max Vex is an actual strategic disadvantage.

📝 Development Notes
- Core law: Max is a character who happens to be nonbinary, not Eurobound's designated representative of nonbinary people.
- Drag-show law: The performers and Max control the room. Drag itself is never the humiliating spectacle; the bros are.
- Bradley law: His attraction is real. The comedy comes from the incompatibility between the evidence supplied by his body and his fossilized 2002 worldview.
- TROUSER SNAKE law: Do not turn it into punishment for attraction. The status represents physical inconvenience, concentration failure and Bradley's inability to behave normally.
- Poker law: Max isn't stupid or arbitrarily bad at cards. Their strength causes their weakness: extraordinary social perception + extraordinary emotional legibility.
- Poker variance: Skilled players read Max. Max reads novices. Every sufficiently satisfying victory reinforces Max's belief that they are much better at poker than their lifetime results suggest.
- Aaron separation: Max never intrudes upon “The Kings Were Humming.” That scene belongs completely to Aaron and the bros. The canonical quest deliberately gives the player only embarrassing dialogue choices while Aaron takes the fixed 500-Sov collateral with pocket kings.    thekingswerehumming
- Representation law: Do not make Max morally perfect, endlessly patient, educational, sexless or universally beloved in an attempt to make them “good representation.”
- Recurring-character law: Max remembers the bros because they're funny, not because they have become central to Max's life.
- Chadwick law: Max pretending not to remember Chadwick is significantly funnier than giving him a nickname.
- Visual law: Max must be genuinely desirable. Bradley's reaction cannot depend entirely on dialogue telling the player Max is attractive.
