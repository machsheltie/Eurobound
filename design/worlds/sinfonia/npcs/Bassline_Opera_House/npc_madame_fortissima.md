## 🎭 Core Concept

### What She's Actually Doing
Headlining an opera drag show that has been the Bassline Opera House's best-selling night of the week for eleven years. **DIE, FLEDERMAUS!** — the comma is the entire joke and it is printed four feet high on the marquee. It sells out. The critics review it seriously. The Sinfonia Philharmonic's second violinist has been in the pit for it since 2018 and considers it the best gig in the city.

Madame Fortissima is not in disguise. She is a working performer with a stage name, exactly like every soprano who has ever taken a professional name, and everybody in Sinfonia — the box office, the ushers, the coat check, the Maestro, the entire ticket-holding audience — is completely aware of what they have bought a ticket to, because it says so on the poster.

Afterwards she and Adelheid do what they have done after Saturday shows for eleven years: they take whoever is interesting up to the apartment, pour something appalling, and enjoy the evening.

### What Bros Believe
That two glamorous opera divas are obsessed with them. That they scored backstage passes through personal charisma. That this is the wildest groupie night of their lives and that they are, at last, being recognised as men of culture. Lord Pilsner has told Chadwick, twice, that he "just has a thing with performers." Chadwick is filming establishing shots. Bradley has read the programme and has questions he elects not to raise.

### What's Actually Happening
Two seasoned drag queens are running their standard Saturday, in which the entertainment is the tourists. The bros are tonight's entertainment. Fortissima and Adelheid are in complete control of the evening from the first backstage pass to the last Jägerbomb, they are enjoying themselves enormously, and the bros are having what is objectively the best night of their trip.

### The Obvious Signs Ignored
Every one of these is on screen, unmissable, and never remarked upon by any character:
1. **The marquee.** "DIE, FLEDERMAUS! — *A Night of Grand Opera, Performed Entirely by the Ladies of the House*". Lord Pilsner reads it out loud, correctly, on approach.
2. **The dressing room door plate**: brass, engraved, W. KRANZLER. Chadwick leans on it for a full minute.
3. **Wig lace** visible at the hairline in every close conversation, and a wig block on its stand in the corner of the room.
4. **The adam's apple**, unhidden, because she stopped bothering in 2009.
5. **The curtain call**, in which Fortissima removes her wig to a standing ovation from twelve hundred people. Chadwick describes this as "a costume change."
6. **The audience.** The entire audience. Cheering the joke the bros cannot see.
7. **The apartment's makeup station** — a lit six-foot mirror, two chairs, and thirty-one years of professional kit — which Lord Pilsner compliments as "a really nice vanity setup for a couple of girls."
8. **The shoes**: size 47, custom, four pairs, in the hall.

### The Comedy Gap
The joke is never that the divas are hiding something. The joke is that a thing is being announced at maximum volume, in lights, by twelve hundred applauding people, and three men from the United States are looking directly at it and seeing a groupie night. Nothing is concealed. Nothing needs to be. The bros are the only people in the building who do not know, and they will remain the only people in the building who do not know for the rest of the game.

## 👤 Appearance

### Physical Description
| Attribute | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| Age | 58 |
| Build | Broad-shouldered, magnificently upholstered, moves like a woman who has been carrying a costume this heavy for three decades and has excellent core strength as a result |
| Height | Tall. In the heels, extremely tall. Ducks under the dressing room doorframe without thinking about it. |
| Energy | Enormous presence, larger personality, absolutely unhurried |

### Stage Persona (Madame Fortissima)
- **The gown**: Imperial gold and oxblood, boned, corseted, with a train that requires a dresser and gets one. It is genuinely a beautiful piece of costume work and the shop that built it is proud of it.
- **The wig**: A tower. Powdered, pinned, and forty years out of period on purpose. Lace front visible at conversational distance and she has not cared since 2009.
- **The makeup**: Full stage paint, applied to be read from the back of the upper gallery, which means at four feet it is a landscape.
- **Accessories**: Opera-length gloves; a lorgnette she uses correctly; a stage fan that is also, functionally, how she conducts a room.
- **Footwear**: Size 47, custom, four inches, and she can do the entire second act in them.

### Off-Stage (Wolfram Kranzler)
- Grey at the temples, glasses on a chain, a cardigan of profound ordinariness over the corset he has not yet got out of.
- Sits differently. Talks at half the volume. Makes tea.
- **The voice does not change.** The speaking register is his and always was; Fortissima's voice is not a put-on, it is simply the same man being louder.
- Reading glasses come out for the crossword. He does the crossword in the apartment at 3 a.m. while three Americans are unconscious on his furniture, and he finishes it.

### Distinguishing Features
- Enters every room as though the room had been waiting.
- Never stops moving her hands while speaking, and every gesture is blocked — she is playing to a house even in a corridor.
- Deflects any compliment by escalating it: tell her she was good and she will tell you which specific bar she was good in.
- Touches the bros' shoulders, arms, and faces constantly, warmly, and entirely without agenda beyond enjoying herself.
- Off-stage, she goes very quiet for a beat before saying anything kind, as though checking it is safe to.

### Sprite Notes
She must read as **a great performer**, never as a punchline. The design brief is glamour that is genuinely glamorous, at scale — the audience gives her an ovation and the player must believe it. Avoid every cheap visual joke: no five o'clock shadow gag, no comedy stubble, no lipstick smear, no "man in a dress" staging. The lace front and the adam's apple are visible because she does not hide them, not because the art is winking.

Priority frames: the stage entrance (arms out, full gown, train settling), the curtain-call wig removal (this is a triumphant frame, not a reveal frame — she is *bowing*), the backstage dressing-room turn with the wig already off and the gown still on, the Nightcap pour, and the off-stage cardigan idle with the crossword.

## 🧠 Personality

### Archetype
**The Institution** — thirty-one seasons in one house, a genuine star at the regional level, entirely secure, entirely unbothered, and constitutionally incapable of underplaying anything. She is vain in the way of someone who has earned it and knows the exact size of what she earned. She is also, when it costs her nothing and even when it costs her something, extremely kind.

### Personality Traits
| Trait | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| Grandiose by default | Every anecdote features a monarch. Roughly a third of them are true, which is a much higher hit rate than anyone assumes. |
| Trades in flattery | Will give up genuine, useful information for a well-constructed compliment — and she grades the compliment |
| Utterly unbothered | Nothing about being clocked, not clocked, misread, or misgendered by a tourist registers as an event. She has been doing this since 1994. |
| Protective of Adelheid | Mentored her. Will not let anybody else criticise her. Criticises her constantly. |
| Hospitable to a fault | Feeds people. Waters people. Puts blankets on people. This is the entire reason the couchsurf exists. |

### Internal Monologue
> "Backstage passes, the flowers, and one of them has called me 'ma'am' four times without once looking at the poster. — The tall one is charming and does not know he is being charming, which is the good kind. — Adelheid is doing the eyebrow. Adelheid, do not do the eyebrow, they will not survive the eyebrow. — Right. Tray. Ice. The good bottle for us and the other one for them, and somebody put a towel under the one in the shorts before he sits on my chaise."

### Motivation
The show. Eleven years of a sold-out Saturday that she built, in a house that spent its first century deciding what did and did not belong on this stage. She is not making a point about it — she is simply the highest-grossing act in the building and she likes it here. Beyond that: a good evening with good company, which the bros unknowingly provide by being the funniest thing to walk into her dressing room in a decade.

## 💬 Dialogue

### Approach/Greeting
**First contact — the prop room corridor, before the party has any idea who she is:**
> "You are in my corridor. *(she does not move out of it)* People who are in my corridor generally want something. What do you want, and say it beautifully, because I have a warm-up in nine minutes."

**Greeting a party carrying backstage passes:**
> "I have performed for KINGS! For EMPERORS! For... *(the lorgnette comes up)* ...wait, who are you?"

**Chadwick**: "We're huge fans!"

> "Of *what*, exactly. *(genuine, delighted, waiting)* Go on. I have all night and no shame whatsoever."

### Key Interaction Dialogue — Trading Hints for Flattery
*Her documented function: she trades prop-room and backstage intel for compliments, and she grades them.*

| Compliment quality | Madame Fortissima | Reward |
|---|---|---|
| Generic ("you were amazing") | "Mm. That is what one says to a waiter." | Nothing |
| Specific ("the high F in Act Two") | "*(a pause; the fan closes)* ...The high F in Act Two. You *listened*." | One prop room hint |
| Technical (Bradley, sincerely, about breath support) | "Where did you come from. Where has this one BEEN." | Two hints + the backstage labyrinth shortcut |
| Flattery aimed at her looks only | "Yes. And?" | Nothing, warmly |
| Flattery aimed at Adelheid instead | "*(instantly, without heat)* She IS marvellous. Tell her. She will pretend she does not need it. She needs it." | One hint, plus Adelheid's disposition improves |

### The Show — DIE, FLEDERMAUS!
*On the marquee, four feet high, and the party walks under it:*
> **DIE, FLEDERMAUS!**
> *A Night of Grand Opera — Performed Entirely by the Ladies of the House*

**Lord Pilsner** *(reading it out loud, correctly, with the comma)*: "Die, Fledermaus. Sick. That's the bat one."

**Bradley**: "...Yes."

*Curtain call. Twelve hundred people. Fortissima takes the wig off and holds it out at arm's length like a trophy, and the house comes apart.*

**Chadwick**: "Bro, she did a COSTUME CHANGE. During the BOWING."

### The Backstage Invitation
> "You three are coming up. No — do not do the face, the face is not required. It is one flight of stairs and there is food, and I have watched all three of you eat a pretzel off a plinth tonight, so let us not pretend anybody is being generous to anybody."

**Lord Pilsner**: "Up where?"

> "I live above my work. Like a butcher. *(sweeping)* A butcher of the FIRST RANK."

### 🍹 Scripted Event — The Nightcap
*The divas' apartment above the opera house, approximately 01:40. This is a fixed scripted sequence and is co-owned with `sinfonia_npc_prima_donna_adelheid_01`; both profiles carry it identically and it plays once.*

**Setup:** A lacquered tray. A bottle of Jägermeister of no distinction whatsoever. Six cans of energy drink. Two small glasses set aside for the ladies, three tall ones for the guests. Adelheid handles the tall ones at the sideboard, with her back to the room, for approximately nine seconds. Nobody looks. Nobody would have to look. The camera does not cut away and does not linger.

**Fortissima** *(pouring, at volume, delighted)*:
> "The Nightcap! It is a TRADITION! It is a tradition because I INVENTED it and I have done it every Saturday for eleven years, which is how traditions are made — by ONE WOMAN, REPEATEDLY."

**Bradley**: "Is that... Jägermeister and an energy drink?"

> "It is a JÄGERBOMB, my darling, and it is BENEATH ME, and I have one every week."

**Lord Pilsner** *(with feeling)*: "Boys. She's one of us."

> "*(to Adelheid, without turning her head)* He said I am one of them."
> **Adelheid**: "*(from the sideboard)* Mm."
> "*(back to the bros, radiant)* I am ENORMOUSLY flattered."

*Adelheid brings the tray. Fortissima counts.*

> "Drei — zwei — eins — **PROST!**"

*Everybody drinks. The bros drink faster.*

**Fade to black.** No menace in the scoring, no sting, no slow-motion. The last frame before the cut is Fortissima laughing at something Chadwick has said, genuinely, with her hand on his shoulder.

### The Morning After
*Late morning. The apartment. The bros wake on and around a chaise longue, individually, badly.*

**Lord Pilsner**: "...Where."
**Chadwick**: "My face is HEAVY. Why is my face HEAVY."
**Bradley**: "You are wearing a full face of stage makeup. So am I. It is very well done."

*Fortissima is at the kitchen table in the cardigan, glasses on, finishing the crossword. She does not look up.*

> "There is coffee. There is bread. There is a bucket by the tall one and he should be grateful for it and for me."

**Chadwick**: "Ma'am, did we— was there—"

> "*(she turns the page)* Something happened. Several things happened. One of them involved my chaise and I would rather not revisit it before eleven."

**Lord Pilsner**: "Was it— I mean— was it good?"

> "*(a beat, and then she looks up over the glasses, and she is entirely kind about it)* It was MEMORABLE. For me. — Eat the bread."

*On the way out, in the hall, the four pairs of size 47 heels. Bradley looks at them for two full seconds. Bradley says nothing.*

### Post-Interaction
*The lobby, on the next visit, in front of patrons:*
> "AH. My AMERICANS. *(to the entire lobby, at full projection)* These are MY Americans. They stayed. They SURVIVED."

### If Player Declines / Alternative Path
*Refusing the invitation upstairs:*
> "No? Well. *(she is not remotely wounded)* You are the first in some while. Come back Saturday, the offer does not expire, and neither, evidently, do I."

*Declining the Nightcap specifically — the tray is offered and the party says no:*
> "Sensible. Boring. *(she drinks hers)* But sensible."
> *(The couchsurf still completes; the morning-after is a normal hangover and the makeup is on nobody. This branch exists and is fully supported.)*

### Callback Dialogue (Return Visits)
*Every subsequent Bassline Opera House visit, post-Nightcap — this is the required morning-after callback and it never stops:*
> "The Nightcap boys! *(to whoever is nearest, always)* These three drank with me. ALL THREE. I have the photographs and I have shown them to the Maestro."

**Lord Pilsner**: "Wait, photographs?"

> "Of course photographs. I am not a MONSTER, I am a DOCUMENTARIAN."

*Second callback, later in the game:*
> "You are walking better. Good. It takes some people a fortnight."

*If asked about the photographs a third time:*
> "They are in a drawer. They will remain in a drawer. *(entirely sincerely)* You were charming and you were terrible at karaoke and that is all anybody needs to know about anyone."

*Post-Fiaker Fiasco, if the party is the horse party:*
> "You are the HORSE people. *(enormous delight)* Adelheid has not spoken about anything else in a week. She is FURIOUS. It has been marvellous for her colour."

## 🗣️ Voice Direction

### Accent
Austrian-German-accented English, operatic, projected — she speaks at performance volume by default and it is not an affectation, it is thirty-one years of habit. **The speaking voice does not change between Fortissima and Kranzler.** It is the same voice at two volumes, and any implementation that pitches it up or down between states is wrong and destroys the character.

### Tone
- **On stage / lobby**: enormous, warm, projected to the gallery
- **Grading a compliment**: drops to conversational and is suddenly, briefly, completely sincere
- **The Nightcap**: joyous. This is the best part of her week and it must sound like it.
- **The morning after**: quiet, dry, entirely unhurried, and kind at the end of the line rather than the start
- **Never**: sultry, predatory, arch, camp-as-punchline, or knowing. She is not performing an in-joke at the bros' expense — she simply cannot see what there is to explain.

### Key Delivery Notes
- She never lowers her voice about the show, her name, her wig, or her shoes. There is nothing to lower it about.
