# CREATIVE NORTH STAR: EarthBound Character Design

**Status:** Canon — author ruling (2026-08-03). **Binding on every character, location, quest and item in the game.**
**Read before authoring any NPC.** This is the standard the work is measured against.

---

## 🚨 THE PRINCIPLE

> **"Our game is based off of EarthBound, the Super Nintendo game from the 90s that was really out there and wild. We are operating within that type of spectrum where it's really out there. None of their characters were just average characters — there was always something kind of cool and unusual about them. It's why even to this day EarthBound has a cult following. It was different. It was unique. It was interesting. It was fun."**
> — Author, 2026-08-03

**There are no average characters in this game.** Not one. If a character could be summarised as "a plausible person with an interesting job," it is wrong and must be reworked.

---

## What EarthBound actually did

Its cast included: a man who became a dungeon. A pile of sentient vomit that would only negotiate for fly honey. An entire village-wide cult obsessed with the colour blue. A photographer who materialises out of the sky, takes your picture, and leaves. Rival child inventors. A touring band permanently in debt. Bow-tied creatures who speak in their own font and say "BOING."

None of that is explained. Nobody in the world finds it strange.

---

## The Five Rules

1. **Mundane-modern setting, total absurdity played completely straight.** The world is recognisable; the people in it are not. Nobody in-world reacts to how strange anything is. That straight face is what makes it funny.
2. **One weird premise per character, committed to absolutely.** The premise IS the person — not a quirk bolted onto a normal person. "Backpack Girl Who Never Left" is a premise. "A traveller who's a bit of a character" is a quirk.
3. **Nothing explains itself.** No origin story, no lore justification, no wink to the audience. The rubber duck is in the fountain. Never explain the duck.
4. **Deadpan delivery.** The stranger the character, the more matter-of-fact the presentation. Escalate the premise, never the telling.
5. **Memorable on sight.** You should be able to describe them in one sentence that makes someone laugh.

---

## Names are part of the joke

Look at the existing roster: **Miss Morphine · QUEEN Riviéra Renata · Vape Witch Vanessa · Backpack Girl Who Never Left · CryptoBro69's "Ex" · Suspicious Twins (Polina & Paulina) · Lady Geheimnis · The Eternal Student · The Understudy**

Several have no ordinary name at all. They are **epithets, titles, self-appointed ranks, relationship labels, behaviours-as-names.** One is defined entirely by who she used to date. One is a rank in capitals with an accent on it.

**Invent the persona first, then check the name is unused** — never the reverse. The one-first-name-one-character registry (`design/style_guides/design_optionality_principle.md` and the name-dedup canon) must never push authoring toward bland names.

---

## The Test

> **Could this character sit in EarthBound's cast, next to Miss Morphine and Backpack Girl Who Never Left, without looking like they wandered in from a census?**

If no, rework it. Not polish it — rework the premise.

---

## Anti-patterns (all observed in this repo and rejected)

| ❌ Wrong | Why |
|---|---|
| "Nora Fitzgerald, 40s, café owner, literature degree" | A plausible person with a job. Nothing is out there about her. |
| "Tour Guide Fiona, five years' service, survived a yeast incident" | The incident is a detail, not a premise. She is still just a tour guide. |
| Repurposing incidental location staff as major characters | Staff exist to furnish a location. Major characters are built premise-first. |
| A quirk added to a normal person | The premise must be load-bearing, not decorative. |
| Explaining why a character is the way they are | Kills it instantly. |
| A sober, realistic character acting as the straight man | There is no straight man. Everyone is ridiculous. |
