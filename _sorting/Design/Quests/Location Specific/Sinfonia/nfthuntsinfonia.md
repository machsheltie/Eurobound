NFT Hunt — Sinfonia Leg
Premise: Chadwick & Bradley have decided that "brochain" NFTs hit different in a culture city. Same collection, same wallet, but the acquisitions here are, in Chadwick's professional assessment, "curated." Sinfonia's four are the fine-art tranche.

Sinfonia Targets (4 of 4 — collection total is 12, four per city):

1. Auction QR Catalog — the hush-hush auction rooms.
The printed lot catalog is handed to anyone who walks in dressed correctly. The auction house's own QR is on the back cover and goes to a bidding app. The QR on the Lot 41 page is a different QR, hand-stamped, slightly crooked, added by somebody who is not employed there. Chadwick scans the crooked one, because it is the one that looks exclusive.
NFT: "Lot 41: Untitled (Gilt Frame, Empty)" — a 340×340 JPEG of an empty picture frame photographed at a slight angle, the photographer's flash clearly visible in the varnish, saved at quality 40. The lore card describes it as "negative space as provenance."
Bro Read: Bradley: "It's a frame. So it holds whatever you project onto it. That's not a bug, boys, that's the ENTIRE ART MOVEMENT."
Reality: an assistant at the auction house minted forty of them on a lunch break and has been stamping catalogs for a month. She knows exactly what it is. She lets Chadwick photograph her holding it and does not correct one word of what he says on the recording.

2. Backstage Terminal — The Bassline Opera House.
Coin-operated CRT kiosk in the backstage labyrinth, two euro for ten minutes, sticky keyboard, orange-on-black text mode, still logged into the previous user's account. Nobody has ever logged out of this terminal. It is not clear that it can log out.
NFT: "Backstage Laminate (Unauthorized)" — a flatbed scan of a laminated backstage pass, someone's thumb visible in the bottom corner of the scan, the lamination glare wiping out the name field entirely. Minting it prints a receipt on a dot-matrix printer that nobody has reloaded since the last century.
Bro Read: Chadwick: "This is ACCESS, chat. This is documented, timestamped access. I have been backstage in a European opera house and I have the token to prove it." (He is backstage in a European opera house. He is standing next to a mop.)
Reality: the previous user minted it as a joke about being made to work a double. The kiosk takes the two euro either way.

3. Latte-Art AR Filter — Grounds For Concern, Kaffeehaus Row.
The espresso stand's tablet runs an AR filter that renders a milk-foam heart over the customer's face while the barista judges the order. The filter has a MINT button in the corner in a font nobody at the stand chose.
NFT: "Foam Face #17" — a 128px capture of a cappuccino in which the heart has been dragged into a smeared oval, with a human face very slightly visible behind it. There are at least sixty of #17.
Bro Read: Lord Pilsner: "Number seventeen. Low number. That's a founder position."
Reality: the barista sighs, delivers the latte, applies the standard visible disappointment, and takes the five euro. She has watched roughly two hundred tourists mint Foam Face #17 and has never once mentioned the sixty.

4. Aaron's Tractor Token — Aaron's Corner Table, The Black-Tie Backroom.
"She Thinks My Tractor's Sexy" (Mint #42), `item_tractor_nft`. Handed over in person, sincerely, as a gift, at the grant beat of `blacktie_event_kings_were_humming_01`. The acquisition scene is staged in full in `Design/Quests/Location Specific/Sinfonia/thekingswerehumming.md` and is not restated here. Item spec: `Design/Inventory/items/tractor_nft_item.md`.
Bro Read: Chadwick: "Bro. He's cutting us IN."
Reality: it is a scratched aluminum token. It is worth €0 at any bank, exchange, or legitimate vendor in the game.

NFTs Earned: "Lot 41: Untitled (Gilt Frame, Empty)," "Backstage Laminate (Unauthorized)," "Foam Face #17," "She Thinks My Tractor's Sexy" (Mint #42).

Satire Bite: Sinfonia's four are exactly as ugly as Debaucheryville's, but they come with wall text. Every lore card here is written in gallery voice — "negative space as provenance," "an interrogation of access," "the ephemerality of foam" — and the bros read the wall text as valuation. The city's whole trick is that expensive framing makes cheap things look considered, and the hunt runs that trick on a JPEG of an empty frame. In every acquisition the person handing it over knows precisely what it is worth, says nothing untrue, and lets the bros do all of the talking. They leave each one convinced they took something.

Improvement Layer: Influencer Syndicate members work the Opera House Plaza in evening wear, flaunting NFTs between takes. Beat one in a Sinfonia-flavored goofy mini-game ("Applause-Off," "Etiquette Duel") to swipe it — same swipe mechanic as the Debaucheryville leg, opera-house dressing. Swiped tokens are cosmetic duplicates and do not substitute for any of the four listed targets.
