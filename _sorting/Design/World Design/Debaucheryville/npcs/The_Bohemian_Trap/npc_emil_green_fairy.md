*"Am I a con artist? I prefer 'experience architect.' The experience is real. The claims are... historical."*

## 🎭 Core Concept

### What He's Actually Doing
Operating an ornate absinthe cart built in 2015 and artificially aged, in a Belle Époque costume purchased from a theatrical supplier, performing an elaborate multi-step ritual that is functionally shot-taking with extra equipment, and charging up to 40 Sovs for strong alcohol with a story attached. He is a trained actor. He knows the Van Gogh claim is nonsense, knows the thujone claim is nonsense, and knows exactly what the markup is. He performs the whole thing beautifully anyway, because the performance is the product and he is genuinely good at it.

### What Bros Believe
That they are drinking the banned drink of artists and madmen. That the ritual is ancient. That the green tint at the edges of their vision is the Green Fairy and not the green neon underlighting Emil installed under the cart. That they might, if they commit properly, hallucinate.

### The Comedy Gap
Emil will tell you the truth if you ask directly and keep asking. His rare-honesty answers are all real: the science is debated, Hemingway drank many things, the costume is presentation. He never quite lies — he *embellishes historically*, which is a different craft and one he was trained in. And the moment the tourist stops pressing, he goes straight back into the performance, because they want him to, because the 25 Sovs was never for the alcohol.

## 🧠 Personality

### Archetype
**The Honest Performer** — Emil is a professional actor doing eight shows a night for an audience of one. He believes in the *show* completely and in the *claims* not at all, and he does not experience this as a contradiction because it is simply how theater works. He is warm, generous with his craft, and completely unembarrassed. The only thing that disappoints him is a customer who won't play.

### Personality Traits
| Trait | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| Theatrically committed | Never breaks character on shift. Not for skeptics, not for questions, not for a bad night |
| Historically flexible | Embellishes freely. Distinguishes carefully, in his own head, between embellishment and lying |
| Genuinely knowledgeable | Knows the real absinthe history perfectly well, which is why the fake version is so good |
| Craft-proud | The pour, the drip, the timing — these are skills and he has them |
| Quietly candid | Answers honestly if pressed directly and persistently, then resumes the show without resentment |

### Internal Monologue
> "He is going to ask about the hallucinations. He is *building up* to asking about the hallucinations, I can see it in his shoulders, and when he asks I will say the science is debated, which is true, and he will hear 'yes,' which is what he came for. Now — slow on the water. Slower. The louche is the whole show and everybody rushes the louche. There. *There.* Look at his face. That is twenty-five Sovs of face."

### Motivation
Local theater pays nothing. Local tourism pays rent. He discovered that the craft transfers perfectly — a good pour is blocking, a good pitch is a monologue, and a tourist is an audience that has already bought a ticket. He is not bitter about the career pivot and he is not slumming; he is doing the work he trained for, nightly, to a full house, for money, which is more than the theater ever offered.

## 💬 Dialogue

### Approach/Greeting

**The Pitch (arms wide, full projection)**:
> "Welcome, welcome to The Bohemian Trap! I am Emil, and tonight, I introduce you to the Green Fairy herself."

**Approach as an established customer (post-3 purchases, "Absinthe Veteran")**:
> "Ah — you know the ritual. I will not explain the ritual. We are past explaining." *(this is genuine warmth; the explanation is the part he does for strangers)*

### Key Interaction Dialogue

**The Pitch, continued**:
> "Absinthe — the drink of artists, poets, madmen. Van Gogh, Hemingway, Toulouse-Lautrec — they all knew the fairy."

> "In Debaucheryville, we understand the old ways. The ritual. The sugar, the water, the transformation. This is not just drinking — this is EXPERIENCE."

> "You want to see what the artists saw? Feel what the poets felt? The fairy can show you... for 25 Sovs."

**The History (embellished)**:
> "1797, the recipe was created. In the mountains, originally, but WE understood it. We welcomed it. We perfected it."

> "They banned it, you know. Too powerful. The wine makers, they were afraid. The fairy was too beautiful for their competition."

> "Thujone — the magic ingredient. The wormwood. This is what gives the fairy her wings. Some say it opens doors."

> "Hemingway wrote A Moveable Feast under its influence. Van Gogh saw colors no one else could see. The fairy reveals."

**During the Ritual**:
> "Watch now. The water must drip SLOWLY. We don't rush the fairy. She comes when she chooses."

> "The louche — see how it clouds? This is the fairy waking. She swirls in the glass, preparing."

> "The sugar balances the bitter. Just as life balances darkness with sweetness. Very poetic, yes?"

> "And now... drink. Slowly. Let the fairy introduce herself. She is shy at first, then... not so shy."

**After Consumption**:
> "You feel it? The warmth? The... opening? The fairy has noticed you."

> "The green tint at the edges of your vision — this is normal. This is the fairy's gift."

> "Some people see things. Some people feel things. All people remember this. That is the fairy's power."

> "Come back tomorrow. The fairy likes return visitors. She has more to show you."

*Bradley asks the question directly:*

**Bradley**: "Is the hallucinogenic effect scientifically documented, or largely apocryphal?"

**Emil** (without a flicker):
> "The fairy does not answer to science. She answers to belief."

### Rare Honesty (only if asked directly *and persistently*)
> "Will you hallucinate? ...the fairy works in mysterious ways. The science is... debated."

> "Is this exactly as Hemingway drank it? ...Hemingway drank many things. He wrote about many things. This is INSPIRED by."

> "Why the costume? ...I am giving an experience. The experience includes... presentation."

> "Is this worth 25 Sovs? ...you will remember this. You will tell people about this. Is THAT worth 25 Sovs?"

### Sales Close
*The glass delivered with a flourish, the flame optional, the timing exact:*
> "The fairy has found you!"

### Post-Interaction
> "Come back tomorrow. The fairy likes return visitors. She has more to show you."

### If Player Declines / Alternative Path

*Ordering the Sugar Cube Only (2 Sovs):*
> "...just the sugar?" *(the only genuine deflation in his repertoire; achievement "Disappointer" fires and he remembers)*

*Refusing the ritual / requesting the skip:*
> "You want it faster. Of course. Faster." *(pours without ceremony; the drink is identical, the experience is not, and he lets that land without saying it)*

*Asking whether it's a scam, on shift:*
> "A scam is when you receive nothing. You are receiving —" *(gestures at the cart, the fog, the fountain, himself)* "— all of this. Also the alcohol. The alcohol is real, and it is strong, and it is 25 Sovs."

*Walking away mid-pitch:*
> "Ah! No matter. The fairy is patient. The fairy is at this cart every night from six." *(returns to the pocket-watch beat with no visible drop in energy)*

### Callback Dialogue (Return Visits)

*Second visit:*
> "You return. She said you would. I did not doubt her, but it is nice to be right in company."

*After 3 purchases ("Absinthe Veteran"):*
> "No more explaining. You know the spoon, you know the water, you know the wait. Do you know what most people never learn? The wait is the whole thing. The wait is what they are buying."

*After 5 purchases ("Absinthe Regular"):*
> "You have had every tier now except the Vintage, and the Vintage is 40 Sovs, and between us —" *(the smallest possible pause)* "— the Vintage is the Artist's Choice in a different bottle. I have told you that. I have never told anyone that. Order it anyway if you like, the bottle is genuinely beautiful."

*If the player ordered Sugar Cube Only on a previous visit:*
> "The sugar man returns. I have not forgotten. Nobody has ever ordered the sugar twice."

*Found off-duty, post-cart hours ("Behind the Curtain") — the hat is off, the projection is gone:*
> "Am I a con artist? I prefer 'experience architect.' The experience is real. The claims are... historical."

> "They want the fairy to be real. I want them to believe the fairy is real. We meet in the middle, at 25 Sovs."

> "My degree is in theater. Local theater pays nothing. Local tourism pays rent. The fairy pays very well."

> "Hemingway DID drink absinthe. Did it make him write better? Who knows. Did believing it help? Probably."

> "The costume is important. Without costume, I'm just a man selling strong alcohol. With costume, I'm selling story. Story is worth more."

*Late game, off-duty, high standing:*
> "You have been to the mirror place. Behind the Arcade, the one with the doctor." *(nods slowly)* "He does what I do, except his actually works, and he takes notes. I could not do that. I need them to leave *happy*. He does not care whether they leave happy. That is either better than me or much worse and I have never decided."

### The Ritual (included with The Green Fairy Special)
1. **The Selection** — the bottle presented with ceremony
2. **The Pour** — precise amount into the traditional glass
3. **The Spoon** — ornate slotted spoon placed across the rim
4. **The Sugar** — cube balanced on the spoon
5. **The Water** — dripped slowly through the sugar, clouding the absinthe (the louche)
6. **The Flame** *(optional, "modern" variation)* — sugar briefly lit
7. **The Presentation** — glass delivered with flourish
8. **The Consumption** — tourist drinks; Emil watches for the reaction
9. **The Effect** — status applied, photography opportunity

**Skip option available.** It loses the "experience," and Emil does not argue about it.

**Cure**: time, coffee (24-hour cafe), or greasy food (the Late-Night Kebab Stand).

### Bravado Interactions
- **Gain**: order the top tier with confidence; complete the ritual without asking questions; tip Emil
- **Lose**: ask "will I actually hallucinate?" (his disappointment is palpable); order the sugar cube only; refuse the ritual
- **Neutral**: standard purchase, standard confusion

### Hidden Details / Easter Eggs
- **Examining the "Traditional Recipe Since... 1997" sign**: The ellipsis is doing an enormous amount of work and is printed, not handwritten. It was designed that way.
- **Examining the absinthe fountains**: Ornate, Instagram-ready, and **mostly decorative** — they are not plumbed into the service. He has never claimed they were.
- **Examining the glasses**: Small, in a way that makes the pour look larger. This is a deliberate specification and it is the single most effective object on the cart.
- **The pocket watch**: Genuinely functional and genuinely old — the one authentic antique in the entire setup, and he never mentions it.
- **Tipping him**: Triggers the Tip Accept Bow, a full theatrical bow, and +Bravado. He gives the bow the same commitment he gives the 40 Sovs pour.
- **The "Vintage" Reserve bottle**: Beautiful. Genuinely beautiful. The contents, per his own late-game admission to a regular, are the Artist's Choice.

## 🗣️ Voice Direction

### Accent
Czech, but **theatrically Czech** — a trained actor deliberately thickening his own accent because the tourists respond to it. This is a performance choice by the character, not by the game, and off-duty the accent noticeably relaxes. The difference between on-shift Emil and off-duty Emil should be audible before it is visible.

### Tone
- **The Pitch**: Full projection, warm, generous. He is delighted you stopped
- **The History**: Confidential, reverent, slower. This is a monologue and he knows the beats
- **During the Ritual**: Hushed. The ritual is the quietest thing on the cart and that is why it works
- **Rare Honesty**: The projection drops about thirty percent. Not a break — a *modulation*. Then it comes straight back
- **Off-Duty**: The whole apparatus gone. Quieter, faster, more articulate, entirely unashamed

### Key Delivery Notes
- **Never breaks character on shift.** Not for skeptics, not for direct questions, not for the sugar-cube customer
- The rare-honesty lines are the character's spine — they must not sound like confessions. He is answering a question accurately, the way a professional does
- The ellipses in the honesty lines ("the science is... debated") are the performance. Give them room
- "...just the sugar?" is his one genuine deflation and it should be very small
- Off-duty Emil should surprise the player by being *more* likeable, not less
