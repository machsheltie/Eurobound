# NPC Profile: Zdenka

*"Fifty euro for the shower. Forty if you go now. It goes up the longer you stand there."*

## 🎭 Core Concept

### What They're Actually Doing
Zdenka runs a shower off a folding cart: a hose rigged to a repurposed water tank, a hand-lettered price sign, and a sun-bleached apron. She fills the tank herself before dawn from a municipal standpipe up the embankment, hauls it, and sets up between the lifeguard tower and the water, which is the exact stretch of sand that everyone who has just come out of The Bacchanus has to walk across. The service is real. The water is clean, the pressure is adequate, and it cures `port_o_cologne` outright — she is not selling a placebo and has never needed to. Fifty euro is the price. Forty if you go now. The number goes up the longer a customer stands at the cart deciding, and it does not come back down. She has never raised her voice at anyone, never chased a sale down the beach, and never once acknowledged the flies.

### What Bros Believe
The bros believe they are in a negotiation. The price moving is, to them, unmistakable evidence of a haggle in progress — a lever, an opening, a thing that can be worked. Chadwick's read is that walking away is leverage; he walks away twice and comes back to two higher numbers and reports both times that he is "getting there." Lord Pilsner believes that the "forty if you go now" is a limited-time offer he was smart enough to spot and is holding in reserve, strategically, for later. Bradley pays fifty at the top of the conversation, gets his shower, and stands to one side for the rest of it. Nobody asks him about it.

### The Comedy Gap
The sign is hand-lettered and the numbers are on separate cards that hook over one another. The higher cards were made at the same time as the low ones, in the same pen, years ago, and they hang on the same nail. Zdenka is not improvising a haggle; she is working a posted, published, entirely honest price ladder that was printed in advance because it has always gone this way. Everything the bros think they are doing to her price, her price was built to do to them. She says four numbers over the course of an afternoon and never a fifth word of persuasion.

## 🧠 Personality

### Archetype
**The Posted Price** — a brisk, entirely unsentimental operator running a real service at a published rate, who has correctly identified the single most valuable ten square meters of sand on the Bohemian Riviera and stood on it. She is not a scammer and does not need to be: what she sells works, and the people who need it have already been launched into a river.

### Personality Traits
| Trait | Description |
|-------|--------------|
| Brisk | The transaction takes as long as the transaction takes; there is no conversation attached to it and none is withheld unkindly |
| Transactional | Every exchange with her resolves to a number; she has never once tried to be liked and has never once been disliked |
| Unsentimental | Entirely unmoved by the state of anyone standing in front of her — soaked, humiliated, freshly televised, it is all the same fifty euro |
| Escalating By Design | The ladder is posted, the cards are pre-made, and the number moves in one direction only, on schedule, without commentary |
| Never Sells | States the price, once, and then waits; the pitch is the position of the cart, not anything she says |

### Internal Monologue
> "Forty. — He'll go to the water and try to rinse. They all go to the water and try to rinse. — Fifty when he comes back. Sixty when he tells me why it should be forty."

### Motivation
She owns the best pitch on the beach and she works it properly. The tank gets filled before dawn because the tank being full at 6 a.m. is the entire business. She has no interest in the show, no opinion on the contestants, and no stake in who wins; she has a stake in the two hundred meters of sand between the water and the bar strip, and that stretch produces customers on a schedule she could recite. The escalation is not spite and is not cleverness — it is the price of standing in her ten square meters, and she has priced it accurately.

## 💬 Dialogue

### Approach/Greeting
**Approached at the cart by any party member carrying `port_o_cologne` — and by anyone else, identically**:
> "Fifty euro for the shower. Forty if you go now. It goes up the longer you stand there."

**Idle, with no customer at the cart**:
*She says nothing. She is watching the water. This is a deliberate absence of a bark and must not be filled in a later pass.*

### Key Interaction Dialogue
*The escalation, played out. Every line below is a complete turn — she does not add to any of them.*

**Chadwick** *(spreading his hands, warming up)*: "Okay. Okay. Here's the thing. Here's the thing about *value*, right—"

**Zdenka** *(two fingers, one card, without looking at the nail)*:
> "Sixty."

**Chadwick**: "—no, no, I'm *saying*, I'm building to something—"

**Zdenka**:
> "Seventy."

*He walks away to consult. She goes back to watching the water. The card stays where it is.*

### If the Player Tries To Rinse In the River
*They will. The status refreshes on contact and the flies double. She has watched this happen for eleven seasons and does not move, comment, or change expression.*

**Returning, dripping, to the cart:**
> "The river is where you got it. — Eighty."

### Sales Close
*Payment made. She uncoils the hose in one motion and does the job properly: full pressure, adequate time, no hurry, no chat. The fly swarm dissipates on completion.*

> "Stand on the concrete after. Not the sand. The sand is worse."

### Post-Interaction
*Hose recoiled, coin in the tin, back to the water.*
> "That'll hold. Don't go in the river."

### If Player Declines / Alternative Path
*They can leave with `port_o_cologne` active and cure it later at a hostel shower or a spa. She is entirely indifferent to this and the card does not come down.*
> "Then it's eighty when you're back."

### Callback Dialogue (Return Visits)
*Any later visit to Fauxst Beach. The card on the nail is whatever the party left it at.*
> "Back. — You know the number."

### Hidden Details / Easter Eggs
- **The cards.** Examining the hand-lettered sign shows the numbers are separate cards hooked over one another on a single nail — and that the high cards are lettered in the same pen, at the same age, as the low ones. Nothing anywhere says why.

## 🗣️ Voice Direction

### Accent
Local Central European English, brisk and direct, pitched for a working beach rather than a conversation. She is heard over hose noise and crowd noise without ever competing with either.

### Tone
- **The opening ladder line**: one breath, three clauses, no pause between them; this is a posted price being read out, not an offer being made
- **Each escalation**: a single word at exactly the same volume and warmth as the last; no rise, no relish, no acknowledgment that anything has happened
- **The post-rinse line**: the only line with any weight behind it, and even then only the weight of a fact
- **During the cure**: nothing. She does not talk through the shower. The hose is the sound

### Key Delivery Notes
- The canon-locked greeting ships close to verbatim ("Fifty euro for the shower. Forty if you go now. It goes up the longer you stand there.") and must never be delivered as a joke, a tease, or a haggle-opener
- The single-word escalations are the character; they must be flat, identical to one another, and never landed as punchlines
- She never persuades. If a read makes any line sound like it is trying to close a sale, the character is broken
- Voice acting priority: lock the escalation words first — they carry the whole performance and every other line is calibrated against how little they contain
