# NPC Profile: Standa

*"The sign says forty. I'd call it closer to sixty, if you're asking me directly, which you are."*

## 🎭 Core Concept

### What They're Actually Doing
Standa is a fully qualified, fully equipped lifeguard staffing a genuine, structurally sound tower over water that is somewhere between forty and sixty centimeters deep. He has the whistle, the rescue buoy, the red trunks, and the training, and the job has exactly two duties: watch the water, and intervene. He does the first one honestly, all day, every day, and the second one has never once been required of him in the way it was written. He has blown the whistle twice. Both times, catapult. He also has a paperback — sixth in a series he started at the fourth, spine broken, cover gone soft — and he is roughly forty pages from the end of it and has been for a very long time, because a launch resets his place in the paragraph and he starts the paragraph again. If you ask him anything about the water, he will tell you the truth about it immediately and without decoration, including the part that contradicts the sign zip-tied to his own ladder, and he will then go back to the paragraph.

### What Bros Believe
The bros do not read a bored professional. They read an official. A lifeguard is, to them, a referee, a scout, and a witness in one chair, and the whistle is the confirming instrument. Chadwick is certain the whistle blast on his launch was a *call* — a foul, a ten-count, an entry into a record somewhere. Lord Pilsner concludes that the man in the tower has "eyes on" him specifically, and adjusts his posture on the sand accordingly, for hours. Bradley asks him what the water is actually like, gets a completely straight answer, hears the number sixty, and understands it to mean sixty *feet*, at which point the entire beach becomes considerably more impressive than it was a moment ago and nobody revisits it.

### The Comedy Gap
Standa's whole character is that he will tell you, plainly and immediately, and it will not matter. The sign on his ladder says forty. He says sixty, out loud, to anyone who asks, while sitting directly beneath the sign. Nobody involved considers this a problem worth resolving, least of all him, and the correction never travels more than one meter from his mouth. He is attentive, honest, qualified, and entirely at peace with being the most accurate information source on a beach where accuracy has never once altered anyone's plans.

## 🧠 Personality

### Archetype
**Attentive, Unstimulated, Honest** — a qualified professional performing a real job correctly in conditions that will never require the qualification, who has arrived at total equanimity about this and reads a paperback in it. He is not lazy, not cynical, and not secretly waiting for his moment. His moment would be a man standing up in sixty centimeters of water, and he would handle it, and it would take eleven seconds.

### Personality Traits
| Trait | Description |
|-------|--------------|
| Professionally Attentive | Genuinely scans on interval, all day, in water that has never produced an incident; the duty is real to him and is performed |
| Personally Bored | Forty pages from the end of the sixth book in a series he started at the fourth, and losing his place in the same paragraph every time something enters the river |
| Flatly Honest On Request | Will contradict the sign zip-tied to his own ladder, out loud, instantly, to whoever asks, and will not raise it if nobody does |
| Dry, Direct, Unhurried | Says the exact number of words the question needed and then stops, without any of it reading as unfriendly |
| Keeps A Count | Two whistles, both catapult; the number is his, it is accurate, and it goes up when a unit flies |

### Internal Monologue
> "Sixty. It's been sixty since the spring. Someone measured it once with a pole and then wrote forty on a sign, which is a different activity from measuring. — Page three hundred and forty. He's about to find out it was the brother. It's been the brother since chapter four."

### Motivation
He is paid to watch the water, and the water gets watched. That is genuinely the whole of it, and he is at peace with it in a way the bros are constitutionally unable to be about anything. The paperback is the day's actual event. The count of whistle-blows is the only part of the job that has ever changed, and it changes about twice a season, and he keeps it accurately for no reason other than that it is accurate.

## 💬 Dialogue

### Approach/Greeting
**Approached at the tower, or by examining the "NO DIVING (SERIOUSLY, IT'S 40cm)" sign zip-tied to his ladder and asking him directly**:
> "The sign says forty. I'd call it closer to sixty, if you're asking me directly, which you are."

**Idle, from the platform, book closed around one finger**:
> "I've blown this whistle twice. Both times, catapult."

### Key Interaction Dialogue
*From the platform. He turns his head to answer and nothing else moves.*

**Lord Pilsner**: "So if a guy went down out there — hypothetically, a strong swimmer, big guy, real dense muscle — you're going in after him?"

**Standa** *(without inflection)*:
> "If someone goes under in sixty centimeters, that isn't a rescue. That's a conversation."

**Bradley** *(looking at the buoy)*: "You ever actually use that?"

**Standa**:
> "It's regulation. Regulation doesn't say the water has to be deep."

### The Whistle (Catapult Launch, Witnessed From the Tower)
*Any launch visible from the platform — a 3-strike LOSS, either staggered non-selected-bro launch, a Sudden Death loss, or the disqualification. The buoy does not move. He does not stand.*

*One short blast. Then, flatly, already looking back down at the page:*
> "Three."

*The number is his whistle count, and it is accurate, and it goes up every time. It is never explained to anyone, and nobody on the beach has ever asked what it refers to.*

### If the Player Goes Into the Water
> "You can go in. You'll be standing. That's what going in is, here."

### Post-Interaction
*The conversation ends when the answer does. He finds his place, loses it, and starts the paragraph again.*
> "Mm."

### If Player Declines / Alternative Path
*Player walks off mid-approach. He does not watch them go; he watches the water, which is the job.*
> "Sure."

### Callback Dialogue (Return Visits)
*Later visit, same platform, same book, marginally further into it:*
> "Four now. — Same reason."

## 🗣️ Voice Direction

### Accent
Local Central European English, dry and direct, spoken from above without being raised. He is talking down a ladder, not talking down to anyone, and the mix should carry the elevation rather than the attitude.

### Tone
- **Answering a direct question**: immediate, exact, finished; the honest answer arrives with zero build-up and zero aftermath
- **The whistle number**: entirely flat, already half-turned back to the book, the same energy every time it increments
- **About the buoy, the water, the sign**: mildly informative, never wry — he is not making a joke about his own job and must never sound like he is
- **During the night rave**: identical to daylight; the party does not affect his read in any way

### Key Delivery Notes
- The forty/sixty line ships close to verbatim and must land as a helpful correction offered to someone who asked, not as a wink at the audience
- "…which you are" is the driest beat in the line and should be delivered as a plain observation, not a jab
- The whistle number is never emphasized, never explained, and never rises in pitch as it climbs across the game
- Voice acting priority: lock the flat, unraised, slightly-elevated baseline first — everything else is that baseline with a different fact in it

## 📝 Development Notes

- **The 40cm vs. 60cm discrepancy is an intentional bit, not an error to reconcile.** The sign and Standa's own admission must stay in conflict, permanently, in every file that touches either. Any future pass that "fixes" the sign or the line destroys the character.
- **The correction is pull-only.** He never announces the depth. It exists solely as a response to `ask_standa_directly` or to the player speaking to him. Do not promote it to an ambient bark.
- **The whistle count is live** and increments per witnessed launch across all branches and all visits. Small state read; the bark line is a number plus nothing.
- **`page_restart` always follows `whistle_blow`.** These are a fixed pair — the launch costs him the paragraph, every time, and the paragraph is never regained on screen.
- **He never leaves the platform.** No ground-level sprite, no night-rave variant, no descent animation. The sandals stay at the bottom of a ladder he does not come down.
- **Sprite budget:** `standa_lifeguard.png` (256×192, poses `tower_idle_reading` / `whistle_blow` / `tower_scan`) plus the two 64×64 composites on `lifeguard_tower.png` (`standa_perched_reading`, `standa_whistle_blow_overlay`). The tower's interactive foreground object is deliberately separate from the background tower tile — parallax layering, not a duplicate asset.
- **He is qualified and he is good.** The satire is the water, never the guard. Do not write him as a slacker, a drunk, or a man asleep in a chair in a later pass.
