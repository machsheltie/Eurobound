# NPC Profile: Token Goblin

*"Today I pay you in trust; tomorrow we settle in interest."*

## 📋 NPC Identity
- **NPC ID**: `debaucheryville_npc_token_goblin_01`
- **Display Name**: Token Goblin
- **Internal Name**: Token Goblin — Shadow Exchange specialty-currency dealer. A trade name, worn by choice, origin unrecorded
- **Role**: Token Vendor / Specialty Currency Dealer / Queue-Skip Chip Supplier
- **Associated Quest**: None owned. His queue-skip chips are the alternative access route to The Tap Dat Vault's midnight-only entry
- **Primary Location**: Shadow Exchange, Debaucheryville — the corner stall, past the UV detection station
- **Secondary Appearances**: None. The chips travel; he does not

---

## 🎭 Core Concept

### What He's Actually Doing
Working the corner of the vault where things that are *almost* money get traded — arcade tokens, metro transit cards, casino chips, queue-skip chips, and whatever else has a face value nobody will honor at a bank. He is the specialist in the margin between "worth something" and "worth something *here*," and he has an exact price for every item in that margin, arrived at by a method he will happily describe and which nobody has ever followed to the end.

### What Bros Believe
That the corner is where the *real* deals are — past the official counter, past the light, in the dark bit with the little man and the trays. That the tokens are collectibles. That the queue-skip chip is a VIP pass. That he is offering them credit out of goodwill.

### The Comedy Gap
He does offer them credit. He also tells them, in the same breath and in plain language, that tomorrow it settles in interest, and they hear the first clause as generosity and the second as flavor text. Token Goblin operates the most transparent lending arrangement in Debaucheryville — the terms are one sentence long and stated before every transaction — and he has never once had a customer read the second half.

## 👤 Appearance

### Physical Description
| Attribute | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| Age | Genuinely difficult to place; anywhere in a thirty-year band |
| Build | Small, hunched over the trays, quick-handed. Sorts faster than the eye follows |
| Height | Short |
| Energy | Quick, chattery, mercantile |

### Clothing
- **Layered coats**: Two, possibly three, worn simultaneously in a cellar of stable temperature. The outermost has pockets that have been added, not manufactured.
- **Apron of trays**: Not literally worn, but the compartmented tray rack sits so close across his stall that it reads as part of his outfit.
- **Accessories**: A jeweler's loupe used exclusively on tokens; a coin scale accurate to a tenth of a gram; a tally book in a script nobody else can read
- **Footwear**: On a wooden riser behind the stall so he can see over the trays. Everyone assumes he is taller than he is.

### Distinguishing Features
- Sorts tokens by touch alone, without looking, while holding a conversation
- His hands are always in a tray — mid-sentence, mid-negotiation, mid-anything
- The tally book comes out for every deal, and what he writes in it is not numbers
- Grins during negotiations in a way that is entirely friendly and entirely unreadable

### Sprite Notes
Read must be *specialist*, not *creature* — "Goblin" is a trade name he has embraced, not a species, and the art must not literalize it. No green skin, no ears, no fangs. What sells the nickname is the hunch, the speed of the hands, the hoard of sorted trays, and the corner he never leaves. ## 🧠 Personality

### Archetype
**The Honest Usurer** — Token Goblin lends, trades, and extends credit on terms he states out loud, once, cheerfully, at the start of every deal. He is not predatory in any sense he would recognize; he has simply discovered that people will accept any terms if the terms are delivered warmly and quickly, and he delivers them warmly and quickly. He genuinely likes his inventory. He can talk for an hour about a metro card.

### Personality Traits
| Trait | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| Chattery | Fills the whole transaction with talk. Unlike Vance's patter, his is *informative* |
| Terms-first | States the arrangement before the deal, every time, in one sentence, and never repeats it |
| Genuinely expert | Knows the real market for every near-currency object in four cities |
| Tactile | Sorts by touch. Has never been observed looking at a tray he is working in |
| Corner-bound | Has a stall, not a floor. Does not stray and does not need to |

### Internal Monologue
> "He wants the queue-skip chip and he wants it tonight and he has not got it tonight, which is fine, that is what the arrangement is for. Say the sentence. Say it exactly. Trust today, interest tomorrow — there, said, witnessed, done. He is nodding at the first half. They always nod at the first half. Write it in the book. He will be back Thursday and he will be surprised on Thursday and I will have said the sentence."

### Motivation
He collects. Not for value — for *completeness*. Somewhere behind the trays there is a run of arcade tokens from venues that no longer exist and a metro card series nobody else in the city has finished, and the lending business funds the collecting business, and the collecting business is the point. He would trade at a loss for the right token and has.

## 💬 Dialogue

### Approach/Greeting

**First approach at the corner stall**:
> "Ah — you've come past the light. Good. Everything past the light is *interesting*. Tokens, cards, chips, anything with a number on it that a bank would laugh at. What have you got and what do you want?"

**Approach without money**:
> "No cash. That is not a problem, that has never been a problem down here. Today I pay you in trust; tomorrow we settle in interest. Now — what do you want?"

### Key Interaction Dialogue

*A bro wants a queue-skip chip for The Tap Dat Vault. It is 23:40. The vault door opens at midnight and he is not going to make the queue.*

**Lord Pilsner**: "I need that chip. Tonight. Right now."

**Token Goblin** (hands still sorting, not looking down):
> "Midnight chip. Yes. Of course you do — it's twenty to. Everyone wants it at twenty to; nobody wants it at nine." *(grin)* "Today I pay you in trust; tomorrow we settle in interest."

**Lord Pilsner**: "Yeah, yeah, trust, whatever — give me the chip."

**Token Goblin** *(writing in the tally book)*:
> "There it is. Go. Door's a walk from here and the glyphs don't wait."

*If the player asks what the interest is:*
> "You are the first person this week to ask that." *(genuinely pleased)* "It goes up. That is the answer. It does not go up quickly, and it does not stop."

*If the player asks about arcade tokens:*
> "These? These are from the Arcade, and these three are from a place that closed in the nineties, and *this* one — this one nobody can tell me about, I have asked nine people. It is worth nothing. I will not sell it."

*If the player asks about transit cards:*
> "Day passes, week passes, one that says 'employee' which I am not asking about. They all work. That is the thing about a transit card — nobody has ever looked closely at a transit card in the history of this city."

*If the player asks whether the chips are legitimate:*
> "The chip is real. The chip is *absolutely* real. Whether the venue honors a real chip is between you, the venue, and a door. Take it two arches back if you want it under the light — he will tell you it is genuine, and he will be right, and it will not help you at the door."

*If the player asks about his name:*
> "It is a name people gave me and I kept, because a name people give you is free and a name you choose costs a lifetime of correcting them."

*If the player asks about Mrs. Rata:*
> "She converts. I do not convert. She would not touch half of what is on this table and I would not touch a rate board with a stick. It works. It has worked for a very long time and nobody wrote it down."

### Sales Close
> "Sorted, counted, yours. Do not lose it — I do not do replacements, I do *second sales*, and the second sale is not the same price as the first."

### Post-Interaction
> "Come back with anything strange. I mean it — anything. If a number is printed on it and someone somewhere honors it, I want to see it."

### If Player Declines / Alternative Path

*Declining the credit arrangement:*
> "Paying cash! Ha. Good. Rare. Cheaper too, though nobody ever asks whether it's cheaper before they say yes to the other thing."

*Trying to settle a debt late:*
> "Later than Thursday. Yes. That is what the interest is *for*, that is the entire mechanism, I said the sentence and you were standing right there." *(no anger whatsoever)* "It is fine. It is more now. Pay it or do not, it will keep being more."

*Offering him an item he has no market for:*
> "No. Nothing honors that anywhere. It is not a currency, it is a souvenir, and souvenirs are two arches back and one flight up."

*Trying to buy the token he will not sell:*
> "Not for sale. Not for that, not for triple that. If you find out what it is I will pay *you*."

### Callback Dialogue (Return Visits)

*Second visit:*
> "Back, and past the light again. You are getting comfortable down here. That is either good or expensive."

*With an outstanding debt:*
> "You owe. It is not urgent. It is never urgent — that is the design. It is *more*, though. It is always more."

*After the player buys a second queue-skip chip:*
> "Twice now. You are learning the door, or you are learning that you never make the queue. Both are learning."

*If the player brings him a genuinely rare token:*
> *(the chatter stops completely — the only time)* "...where. Where did you get that. No, do not — do not tell me the price you want, tell me *where*."

*Late game, debts settled:*
> "Paid in full. Do you know how many entries in this book are marked paid in full? Four. Including yours. I am going to write the date next to it, which I do not normally do."

## 🎒 Inventory / Services

### Items Sold / Services Offered
| Item/Service | Price | Actual Value | Effect/Result |
|--------------|-------|--------------|---------------|
| **Arcade tokens** | Varies by venue and rarity | Real, at the venues that still exist | Specialty currency for Absinthe Arcade and related venues. Several series are from venues that have closed and are worth exactly nothing, which he will tell you |
| **Transit cards** (day / week / "employee") | Varies | Genuinely functional | City traversal. He is candid that nobody has ever inspected a transit card closely in Debaucheryville |
| **Queue-skip chips** | Varies | High, at the right hour | **Midnight-only venue access for The Tap Dat Vault**, per `shadowexchange.md` Cross-Location Dependencies. The alternative to standing in the queue for a door that opens at exactly 00:00 |
| **Casino chips** | Varies | Depends entirely on the house | Specialty currency; he grades them by house and by decade |
| **Credit arrangement** | "Today I pay you in trust; tomorrow we settle in interest" | The terms are stated in full, once, before every deal | Deferred payment. Interest accrues, does not accelerate, and does not stop. He answers honestly if asked and almost nobody asks |
| **Specialty negotiation** | Item-dependent | He knows the four-city market | Non-traditional currency deals; feeds the Exchange's barter window alongside the UV Man's authentication |

### Hidden Details / Easter Eggs
- **The token he will not sell**: Present in every visit, in the tray nearest his left hand, unidentified. He has asked nine people. If the player ever identifies it, he offers to pay *them*.
- **Examining the tally book**: The script is not numbers and is not Czech. Asking about it: *"It is mine. It is the only thing here that is only mine."*
- **Examining the coin scale**: Accurate to a tenth of a gram, and calibrated — there is a certified calibration weight beside it in a felt-lined box, which is the single most legitimate object in the Shadow Exchange.
- **Four entries marked paid in full**: The tally book's running count. It is four for the entire game unless the player becomes the fifth.
- **The wooden riser**: Visible if the player examines behind the stall. Nobody in the Exchange has ever mentioned it and he has never hidden it.

### Professional/Social Network
| NPC | Relationship |

| Mrs. Rata | Adjacent trade, absolute boundary. She converts currency; he handles everything that is *nearly* currency. Neither has ever crossed and neither wrote it down |

| Rate Sharks | They work the floor; he works a corner. He regards their ledgers as impressive and their inventory as boring |
| Expat Flippers | Direct competition in travel cards and concert wristbands. He is better at it and does not say so |

### Territory / Social Dynamics
- The three-stall division — Rata converts, the UV Man authenticates, Token Goblin handles the near-currency margins — is the Exchange's constitution and has never been formalized or disputed
- His corner is the deepest point in the vault, so reaching him is a small act of commitment; this is why the credit business works there and would not work at the stairs
- He is the Exchange's only lender. The Rate Sharks trade; he *extends*. Nobody else has attempted it in his corner
- His transparency about terms is total and functions exactly like Mrs. Rata's rate policy and Mr. Chadz's health disclosures — the Debaucheryville pattern, stated aloud, ignored universally

## 🎯 Quest Integration

### Primary Quest (If Applicable)
None owned. Token Goblin is a **specialty-currency distribution node** — a systems NPC whose inventory changes how the player accesses content elsewhere.

### Quest Gate Function (If Applicable)
**Yes — access alternative.** Per `shadowexchange.md` Cross-Location Dependencies, his **queue-skip chips provide The Tap Dat Vault's midnight-only venue access**. This makes him the alternative route past a door that opens at exactly 00:00 and is gatekept by the Glitching Bouncer. He gates nothing himself; he *un-gates* something, for a price, on credit, at terms.

His **credit arrangement** functions as a soft economic gate in the opposite direction: a player who takes deferred payment accrues an obligation that grows for the rest of the game and never accelerates, which is the least punishing and most persistent debt mechanic in Debaucheryville.

## 🗣️ Voice Direction

### Accent
Czech, quick, warm, mercantile. The fastest speaker in the Shadow Exchange and the most cheerful. His English is fluent and conversational rather than institutional — he learned it across a table, not in a classroom.

### Tone
- **Standard trade**: Rapid, warm, informative. He is enjoying this
- **The terms sentence**: Delivered at exactly the same speed and warmth as everything else, which is why nobody registers it. **Do not slow down for it.** Slowing down would be a wink
- **Late settlement**: Genuinely unbothered. No menace, no disappointment. "It is more now" is a weather report
- **The rare-token silence**: Total stop. The absence of his voice is the performance

### Key Delivery Notes
- His chatter must be *substantive* — Vance's patter is selling, Token Goblin's is teaching. Every aside contains a real fact about a real object
- The credit sentence is the single most important line in the profile and it must be thrown away. Every time. Identically
- Never let him sound predatory. The comedy dies instantly if he sounds like he is trapping anyone
- "Where did you get that. No — tell me *where*" is his one moment of naked want and should be the most human he ever sounds
