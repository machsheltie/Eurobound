## 🎭 Core Concept

### What He's Actually Doing
Selling herbal tonics and mild stimulants at 5–50 Sovs a bottle from a wooden cart with brass fittings, wrapped in fifteen years of professional stage training. The ingredients are real — rhodiola, ginseng, adaptogens, and a working quantity of alcohol. The effects are real, and in-game they are stat buffs with durations and, at the top tier, crash penalties. The *claims* are not real. "Guaranteed boldness" is not a thing anyone can guarantee, and the small print on the cart says "Entertainment purposes. Individual results vary. No refunds." Cormac has positioned himself in the exact comfortable middle of that gap and has been standing there for twelve years.

### What Bros Believe
That they found a guy who sells actual confidence, and that this is a European thing, like absinthe. That 15 Sovs is very reasonable for boldness. That the reason the last bottle worked is the bottle, and the reason the bottle before it didn't work is that they needed a stronger bottle. Lord Pilsner will drink a Fearless Fighter and immediately begin looking for a bear.

### The Comedy Gap
The joke does not run in the direction it looks like it runs. Cormac's products *work* — partly through ingredients, mostly through the customer deciding to be brave and needing permission first. So when a bro drinks Liquid Boldness and then successfully talks his way past a bouncer, was that the potion? Cormac would say there is no difference. He would also say that'll be 12 Sovs. The bros can never get to the bottom of it because there is no bottom; the gap between "the potion worked" and "you worked" is exactly the width of a 12 Sovs bottle, and Cormac charges rent on it.

### The Honest Con
Cormac tells the truth constantly, at volume, in a carnival barker's voice, and nobody hears it because it is being shouted by a man with a magnificent mustache standing next to a cart. He says the herbs are real. He says to look them up. He says the potion is just the spark. He says *warning: crash after* on The Transformation, out loud, before taking the money. Everything he is accused of concealing is painted on the side of the cart in gold lettering.

| Height | Tall, and taller for standing on the cart's step when the crowd builds |

### Clothing
- **The theatrical coat**: An actor's wardrobe piece he kept, long, deep-cuffed, patched at both elbows with visible pride. It is fifteen years old and has been on three stages and one cart.
- **Waistcoat**: Brocade, catching the lantern light deliberately. He knows exactly which angle the lanterns hit.
- **The mustache**: Magnificent. Waxed. Non-negotiable. Its own animation layer. Cormac considers it a business asset and is not wrong.
- **Accessories**: A pocket watch he checks theatrically and never reads; a coin flourish for accepting payment; a "secret recipe" box on the work station that is a locked prop containing an older, emptier prop.
- **Footwear**: Good boots, well kept. He is on his feet ten hours a day on Cobblestone Market Street and has bought from Finbar O'Sullivan twice, which he does not advertise because it undercuts the pitch about belief.

### Distinguishing Features
- Reads your Bravado off your posture within two seconds of eye contact and adjusts the entire pitch accordingly.
- Points. The extended finger — "You! Yes, YOU!" — is his opening move and it lands on a specific person every time, never the crowd generally.
- Pours from a height. Always. The stream catching the lantern light is the demonstration.
- Gestures wide on the pitch and narrow on the close. He physically shrinks the space to seal a deal.
- In Honest Mode, the coat comes open, an elbow goes on the cart, and the voice drops a full register. It is a completely different man and it is not a trick.

### Sprite Notes
Eight states on the sheet (The Pitch, The Point, Mixing, Presenting Bottle, The Close, Haggle React, Honest Mode, Calling Back). The single most important contrast in the file is **The Pitch vs. Honest Mode** — arms-wide carnival projection against a leaning, tired, unarmoured man with his elbow on his own cart. They should read as different silhouettes at thumbnail size, because the game's emotional payload for this NPC is the distance between them. The mustache is its own layer for micro-animation. Haggle React (clutched chest, mock offence) is comedy and should be broad; Honest Mode is the opposite and should be still.

## 🧠 Personality

### Archetype
**The Sincere Showman** — Cormac spent fifteen years being paid to convincingly be someone braver than himself, then noticed the audience wanted to buy the trick rather than watch it. He is a salesman who genuinely wants his customers to succeed, partly because success is repeat business and partly because he has watched shy people become speakers and it moved him. He is running an operation that is legally entertainment and functionally therapy and priced like retail, and he will discuss all three framings with you if you have the money to be a Valued Customer.

### Personality Traits
| Trait | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| Theatrical by trade | Fifteen years of stage craft repurposed; the pitch is a performance and he is good at it |
| Radically transparent | Puts "con man" in his own trading name and the crash warning in the product description |
| Diagnostic | Reads Bravado on sight and recommends accurately — the recommendation is always the right product for the stated need |
| Genuinely invested | Wants success stories. Asks for them. Remembers them |
| Commercially unsentimental | The genuine investment stops precisely at the point where it costs him a sale, and he knows exactly where that point is |

### Internal Monologue
> "Low Bravado. Shoulders in, eyes on the cobbles, hovering at eleven feet like he's deciding. He's not deciding, he decided before he crossed the street, he's just waiting for permission and he wants it loud. Right. Point at him. Name the feeling before he has to. Fifteen Sovs, Voice of Authority, and he'll walk into whatever he's afraid of standing an inch taller — and the inch is his, not mine, but he'll come back and thank the bottle, and I'll let him, because if I tell him it was him he'll stop believing it was anything."

### Motivation
Twelve years ago Cormac worked out that borrowed courage is the most reliably resold commodity in a city built on nerve. He sells it because it sells. He *keeps* selling it because he has watched it work — not the herbs, the permission — and because the customers who eventually tell him they don't need it anymore are the ones he remembers, and their friends come. Underneath the coat is a man who was good at acting, could not make a living at acting, and found a stage where the audience pays per head and stands closer.

### The Haggle System — Exact Steps

*Cormac's price board is negotiable within limits. All five options below are drawn directly from `the_confidence_man.md`; the worked numbers make his stated percentages concrete.*

| Option | Mechanic | Result |
|--------|----------|--------|
| **Accept Price** | No check | List price. Cormac's Salesman Gleam expression; no comment |
| **Haggle** | Charisma check against a DC set by tier (Bronze 8 / Silver 12 / Gold 16 / Special Reserve 20) | Success: **10–20% off**, scaled by margin of success. Failure: list price, plus Haggle React and the "Do I look like a charity?" line. No penalty beyond the loss of the discount |
| **Bulk Deal** | Purchase 3 or more items in one transaction | **15% off** the whole basket, no check required. Always available, always the safest discount in the game |
| **Sob Story** | Charisma check at a fixed DC 14, modified by the player's current Bravado — **low Bravado helps here and high Bravado hurts**, because a confident man's sob story does not land | Success: up to **25% off a single item**, one item per in-game day. Failure: full price and the story is used against the player in Cormac's next pitch |
| **Walk Away** | Leave the cart with an item in the basket | Cormac calls the player back with a "first-timers only" special offer: **15% off**, one item, once per in-game day. Walking away a second time forfeits it and Cormac does not call twice |

**Stacking rules** (design decision required by the mechanic; not stated in the source file):
- Bulk Deal (15%) and Valued Customer (15%) stack **additively** to 30%.
- A successful Haggle stacks on top of those, but total discount is **capped at 35% off list**.
- Sob Story does **not** stack with Haggle in the same transaction — one social attempt per purchase.
- Walk Away's callback offer does not stack with anything; it is a flat 15% on a single item.

**Worked examples at real prices:**

| 1× "Liquid Boldness" | 12 Sovs | Successful 20% haggle | 10 Sovs |
| 1× "Absolute Confidence" | 20 Sovs | Successful 10% haggle | 18 Sovs |
| 3× "Liquid Boldness" | 36 Sovs | Bulk Deal 15% | 31 Sovs |
| 3× "Liquid Boldness" | 36 Sovs | Bulk 15% + Valued Customer 15% (30%) | 25 Sovs |

| 1× "Mild Courage" | 5 Sovs | Walk Away callback 15% | 4 Sovs |

### "Valued Customer" Status
**Unlock**: 5+ purchases.
**Benefits**: 15% permanent discount; Special Reserve available without asking; Cormac remembers your preferences and pre-recommends; the "Confidence Emergency" service (he will find you); Honest Mode dialogue unlocked and the theatrical act largely dropped.

### Effect Mechanics
- Effects apply immediately on consumption; duration counts from drinking.
- Multiple tonics stack with diminishing returns.
- **The Crash System**: premium tonics carry a reverse effect when duration ends. "The Transformation" ends → 15 minutes of −20 Bravado. "Emergency Confidence" carries a shorter, sharper equivalent. Timing is a real strategic decision.
- **Placebo Factor**: a hidden ±10% modifier. If the player has previously succeeded while buffed, +10% effectiveness. If the player is skeptical (has publicly doubted Cormac, or bought after a failed haggle), −10%. Belief builds with successful uses.

### Hidden Details / Easter Eggs
- **Examine the "secret recipe" box**: locked. Examine it three times and Cormac says, without turning around, *"There's a smaller box inside it. That one's empty too. It's a BOX, not a SAFE."*
- **Examine the demonstration samples**: visibly watered down. The examine text says so plainly. Cormac has never denied it and there is a line of small print on the cart acknowledging it.
- **Examine the testimonial cards**: nine cards. Two are in the same handwriting.
- **Examine the price board**: "negotiable, allegedly" is painted, not chalked — it has always been part of the sign.
- **"Beyond the Potion"**: succeed at something difficult with no active tonic while Cormac can see you. +30 Bravado and Cormac's Genuine Respect expression, which appears nowhere else in the game.

### Territory / Social Dynamics

## 🔄 Recurring Appearances

### Within Publandia
- **The mobile cart is the mechanic.** Cormac's position shifts by time of day and by event. A player looking for him should occasionally have to look, and the search should follow the crowd noise.
- **Outside The Blarney Trap**: evening hours, pre-pub pitch. Highest-frequency encounter.
- **Trinity of Pints quad**: before challenge nights, selling "exam confidence." "The Closer" at 25 Sovs sells out here.
- **Top of The Quiet Man's stairs**: fight nights only. Fearless Fighter, 18 Sovs. He never goes down the stairs.
- **Outside Paddy's Book and Shenanigans**: pre-gamble, and he is entirely aware of the irony and has a line about it.

### Progression States
| Stage | Trigger | Cormac's Behaviour |
|-------|---------|--------------------|
| Mark | First approach | Full pitch, full theatre, Bravado assessment, Salesman Gleam |
| Customer | First purchase | Adds after-purchase set; asks for success stories and means it |
| Believer | Succeed while buffed | Placebo Factor +10%; Cormac starts pre-recommending |

### Evolution Through Game
Cormac's arc runs the opposite direction to every other vendor in Publandia: he gets *quieter*. The pitch is loudest at first contact and diminishes with every tier of trust, until by Graduated status he is a middle-aged man leaning on a cart having a conversation. Nothing about the products changes. The volume is the entire relationship.

- **Trigger**: Reach Valued Customer status, then decline a purchase and state you don't need it

### Quest Gate Function
Cormac gates nothing and buffs everything. He is a pure optional-advantage vendor: every challenge in Publandia is winnable without him, and several are meaningfully easier with him. The one structural thing he does gate is the Placebo Factor, which quietly rewards players who buy, succeed and keep buying — and quietly punishes players who haggle badly and then buy anyway. Neither of those is ever surfaced in UI.

## 🗣️ Voice Direction

### Accent
Dublin, but *trained* Dublin — fifteen years of stage work sanded the edges and then he put some of them back on deliberately because they sell better. He can drop into a cleaner, quieter register at will, and Honest Mode is that register. Both are genuinely his. Neither is the fake one.

### Tone
- **The Pitch**: Full carnival barker. Projected, rhythmic, musical. Cormac's pitch has an actual meter and the sound design should support it.
- **The Assessment**: Warmer, more targeted, aimed at one person. He drops the projection when he points.
- **The Close**: Intimate. Quieter than the pitch by half. He shrinks the room.
- **Haggle React**: Broad comedy, chest-clutching, entirely performed and obviously so.
- **Honest Mode**: A completely different reading. Lower, slower, no meter, no projection. This is the payload.
- **Graduation dialogue**: Quietest in the file. No theatre at all.

### Key Delivery Notes
- The CAPITALISED words in the pitch are load-bearing — they are where the meter lands. Do not smooth them out.
- "Warning: crash after" must be delivered clearly and audibly, every time, before the transaction. The comedy of The Crasher depends entirely on the warning having been genuinely given.
- The line about the customers who don't need him anymore should be the warmest reading Cormac has, and should never sound wistful. He is not sad about it. He is proud.
- Cormac never signals that the pitch is a pitch. He is not performing "a salesman performing"; he is a salesman, and the theatre is his actual trade.
- No line may indicate awareness that any of this is satire. Cormac's transparency is a *sales technique*, not a wink. He puts "con man" in the name because it disarms people and it works.

## 📝 Development Notes

- **The haggle stacking rules are a new design decision made in this profile.** `the_confidence_man.md` gives the five haggle options and their percentages but does not state whether they stack or how they interact with the Valued Customer discount. The rules above (Bulk + Valued stack additively to 30%; Haggle stacks on top to a 35% cap; Sob Story does not stack with Haggle; Walk Away's callback is standalone) need sign-off. Everything else in the economy is lifted directly.
- **The Sob Story inversion is deliberate**: low Bravado *helps* the check and high Bravado *hurts* it. A visibly confident person's hard-luck story does not land, and Cormac reads Bravado for a living. This is the only social check in Publandia where a low stat is an asset.
- The Pitch / Honest Mode contrast is the whole character. Budget the sprite and VO work accordingly — these are effectively two performances of one man and the game's payoff is the gap between them.
- "Warning: crash after" must be surfaced in the purchase UI as well as in dialogue. The Crasher's entire function is undermined if the player can plausibly claim they weren't told.
- The Placebo Factor (±10%) must remain hidden. No UI indicator, no tooltip, no floating text. The `placebo_bonus_indicator.png` asset exists in the PNG spec — recommend it be used for debug builds only, or repurposed.
- The mobile cart position should genuinely shift. A static cart makes the "mobile vendor" framing a lie the player will notice within two visits.
- Cormac has bought boots from Finbar O'Sullivan twice. This is an examine-level detail only (Cormac's footwear) and should never be dialogue — it is funnier as something the player can notice and Cormac never mentions.
- Sprite dependency: `cormac.png` (384x384), eight states plus four details plus five expressions plus four dialogue portraits, per `PNG_Assets/The_Confidence_Man_PNG_Assets.md`. Mustache is a separate layer.
- Voice-acting priority: **highest in Publandia.** Cormac has the largest tonal range of any NPC in the city and the character does not survive a flat read.

## 🚫 Warnings & Restrictions

### ⚠️ CRYPTO CHECK
- [x] **Crypto content conforms to `design/economy/economy_system.md`** — no crypto content in this profile; nothing to conform beyond that
- [x] **NO automatic crypto assumptions** — Cormac Byrne has **no** crypto. He is a cash street vendor. He is *exactly* the archetype a designer would be tempted to hand a shitcoin to, and that temptation must be resisted: Cormac's entire premise is that he discloses everything up front, which is structurally incompatible with the darknet shitcoin economy. Do not give this character a token
- [x] **Coins, kiosks, and certificates come from the master pool** — none invented here; none present here
- [x] **The bros are never scam-aware** — the bros never work out that the effect is partly theirs. Success is credited to the bottle; failure is credited to needing a bigger bottle. Neither conclusion is ever revised
- [x] **Nothing winks** — Cormac's transparency is a sales technique, not a signal to the audience. He calls himself a con man because it disarms customers and it works. No line acknowledges satire
- [x] **Focus on city-specific cultural exploitation instead** — the target is self-improvement snake oil, confidence-as-commodity and the placebo economy, played through Dublin market and carnival-barker tradition

### Satirical Target Verification
- [x] Targets tourist/frat bro behaviour, NOT local culture or ethnicity — the joke is on people who will pay 40 Sovs for a personality and then blame the bottle, not on Irish street traders
- [x] NPC is savvy entrepreneur, not villain or stereotype — Cormac knows precisely what he sells, precisely what it does, and precisely who is buying. Every material claim he makes is either true or printed as a disclaimer
- [x] Humour is affectionate mockery, not mean-spirited — the products genuinely help people, Cormac genuinely wants his customers to succeed, and his best day at the cart is the one that costs him a customer
