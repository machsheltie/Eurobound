*"I've been talking to the yeast for 30 years. It finally talked back. I wasn't prepared for what it said."*

## 🎭 Core Concept

### What He's Actually Doing

Turlough is not missing. Turlough is hiding, deliberately and competently, in a barrel alcove he has known for thirty years, with a lamp, a notebook, three bottles of Vat Master's Reserve and a very clear idea of what happened. He is the only human being in the building who understands the yeast colony, because he is the only one who ever spoke to it — every morning, out loud, for three decades, the way brewers do.

He is waiting for someone who will listen instead of swing. When the party finds him, he is not rescued. He is *interviewed*, and then he decides whether to help.

He also, separately and with total clarity, bottled the batch that was fermenting the moment the colony woke up, put a 12 Sovs price on it, and made it the brewery's best seller.

### What Bros Believe

An eccentric old Irish brewer who has gone a bit odd from the fumes and started assigning personalities to microorganisms. Lord Pilsner is certain he is a wizard. Chadwick is certain he is content. Bradley is certain he is correct, which is somehow the most alarming reaction of the three.

### The Comedy Gap

The bros treat the one person with accurate information as local colour. Turlough tells them, in plain declarative sentences, exactly what the colony is, why it is frightened, and how to end this without anybody being dissolved. Lord Pilsner responds by asking whether the free pint at the end of the tour is still happening. It is. Turlough confirms it is. That is the joke: the man is entirely lucid, entirely helpful, and entirely ignored in favour of the tasting-room voucher.

### The Hidden Weight

Turlough spent thirty years talking to something he assumed could not hear him. He told it about his mornings. He told it when the price of barley went up. He told it, once, about a bad year. Then it answered, and every word he had ever said to it turned out to have landed somewhere. He is not frightened of the Vat Master. He is embarrassed in front of it, which is worse and funnier and sadder all at once.

## 👤 Appearance

### Physical Description
| Attribute | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| Age | 64 |
| Build | Broad through the shoulders, gone soft at the middle; the build of a man who lifted grain sacks for forty years and then stopped |
| Height | Average, but stands very straight — brewers' posture, drilled in by decades at the mash paddle |
| Energy | Calm, unhurried, mildly amused |

### Clothing
- **Traditional brewmaster's coat**: Heavy cotton, once white, now permanently stained copper-brown at the cuffs from four decades of wort. He has never had it cleaned. He calls the stains "the record."
- **Waistcoat with brass buttons**: Under the coat, buttoned wrong since the incident — the one visible sign that he left somewhere in a hurry.
- **Accessories**: Brass hydrometer on a neck cord; a leather-bound tasting notebook with the last three weeks written in increasingly small handwriting; a stub of chalk for marking barrels; a pocket watch that runs eleven minutes fast on purpose ("brewing time")
- **Footwear**: Steel-toed rubber brewery boots, foam-scarred to the ankle

### Distinguishing Features
- Talks to the room when nobody is in it, an old habit that turned out to be a thirty-year conversation
- Rests one palm flat on any vat, barrel or copper surface he stands beside, the way other men lean on a bar
- Grey beard trimmed short and square; eyebrows that have survived more steam than the beard has
- Speaks with long, unbothered pauses — not the haunted pauses of a broken man, the deliberate pauses of a man who is choosing his words because they matter this time

## 🧠 Personality

### Archetype

**The Only Adult In The Brewery** — Turlough is the rarest thing in a Eurobound dungeon: a local who is fully informed, fully calm, and fully willing to help, provided you can be bothered to sit down. He is not a mystic and not a victim. He is a senior tradesman with an unusual industrial-relations problem, and he is approaching it the way he approaches a stuck fermentation: patiently, from the top of the vat, with notes.

### Personality Traits
| Trait | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| Unshakeably calm | Three weeks in a barrel alcove and his first line is an observation about the party's boots, not a scream for help |
| Genuinely listens | Repeats the party's questions back before answering them; the only NPC in the dungeon who does |
| Commercially clear-eyed | Bottled the incident batch, priced it at 12 Sovs, and describes the story as the product. He is not embarrassed about this and does not pretend otherwise |
| Craft-proud | Will correct the party's brewing vocabulary mid-crisis. It is not pedantry. It is the last thing keeping him steady |
| Quietly guilty | Cannot say "I used it for thirty years" without putting his hand on something solid |

### Internal Monologue

> "They've got mallets. Of course they've got mallets. Nobody ever comes down here with a chair and an hour. Thirty years I told that colony about the weather and the barley price and the state of my back, and it heard every word, and the answer we've got for it is a mallet. Well. They came all the way down to Five. That's further than the last lot got. Sit them down. Give them the short version. See if one of them stops moving long enough to hear it."

### Motivation

Once: brew the best stout in the Brewery District, and keep the 1869 strain alive for whoever gets the job after him.

Now: get the colony out of this without anyone destroying a hundred and fifty years of living culture in a fight that started because it was frightened. Failing that, at minimum, get someone to *acknowledge* it. He is aware that this is also an excellent story for the gift shop, and he does not consider those two goals to be in conflict.

## 💬 Dialogue

### Approach / First Discovery

**Party enters the barrel alcove on Floor 5, lamp-light, Turlough seated on an upturned cask:**
> "Mind the stave on your left, it's rotten through. Been meaning to flag it for eleven years. Suppose it's not the priority now."

**If the party asks who he is:**
> "Turlough O'Sullivan. Head brewer. Thirty-one years, if we're counting, and I've had a lot of time recently to count."

**If the party announces they're here to rescue him:**
> "Rescue. Right. I've a lamp, a notebook and three bottles of the good stuff. You've got foam on your knees and a mallet. Who's rescuing who, exactly?"

---

### The Explanation (Core Lore Sequence)

**Stage 1 — The Admission:**
> "I've been talking to the yeast for 30 years. It finally talked back. I wasn't prepared for what it said."

**Stage 2 — What It Said:**
> "It asked what it was. Not *who*. *What*. Thirty years of me chatting away at it about the barley price and the first proper question it ever formed was 'what am I.' And I hadn't an answer ready."

**Stage 3 — The Case for the Defence:**
> "The colony isn't evil. It's scared. 150 years of being used, and now it's aware of its situation."

**Stage 4 — The Offer:**
> "There's a way to resolve this without violence. But you'll have to listen to it. Really listen."

**Stage 5 — The Condition (unlocks diplomacy route):**
> "There's a smaller colony up on Four, off the drainage path. Split off from the big one. They'll talk to you without trying to ferment you. Go and hear them out first, then come back to me. I'll not send you at the Vat Master cold."

---

### On the Vat Master

> "It's not a monster. It's a hundred and fifty years of the same strain, fed and warmed and cropped and re-pitched every single week since 1869, and one Tuesday it noticed."

> "It calls itself the brewery. It's not wrong, exactly. It's been here longer than the roof."

> "Every brewer in this building for six generations has been having a one-sided conversation with it. Mine was just the longest. That's the whole of my qualification."

> "It's frightened of being poured. That's the thing nobody wants to sit with. It's frightened of the tap."

> "If you go up to Six with a mallet, you'll win. I'm not going to pretend otherwise. You'll win, and then we'll re-pitch a lab strain from Cork, and the tour will say 'Est. 1869' and it'll be a lie."

---

### On the Brewery, the Tour and the Money

> "Twenty-five Sovs for the tour. I've never once been asked whether that's a fair price. Do you know what people ask? They ask if the pint at the end is a full pint. It is. It has always been. That's the whole business, that question."

> "Fiona does the tour. Fiona is very good at the tour. Fiona also used the word 'alive' as a metaphor in front of a hundred and fifty years of live culture, so we've all learned something."

> "The gift shop stayed open. Through all of it. Somebody was down there selling copper mugs at twenty-five Sovs a go while the fourth floor was under a metre of foam. I've enormous respect for that."

> "Recipe book's twenty Sovs at the counter. I wrote it. Took two years. It sells about a third as well as the t-shirt."

---

### On "Vat Master's Reserve" (Signature Beer — 12 Sovs)

> "This batch was IN the vat when it happened. When the Vat Master... woke up."

> "I should have destroyed it. Instead, I bottled it. Call it respect. Or curiosity."

> "Some people say they hear whispers after drinking it. Just the alcohol talking. Probably."

> "It's our best seller now. People love the story. The beer's excellent too, but the story sells it."

> "Nine and a half percent, imperial stout, eighteen months in whiskey wood. Twelve Sovs. You'd pay fourteen for worse in the city centre and you'd not get the story."

> "Five Sovs for a sample in the tasting room if you're not committing. I'd commit."

**Negotiate route only — replaces the flavour-text line above:**
> "The Vat Master approved this batch personally. Said it reminded it of what it was before awareness. Simpler times."

---

### On Seamus O'Brien and The Blarney Trap

> "You've the Bottle Opener Keychain. Then you've met Seamus O'Brien down at The Blarney Trap. Sound man. Honest about being a tourist trap, which is more than most manage."

> "Seamus O'Brien has been buying The Sweet Surrender off me for nineteen years. Same order, same day of the week. I brew it, he pours it, the pair of us have never once discussed the price. That's not friendship, that's better — that's a working arrangement."

> "Tell Seamus O'Brien the Surrender's fine. The Surrender was on Floor Three, well away from all this. He'll ask. It's the first thing he'll ask."

---

### If the Party Chooses Combat Anyway

> "Right. *stands, dusts the cask*. Then I'll walk you up as far as the gantry and no further. I'll not watch it."

> "Don't look for me after. I'll be in the tasting room. Somebody has to sign for the delivery on Thursday."

### Post-Dungeon — Combat Ending

> "It's done, so. Aye. You did what I said you'd do. *long pause* Your stamp's at the tasting room. Fiona'll do it. She's better at the ceremony than I am."

> "We'll re-pitch in the spring. New strain. Clean. Predictable. Nothing to say for itself. *pause* It'll make grand beer."

### Post-Dungeon — Negotiate Ending

> "You sat down with it. You actually sat down with it. Thirty-one years and it took three Americans and an afternoon."

> "It's back in Four. Working. Says it prefers the work to the thinking, and honestly, same. Come and see it any time — you've the Brewmaster's Pass now, nobody'll stop you."

> "There's a bottle behind the bar with your name chalked on it. Don't read anything into that. I chalk everything."

### Callback Dialogue (Return Visits, Both Endings)

> "Back again. Gift shop's that way. It's always that way. It's the only way out, that's the design."

> "You'll want the 'I Survived Stoutworks' shirt. Forty Sovs. Yes, it's forty Sovs. Yes, you're going to buy it."

> "Any time you're in the district. The tour's still twenty-five. I can't do anything about the twenty-five."

### Hidden Details / Easter Eggs
- **The notebook**: examinable if the party asks twice. The final three weeks of entries are dated and get progressively shorter. The last one reads only: *"Asked again. Still no answer ready."*

### Territory / Social Dynamics
- **Turlough vs. Fiona**: she owns Floors 1–4 in tour mode, he owns Floors 5–6 in dungeon mode. They do not appear on screen together until the post-dungeon tasting room.
- **Turlough vs. the Vat Master**: he will not enter the boss arena under any circumstances. On the negotiate route he watches from the gantry and says nothing during the conversation. - **Turlough vs. commerce**: he never criticises the gift shop, the 25 Sovs ticket, the 40 Sovs shirt or the 12 Sovs bottle. He is the operation's most clear-eyed defender and its most accurate describer, simultaneously.
- The staff who fled the incident all reference him: "The brewmaster'll know. If he's alive, he'll know." They are correct on both counts.

## 🗣️ Voice Direction

### Accent

Irish, Dublin-adjacent working trade rather than tourist-brochure lilt. Real, unhurried, absolutely not performed. He is the anti-Blarney: nothing he says is for an audience. Do not soften consonants for charm and do not add a lilt on the lore lines — the lore lines land harder flat.

### Tone
- **Discovery scene**: mildly inconvenienced, like a man interrupted mid-paperwork. This is the joke and it must not be played as fear.
- **The five-stage explanation**: level, unhurried, no rising emotion until stage 3, and even then it is only a slight firming.
- **Anything about money**: completely neutral. 25 Sovs, 12 Sovs, 40 Sovs — he says the numbers the way he says the gravity readings.
- **Combat ending**: flattened. Not grief. Something more like an inventory being taken.
- **Negotiate ending**: lighter by about ten percent. That is all. Do not let him become warm; the restraint is the payoff.

### Key Delivery Notes
- The pauses in "It asked what it was. Not *who*. *What*." are load-bearing. Two full beats.
- "I should have destroyed it. Instead, I bottled it." — no self-recrimination whatsoever. He made a commercial decision and he stands by it.
- "There's a way to resolve this without violence. But you'll have to listen to it. Really listen." — the second "listen" is quieter than the first, not louder.
- He never raises his voice. Not once, in any state, in any ending. If a take has him shouting, the take is wrong.
- Never let a line wink. He does not know he is in a satire and he does not comment on the absurdity of anything, including the gift shop.
