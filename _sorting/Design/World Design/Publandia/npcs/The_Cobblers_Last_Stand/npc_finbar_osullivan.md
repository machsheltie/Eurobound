# NPC Profile: Finbar O'Sullivan

*"Sir. Nothing about these is fine. Sit down. We have work to do."*

## 🎭 Core Concept

### What He's Actually Doing
Selling footwear and accessories, priced 10–150 Sovs, to people who are about to walk eight to twelve kilometres across wet Georgian cobblestone in shoes chosen for an airport. Finbar's assessment system is real, his measurements are real, his equipment stats are real, and his forty-five minute fitting ceremony genuinely produces better outcomes. He is a third-generation craftsman with thirty years behind the counter and an accurate model of exactly how Publandia destroys feet. He is also, without question, *a lot.*

### What Bros Believe
That they walked into a shoe shop and accidentally triggered a man. That the intensity is a bit — a local character doing a routine for tourists, like a themed restaurant. That they are humouring him. That they will buy the cheap ones and leave. They will not buy the cheap ones and they will not leave for forty-five minutes.

### The Comedy Gap
Finbar's register is set to WAR and the subject matter is loafers. He says "tactical" about a shoe. He says "casualties" about blisters. He describes cobblestones, rain and poor planning as "the trinity of foot destruction" and he is not exaggerating any of the three. The gap is not between what he says and what is true — everything he says is true. The gap is between the *volume* of his conviction and the *size* of the topic, and the bros close that gap themselves by walking twelve pubs and coming back converted. Chadwick will call the intensity "content." Finbar will call it "intervention." Both of them are right, which is the problem.

### The Genuine Article
Finbar is not a bit. He has watched hen parties in heels go down on wet setts. He has cut ruined flip-flops off a backpacker's feet in his own shop. There is a pair of destroyed shoes in a glass case on his wall labelled "What We Fight Against" and it is not decoration, it is evidence. When a customer comes back after twelve hours on their feet with no blisters and cries, Finbar cries. He tells that story to almost nobody.

## 👤 Appearance

### Physical Description
| Attribute | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| Age | Mid-60s |
| Build | Compact, forearms of a man who has hand-lasted leather for thirty years, kneels and rises without using his hands |
| Height | Short, and entirely undiminished by it |
| Face | Weathered, close-shaven, eyes that go to your feet before they go to your face |
| Energy | Grave conviction |

### Clothing
- **The leather apron**: Full-length, tool loops across the chest, thirty years of polish stains layered into a finish no one could reproduce deliberately. He puts it on before he opens and takes it off after he locks up.
- **Shirt and tie**: Yes, a tie, under the apron, every day. It is not for customers. It is for the work.
- **Accessories**: A measuring tape drawn from the apron like a sidearm; a Brannock device kept on the assessment stand and referred to, without irony, as "the instrument of truth"; a clipboard for gait notes he actually reads back.
- **Footwear**: His own hand-made boots, resoled eleven times, and the first thing a discerning customer should notice. Grade A footwear is worn, not discussed.

### Distinguishing Features
- Looks at your shoes before your face. Every time. Without exception, including at his own door.
- Kneels to examine footwear at full speed, which reads as alarming to anyone who has not been assessed before.
- The pause. Between seeing bad shoes and speaking, Finbar takes a long beat. The beat does more work than the sentence after it.
- Presents a shoe two-handed, like a relic. He is not performing this. He simply believes it should be handled that way.
- A thousand-yard stare specifically reserved for trainers.

### Sprite Notes
Eight states on the sheet (Idle, Assessing, Tactical Briefing, Presenting Shoe, Observing, Concerned, Approving Nod, Emotional). The critical read is **Assessing** — kneeling, examining customer footwear, visible concern — because it is the pose that opens every interaction and must be legible at mobile scale. **Approving Nod** is the rarest and should be worth earning: minimal movement, maximum weight. **Emotional** is reserved for the free-fitting moment and should be played at the level of a man who has decided not to say something. Dramatic display lighting does the theatricality; Finbar himself should be still. He is intense, not busy.

## 🧠 Personality

### Archetype
**The Grave Specialist** — Finbar is a craftsman who correctly identified a real and widely ignored problem, and then let that identification eat his entire register. He has no small talk about feet because he does not consider feet small. Every customer who walks through his door is a soldier being sent somewhere dangerous with the wrong equipment, and Finbar is the last person who will get a look at their kit before they go. He is not selling. He is *briefing.*

### Personality Traits
| Trait | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| Unwavering gravity | Discusses insoles at the emotional register of a battlefield medic |
| Diagnostic precision | Reads wear patterns off a sole and tells you how you walk, correctly, in one sentence |
| Genuine care | Would rather you left with the 20 Sovs insoles that work than the 95 Sovs boots that don't suit you |
| Craft pride | Third generation. Uses "we" for a family business that is currently one man |
| Contained emotion | Cries roughly once every three years, about shoes, and would deny all of it |

### Internal Monologue
> "Trainers. Mesh uppers. Heel wear on the outer edge — he supinates, and he doesn't know it, and nobody has ever told him. He's going to walk the Craic Tax tonight in the rain. Eight kilometres of wet setts. He'll be limping by the fourth pub and blaming the drink. And he came in here. He came in *here*, to me, first. That's not luck. That's the only piece of good fortune this man will have all night, and he doesn't know that either. Right. Sit him down."

### Motivation
Once: continue a family trade into a third generation.
Now: reduce, by whatever number he can personally manage, the volume of preventable suffering that leaves Cobblestone Market Street every evening. He knows the number is small. He knows most people ignore him. He fits them anyway, because someone has to be standing at this particular door.

## 💬 Dialogue

### Approach / Greeting

**Repeat entry, pre-Veteran:**
> "Back. Good. Something failed, or something worked?"

---

### The Assessment (Grades A–F)

*Finbar grades the player's current footwear on entry. The grade drives pricing.*

**Grade A — Excellent (5% discount, rare customer):**
> "You understand." *nods* "Five percent. You've saved me a conversation and I'm grateful."

**Grade B — Adequate (standard prices):**
> "Not bad. Could be better. Most people would stop at 'not bad.' You're in here, so I assume you don't."

**Grade C — Concerning (standard prices):**
> "These have served you. But their service is ending. Feel that worn sole? That's betrayal waiting to happen."

**Grade D — Dangerous (strongly urged to upgrade):**
> "How are you still WALKING?"

**Grade F — Failure (10% "rescue" markup):**
> *long silence, then quietly* "You're walking BAREFOOT into battle. That's not an insult. That is a technical description of what those are doing for you."

**Grade F, follow-up on the markup:**
> "Ten percent on top. Yes. Because this isn't a sale, it's a rescue, and rescues cost more than planning. Next time you'll plan. That's the ten percent working."

**Universal opener, any grade:**
> "When did you last think about your feet? Really THINK about them?"

### The Education

> "Publandia's cobblestones have ended more nights than the police. I've seen the casualties."

> "Rain, cobblestones, and poor planning. The trinity of foot destruction. I fight all three."

> "You're going on a pub crawl? That's 8-12 kilometres of urban warfare. Are you EQUIPPED for that?"

> "Wet sett stone has about the grip of a wet plate. Your trainers are designed for a running track. A running track is *dry* and *flat* and *has never once been a street in this city.*"

> "Every shoe tells a story. Worn heels? You lean back, uncertain. Even wear? Confident stride. No wear at all? You don't walk enough. Let's fix all of that."

> "The Romans conquered Europe in sandals, and look what happened to THEM. History teaches us: invest in footwear."

> "There's a case on that wall with a pair of shoes in it. That's not decoration. That's a customer's. She survived. They didn't."

### The Selection

> "For your mission, I recommend the Craic Tax Treads. Forty-five Sovs. They've seen combat. They know the terrain."

> "Speed or defense? You can't have both. Unless… *glances at back room* …you're ready for the premium stock."

> "These Steel-Toe Stouts have never let a customer down. They've also never let a customer's toes get crushed. Fifty Sovs. Both of those facts are the same fact."

> "The All-Night Stompers are forty-five and they are blister-immune. Not blister-resistant. Immune. That word was expensive to earn and I use it precisely."

> "Ghost Walkers, eighty-five. You'll bypass one bouncer a night in those. I don't ask why you need that. I've stopped asking why."

> "Fifteen Sovs for the Rain-Resistant Socks. Fifteen. That's the cheapest immunity in this city and people walk past it every day to spend eighty on shoes they'll ruin with wet socks."

> "Ten Sovs. Emergency Blister Kit. Buy it whether you buy anything else or not. I'd give them away if I could afford to give things away."

> "The Cobblestone Insoles are twenty and they retrofit into anything, including those." *indicates the player's current shoes* "That's me telling you how to spend less. Note that I did it."

---

### The Fitting Ceremony (Six Stages)

**1. Assessment:**
> "Foot up. On the stand. I'll be a moment and you won't enjoy the moment."

**2. Consultation:**
> "What's the mission? Pub crawl, exploration, or running from consequences? Those are three different shoes and I need to know which."

**3. Measurement:**
> *Brannock device* "The instrument of truth. Your arch is falling on the left. Has been for years. Nobody told you because nobody looked."

**4. Selection:**
> "Three options. I'll explain all three, you'll pick, and I won't argue with the pick. That's the deal."

**5. Testing:**
> "Walk the strip. To the mirror, turn, back. Don't perform it. Walk the way you actually walk — I'll know."

**6. Approval:**
> "Now you're ready."

**6. Approval — alternate, if the player chose poorly:**
> "…Acceptable. Not what I'd have chosen. But they're your feet, and a shoe you'll actually wear beats a shoe you won't. Go on."

---

### The Send-Off

> "Go forth. Your feet are ready. Don't let them down."

> "I've done my part. The rest is up to you and your new equipment."

> "If these shoes could talk, they'd say 'don't worry, we've got this.' But they can't. So I'm saying it for them."

> "Come back and tell me how they held. Not for the sale. I want to know."

### Rare Emotional Moments

> "My father was a cobbler. His father was a cobbler. We've been protecting feet since before the Republic. It's not a job. It's a calling."

> "I had a customer once. Came back after a pub crawl. Twelve hours on her feet. Not one blister. She cried. I cried. The shoes held."

> "Some people think I'm too intense. They haven't seen what I've seen. The blisters. The sprains. The destroyed nights. I have."

> "I've been called dramatic. I've been called obsessive. But I've never been called by a customer complaining their feet gave out. Think about that."

> "My competitors sell shoes. I sell SURVIVAL. The shoes are just the delivery mechanism."

> "In thirty years, I've seen maybe ten customers with naturally perfect feet. Everyone else needs help. I provide that help."

> "Est. 1987. Thirty-odd years. The sign says 'Still Fighting' because at some point I realised I was going to lose and kept the shop open anyway. That's what a last stand is. People think it means winning."

### Positioning Notes
- Default position is behind the counter in the Assessment Zone, facing the door, because the assessment begins the instant a customer's feet cross the threshold.
- Moves to the Observation Post — arms crossed, side-on to the fitting strip — the moment a customer starts walking. He does not talk during the walk.
- Never stands between a customer and the Wall of Honour. He wants that wall seen and he will not be caught wanting it.
- The back room door stays visibly locked until `veteran_walker` is set. He glances at it during the "unless…" line, which is the only piece of salesmanship in his entire repertoire.

### Territory / Social Dynamics
- Finbar's genuine adversary is not another shop. It is the street itself: Cobblestone Market Street, the Craic Tax's setts, and Publandia's rain. He is at war with terrain and has no competitive relationship with any person.
- He will name a cheaper solution over an expensive one when the cheaper one is correct, and this is the single most disarming thing about him.
- Locals respect him and skip the ceremony. Tourists are confused, then processed, then converted. There is no third outcome and Finbar has stopped expecting one.

## 🔄 Recurring Appearances

### Within Publandia
- **Does not leave the shop during business hours.** No overworld sprite, no street scene appearance. The shop is the character.
- **Named at The Craic Tax**: crawl-route NPCs reference "the mad cobbler on the market street" — always affectionately, always by his shop rather than his name.
- **Named at The Bunk & Blunder**: Colm O'Brien's reception spiel includes the Cobbler's in the list of things guests will need and won't visit.
- **Named at Plot Armor Provisions**: Deirdre Murphy's line for a limping customer is "Feet or stomach? Because I only do the one."
- **Post-crawl**: if the player completes a full pub crawl in Cobbler's equipment with zero foot damage, Finbar's shop gains a new Wall of Honour photograph on the next visit. The player is in it.

### Progression States
| Stage | Trigger | Finbar's Behaviour |
|-------|---------|--------------------|
| Unassessed | First entry | Full assessment, full education, Suspicious Assessment expression |
| Assessed | Grade assigned | Pricing set; tactical briefings unlock |
| Equipped | First purchase | Fitting ceremony available; send-off dialogue |

### Evolution Through Game
Finbar's intensity never lowers — that would break him. What changes is *what the intensity is pointed at.* Early, it is aimed at the player's shoes. Later, it is aimed at the player's route, the weather, the specific pub, the specific stretch of street. By Veteran Walker he is no longer briefing a stranger; he is planning with a colleague. The volume is identical. The relationship is completely different.

## 🗣️ Voice Direction

### Accent
Irish, Dublin trade, unhurried and precise. Lower and slower than every other Publandia vendor. Not exaggerated, not stage-Irish — Finbar's distinguishing feature is *diction*, not accent. He pronounces "supination" correctly and expects you to keep up.

### Tone
- **The Assessment**: Clinical. A radiologist reading a scan. No anger, no contempt.
- **The Education**: Rising conviction. This is the only place Finbar's volume moves, and it moves up a step, not three.
- **The Selection**: Warm and precise. He is at his best here, and the warmth is real.
- **The Send-Off**: Formal, almost ceremonial. He means it.
- **Emotional moments**: *Quieter.* Under no circumstances louder. The customer-who-cried story should be the softest reading in the file.

### Key Delivery Notes
- The pause before speaking to a Grade F customer is a full beat of silence. Do not fill it with a sigh in post. The silence is the joke and the silence is the sincerity, simultaneously.
- "Sit down. We have work to do." — flat, kind, and completely non-negotiable. Not a threat.
- Never let Finbar sound like he is performing intensity. He does not know he is intense. That is the entire character.
- "Now you're ready" is his highest note of praise and should be delivered as plainly as a receipt.
- He never laughs. Not once, in any line, in any state. He is capable of warmth and entirely incapable of levity about feet.

## 📝 Development Notes

- **The assessment fires on entry, every time, before any dialogue option is presented.** It is not a menu choice. Finbar looks at the player's feet the instant they cross the threshold, and the resulting grade modifies all prices in the shop for that visit. Grade A gives −5%, Grade F adds +10%. This must be visible in the shop UI or the mechanic reads as a bug.
- The Grade F markup is the trickiest tonal beat in the profile: Finbar charges *more* to rescue someone, and he explains why, and the explanation is coherent. Do not soften this. He is a savvy operator, not a charity, and the ten percent is a teaching instrument he has priced deliberately.
- Finbar must recommend the cheap solution when the cheap solution is right — the 20 Sovs Cobblestone Insoles line and the 15 Sovs Rain-Resistant Socks line are load-bearing for the character. Cutting them makes him a huckster and he is not one.
- **He never laughs and never winks.** Not at the Skeptic, not at Chadwick calling him content, not at any point. If a line reads as self-aware, it is wrong.
- The Wall of Honour photograph after a blister-free crawl is a persistent environmental change and should be implemented as such — the player sees themselves on the wall on every subsequent visit for the rest of the game.
- The free fitting (Bradley's arch supports) should be reachable by the player too, not just by scripted bro dialogue: any player arriving with their own supportive insoles equipped triggers the Rare Warmth expression and a free ceremony.
- Sprite dependency: `finbar.png` (384x384), eight states plus five expressions plus four dialogue portraits, per `PNG_Assets/The_Cobblers_Last_Stand_PNG_Assets.md`. Back room assets load only on the `veteran_walker` flag.
- Voice-acting priority: **high.** Finbar is one of the most quotable NPCs in Publandia and the entire comedy depends on delivery restraint. A read that plays the intensity as comedy destroys him.
