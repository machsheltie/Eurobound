*"The trap is in the name. That's marketing."*

## 🎭 Core Concept

### What He's Actually Doing

Running the most professionally executed tourist pub in The Craic Tax and being completely honest about it. Seamus is a third-generation publican who inherited an 1847 building and rebranded it in 2003 for the tourist era. He pours Guinness correctly, employs live musicians, charges Craic Tax prices (8 Sovs a pint, 6 Sovs across the river), and greets every American who walks through the weathered green door with a genuine welcome he has performed roughly forty thousand times without it ever once curdling into contempt.

He also runs something bigger. The brass shamrock bottle opener behind his bar (15 Sovs) is not a souvenir — it is a credential. Seamus's grandfather started a mutual-aid arrangement between publicans across Europe: you look after each other's travellers. Seamus maintains it. When a bro says "Seamus sent me" at a taproom in Debaucheryville or a proeflokaal in Shamsterdam, doors open, tours get enhanced, and a stamp gets pressed into a leather-bound book. Every "Seamus" in that phrase, anywhere in Europe, is this Seamus.

### What Bros Believe

That they have found a hidden gem, that Seamus has personally taken a liking to them specifically, and that they are now — after roughly two hours — his friends. Lord Pilsner has heard Seamus say the word "friend" and taken it as a formal designation. The bros believe the bottle opener is a cool piece of merch, that the passport book is a fun freebie, and that their access to breweries across Europe is a result of their own charm rather than a hundred-year-old publicans' agreement they stumbled into by spending 15 Sovs.

### The Comedy Gap

The word **TRAP** is in the name of the building. It is painted in gold Celtic lettering above the door. There is a sidewalk board reading "Craic Tax Pricing Applies." Seamus tells them, out loud, in the first thirty seconds, that everyone loves it here and *that's the trap*, and then he winks. They hear all of this and conclude they've found the real Ireland. The comedy is not that Seamus is deceiving them. It's that he is being completely transparent and they are physically incapable of processing it as anything but charm.

The second-order joke: the network is real. The opener works. Seamus really does have friends in eight cities who will really do them a favour. The bros will spend the entire game cashing in on a genuine act of hospitality while believing they're being clever.

### Clothing
- **White shirt, sleeves rolled to the elbow**: The uniform of every real publican and every publican playing one. Both are true at once. Sleeves go up at open and never come down.
- **Dark waistcoat**: Slightly too warm for the room, worn anyway, because it reads correctly in photographs and Seamus knows exactly what reads correctly in photographs.
- **Bar towel over the left shoulder**: Functional. Also the single most photographed object in The Craic Tax.
- **Accessories**: Wedding ring, worn thin. A stopwatch on a lanyard under the bar he claims is for the 119.5 seconds and which he has not needed since 1998. The brass shamrock bottle openers live in a wooden box beside the register.
- **Footwear**: Black work shoes, resoled three times, absolutely silent on the duckboards.

### Distinguishing Features
- **The red face**: Not from drinking. From forty years of standing between a hot kitchen pass and fourteen taps in a room with a permanently lit gas fire. He'll tell you that if you ask. Nobody asks.
- **The wink**: Deployed with surgical precision, exactly once per new group, on the word "trap."
- **Hands that never stop**: Polishing a glass that is already clean, squaring a beer mat, turning a pint a quarter-turn so the harp faces the customer.
- **The pour stance**: When a Guinness is settling, Seamus folds his arms and watches it. He will not take an order during this. He is not being rude. He is simply not available.
- **Expression**: Permanent welcome. It does not switch off when he turns away. After 11 PM it softens into something quieter and, crucially, different — that's the tell that the real pub has started.

## 🧠 Personality

### Archetype

**The Honest Trap** — Seamus has arrived at a philosophical position most people never reach: he sells an experience he knows is packaged, he charges a premium for it, he is completely upfront about both facts, and he refuses to be ashamed of any of it. He believes that manufactured authenticity is still authenticity, that hospitality is hospitality regardless of the invoice, and that a welcome delivered for the ten-thousandth time is still a welcome. He is not cynical. Cynicism would be easier and he has declined it.

### Personality Traits
| Trait | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| Professionally warm | Calls everyone "friend." Means it, in the specific and limited way a publican can mean it — which is more than most people mean anything. |
| Radically transparent | Volunteers the Craic Tax markup unprompted. Points at the sign. Has never once denied that the pub is a tourist pub. |
| Craftsman's pride | The Guinness ritual is non-negotiable. He will let a queue build rather than serve a pint before the surge settles. |
| Long-game hospitality | Everyone is treated as a future regular, on the odds that one in three hundred actually becomes one. |
| Custodian of the network | Takes the publicans' agreement seriously as an inherited obligation, not a marketing gimmick. Will not extend it to someone who's been rude to Aoife. |
| Unembarrassable | Cannot be shamed about the shamrocks, the prices, the 2003 refit, or the fact that "Est. 1847" refers to the masonry. |

### Internal Monologue

> "Three of them. Americans, mid-thirties, one's already got his phone up before he's got a drink in his hand. They'll sit at the bar because they think that's where the locals sit — the locals are in the Snug and they'll never know. He'll order Guinness because the guidebook said to. I'll pour it right, because it costs me nothing to pour it right and it costs me everything not to. Four hours from now the loud one will tell me he understands Publandia. He won't. But he'll have had a genuinely good night, paid a fair price for a slightly unfair product, and that's the whole business. Céad míle fáilte. Here we go again. God, I do love it."

### Motivation

His grandfather asked his father, and his father asked him: *are we selling beer or selling Publandia?* The answer was "both," and the answer was correct. Seamus's motivation is to keep a building his family has held since before the euro, to employ four people properly, and to run the arrangement his grandfather started without letting it rot into a loyalty-card scheme. He wants the pint poured right. He wants the tourists to leave happy. He wants the regulars to still have somewhere to go at midnight when the tourists have gone up to bed. He has structured an entire business so that all three can be true at once, and it works.

## 💬 Dialogue

### Approach/Greeting

**First entry into The Blarney Trap**:
> "Céad míle fáilte! A hundred thousand welcomes! Take a seat, what'll ye have?"

**Recognising Americans (immediately)**:
> "Americans? Brilliant. You'll love it here. Everyone does. That's the trap. *winks*"

**If they say it's their first time in Publandia**:
> "First time in Publandia? You've come to the right place. Or the wrong place, depending on your budget. Same thing, really."

**If they arrive soaked (frequent — it's Publandia)**:
> "Ah, you've met the weather. It does that. Sit by the fire, it's gas but it's warm, and I'll not hear a word against it."

**If they ask if this is a real Irish pub**:
> "It's a real pub. It's in Publandia. Work it out from there, friend."

---

### The Pint Ritual

> "Guinness? Good choice. Now, I'm going to pour this properly. Takes two minutes. Worth every second. Trust the process."

> "See that? The surge? That's 119.5 seconds of patience. You can't rush perfection. Or Guinness. Same thing."

> "There she is. Perfect dome. Drink that, and tell me it wasn't worth the wait. You can't. Because it was."

**If the player tries to order during the settle**:
> "One moment. *doesn't look up* I'm with someone."

**If the player drinks before the dome settles**:
> "You've — no. No, that's grand. It's your pint. I'll just be over here, grieving."

**Ordering a Smithwick's instead (7 Sovs)**:
> "Smithwick's! Now that's a local's order. Either you've done your reading or you've got taste. I'll not ask which."

---

### The Business

> "Craic Tax prices? Aye, guilty. But you're paying for location, atmosphere, and my sparkling personality. Fair trade."

> "Eight Sovs. It's five-fifty across the river. I'm not going to pretend otherwise — you'd find out and then you'd feel foolish, and I don't sell that."

> "The tourists keep the lights on. The regulars keep me sane. You could be both if you stick around."

> "My grandfather charged what he charged. I charge what the market bears. He'd understand. He was a businessman too."

> "My father asked me once: 'Seamus, are we selling beer or selling Publandia?' I said 'both.' He said 'correct.' That's the business."

> "Every pub in the Craic Tax is a trap. The difference is, I'm honest about it. The trap is in the NAME. That's marketing."

> "The tourists photograph the outside, drink inside, and leave thinking they've seen Publandia. They've seen one street. But they've seen it WELL."

> "The craic is a product. But it's a product I believe in. Manufactured authenticity is still authenticity. Fight me on that."

**If asked about the 847 shamrocks**:
> "Eight hundred and forty-seven. Aoife counts them in January. It's not a joke, it's an inventory. Two of the antiques are real — the mirror and one chair. Everything else went in in 2003 and I'd do it again."

**If asked about "Est. 1847"**:
> "The building's from 1847. The pub's from 2003. The sign doesn't specify. *beat* That's also marketing."

### The Genuine (After 11 PM / Pub Regular Status)

> "You want REAL Publandia? Come back at 11 PM. After the early crowd leaves. That's when the city actually drinks."

> "Pub Regular status? Earn it. Keep coming back. Remember staff names. Buy rounds. Don't be a tourist, be a guest."

> "The trap is comfortable, isn't it? That's the trick. You're welcome to stay as long as you like. Most do."

> "Real regulars? They come after 11, stay until close, and never post about it online. That's how you know they're real."

**Granting Pub Regular status**:
> "Right. You've been in four nights running, you've bought your rounds, and you called Siobhan by her name without reading her badge. *slides a key along the bar* The Snug's through there. Don't make me regret it."

**First time the player is let into The Snug**:
> "Quieter in here, isn't it. No shamrocks. Funny, that."

### The Bottle Opener & Passport (Key Item Handoff)

> "That opener? It's not just for show. That's a key to every brewery worth visiting in Europe."

> "The brewers, they recognize it. Flash that, and doors open. Tours get... enhanced."

> "My grandfather started that tradition. Publicans across Europe. We look after each other's travelers."

> "Take the book too. You'll want to document this journey. Trust me."

> "This book? It's your passport to the real beer culture of Europe."

> "Every brewery, every pub worth its salt — they'll stamp you. Proves you were there. REALLY there."

> "Fill a city's page, you earn their Legendary Hangover. Fill the whole book? Continental Beer Master."

> "The stamps aren't just proof. They're RESPECT. Brewers see a full page, they know you're serious."

> "Stoutworks brews our house beer — The Sweet Surrender. Get their stamp too, and you're halfway to Publandia's Legendary."

**On the network specifically (if asked "who's Seamus?" elsewhere and the player returns)**:
> "You said my name in Shamsterdam and a man poured you a jenever for free? Aye. That's the arrangement. It's older than me and it'll outlast me. Don't abuse it and it'll keep working."

**If the player asks how many people are in the network**:
> "Enough. Not a list, not a club, no cards. Just publicans who owe each other favours across four hundred miles. You've been let into something, friend. Behave accordingly."

**Handing the opener over after a Pint-Off win instead of a purchase**:
> "You didn't buy it, you won it. That's better. Word travels differently when you've won it."

### The Sweet Surrender (Post-Stamp)

> "The Sweet Surrender? That's OUR beer. Stoutworks brews it, but it's MY recipe. My grandfather's, actually."

> "Named it for what happens to every tourist who walks through that door. They surrender. To the prices, the atmosphere, the craic."

> "First pint's on me when you earn that stamp. You've proven you belong here. Even if you're still a tourist."

**Stamping the passport**:
> *presses the Celtic knot stamp, deep emerald green* "There. The Blarney Trap. Est. 1847, Publandia. Now go get the other three and stop cluttering up my bar."

### Positioning Notes
- Default position is the centre of the bar, directly under the brass tap array, facing the door. He sees everyone who enters before they see him. This is deliberate and has been deliberate since he was nineteen.
- The bottle opener box and the vintage register are both within arm's reach and both are on his left, so the opener pitch and the transaction are one continuous motion.
- During a Guinness settle he shifts one metre left to the settling station and becomes non-interactive for the duration. Players who try to talk to him get the "I'm with someone" line. This is the tutorial for the pint ritual and should be discoverable by accident.
- After 23:00 he leaves the bar unattended for the first time all day and moves toward the Snug. If the player has Pub Regular status, following him is the trigger for the entire Genuine dialogue tree.
- He will physically walk out from behind the bar for exactly three reasons: a great toast, a musician arriving, or Aoife needing help on the stairs with luggage.

### Territory / Social Dynamics
- **The bar is a hierarchy and Seamus is the top of it, invisibly.** Siobhan and Declan run their own shifts without checking in; Aoife owns the floor and the stairs. Seamus's authority is exercised entirely through where he chooses to stand.
- **Tourists at the bar, locals in the Snug.** This is not enforced by anyone. It simply happens, every night, and Seamus has never explained it to a tourist unprompted.
- **The Round System is socially policed by the room, not by staff.** Refusing a round doesn't get you barred; it gets you quietly deprioritised, which is worse.
- **Cross-street relations**: The other Craic Tax pubs all claim authenticity and all know Seamus does it better. There is no feud. There is an unspoken agreement about who takes the overflow on Saturdays.

## 🔄 Recurring Appearances

### Within Publandia

Seamus does not leave The Blarney Trap. He does not have to — **the Seamus network means his name arrives everywhere before the player does.**

- **Shenanigans**: Mickey Finnegan greets any opener-holder with "Seamus sent you, did he? Course he did. He sends me all the good ones and keeps the money." Mentioning Seamus to Mickey unlocks the Fool's Gold cross-stock and a warmer opening to the Wise Fool track.
- **The Stoutworks Brewery**: Flashing the brass shamrock at the Welcome Center upgrades the standard tour to the enhanced route. Tour Guide Fiona: "Seamus's opener. Right, you're getting the good tour, not the coach-party one."
- **Trinity of Pints**: The 10 Sovs "tuition" is waived at the registration desk for opener-holders. The gag: they call it an "articulation agreement with a partner institution."
- **Paddy's Book**: "Seamus sent me" is one of the nightly passwords at the unmarked door behind the dumpster. It works *sometimes* and it is *risky* — Tiny's discretion. Seamus has never authorised this and knows perfectly well it's happening. If asked: "Paddy uses my name at his door. I've asked him not to. He says it's a compliment. It isn't."
- **The Craic Tax streets**: Touts and scouts recognise the opener and adjust their pitch — they'll still try to sell you something, but they'll stop pretending you just arrived.
- **The Blarney Trap itself**: After the player earns Pub Regular status, Seamus's default idle shifts to Genuine Mode two hours earlier each night, which is the game's quietest and most-missed reward.

### Evolution Through Game
- **Act 1**: Pure welcome. Seamus is a warm wall of hospitality and a vending machine for key items. Every line is professional.
- **Act 2**: Once the passport has three or more stamps, his dialogue shifts from *pitch* to *report* — he asks about the other cities and remembers the answers. The network stops being a mechanic and starts being a relationship.
- **Act 3**: Genuine Mode becomes the default whenever the player enters after dark. He stops selling. He starts asking. The Snug is permanently open.
- **Post-Continental Beer Master**: Seamus sits down. This is, mechanically, an unprecedented event — he has no sitting sprite in Act 1 and gets one here. He pours two Sweet Surrenders and does not ring either of them into the till.
- **What never changes**: He never admits the pub is anything other than what he said it was on day one. There is no reveal. There was never anything hidden.

## 🎯 Quest Integration

### Primary Quest: The Continental Beer Trail
- **Quest**: The Continental Beer Trail (Brewery Passport chain)
- **Quest ID**: `publandia_sidequest_continental_beer_trail_01`
- **Role**: Quest Giver, Key Item Giver, Stamp Giver, and the origin point for the entire multi-city collectible track
- **Trigger**: Purchasing the Bottle Opener Keychain (15 Sovs), winning a Pint-Off, or reaching "Pub Regular" status — whichever comes first
- **Outcome**: Player receives the opener and the passport book. The Pause Menu gains a permanent "Brewery Passport" tab. Eighteen stamp slots across four cities unlock as reachable content.

### Quest Gate Function

**Seamus is a hard gate on roughly a third of the game's optional content.** Without the Bottle Opener Keychain, no brewery or pub in any city will award a stamp — the item is checked on entry at every one of them. This is the single most consequential 15 Sovs in Eurobound, and Seamus mentions it exactly once per visit without ever pressuring the player. The design intent is that players who skip it feel the absence in Debaucheryville and have to travel back, at which point Seamus says nothing about it at all, which is worse.

Secondary gate: **The Snug and the Backroom.** Both require "Pub Regular" status, which Seamus grants personally. The Backroom unlocks the unlicensed poker, the trivia-fights, and the POS-terminal NFT acquisition. Declan is the door; Seamus is the decision.

## 🗣️ Voice Direction

### Accent

Dublin, north-side-inflected, softened by four decades of making himself intelligible to Americans. Not exaggerated. Seamus is a professional communicator — he has consciously slowed his own speech over the years and the actor should sound like a man who did that on purpose. Irish-language phrases (*Céad míle fáilte*, *Gaiste an Blarney*) are delivered fluently and without ceremony, as furniture rather than performance.

### Tone
- **The Welcome**: Full-beam, hands-open warmth. It should be impossible to hear it as insincere, because it isn't.
- **The Business**: Flat, cheerful, matter-of-fact. Quoting the Craic Tax markup should sound exactly like quoting the weather.
- **The Pint Ritual**: Reverent, and completely unironic. This is the only register where Seamus is not selling anything.
- **The Genuine (after 11 PM)**: Quieter, slower, fewer words. The volume drops and nothing else changes, and that's the whole effect.
- **Deflecting an accusation**: Pleasant. Never defensive. He agrees with you and waits.

### Key Delivery Notes
- The wink line — "That's the trap" — must land as a genuine friendly disclosure, not a smirk. He is telling them the truth and they are not hearing it. Any smugness kills the joke.
- "I'm with someone" (about a pint) should be delivered with complete sincerity and no comic timing whatsoever.
- "Yes. Was there a second part to that?" is the single hardest line on the sheet. No sarcasm, no edge. Total agreement, total absence of shame.
- Emotional range is deliberately narrow — Seamus operates between "warm" and "slightly warmer." The one exception is the Continental Beer Master scene, where the setting-down of the glass carries everything and the line reading should be almost flat.
- He never raises his voice. Not once, in any branch. The nearest he gets is "Right so."

## 📝 Development Notes

- **Seamus owns the "Seamus" network game-wide.** Any bare "Seamus", "Seamus sent me", or "Seamus's network" reference in any city's dialogue, at any location, resolves to Seamus O'Brien of The Blarney Trap. No second Seamus may be authored anywhere in Eurobound.
- The Bottle Opener Keychain is a hard content gate on the entire 18-stamp Brewery Tour. Verify at implementation that every brewery/pub entry check reads the same inventory flag.
