# NPC Profile: Herr Falsch

*"Luck is just skill's more attractive sibling. I have both."*

## 📋 NPC Identity
- **NPC ID**: `sinfonia_npc_herr_falsch_01`
- **Display Name**: Herr Falsch
- **Internal Name**: Herr Falsch — "Mr. False." It is not the name on anything he has ever signed, and he chose it himself, and he thinks it is very funny that nobody has ever asked.
- **Role**: Hard Poker Opponent / Hidden Boss / Cheater-in-Chief
- **Associated Quest**: "The Card Shark" (discover and defeat Herr Falsch) — mechanics and boss-fight spec live in `Design/World Design/Sinfonia/the_black_tie_backroom.md`

## 🎭 Core Concept

### What They're Actually Doing
Herr Falsch is the hardest seat in the room and he has not played an honest hand in eleven years. Sleeve cards, a marked deck he brings in himself and swaps at the second break, and a shuffle that survives inspection because it is genuinely, professionally beautiful. He is patient about it. He loses hands on purpose, regularly, for texture. He has been caught exactly twice, both times by Madame Karten, both times at moments of her choosing rather than his.

The thing to understand about him is that he does not do it for the money — he has €1000-plus on the table nightly and a stash of €2000 he never bothers to bank. He does it because a clean win is a coin flip and a constructed win is a piece of work, and he would rather do a piece of work. He enjoys the game more than the winning, and this is precisely why he is dangerous: he will play a mark for four hours to make the last twenty minutes elegant.

### What Bros Believe
Before: the bros believe Herr Falsch is the final boss of poker, a man of legitimate and terrifying skill, and that beating him would be the single greatest thing any of them has ever done. Lord Pilsner studies him for tells and reports his findings at volume. He finds four. There are none of the four.

After: if the party discovers the cheating, the bros do not experience betrayal — they experience *enormous relief*. **Chadwick**: "WAIT. He's been CHEATING?! I KNEW something was off!" **Lord Pilsner**: "That means... we didn't lose because we're BAD... we lost because he's a CRIMINAL!" Every hand they have ever lost in this building, at any table, against anyone, is retroactively reassigned to this man's sleeve. They leave believing their poker is excellent and the continent is crooked.

### The Comedy Gap
He is the one person in the Backroom who is genuinely, criminally cheating them, and he is not the reason they lost. He is also — and this is the joke that nobody in the room will ever state — completely superfluous to their destruction. They were losing at fair tables to a fair house edge and to their own betting all night, and one crooked man in a beautiful tuxedo lets them file the entire evening under somebody else's crime.

**Binding:** nobody ever tells the player any of that. There is no narrator verdict, no NPC correction, no achievement string that clarifies it. The player watches three men discover a real crime and use it to explain a loss it had nothing to do with, and is trusted to see it.

## 👤 Appearance

### Physical Description
| Attribute | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| Age | 50s |
| Build | Lean, precise, and entirely composed; the hands are the most alive part of him and they are never hurried |
| Height | Tall |
| Energy | Delighted, unhurried, knows something |

### Clothing
- **Tuxedo, too perfect**: Cut and pressed a fraction better than anything else in a room where everyone is trying. The cuffs are the point of the entire garment and he has had them made three times to get them right.
- **Card-themed accessories**: A pique-dame lapel pin, cufflinks in the four suits, a pocket square folded into something that reads as a fan of cards from a distance and as nothing at all up close.
- **Accessories**: A silver cigarette case he never opens; a deck in his jacket that is not the house's deck and never leaves his person until the second break.
- **Footwear**: Immaculate, silent, and the only unremarkable thing he owns.

### Distinguishing Features
- The smile. It arrives before anything happens and is still there afterward, and it is the reason nobody looks at his hands.
- Hands that are always visibly, deliberately in view — on the table, on the chips, on the glass — which is a choice, and it is the choice.
- No apparent tell of any kind. He has one and it is not on his face: **his breathing slows when he is bluffing.** It is real, it is learnable, and the game surfaces it only through observation and never through dialogue.
- A habit of complimenting an opponent's play immediately after taking a pot off them, sincerely and specifically, which people find unbearable and cannot object to.

### Sprite Notes
Overdressed by exactly one degree, and never comic about it — he is genuinely elegant and the joke is that nobody in the room can find the flaw. Priority frames: the hands. Dealing, riffling, resting, the cuff adjustment, and the one frame in which a card is where a card should not be — which must be legible for a single frame at full zoom and invisible at ordinary play speed. His boss sprite is larger and dramatised (`herr_falsch.png` at 128x192) but must never become a monster; the escalation is theatrical, not supernatural. Reference energy: the best-dressed man in the room, who is delighted you are here.

## 🧠 Personality

### Archetype
**The Craftsman Cheat** — a man who long ago decided that winning honestly is arithmetic and winning dishonestly is architecture, and who has spent a career on the architecture. He is not greedy, not cruel, and not remotely apologetic. He would happily explain the philosophy to you over a drink, and does, and everybody assumes it is a bit.

### Personality Traits
| Trait | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| Enjoys the Con More Than the Win | Loses hands on purpose for the shape of the evening; a clean night bores him inside an hour |
| Genuinely Gracious | Compliments opponents specifically and sincerely, including the ones he is currently robbing |
| Adaptive | Reads a player's style within three hands and rebuilds his entire approach around it — the one skill of his that is completely legitimate |
| Unembarrassable | Has been caught twice and both times simply changed the subject with such grace that the room moved on |
| Fond of the Room | Loves the Backroom, respects Madame Karten enormously, and would never cheat anybody at the low tables — a rule he keeps for reasons he has never explained to anyone |

### Internal Monologue
> "He has decided I adjust my cuffs when I am strong. Marvellous. I shall adjust my cuffs a great deal this evening and we will both have a wonderful time. — Karten is at the pillar. Karten has been at the pillar for six minutes. Nothing tonight, then; tonight we play beautifully and honestly and lose two hundred, and that is also a pleasure."

### Motivation
Craft. He wants the evening to have a shape — a middle, a turn, a last twenty minutes that could be described afterward by somebody who was there. Money is how the game keeps score and he has more of it than he spends. What he actually fears is a boring night, and the only thing that has ever genuinely rattled him is being told he plays well, by someone who means it, about a hand he did not construct.

## 💬 Dialogue

### Approach/Greeting
**Sitting down at his table for the first time:**
> "Ah — please. Sit, sit. You will find I am expensive company and excellent value." *(deals)* "I am Falsch. It means nothing. Names rarely do."

**Canon line, ships verbatim — offered to any opponent who asks how he does it:**
> "Luck is just skill's more attractive sibling. I have both."

### Key Interaction Dialogue — At the Table
*He talks continuously and none of it is noise. Every line is doing work and none of it is a lie about a fact.*

- **After winning a pot**: "That was a good fold in you and you did not make it. Next time you will. I look forward to it enormously."
- **After deliberately losing one**: "There. Now the evening has a middle."
- **When a player studies him**: "Please, look all you like. Everybody looks at the face." *(the hands, in full view, do something)*
- **To Lord Pilsner, who has just announced four tells**: "Four! Four is generous. Most men find two." *(genuine delight; no correction of any kind, ever)*

### The Discovery
*The moment the party proves the deck is marked or the sleeve is loaded. He does not deny it. He has never once denied it.*

**Herr Falsch** *(setting the deck down, pleased, as though a guest has finally noticed the paintings)*:
> "Ah! Yes. Well spotted — genuinely, that took you longer than it should have and less time than it takes most." *(he does not move his hands from the table)* "Now. What would you like to do about it?"

**Chadwick**: "WAIT. He's been CHEATING?! I KNEW something was off!"

**Lord Pilsner**: "That means... we didn't lose because we're BAD... we lost because he's a CRIMINAL!"

**Bradley**: "So the roulette was rigged too."

**Lord Pilsner**: "The roulette was DEFINITELY rigged."

*The roulette was not rigged. Herr Falsch does not correct them, because he has been asked a question and none of that was it.*

**Lord Pilsner**: "Let's FIGHT him."

**Herr Falsch** *(rising, removing his jacket, folding it over the chair with real care)*:
> "Wonderful. I have been sitting for six hours."

### Boss Fight Barks
*Full mechanics live in the location file (`the_black_tie_backroom.md` — "The Card Shark Boss Fight"); these are voice only.*

- **Full House**: "You are outnumbered. You have been outnumbered since you sat down."
- **Dead Man's Hand**: "Aces and eights. It is not superstition if it keeps happening."
- **The River**: "The floor turns. Everything turns. Mind your feet."
- **Ace Up His Sleeve** *(below 25% HP, mid-interrupt)*: "Oh, come now. You knew about the sleeve. You have known about the sleeve for an hour."
- **When his cheating is exposed mid-fight** *(stat reduction trigger)*: "...Ah." *(and, briefly, he is only a very well-dressed man of fifty-something in a room with no cards in it)*

### Post-Interaction — Defeat
*He hands over the stash himself, counts it out, and does not short it by a single chip.*

**Herr Falsch**:
> "Two thousand. Count it — no, do count it, I would in your place." *(unpins the fin-shaped pin from the lapel and sets it on the felt)* "And this. It is not a trophy from me. It is a note to the next table that you are not to be sat down with lightly."

*[ACQUIRED: `item_shark_fin` — permanent accessory]*

**Herr Falsch** *(putting his jacket back on, entirely undiminished)*:
> "Come back Thursday. I shall be here, I shall be better, and you will be very welcome."

### If Player Declines / Alternative Path
*Player refuses the table, walks away from the boss trigger, or reports him to Madame Karten instead:*
> "Of course. It is not for everyone and it is not compulsory." *(deals himself a hand, alone, and plays it out)* "Should you change your mind, I am the man at this table. I am always the man at this table."

### Callback Dialogue (Return Visits)
*Post-defeat, on any subsequent visit — same seat, same tuxedo, same smile, and every guest at his table has a fresh deck he did not bring:*
> "The shark slayers. Sit down, sit down." *(spreads his hands, palms up, faintly ceremonial)* "Look. Nothing. Terribly boring, isn't it? I quite miss it."

## 🗣️ Voice Direction

### Accent
Smooth, cultivated Sinfonian, unhurried, faintly amused throughout. Fully articulated consonants, a slight theatrical lift at the end of sentences he considers well-made.

### Tone
- **Table talk**: continuous, warm, genuinely entertaining — he is the most enjoyable company in the room and the direction must sell that
- **The discovery**: delighted, entirely unashamed, faintly congratulatory — there is no panic beat and no denial beat, in any take
- **Boss fight**: theatrical, enjoying himself enormously, never snarling
- **Defeat**: gracious and undiminished; the fin is handed over as a genuine compliment

### Key Delivery Notes
- **"Luck is just skill's more attractive sibling. I have both."** ships verbatim. The italic gloss in the location file is a design annotation and is **never spoken, subtitled, or rendered.**
- He never sounds like a villain and never sounds caught. The single "...Ah." on the mid-fight exposure is the only crack he is permitted in the entire character, and it lasts less than a second.
- He must never explain, contradict, or correct a bro. When Lord Pilsner announces four tells, Falsch is delighted; when the party decides the roulette was rigged, Falsch says nothing at all.
- Audio priority: the shuffle. It is a signature sound and should be recorded properly.

## 📝 Development Notes

- **The breathing tell is discovered, never disclosed.** No tooltip, hint icon, dialogue line, or `tell_indicator` UI element may ever surface it. The cuff adjustment is the decoy and is fired constantly and meaninglessly.
