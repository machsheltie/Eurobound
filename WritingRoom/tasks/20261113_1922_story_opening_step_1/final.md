## SCENE 1 — INT. MOMMY'S HOUSE, BASEMENT — DAY

*A basement preserved exactly as its occupant left it, which was 2003. Inflatable couch. A framed "EMPLOYEE OF THE MONTH" certificate for a job he does not currently have. Twelve bottles of hair gel arranged by height. LORD PILSNER — frosted tips, puka shells, navy tee riding up over a modest gut, cargo shorts, white crew socks, black flip-flops — is on the couch, holding a remote at a television that is off.*

*The doorbell rings. PILSNER does not move.*

MOMMY: *(from upstairs)* Sweetie? There's a man at the door with a clipboard. He needs an adult.

PILSNER: Tell him I'm at work.

MOMMY: You're in the basement.

PILSNER: Tell him I'm at work in the basement.

*The front door opens and closes. A pen scratches. The front door opens and closes again. Footsteps on the basement stairs, slow, one at a time, the way you carry something you don't want to spill.*

*MOMMY (60s, cardigan, reading glasses on a chain) arrives with a thick envelope held flat on both palms like a tray of drinks. She sets it on a coaster. On the arm of the couch.*

MOMMY: It's from her lawyer. It says final. There's a page about the dog.

PILSNER: I don't want the dog.

MOMMY: You're not getting the dog. I'm telling you so you don't ask.

*She goes back up. PILSNER looks at the envelope. He picks up the remote. He puts the remote down. He picks up the envelope.*

*He opens it with a steak knife from a plate on the floor. He reads the top page. He reads it again, moving his lips.*

PILSNER: *(calling up the stairs)* Mom. It says "irreconcilable differences."

MOMMY: *(off)* Yes.

PILSNER: That means we both tried, right?

*Pause.*

MOMMY: *(off)* It means she tried.

*He turns the page. Runs a finger down a list. Stops.*

PILSNER: There's a page called "Division of Personal Property."

MOMMY: *(off)* Read it out loud. I want to hear it in a man's voice.

PILSNER: "'The contents of the garage, excluding the chest freezer.'" ...There's a chest freezer?

MOMMY: *(off)* There was.

*PILSNER gets to the signature page. He signs the acknowledgment with a pen he has to shake first. He dots the "i" in his own name with a small heart.*

## SCENE 2 — INT. MOMMY'S HOUSE, BASEMENT — LATER

*PILSNER is on the couch with the papers fanned around him like a card trick. The basement door bangs open. CHADWICK — backward trucker hat, popped collar, chain wallet, ring light already clipped to his N-Gage — comes down filming. BRADLEY — basketball jersey over a long-sleeve tee, baggy jeans, Livestrong bracelet, pager on the belt loop — follows, carrying a laptop and a folder with actual colored tabs.*

CHADWICK: Don't say anything. We're filming the reaction.

PILSNER: It's final.

CHADWICK: *(to camera, whispering)* Final. Say it again.

PILSNER: It's final.

CHADWICK: *(to camera)* Twice.

BRADLEY: *(opening the laptop on the foosball table)* We made a deck.

PILSNER: A what?

BRADLEY: A deck. It's slides. I did the slides.

*The first slide: a clip-art airplane over clip-art mountains. Title, in Comic Sans, 96-point: **THE BOYS ARE BACK.***

CHADWICK: Volume up. There's music.

*There is no music. CHADWICK turns the laptop toward himself and turns it up anyway.*

**SLIDE TWO.** *Clip art of a pint. Bullet points: "Publandia. Pubs. Pubs that don't close. Pubs that close and open again."*

BRADLEY: Publandia.

CHADWICK: That's the whole slide.

BRADLEY: That's the whole city.

**SLIDE THREE.** *Clip art of a neon sign. Bullet points: "Debaucheryville. Clubs. Absinthe. A street where the windows are red and the women are IN the windows. A whole street, bro. It's a district."*

PILSNER: ...In the windows.

CHADWICK: In the windows.

**SLIDE FOUR.** *Clip art of a violin. Bullet points: "Sinfonia. Opera. Then the back room of the opera."*

BRADLEY: That one's fancy. I'm bringing a blazer. I'm wearing it over the jersey.

**SLIDE FIVE.** *Clip art of a tulip. Bullet points: "Shamsterdam. Everything's legal. Everything. They have a menu."*

PILSNER: A menu of what?

CHADWICK: *(gently)* Everything.

**SLIDE SIX.** *A map of a city with a small red pin on it. Bullet point: "Gym, 400 meters from the hotel. I checked. All four cities. I mapped it."*

BRADLEY: That's for me. That's not for you guys. That's my slide.

**SLIDE SEVEN.** *No clip art. Black slide. White text: "NON-REFUNDABLE."*

BRADLEY: That's the last slide.

*PILSNER looks at the black slide for a long time.*

PILSNER: I have a girlfriend.

BRADLEY: Bring her.

CHADWICK: No.

*Bradley clicks back to slide seven.*

PILSNER: I live in a basement.

BRADLEY: Basements are big in Europe. They call them cellars.

*PILSNER picks up the divorce papers, taps them into a neat stack, and puts them in his cargo pocket. It does not fit. He puts them in the other cargo pocket. It does not fit there either. He holds them.*

PILSNER: When do we leave.

CHADWICK: *(to camera)* He's in.

BRADLEY: *(closing the laptop)* Thursday. I already told my wife.

## SCENE 3 — THE LEAVINGS — MONTAGE

*Three doorways. No music underneath any of it.*

**INT. THE GIRLFRIEND'S APARTMENT — DAY.** *A laundry basket by the door, already packed, already folded. THE GIRLFRIEND (30s, on a call, one earbud in) does not hang up.*

PILSNER: So it's a trip. It's not a breakup. It's a trip.

GIRLFRIEND: *(to the phone)* — no, he's doing it now. *(to PILSNER)* Mm.

PILSNER: The boys booked it. It's non-refundable. That's how much they love me.

GIRLFRIEND: *(to the phone)* He's explaining the ticket policy. *(pause)* I know. *(to PILSNER)* The basket's yours. Don't take the towels, I counted them.

PILSNER: *(picking up the basket)* You folded it.

GIRLFRIEND: I'm not a monster.

*She holds up one finger — wait — goes to a drawer, comes back, and puts a phone charger in the basket. Then she takes it back out.*

**INT. CHADWICK'S HOUSE, LIVING ROOM — DUSK.** *Book club. Seven women, wine, one actual book. CHADWICK comes down the stairs with two rolling bags and a neck pillow, filming.*

CHADWICK: Babe. Babe. Say something for the stream.

CHADWICK'S WIFE: *(not looking up from pouring)* Have a nice flight, Chad.

CHADWICK: *(panning the room)* Book club. These are my wife's friends. *(beat)* Say hi.

ONE OF THE WOMEN: *(without turning)* Bon voyage.

CHADWICK: *(to camera, genuinely moved)* They love me.

CHADWICK'S WIFE: Put the ring light away, Chad.

CHADWICK: It's for the memories.

CHADWICK'S WIFE: So is the lawyer.

*She raises her glass to the room. The room raises theirs back. Nobody raises one to CHADWICK.*

**INT. BRADLEY'S HOUSE, BEDROOM — DAY.** *Painters' plastic on the floor. BRADLEY'S WIFE is standing in what used to be Bradley's side of the closet, holding a color swatch up to the wall.*

BRADLEY: *(duffel over one shoulder)* Babe. The closet.

BRADLEY'S WIFE: Mm.

BRADLEY: You're expanding?

BRADLEY'S WIFE: Something like that.

BRADLEY: *(deeply pleased)* It's a surprise.

BRADLEY'S WIFE: *(handing him a protein shake)* Last one I'm making. *(beat)* I mean it as a fact, Bradley. Not a threat.

BRADLEY: *(taking it, flexing at the doorway on the way out)* I'll come back shredded.

BRADLEY'S WIFE: *(to the painter)* Eggshell. Everything eggshell.

## SCENE 4 — EXT. MOMMY'S HOUSE — DEPARTURE

*Front porch. A rolling duffel, a cooler bag, and a man in flip-flops. MOMMY comes out last, carrying a laminated card.*

MOMMY: Mommy loves you. It's going to be okay, sweetie.

*She hugs him. It is a real hug and it lasts. While she holds him she checks his scalp with two fingers, finds something, and does not say what.*

MOMMY: Call when you land. Call when you're bored. Call when you're not bored. Your father called his mother every day of his life and he was forty-one when he stopped.

PILSNER: Mom.

MOMMY: Blood pressure meds. They're in the side pocket. I put them in the days-of-the-week box and I labeled the box, and if you take Thursday's on a Wednesday the world will not end but Mommy will know.

PILSNER: Mom.

MOMMY: *(taking his wrist, tapping the laminated card)* This is my number. It's on a card because you lose phones. If something happens, you call Mommy first, and then you call the other one.

PILSNER: The ambulance.

MOMMY: After.

*She pulls a pair of socks out of her cardigan pocket and presses them into his hand.*

MOMMY: Fresh socks. Every day. I sewed your name in them so if you leave them somewhere they can find their way back to you. *(beat)* I sewed it into all of them. It took me a week.

PILSNER: *(looking at the sock)* ...Mom, this says "LORD."

MOMMY: That's your name.

*She zips the cooler bag herself, then unzips the duffel and slides the divorce papers out of his cargo pocket, folds them flat, and puts them in the outside pocket of the bag.*

PILSNER: What are you doing.

MOMMY: You'll want to show somebody you're single. *(patting the pocket)* In the outside pocket, so it's quick.

*She stands back. Straightens his collar. Takes a piece of lint off his shoulder, looks at it, and puts it in her own pocket.*

MOMMY: Now. If a girl is mean to you, that's not a sign. That's just a girl being mean to you. And if she's nice to you, that's not a sign either, that's a girl being nice. *(beat)* A sign is a sign. It's on a post. It's got words on it.

PILSNER: I know how signs work.

MOMMY: You drove into a mailbox in April.

*An Uber pulls up at the curb. MOMMY looks at it, then at him, and then she does the thing where she pats her own pockets for money she has already put in his bag.*

MOMMY: There's something in the front pocket. Don't spend it on a hat.

*PILSNER climbs in with the duffel and the cooler. He rolls the window down. She takes his face in both hands through the window and kisses his forehead, and then she holds on to his chin for one more second than the situation requires.*

MOMMY: You're still my little man.

PILSNER: Mom.

MOMMY: I know.

*The car pulls away. In the rear window: MOMMY, on the porch, waving with both arms. Then she stops waving, goes inside, and the deadbolt turns, and the chain goes on.*

## SCENE 6 — INT. PLANE — TAKEOFF

*Economy cabin. The bros have three seats in a row: PILSNER window, BRADLEY middle, CHADWICK aisle with the ring light still on. A FLIGHT ATTENDANT does the safety demo. The bros watch like it's a halftime show.*

BRADLEY: *(counting rows to the exit)* Two rows behind. I'm counting. If we go down over the ocean I'm getting the door.

FLIGHT ATTENDANT: *(demo, flat)* ...exits are here, here, and the one this gentleman is making eye contact with.

CHADWICK: *(whispering, filming)* She's looking at me.

PILSNER: *(as the engines spool)* This is it. This is the takeoff. This is where it starts. I feel like a young Tony Hawk.

*The plane lifts. All three of them whoop — a full-throated, arm-raised, frat-lawn whoop. The rest of the cabin turns around. None of the bros notices.*

BRADLEY: *(still whooping)* Turbulence.

CHADWICK: *(still whooping)* Content.

*The seatbelt sign dings off. PILSNER immediately reclines all the way back into the lap of a man behind him who was already eating.*

MAN BEHIND: *(to no one)* It's a four-hour flight.

*PILSNER, eyes closed, fully reclined, does not answer. He is already asleep with his mouth open.*

*SMASH CUT: the plane's shadow crossing a cartoon map. A dotted line. A little plane icon. The map is labeled EUROMEMEIA.*

## SCENE 7 — EXT./INT. PUBLANDIA AIRPORT → THE BUNK & BLUNDER — DAY

**INT. [ARRIVAL AIRPORT] — BAGGAGE CLAIM.** *[PROPOSED signage — author to pick one: NOTHING TO DECLARE INTERNATIONAL / FIRST REGRET INTERNATIONAL / HUBRIS FIELD.] A single carousel. The bros' three duffels come out one at a time, each somehow more disreputable-looking than the last. A CUSTOMS OFFICER waves them through without looking up.*

CUSTOMS OFFICER: *(flat, stamping)* Purpose of visit.

PILSNER: Culture.

CUSTOMS OFFICER: *(stamps again, still not looking)* That's what they all say. Next.

CHADWICK: *(to camera)* He knew. He could tell.

BRADLEY: *(stretching at the carousel, grunting)* Deep vein. I did the calf raises. I'm basically a local now.

**EXT. AIRPORT → TAXI — PUBLANDIA STREETS.** *A montage through the window: cobblestones, a kebab shop, a neon sign in a language none of them will attempt, a hen party already in progress at what appears to be breakfast time. The bros' three faces are pressed to the glass like dogs.*

PILSNER: Look at it. LOOK at it. The whole city smells like a pub.

CHADWICK: *(filming the window)* Ten out of ten. Already. We haven't even gotten out of the car.

**INT. THE BUNK & BLUNDER — RECEPTION.** *A converted Georgian townhouse, paint peeling artistically. A hand-painted star rating on the wall, self-awarded. A chalkboard reads "Tonight's Vibe: CHAOTIC." It never changes. Behind the desk: COLM O'BRIEN (30s), a man who started this as a gap-year job eight years ago and has been tired ever since.*

COLM: One room. Three grown men.

BRADLEY: We can share. We're brothers.

COLM: *(typing, not looking up)* Rooms are assigned at random. The system decides. The system is a hat.

CHADWICK: *(to camera)* It's a lottery. The whole country runs on vibes.

COLM: *(sliding three key cards across, one at a time)* Key cards. They work about half the time. The trick is to keep trying and to lose hope at your own pace.

PILSNER: *(taking all three keys)* We will.

COLM: *(already turning away)* Breakfast is seven to nine. Continental means toast and sadness. Coffee's real though.

## SCENE 8 — INT. THE BUNK & BLUNDER, ROOM — THE NAP — PLAYER CONTROL

*A room with bunk beds and a chair that has clearly seen things. Their duffels are thrown but not unpacked. The light outside is afternoon going to evening — prime going-out light.*

*CHADWICK is already horizontal on the lower bunk, ring light still on, snoring with his mouth open, filming nothing. BRADLEY is on the upper bunk doing slow, deliberate calf raises off the edge of the mattress.*

BRADLEY: Just a nap. Twenty minutes. Tactical. It's what athletes do.

*He lies back. He is asleep in under four seconds, mid-sentence about REM cycles.*

*PILSNER stands between the bunks in his socks and flip-flops, holding his Sidekick, vibrating at a frequency only he can hear.*

PILSNER: *(to the room)* We are NOT napping. We are in EUROPE. There are women out there who have never met an American with a paddle.

*Beat. The room does not answer. CHADWICK snores. BRADLEY's pager — still clipped to his belt loop, still "just in case" — goes off with a single ancient beep. Neither of them wakes up.*

*PILSNER looks at the bunk. Looks at the window. Looks at the bunk.*

PILSNER: *(already climbing in, fully dressed, on top of the covers)* ...Twenty minutes. Then we go out. Twenty minutes.

*[PROPOSED UI, author to rule: the prompt appears — NAP / DON'T NAP. Both options nap. The only difference is how long PILSNER pretends he's not going to.]*

*The screen dims. The neon sign outside the window flickers on. Somewhere below, a hen party screams.*

*When the screen comes back up, it's full dark. PILSNER sits bolt upright, hair in a perfect vertical shock, Sidekick in hand, eyes wild.*

PILSNER: BOYS. It's NIGHT. We slept through the afternoon. We are THREE HOURS BEHIND SCHEDULE.

*CHADWICK wakes up filming. BRADLEY wakes up doing a calf raise.*

CHADWICK: *(to camera, voice thick)* We good. We back. I got the whole nap. Three hours of content.

BRADLEY: *(cracking his neck)* Tactical. I feel incredible. I feel twenty-five.

*PILSNER is already at the mirror, re-frosting his tips with tap water, tightening the puka shells, sliding the divorce papers back into the outside pocket of the duffel, the way Mommy packed them.*

PILSNER: Gentlemen. Publandia, Debaucheryville, Sinfonia, Shamsterdam — it all starts tonight. We are going OUT.

*He opens the door. Hallway light. Music from somewhere.*

PILSNER: *(over his shoulder, to no one)* ...I should call Mommy.

*He does not call Mommy. The door closes. END OF STEP 1.*
