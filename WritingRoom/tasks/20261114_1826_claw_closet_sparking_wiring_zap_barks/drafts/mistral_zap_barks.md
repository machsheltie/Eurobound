# Mistral — Claw Closet Zap Barks (candidates + picks)

Constraints applied: ≤10 words, floating bark above leader's head, no box, no reply. Never afraid / hurt / disgusted — the bro is pleased, grateful, or files the zap as something good. Never clever: no reframes, no proud shock/current/electric puns, no cool one-liners, no self-awareness. Sincere, wrong, pleased with itself. No real place names ("Europe" etc.). Period 1997–2005 vocabulary only. Reviewer: **Aion** (3a stand-up test on all 12).

The misread I built on: the zap is **the local specialty he ordered, a line item, applause from his chat, or a workout he can log.**

---

## LORD PILSNER — divorced grandeur, paperwork, tabs, "the local specialty," orders

1. "That's the local specialty, boys."
2. "I'll take two of those."
3. "Put it on my tab."
4. "That's how the locals say hello."

**MISTRAL PICK: #1 — "That's the local specialty, boys."**
(5 words. His canon quirk is insisting on ordering "the local specialty" no matter how questionable it looks — so of course the exposed wiring is the dish he ordered. Sincere, wrong, pleased, and it's an announcement to the bros, his only mode. No pun, no reframe; a stand-up gets nothing from it standalone. The laugh is that he was just electrocuted.)

#2 is the runner-up — ordering a second shock is the purest version of the delusion, best pool filler for repeat zaps. #3 is the divorced-paperwork instinct (he'll bill it). #4 is the plainest misread.

Avoided beats already owned: "carded me / still got it" (Kimi pick), "tip the wiring" (Deepseek pick).

## CHADWICK — 400-odd followers, films everything, rates out of 10, ICQ chat, "the clip"

1. "This beats the dorm prank."
2. "The chat is going nuts."
3. "This room gets a ten."
4. "That's the clip. That's the one."

**MISTRAL PICK: #1 — "This beats the dorm prank."**
(4 words. His whole motivation is reliving the 2009 dorm prank that made the local news; he files the zap as a bigger viral moment. Sincere, wrong, pleased — and it's a comparison, not a punchline, so it fails the stand-up test in the right way.)

#4 is the runner-up (his personal quest is reclaiming "the greatest post"). #2 is the livestream/ICQ-chat misread, #3 is his rate-everything-out-of-10 quirk applied to the closet itself.

Avoided beats already owned: "did that go in the video" (Kimi pick), "that's my intro" (Deepseek pick).

## BRADLEY — gym logic, reps, the burn, pump-up playlist, resting heart rate

1. "Counts as chest day."
2. "That's the burn."
3. "Sore tomorrow means it worked."
4. "That my pump-up music?"

**MISTRAL PICK: #3 — "Sore tomorrow means it worked."**
(5 words. Textbook gym-bro logic applied to a wall socket: DOMS means the workout landed. Pleased, sincere, zero fear, terrible reasoning. A comic can't say this and get a laugh without the zap on screen; the bark only works in context.)

#1 is the runner-up (he logs the shock against a muscle group). #2 is the "feel the burn" misread — "burn" here is gym vocabulary, not an electric pun. #4 ties to his canon quirk of refusing to fight without his pump-up playlist; he thinks the crackle is it.

Avoided beats already owned: "free rep / core" (Kimi pick), "trainer / spotting" (Deepseek pick), Kimi's dead "basically a pre-workout" construction.
