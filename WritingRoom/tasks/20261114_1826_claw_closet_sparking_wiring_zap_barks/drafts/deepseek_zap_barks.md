# Deepseek — Claw Closet Zap Barks (candidates + picks)

Constraints applied: ≤10 words, floating bark, no reply. Never afraid / hurt / disgusted. Never clever — no reframes, no proud puns on shock/current/electric, no cool one-liners. Sincere, wrong, pleased with itself. Reviewer: **Aion** (3a stand-up test on all 12).

The misread in every line: the zap is a **free service, a production value, a workout, or a compliment from the building.** The bro does not flinch, does not step back, and does not learn.

---

## LORD PILSNER — divorced grandeur, paperwork, status, being appreciated by staff

1. "Tip the wiring on your way out, boys."
2. "Housekeeping's thorough. They even do the walls."
3. "I'm putting this in the review. Under highlights."
4. "That's hospitality. My ex never greeted me like that."

**PICK: #1 — "Tip the wiring on your way out, boys."**
The building served him; he tips it. Generous, wrong, completely unbothered, and it's an order to his bros, which is his only mode. No pun, no reframe. A comic would have to explain it.

Runner-up #3 — the paperwork instinct. #2 and #4 are the two I'd expect Aion to eyeball: "Housekeeping's thorough" is the most written-sounding of the four, and #4 leans on the ex-wife for its laugh.

---

## CHADWICK — 400-odd followers, films everything, always mid-edit

1. "Dude. That's the intro. That's my intro now."
2. "Four hundred people are gonna love this."
3. "Hold on. Let me get that angle again."
4. "Keep going. I'm filming the whole wall."

**PICK: #1 — "Dude. That's the intro. That's my intro now."**
The shock is branding. He has already re-cut the video in his head and it's better now. Zero concern for his arm, total concern for the open.

Runner-up #2 (the number does the work — specific and pathetic). #3 and #4 both require him to want more of it, which is the correct delusion but #1 gets there first.

Note: I deliberately avoided "did the camera get that" — Kimi's pick owns that beat.

## BRADLEY — gym logic, resting heart rate, protein, lats, "the pump"

1. "Now that's a trainer. Didn't even ask questions."
2. "Whoa. Felt that in the traps."
3. "Good. That's the warm-up sorted."
4. "Somebody's spotting me. That's teamwork, bro."

**PICK: #1 — "Now that's a trainer. Didn't even ask questions."**
Perfect gym-bro grievance (nobody wants the intake form) fused to a total misread: the wall is coaching him. Sincere, dumb, pleased, and it doesn't sound like a joke until you remember he was just electrocuted.

Runner-up #4 — "spotting me" is the cleanest single misread in my set. #2 and #3 are the plainest and probably the best pool filler for repeat encounters.

## Implementation notes for the Orchestrator

- One bark per zap, leader only, floating text, no box, no reply, no reaction from the other two.
- Trigger has a cooldown; do not fire on every frame of proximity. Pool per bro, no immediate repeat of the last line pulled.
- No flinch, no stagger, no damage, no status. He walks in again.
- Bros stay in character: Pilsner issues instructions, Chadwick keeps shooting, Bradley treats it as a set.

## Flags
- None of my twelve use a real place name ("Europe" included — see my note on Kimi's file in `critiques.md`).
- Nothing post-2005 in the vocabulary: "traps", "the pump", "spotting", "the intro", "the review" are all period-safe.
