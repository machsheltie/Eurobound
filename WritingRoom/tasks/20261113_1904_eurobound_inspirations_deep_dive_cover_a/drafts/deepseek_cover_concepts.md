## Shared doctrine (applies to all three)

**The poster is sincere.** Every cover is composed as though the artist believed these three men are heroes. Nothing in the image is staged as a joke — no raised eyebrow, no winking prop placement, no arrow pointing at anything. The comedy is entirely in what the sincere composition includes and what the bros are doing inside it. If a concept needs the viewer to be told it's funny, it's dead.

**They pose like they've seen the poster.** All three know they're being photographed and are performing for it. Chins up, chests out, weight on the front foot. This is the one thing they're all genuinely good at.

**Never sexy, never cool.** Pilsner's silhouette is load-bearing: navy tee riding up over the gut, cargo shorts, white crew socks, black flip-flops. Chadwick's polo collar is popped and his hat is backward. Bradley's jersey is a size too small over a size too large torso. No rim light sculpts anybody's jaw. No low camera angle flatters a gut — the camera is at chest height, the height of a friend taking your picture.

**Three figures, nothing else alive.** No mascot, no pet, no fourth party member, no animal. Backgrounds are populated by NPCs only where the concept requires a witness, and witnesses are always turned slightly away, mid-task, unimpressed.

**No real place names, no real landmarks.** The skyline is built only from canon parody locations: the Astronomical Cock-Up Square clocktower, the Bassline Opera House dome, the Wobbly Windmill, the leaning gable of The Hoisted Pint, the Blarney Trap's hanging sign. Nothing in any cover is traceable to a real street.

**Real products keep their real names but carry no weight.** The Sidekick, the N-Gage, the pager, the Livestrong band are present as texture at 3–4 mm tall. No cover is built around a logo that isn't ours.

**The world is gorgeous; they are the anachronism.** HD-2D painterly diorama, tilt-shift, real light, soft focus at the edges. The bros are in crisp focus and belong to a different decade than the light falling on them.

## The wordmark

**EUROBOUND**, one design, three adaptations.

Heavy collegiate slab, all caps, tight kerning, slight forward lean. Chrome-bevel face with a dawn gradient running pink at the top to teal at the bottom — the exact colour of a sky at 05:40. A thin gold rim light along the upper edges only. The **final O is replaced by a bottle cap**, face-on: crimped edge, one prong bent, a dull highlight on the crown. It reads as a halo at a glance and as a bottle cap at a second glance, and those two readings never resolve.

Set on a soft dark plate so it survives on any background. Played completely straight: this is what a 2003 game logo looked like, and the designer of it was not smiling.

## Cover 1 — "LAST CALL AT THE EDGE OF EUROMEMEIA"

**Format:** full-bleed panoramic, characters small in the lower third, sky doing most of the work. Classic JRPG box.

**Composition.** Chest-height camera, behind the three of them, wide. They stand on the parapet of a budget hostel roof terrace — a satellite dish, a string of dead fairy lights, a sun-bleached plastic chair on its side, a laundry line. They occupy the bottom third, shoulders touching, arms around each other. Above them, two-thirds of sky, and on the horizon the four cities stacked in a single painterly composite: the clocktower left, the windmill and the leaning canal gable right, the opera dome centred and half-lit.

**The bros**
- **Pilsner** (centre): one black flip-flop up on the parapet, arm thrown out toward the horizon in a conqueror's gesture, the other arm around Chadwick. Frosted tips lifting in the wind, puka shells catching the light, Sidekick on his belt. His tee has ridden up. He is mid-yell — mouth open, chin up, a man addressing a crowd that isn't there.
- **Chadwick** (left): ring-light mace held aloft like a torch, phone raised in the other hand filming the horizon, not the moment. Backward trucker hat, popped collar. He is looking at his phone screen, not the sunrise, and grinning.
- **Bradley** (right): double bicep flex, elbows out, jersey riding up, broken pool cue tucked under one arm like a standard. Livestrong band, pager. His face is doing the thing he does in the mirror: absolute conviction, zero result.

**The setting.** Dawn, 05:40. The hostel roof is cheap and real: tar paper, a vent stack, a shopping trolley on a lower roof. The four city silhouettes are warm and soft and slightly out of focus, and every window in them is dark except the ones that shouldn't be.

**Palette.** Peach and rose at the horizon, cooling to a teal-navy at the top of frame. Warm key from behind, so the bros are rim-lit but their faces stay half-lit and ordinary — no heroic underlighting. The chrome of the wordmark picks up the same peach and teal. One acid-green accent, bottom left, from a neon sign.

**The joke it lands.** The triumphant dawn is the end of the night, not the beginning of one. The light is beautiful because they never went to bed, and the only thing open in frame is a kebab stand whose shutter is coming up in the bottom-left corner, its neon still on. They're saluting the sunrise like a beginning. It's a closing time.

**Thumbnail (Steam capsule).** The three silhouettes plus the gut-and-flip-flop shape read at 231×87 as "three guys, sky, logo". The sunrise gradient does the heavy lifting at every size. The gag is a full-size reward, not a thumbnail requirement — which is the correct way round for a box.

## Cover 2 — "THE LINEUP"

**Format:** tight, front-on, three faces filling the frame, shallow depth of field. Sitcom poster energy.

**Composition.** They stand shoulder to shoulder against a pub's exterior wall, arms crossed, feet planted, chins lifted — the exact arrangement of a 1998 boyband sleeve and of a police identification lineup, and they have no idea they're the second one. The wall is wet brick. Behind them, filling the right third of the frame, is the pub's window.

**The bros**
- **Pilsner** (centre, half a step forward): arms folded over the gut, jaw set, doing "alpha." One frosted tip has collapsed. The Sidekick is clipped to his belt and the puka shell necklace is sitting slightly wrong, and he has not adjusted either.
- **Chadwick** (left): arms crossed but one hand still holding the phone up at chest height, ring light on, pointing at himself. Hat backward. He is not looking at the camera — he's checking whether he's in frame.
- **Bradley** (right): arms crossed so hard his shoulders are up around his ears, jersey stretched, pool cue leaning against his leg. One eyebrow is doing something he thinks is intensity.

**The setting.** A cobbled street at 04:00, wet, one strip of neon overhead and everything below it in that drained blue. The pub window is the joke: inside, a dozen locals have turned away from their drinks and are looking out at them. Not angry. Not amused. Just looking, pints paused, in the flat exhausted way people look at weather. One of them is filming. One is pointing at the door.

**Palette.** Cold wet blue and slate for the street and wall, warm amber inside the window — so the brightest, warmest part of the image is the pub they can't get into. The wordmark's chrome picks up the amber. The bros are lit by the neon, which is unflattering and green-white.

**The joke it lands.** They think they're the subject of the photograph. They're the thing on the other side of the glass. It lands in one silent read: a whole pub has stopped to watch them pose.

**Thumbnail.** Three faces and a glowing window. At small capsule size the amber window reads as a light source and the three heads read as three heads, which is enough. The faces inside the window dissolve into warm bokeh and become a full-size payoff.

## Cover 3 — "THE ONLY PHOTO THEY'LL KEEP"

**Format:** diegetic. The cover *is* a photograph, cropped with the phone's own frame edges visible — a selfie taken at arm's length, slightly off-level, thumb in the corner.

**Composition.** Chadwick's arm enters from the bottom right, holding the phone. The three faces are jammed into the top two-thirds, the way a group selfie always is: Pilsner centre with his chin lifted to hide the jaw, Chadwick's hat brim cutting the top edge, Bradley leaning in from the left so only half his face makes it. Behind and below them, the actual location is still happening and mostly out of frame.

**What's in the frame that shouldn't be**
- The bottom left corner: a canal, a bicycle in it, a heron standing on the bicycle.
- The bottom right, behind Chadwick's shoulder: a taxi door closing on someone.
- Extreme left edge, cut off at the eyelid: the face of a bouncer, arms folded, looking directly down the lens.
- Directly behind Pilsner's head, in soft focus, the leaning canal gable of The Hoisted Pint, lit up and gorgeous.

**The bros.** All three grinning. Genuinely, uncomplicatedly happy — this is the best moment of the trip and it's this one, the photo. Pilsner's thumb is up. Bradley's thumb is up. Chadwick's thumb is up. Nobody is looking at the same thing.

**Phone chrome.** Thin UI edges, played straight and small: a battery at 3%, a notification stack of 47, and one message preview from "Mom" reading "Are you eating." Unread. The time reads 05:41.

**Palette.** Warm saturated night: sodium orange, canal black-green, one magenta from a club sign behind. Faces the warmest thing in the shot, because the phone flash fired and flattened them — which is exactly why they love it.

**The joke it lands.** This is the picture that ends up on the fridge. Everything that actually happened is at the edges, cropped out, unfocused, or behind them — and they will never look at the edges. They'll look at themselves and remember it as a great trip, and they'll be right, and it will be worse than they think.

**Thumbnail.** Three lit faces and a bright phone-flash warmth — reads instantly as a photograph, which no other game's capsule does. The wordmark sits top-left on a dark strip so it never fights the faces. The edges are a full-size reward.

## Production notes

- **Safe cropping.** Design every cover with the logo group and the three heads inside the central 40% so the 616×353 capsule, the 460×215 header and the 231×87 small capsule all crop without losing a face or a letterform.
- **Library art (600×900).** Covers 1 and 3 extend vertically without new material (more sky, more canal). Cover 2 needs a taller arrangement of the same three figures — do not stack them, keep the lineup and let the wall run up.
- **Do not animate these.** Covers ship still. Motion belongs to the load screens.
- **No fourth figure, ever.** NPCs appear only as background witnesses, never in the hero group, never posed with the bros.
- **The bros never look at each other.** In all three concepts they look at the camera, the horizon, or their own phone. Nobody's attention is on the others, because this trip is three men performing separately in the same photograph.
