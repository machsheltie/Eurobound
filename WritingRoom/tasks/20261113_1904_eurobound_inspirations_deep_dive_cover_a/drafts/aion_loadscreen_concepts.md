# EUROBOUND — Load-Screen Concept (Aion)

*Deliverable 3. Primary writer: Aion. Reviewer: Kimi (character voice/continuity); second read: Deepseek (antihero comedy). Status: PROPOSED.*
*Adopts Mistral's shared doctrine (variation systems, bounded motion, real progress bar, tips with teeth, parody names only) and Deepseek's wordmark treatment. This concept does not reuse any of Mistral's compositions — no hostel floor phone, no departure board, no Beer Goggles boot.*

## Load Screen — "THE WALK OF SHAME" (the morning commute)

**Composition.** Full-bleed painterly HD-2D diorama. The three bros trudge home along a canal path at 07:52 in full daylight, column formation, single file, chins level with the horizon, faces completely blank. They are wearing last night's clothes. The camera is chest-height, dead-level, side-on, wide — the framing of a nature documentary about a migration. Nothing in the frame is posed; this is the one image where they have forgotten anyone is watching, and it is the least heroic they will ever look, which is the point.

**The bros (each pose and expression)**
- **Pilsner (front of the column, lead).** Navy tee ridden up over the gut, cargo shorts, white crew socks, black flip-flops — full look-B, load-bearing. He has tucked a paper bag with a döner in it under one arm like a baguette. His flip-flop walk is mid-shuffle. His face is a man who believes the night was a victory and is conserving himself for the retelling.
- **Chadwick (middle).** Backward trucker hat over bed-head, popped collar, chain wallet, phone in hand. He is not walking and filming — he is standing still every few seconds to review the footage, then hustling to catch up. His face: squinting at the phone, slightly appalled, never once transferring the appal to himself.
- **Bradley (rear, dragging).** Basketball jersey, baggy jeans, Livestrong band, pager clipped, broken pool cue used as a walking stick, point-down, in the canal-side cobbles. He is three steps behind and losing ground. Livestrong is doing the most ironic work in the frame. His face is not pain, because he has metabolized it: it is a man who thinks this is what the day after winning feels like.

**The setting.** A canal path between the hostel and the last bar, in whichever city the player loaded from — the diorama cycles (Doner Lane service alley in Publandia; Clocktower Plaza outer ring in Debaucheryville; a Sinfonia footbridge with the Bassline Opera House dome behind; The Skewer Mile in Shamsterdam, shutters half-up). Parody signage only. Morning details: street cleaners working around the bros, a first kebab shutter coming up, a delivery cyclist overtaking them while looking at them, laundry lines with one item missing from every line. The world is fresh and bright and beginning its day; the bros are the residue of the previous one.

**Palette.** Clean morning light — pale gold, dusty blue sky, soft greys of wet stone. The bros are slightly desaturated against the fresh world: the picture's own render is treating them like last night's litter. The wordmark picks up the morning gold.

**Title treatment.** The EUROBOUND wordmark small, top-left, chrome slab on a soft dark plate, bottle-cap O catching the pale light. It sits like a timestamp on a security photo.

**What moves.** Idle loop, 4 seconds: the shuffle continues. Pilsner's flip-flops slap at a metronome pace. Chadwick stops, reviews, hustles, falls back into line. Bradley's pool cue stick taps the cobbles on every other step, and he drops one flip-flop step behind on each loop, so over a long load the column stretches. Street cleaner's broom swishes on a loop. No camera moves — the documentary holds still; the migration does not. Reduced-motion toggle: the loop freezes to a single held frame with a subtle 2-frame bob; the drop-back gag becomes a one-time static gap. All motion bounded, nothing strobing.

**The load bar.** Bottom edge, full width, thin and plain: a flat amber bar that maps 1:1 to real load progress. It is deliberately the most boring object on screen. The joke is everywhere else.

**Where the tip sits.** Dead centre-bottom, above the bar, in a plain JRPG dialogue box with no name plate — as if the game itself is the only one willing to speak to them this morning. The tip pool cycles. The box never overlaps the wordmark.

**Tip pool (real info, one vicious line each; shares Mistral's format so the two files can merge pools later):**
1. `TIP: The party is three bros. The queue for the bathroom is also three bros. Do the math on who lost.`
2. `TIP: Bravado is currency. Like all currency, it converts badly at 7 a.m.`
3. `TIP: Hungover halves your opening damage. So does carrying a döner you have no plan for.`
4. `TIP: Side quests are optional. Consequences are not.`
5. `TIP: NPCs see through the bros instantly. You have been watching for three cities.`
6. `TIP: The pager has one function left: finding itself in a drawer.`
7. `TIP: Beer Goggles expire. The photos don't.`
8. `TIP: A smart player steers them around the wall. A brave one aims them at it.`
9. `TIP: Every city has a last call. It is on time.`
10. `TIP: The canal sees everything and tells the heron nothing.` *(swap line pending the animal ruling — replace with:* `TIP: The canal sees everything and tells the traffic cone nothing.`*)*

**The one joke it lands.** The game's own save-and-restore ritual is the morning after: every load IS the walk of shame, taken again. The bros are not heading into the day — they are being reloaded into it, exactly as they were left, and they call this winning. The player, who chose to hit continue, is doing the same thing they are.

**Repetition system.** City diorama cycles (4). Tip pool cycles. Chadwick's footage-review face changes (he winces, shrugs, nods, grimaces — never learns). Pilsner's döner bag gets progressively limper over the loop variants. Bradley's gap grows. The clock on a distant tower reads a different too-early time each load (07:52 / 08:14 / 08:39). Poses and formation constant — that is what makes the variants land.

**Glance read (<2 s).** Three figures, single file, morning light, wordmark. Reads instantly as "they're still going" — and at thumbnail/first-glance the player has already filed them as the survivors of something they did to themselves.

**Production notes.** Same pipeline as the covers (3D characters in painterly dioramas, tilt-shift). Logo inside the top 15% vertically, dialogue box and bar inside the bottom 25%, no overlap at 1280×720. No animals pending the ruling, no mascots, no fourth figure — every passerby is a disinterested adult going to work.
