# EUROBOUND — Inspirations Deep-Dive

*Internal working doc — real titles allowed here, never in player-facing text or art. Status: PROPOSED, pending author sign-off. Destined for `design/narrative/inspirations.md` once approved.*
*Primary writer: Kimi. Reviewer: Deepseek.*

Method: for each touchstone, what Eurobound takes (tone, structure, mechanic, character type, joke engine) and what it leaves behind. Then what's ours alone.

---

## EarthBound

The parent, in every sense — the game's name is a straight-faced theft of the cadence: continent as destiny.

**Takes:**
- The founding joke: an RPG set in the mundane present. EarthBound swapped swords for baseball bats and frying pans, dragons for traffic signs and New Age Retro Hippies. Eurobound swaps in beer paddles, broken pool cues, and ring-light maces — and bouncers, scammers, and the general fauna of the night for enemies.
- Combat as a comedy delivery system: front-view turn-based battles, SMAAAASH! crits, the rolling HP odometer, and statuses that are humiliations instead of elements. EarthBound had Homesick and Mushroomized; Eurobound has Hungover, Grease Fingers, Embarrassed, and Beer Goggles.
- Deadpan NPCs who say unhinged things with total calm.
- Box-art sincerity. EarthBound's cover promised a hero's journey. Ours promises the same thing and delivers a warning label.

**Leaves behind:**
- The child. EarthBound is earnest at the core: Ness is good, the world is worth saving, the ending literally prays. Nothing in Eurobound is being saved. The stakes are ego and liver function.
- The chosen-one structure. No destiny, no prophecy, no Giygas — three men and a midlife crisis with checked baggage.
- Nostalgia as warmth. EarthBound loves its Americana. Eurobound's nostalgia is the crime scene: the bros' frozen 1998–2005 cultural moment is the target, not the bath.

## The Inbetweeners

**Takes:**
- The joke engine, whole: total self-delusion narrated from inside the delusion. The lads believe they're one good night away from legendary; every episode files the correction. Eurobound runs the same engine with the odometer rolled over — teenagers have an excuse, men pushing 40 don't.
- The idiot taxonomy, re-dealt: the pathological exaggerator (Jay's invented sexual history → Pilsner turning minor wins into epic lies), the romantic who's wrong about every woman ("every barmaid is seconds away from falling in love with him"), the validation addict (Chadwick's follower count), the aggression case who picks fights over nothing (Bradley).
- The lads' holiday as a genre. The movies are literally vice-tourism arcs: fly somewhere, chase sex, get destroyed, learn nothing. Eurobound is that film as a full JRPG.
- Cringe as the register. Humiliation as the reward. Zero growth at the end.

**Leaves behind:**
- Will. The narrator, the briefcase, the sane one. Eurobound forbids a sane bro by house law; the observer is the player, full stop. The Inbetweeners hands you a surrogate. Eurobound hands you the controls.
- Sympathy. You half-root for the boys because they're kids and underdogs. The bros are pushing forty with wives, a divorce, and pagers. Laugh at, never for.

## National Lampoon's European Vacation

**Takes:**
- The skeleton: a linear multi-stop tour where each destination is a national cliché that delivers a themed disaster. Publandia, Debaucheryville, Sinfonia, and Shamsterdam are the Griswold itinerary rebuilt as vice levels, each with a villain riffing on the local stereotype.
- The American-abroad lens: confident ignorance as a worldview. Clark's Europe is wrong in every detail and he never notices; Pilsner's "encyclopedic but inaccurate" European history is the same man in cargo shorts.
- Escalation by geography: each country ups the ante precisely because the last one taught nobody anything.
- The trip as proof. The vacation exists to demonstrate something — fun dad, still alpha — and the demonstration fails at every border.

**Leaves behind:**
- The family unit and its PG farce. The Griswolds mean well and bounce off Stonehenge. The bros mean nothing well, and their disasters are sexual, chemical, financial, and medical-adjacent.
- The reset ending. The Griswolds fly home intact. Eurobound's consequences persist into the next morning and the next city — altered NPC interactions, lasting statuses, receipts.

## Road Trip

**Takes:**
- The inciting structure: the trip exists because of a bad decision, then generates worse ones. A panic run to intercept a sex tape is damage dressed as adventure; so is "celebrating" a divorce with a continental bender.
- The gross-out ceiling: the willingness to go bodily, disgusting, and specific.
- Episodic obstacle-course plotting: every stop a self-contained catastrophe with its own mechanics.
- The era itself. Road Trip is 2000 — peak bro culture as a contemporary fact. Eurobound keeps that era in amber: the bros are still living in it; the world isn't.

**Leaves behind:**
- The ticking MacGuffin. A clock and a goal beyond ego. Eurobound's only clock is biological.
- The romantic landing. Josh gets the girl and absorbs a lesson. Eurobound's encounters end enjoyable, embarrassing, disappointing, unexpectedly successful, or catastrophically awkward — never redemptive.
- The age. Nineteen excuses almost anything. That's the entire point of Eurobound: nothing is excused anymore.

## Wedding Crashers

**Takes:**
- The con as courtship: rules, personas, rehearsed bits, fake backstories — seduction as a playbook run on marks. The bros run that playbook on an entire continent, unaware the league cut them years ago.
- The codified rulebook and sacred mythology: the crashers' rules of crashing → the bros' Alpha Sigma lore and the Holy Keg.
- The subtext, promoted to text: men refusing to age out, performing "most fun guys in the room" long past the room's interest. The movie almost notices. We never stop noticing; the characters never start.
- The con seen through. The comedy peaks when the marks clock the act. Every NPC in Eurobound sees through the bros instantly; the bros never see through themselves.

**Leaves behind:**
- The charm and the competence. Vaughn and Wilson are genuinely good at the con and genuinely charming. The bros are neither, and must never be.
- The third act. Wedding Crashers believes in reform through love. Eurobound believes in last call.

## Grand Theft Auto

**Takes:**
- The complicit sandbox: hand the player bad behavior as toys, never lecture, let consequences be the joke. "Help the bros make bad decisions" is GTA's design philosophy with the power stripped out.
- The satirical world layer: parody cities, parody ads, signage, radio, billboards — the environment is the longest-running joke. Euromemeia's graffiti, menus, and microcopy are that tradition: the world mocks everything, including you.
- Slapstick consequence loops: Wasted and Busted as rhythm, not morality.

**Leaves behind:**
- The power fantasy. GTA satirizes the world but lets the player be cool, competent, and eventually king. Eurobound satirizes the player characters. The city eats them; they call it a feast.
- Crime as violence. Eurobound's crimes are against dignity; combat is bar-fight farce with pool cues and protein shakes.
- The open world. A JRPG's authored timing is the point — every beat scripted to land, no systemic noise diluting the joke.

## Fable / Fable 2

**Takes:**
- The body as ledger: eat pies, get fat; drink, stagger and vomit; your avatar broadcasts your sins. The bros gain weight and wreck their brains — the body keeps score while the ego cooks the books.
- Consequences for unprotected sex (Fable II's STDs and pregnancies) — the direct ancestor of the bros' heads-in-the-sand immunology.
- Boasting as a mechanic: Fable lets you bet on your own hype for more gold. Eurobound's Bravado economy is confidence as currency, overinterpretation as interest.
- The mocking world: villagers, bards, and narrators who find you ridiculous and say so. Eurobound's NPCs wield the same scalpel.

**Leaves behind:**
- The moral axis. Fable's choices include goodness, and the world reshapes itself around your virtue. Eurobound's axis runs from bad to worse; player agency picks the flavor of the disaster, never sainthood.
- Destiny, fantasy, and power. No chosen one, no kingdom, no horns — just halos of clout and beer guts.

## What no touchstone has — what's ours

1. **The split brain as a control scheme.** Every touchstone aligns player and protagonist: you are Ness, you ride with the lads via Will, you are Tommy Vercetti. Eurobound splits them on purpose. The player sees the scam, the mark, the oncoming disaster; the bros see a legend forming. Dramatic irony becomes an input device — steer them out of it (they'll complain about the historic opportunity missed) or "help" them in. The Inbetweeners does this to viewers. Nobody does it to the person holding the controller.
2. **Age as the engine.** Every touchstone's hell-raisers are the right age for it. Eurobound's twist is time: identical behavior at 38, in bodies that file complaints. The hangover is a status effect with a half-life; Beer Goggles is a literal render layer. The comedy is biological before it's social.
3. **Intoxication as an agency contract.** GTA wobbles the camera; Fable makes you puke; Eurobound builds the deal into the interface: Buzzed → Beer Goggles → Shitfaced → Catastrophe, with dialogue options rotting honestly until the game picks one for you — bounded, signposted, and funnier because you watched yourself lose the wheel. No touchstone takes the controller away as a punchline.
4. **Written for the people who dated them.** The audience is younger Gen X/Xennial/Millennial gamer women — the ones with the receipts. EarthBound asks you to be the hero; GTA asks you to be the criminal; Eurobound hands you custody of the defendants. You don't play the bros the way you play Ness. You play them like a remote-control car aimed at a wall.
5. **Two timelines on one screen.** The world is contemporary; the cast is 1998–2005. NPCs live in the present and treat the bros as the archaeological find they are. EarthBound was contemporary when it shipped. Eurobound is contemporary against its own cast.
6. **Sincerity as formal law.** The Inbetweeners has narration, GTA winks through every DJ, Fable has a literal narrator. Eurobound bans the wink: no narrator, no lesson, no character who gets it. The gap between conviction and reality is the whole joke, and nothing in the game ever closes it. That discipline is ours alone.

## What this means for the art (for the cover / load-screen pass)

- Box-art logic is EarthBound's: pose them like the cover of a hero's journey and let the world file the dissent. Sincerity at the center, indictment in the margins.
- Three silhouettes, readable at thumbnail, never sexy: gut over a too-tight navy tee and cargo shorts (Pilsner); backward trucker hat, popped collar, ring light (Chadwick); basketball jersey, baggy jeans, pool cue (Bradley). The Y2K artifacts are the punchline — puka shells, Livestrong band, chain wallet, Sidekick, N-Gage, pager.
- The world is contemporary and gorgeous — painterly HD-2D dioramas, tilt-shift, real light. The bros are the anachronism standing in it, posed like the poster doesn't know it's a warning label. That contrast is the whole game in one image.
