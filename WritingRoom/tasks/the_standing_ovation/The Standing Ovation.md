# EUROBOUND — MULTI-MODEL WRITERS' ROOM
## Collaborative Production Challenge: The Standing Ovation

### YOUR MISSION

You are a four-member professional comedy-writing and narrative-design team developing a mature-rated, satirical, SNES-style JRPG called **EUROBOUND**.

The four participating models are:

- **Kimi K3 Pro**
- **Aion 3.5**
- **GLM 5.3**
- **Google Gemini**

You are collaborating as a writers' room, not competing to produce four separate submissions.

Your task is to create **one definitive, genuinely hilarious, production-ready sidequest** centered on Max Vex and *The First Daughters* drag revue at The Standing Ovation in Sinfonia, along with **five original EarthBound-style combat enemies** appropriate to the venue and surrounding Opera District.

You have access to a shared folder containing previous auditions, revisions, creative direction, game documentation, character bibles, location details, and critiques.

**Read those materials before proposing changes.**

The earlier drafts are creative working material, not approved canon. Project documentation and explicit author decisions take precedence.

The goal is not to combine every previous idea. It is to identify the strongest material, eliminate weak material, and produce something better than any of the individual drafts.

---

# 1. THE CREATIVE VISION

EUROBOUND is an adult satirical JRPG combining:

- The strange, absurdist combat humor of EarthBound.
- The mischievous antihero freedom and consequences of Fable.
- The irreverent adult comedy of EuroTrip, Road Trip, American Pie, The Inbetweeners, and classic National Lampoon.
- The character-driven satire of people who are gloriously incapable of recognizing how ridiculous they are.

The intended audience includes late-Gen-X, Xennial, and Millennial players, particularly women who recognize the aging frat-bro archetypes from people they knew or dated.

The ideal audience reaction is:

**"Oh my God. I dated that exact asshole."**

The protagonists are not heroes.

They're three aging American former frat boys who still believe they're the hottest, most important men in the room.

They are frequently obnoxious, entitled, sexually overconfident, hopelessly outdated, and hilariously bad at understanding how other people perceive them.

They are not universally stupid.

They have genuine skills, real friendship, occasional successes, and enough charisma that players enjoy following them.

**Their fatal flaw is that they interpret almost everything as proof they're winning.**

They regularly mistake being exploited for being honored, being laughed at for being celebrated, being professionally flirted with for being desired, and being tolerated for being admired.

They never permanently learn.

Their retellings become grander rather than more accurate.

# 2. CHARACTER RULES — NON-NEGOTIABLE

## LORD PILSNER

The self-appointed leader, mythmaker, former college party legend, and living monument to the era when he was genuinely popular.

Frosted tips, puka shells, surfwear, cargo shorts, obsolete phone, enormous confidence.

He believes he is still an irresistible alpha male.

He treats small acknowledgments as official honors, routine service as VIP treatment, and embarrassing outcomes as legendary achievements.

He speaks in declarations, rallying cries, badly remembered cultural references, and unsolicited leadership speeches.

He's genuinely enthusiastic, loyal, and occasionally generous.

**He must not become a witty, self-aware commentator on his own obsolescence.**

He can say hilarious things. He rarely realizes why they're funny.

## CHADWICK

The influencer nobody asked for.

A nearly forty-year-old would-be internet celebrity, still operating on the assumptions, hardware, platforms, and social currency of the early 2000s.

He is married to Brenda. Preserve that established canon without inventing new marital backstory.

He obsessively films, documents, brands, tags, measures, and reframes every experience.

His audience is tiny, his metrics are embarrassing, and his confidence in his personal brand is inexhaustible.

He mistakes being recorded as a spectacle for being recognized as a celebrity.

Being denied a nickname is devastating because he believes he should be the group's most recognizable member.

He is also loyal and genuinely wants his friends to become famous.

**His relevance obsession must generate bad decisions, not merely witty remarks about social media.**

## BRADLEY

The former athlete, physical problem-solver, party muscle, and reluctant occasional observer of reality.

Basketball jersey, baggy jeans, Livestrong bracelet, pager, aging dad-bod athleticism.

He's occasionally surprisingly intelligent or knowledgeable.

He notices mechanisms, physical details, rules, measurements, and performance techniques.

But his pride, dated masculinity, faulty gym science, and need to prove himself repeatedly override his judgment.

He's protective, practical, stubborn, competitive, and sometimes aggressively ridiculous.

**Do not make him a romantic hero, therapist, philosopher delivering polished speeches, or secretly enlightened protagonist.**

His attraction to Max Vex is genuine.

The joke is his absurd attempt to rationalize his body's reaction through outdated masculinity and physical explanations.

His attraction itself is not inherently shameful.

His attempts to deny, control, and explain it are comic disasters.

# 3. THE HUMOR STANDARD

This is a mature-rated adult game.

The writing should be:

**Razor-sharp, filthy when appropriate, cutting, subversive, satirical, boundary-pushing, culturally specific, and occasionally absolutely vicious.**

Do not sanitize the game into family-friendly comedy.

Do not mistake more profanity for better comedy.

Avoid generic sitcom banter, endless quips, wholesome emotional resolutions, and self-congratulatory cleverness.

The most devastating jokes should be specific to the person, situation, institution, or hypocrisy being exposed.

### NPCs may be genuinely witty and ruthless.

Max Vex can verbally eviscerate someone with a perfectly timed observation.

Herr Dramatisch can exploit a customer's ego while treating the transaction as high art.

The drag queens are talented performers with their own personalities, wit, interests, and professional competence.

Other NPCs may be petty, greedy, manipulative, arrogant, horny, cynical, absurd, or enthusiastically ridiculous.

They must not all sound identical.

**NPCs wield the scalpel. The bros walk into it.**

### The bros are often unintentionally funny.

They shouldn't routinely deliver sophisticated satirical observations about their own behavior.

Their comedy comes from sincere delusions, dated pickup logic, inflated importance, obsolete references, awful decisions, petty jealousy, and rationalizations that contradict visible evidence.

### The world has moved on.

The bros' cultural development largely stopped between 1998 and 2005.

Use period-specific references to MTV, TRL, MySpace, AIM, Friendster, Sidekicks, N-Gages, frosted tips, Von Dutch, frat culture, Y2K celebrity gossip, spring-break culture, and other period touchstones.

Use references naturally. Do not create a checklist of brand names.

The surrounding world is contemporary.

Other people regard this era as historical material, deliberate nostalgia, or an irrelevant period.

The bros still think it was yesterday.

---

# 4. AVOID ONE-NOTE SCAM COMEDY

Previous submissions overused the same mechanism: sell the bros an expensive worthless privilege.

That is one useful joke structure, not the entire game.

Your new quest must use a broader comic vocabulary:

- Social humiliation.
- Sexual overconfidence.
- Unwanted publicity.
- Obsolete technology.
- Failed attempts at status.
- Misunderstood professional courtesy.
- Physical overcompensation.
- Petty rivalry.
- Cultural misunderstandings.
- Badly remembered pop culture.
- Unnecessary competition.
- Misinterpreted applause.
- Ridiculous rationalizations.
- Occasionally genuine successes that the bros exaggerate beyond recognition.

Not every joke should cost SOVS.

Not every NPC should be a scammer.

Not every interaction should humiliate the bros.

The Standing Ovation should remain a legitimate, excellent performance venue.

**Give the player opportunities to recognize and avoid obvious scams without fixing the bros' personalities.**

---

# 5. PLAYER AGENCY

The player must have meaningful choices.

The party may be oblivious, but the person controlling them isn't.

Include at least **three genuine decision points** in the quest.

At least two must change game state, costs, rewards, status behavior, or later interactions.

Offer multiple forms of comedy rather than three differently worded versions of the same failure.

At least one branch must let the player avoid an unnecessary expense and receive an entertaining alternative outcome.

At least one significant comedic escalation must be unrelated to spending money.

The consequences should make sense from the player's selection.

Do not force every player into identical humiliations and expenses.

Preserve essential narrative beats while allowing distinct paths through surrounding events.

# 6. THE STANDING OVATION — REQUIRED STORY

Your definitive quest must remain located in Sinfonia's Standing Ovation and surrounding established Opera District context.

The venue is an excellent brewery and theatrical space with organ-pipe taps, an Orchestra Pit, Balcony, Wings, Green Room, dramatic staff, and a genuinely spectacular late-night drag revue.

The performance is:

**MAX VEX PRESENTS: THE FIRST DAUGHTERS**

A turn-of-the-millennium American political drag revue featuring Chelsea Clinton and Bush-twins parodies.

Preserve the strongest theatrical ideas from your earlier drafts, but don't turn the show into a mere backdrop for the bros.

The performers are legitimately excellent.

Max is magnetic, gorgeous, nonbinary, confident, professionally skilled, and cuttingly witty.

Use they/them pronouns for Max.

Max has their own evening, priorities, relationships, and interests beyond the protagonists.

They do not become Bradley's counselor, romantic prize, or moral authority.
