# Sinfonia Setting Bible

**Purpose:** Canon-facing reference for writing Sinfonia consistently across quests, locations, encounters, ambient dialogue, items, and future design.

**Scope:** This file consolidates the strongest recurring material already present in the project. It does not authorize a new main plot, a city boss, a compulsory quest chain, or new sexual content. Where older files conflict with current author rulings, the current rulings win.

**Player-facing naming rule:** The city is **Sinfonia**. Real city, country, dynasty, language, and nationality names belong only in internal research notes and must not appear in shipping copy.

---

## 1. The City in One Sentence

**Sinfonia is a city where appetite puts on evening wear, receives a certificate of authenticity, and sends the customer an accurate itemized bill.**

The city does not make vice respectable by removing the vice. It makes vice respectable through presentation: velvet ropes, correct glassware, dress codes, provenance cards, professional titles, cultural vocabulary, and staff who are very good at their jobs. The transaction is usually visible. The price is usually stated. The bros participate enthusiastically because the ceremony makes an ordinary appetite feel like status.

The central Sinfonian rule is already stated most clearly in the city’s couchsurfing material:

> **Everything Has a Price and the Price Is Correct.**

That rule should govern far more than money. Entry has a dress requirement. A favor creates an invoice. Gossip requires another secret. An evening of drinking produces a real hangover. A careless horse ride creates real property damage. Access can be purchased, performed, inherited, earned, or stolen, but never treated as weightless.

---

## 2. The Essential Contrast

In Debaucheryville, the bros are **let in**. In Sinfonia, they are **received**.

Being received does not mean being welcomed as equals. It means someone has anticipated their arrival, decided how to classify them, and prepared the correct experience at the correct price. Sinfonian professionals rarely need to lie. They let the bros misunderstand an honest statement in the most flattering possible way.

| Other-city expectation | Sinfonian expression |
|---|---|
| A dive bar | A house with a doctrine of correct tempo |
| A pub crawl | A cultural passport with ritual stamps |
| A nightclub | A licensed after-hours institution beneath a monument to genius |
| A casino | A private room whose losses arrive in silver chips |
| A hookup | An invitation issued by a formidable adult with a schedule and an invoice |
| A tourist trap | A correctly delivered service whose “authenticity” costs extra |
| A scam | An explicit premium the bros insist must indicate quality |
| A hangover | An “Imperial Concerto” with named movements and repeat customers |
| Gossip | A controlled social currency exchanged behind masks |
| Cheap collectible | The same object with wall text, provenance, and a higher perceived valuation |

Sinfonia is therefore not a city of fools preying on innocent tourists. Its operators are generally competent, and the bros are not innocent. The comedy comes from three aging men actively purchasing the version of themselves that the city has packaged for them.

## 4. Governing Themes

### 4.1 Vice in evening wear

Sinfonia’s high and low appetites are not opposites. The same desire crosses the city in different clothes. Opera becomes an after-hours club. Respectable card play becomes expensive gambling. Connoisseurship becomes shopping. Tradition becomes a surcharge. Intellectual life becomes competitive performance. The city’s joke is not that refinement is fake; much of the refinement is real. The joke is that refinement does not abolish greed, lust, drunkenness, vanity, predation, loneliness, or bad taste.

### 4.2 Competence is funnier than fraud

Sinfonian beer can be excellent. The singers can be extraordinary. The clothes can fit perfectly. The café can serve a genuinely excellent torte. The house edge, service charge, and social hierarchy remain intact.

Never make every operator a grifter. A city of obvious frauds is flat and makes the bros passive victims. A city of professionals who can deliver exactly what was purchased—and nothing the bros merely imagined—creates the intended satire.

### 4.3 The price is part of the seduction

The bros often interpret expense as proof of rank. A higher price can make a service more desirable because it suggests that cheaper men were excluded. Sinfonia exploits this without needing to falsify the bill.

Consequences must remain tangible:

- money is actually gone;
- collateral is actually posted;
- damage is actually owed;
- reputation changes how staff receive them;
- a hangover affects play;
- a secret can circulate;
- clothing opens a door but does not create breeding;
- a collectible remains worthless merely because it was acquired beautifully.

### 4.4 Everyone knows what room they are in

Hosts, dealers, doormen, divas, bartenders, café staff, and veteran socialites are not fooled by the bros. They generally do not announce that fact. They classify the men, deliver the experience, and move on.

The bros, by contrast, tell themselves that they have penetrated a hidden layer of the city. Often that “secret” layer is a licensed business with published hours and a staff rota.

### 4.5 Weary social knowledge, not revolutionary fervor

Sinfonia knows its institutions are absurd. Locals can express that knowledge through a tired look, a practiced form, a one-line song, or a flawlessly obeyed useless custom. The royal jingles are affectionate fatalism, not calls to revolt. Decree 402-B exists; no guard enforces it; nobody pauses the scene to explain why.

## 5. Geography and Social Topography

Sinfonia should feel navigable as a set of overlapping social circuits rather than a single linear quest corridor.

### Imperial Square and the Opera House Plaza

The monumental front door of the city: baroque façades, horses, café terraces, ticket sellers, formal entrances, tourists, scalpers, and etiquette checks. This is where culture becomes a visible public commodity. By day the plaza sells access to tradition. By evening it sorts ticket holders, guests, workers, and hopeful intruders. At night its respectable façades become portals to private rooms and after-hours lives.

Primary locations and hooks:

- The Bassline Opera House
- The Black-Tie Backroom entrance routes
- Café Sachertorte
- Fiaker rides and the Fiaker Fiasco
- Scalpers, photographers, formalwear checks, and tourist surcharges

### MusikGasse

Public performance territory: buskers, instruments, competitive rhythm, applause, humiliation, and musical gatekeeping. Skill can earn respect; confidence without skill produces a more expensive kind of attention.

Primary uses:

- street performers and musical challenges;
- The Composer’s Cart;
- route between public culture and nightlife;
- ambient royal jingles;
- performance as social combat.

### Kaffeehaus Row

The city’s intellectual and consumable interior. Coffee, pastry, provenance, gossip, philosophy, and the conviction that sitting correctly constitutes civilization. This district should feel unhurried until a bro violates an unwritten rule, at which point every professional becomes terrifyingly efficient.

Primary locations:

- Café Existenz
- Grounds for Concern
- Torte Royale
- The Connoisseur’s Cache
- clues and gossip leading toward private venues

### Formal retail and residential interiors

Sinfonia’s most intimate pressure often happens under flattering light: fitting rooms, salons, chandeliers, guest rooms, private apartments, and masked halls. These spaces classify bodies and status without raising their voices.

Primary locations:

- Haute Couture Haberdashery
- The Baroness Ballroom
- The Gilded Suite
- couchsurf hosts’ apartments and backstage spaces

### Underground and after-hours Sinfonia

Crypts, backstage corridors, cellars, private boxes, and rooms behind rooms. “Underground” here does not necessarily mean improvised or illegal. It can be a highly organized business whose clientele wants the feeling of discovery.

Primary locations:

- Mozart’s Last Rave
- The Black-Tie Backroom
- the Bassline Opera House after-hours configuration
- future Golden Cutlet basement material, once authored

### Stadtpark and Gustav’s refuge

A necessary breathing space. The park provides contrast to the city’s interiors, transactions, and performances. It can hold genuine quiet, quest resolution, and the single visual payoff to the Klaus naming joke. Do not turn every square metre of Sinfonia into a sales floor.

## 6. The City’s Daily Rhythm

### Morning

- deliveries, cleaning, rehearsals, account settling, and the aftermath of other people’s nights;
- café regulars reclaim their tables;
- bros wake under professional observation, occasionally wearing evidence nobody explains;
- invoices arrive with immaculate arithmetic.

### Afternoon

- public culture, shopping, coffee, pastry, carriage rides, tours, fittings, and status preparation;
- the bros acquire clothes, passes, stories, and debts they believe will improve the evening;
- the city is brightest and most openly commercial.

### Evening

- thresholds harden: tickets, reservations, attire, names, reputation, and collateral matter;
- high culture is performed sincerely while side doors begin to open;
- gossip becomes liquid and social standing becomes playable.

### Late night

- opera architecture houses bass, gambling, hookups, drinking rituals, and reputational damage;
- staff remain more composed than the customers;
- the bros mistake endurance for belonging.

### Next morning

- Sinfonia presents consequences without commentary;
- the bill, status effect, missing SOVS, clip with eleven views, or new rumor is enough;
- the bros retell the night as a triumph and begin shopping for the next threshold.

## 7. Established Vice Taxonomy

The following vices are already supported by Sinfonia’s locations, quests, or systems. They are not all criminal, and that distinction matters. The city’s spectrum runs from ordinary appetite to private corruption, with the same etiquette applied across it.

### 7.1 Respectable gambling

**Core location:** The Black-Tie Backroom.

Poker, roulette, blackjack, card sharks, house advantage, collateral, controlled cheating, and the psychology of being admitted. The Backroom’s central proposition is that gambling becomes “respectable” under warm light with a string quartet nearby. The wager is not fake. The loss is not metaphorical. Aaron’s fixed 500 SOVS event is funny because the room never breaks decorum while the party transfers real value.

Writing rules:

- keep the room calm when the bros are loud;
- give actual tables and beatable opponents their own clear mechanics;
- never have staff announce the house’s thesis;
- do not turn Aaron into a con man or Herr Falsch into the entire city’s mastermind;
- the Backroom is a functioning gambling venue, not a cutscene pretending to be one.

### 7.2 Ritualized drinking and cultivated excess

**Core locations:** The Moderato Pub, The Standing Ovation, Crown & Cask, The Dancing Stein; opera-diva nightcap material.

Sinfonia drinks through performance: applause, rhythm, monarchy, choreography, proper service, old recipes, and the need to complete a set. A drink is often both real craft and social test. The four-brewery route trends performance/social rather than raw volume, but exact unauthored brewery challenges remain open.

The “Imperial Concerto” hangover expresses the city perfectly: physiological misery arranged as culture. It should be real enough to affect play and formal enough that the bros regard it as an achievement.

### 7.3 Nightclubs, after-hours institutions, and death tourism

**Core locations:** The Bassline Opera House at night; Mozart’s Last Rave.

The Bassline Opera House shifts from public monument to electronic nightlife without ceasing to be gatekept. Mozart’s Last Rave packages composer-grave tourism, crypt atmosphere, loud music, and supposed secrecy into a long-running licensed business.

Writing rules:

- nightlife is energetic and desirable to the bros, not a horror sequence;
- ambiguity about ghosts, performers, marketing, and exhausted patrons can remain unresolved;
- do not establish a specific drug mechanic merely because an old tagline mentions one;
- the staff’s organization should undercut the bros’ belief that they discovered an outlaw world.

### 7.4 Luxury consumption and status retail

**Core locations:** Haute Couture Haberdashery; The Connoisseur’s Cache; The Composer’s Cart.

Sinfonian retail sells classification. The Haberdashery’s clothes are excellent, but its protocol makes customers feel that their existing bodies and histories are inadequate. The Connoisseur’s Cache turns ordinary useful goods into curated objects through provenance. The Composer’s Cart sells music and the pain of being judged by someone who cares more than the customer does.

The Gold Circle Coin is housed in the Haberdashery and may be acquired through its authored routes. It suppresses the Herp while equipped by any bro; it does not cure it.

### 7.5 Tourism, “authenticity,” and elective overcharging

**Core locations:** Opera House Plaza; Café Sachertorte; Fiaker route; Torte Royale; future Golden Cutlet.

The city can charge for the base service, the preferred view, the correct pronunciation, the certificate, the heritage tier, the photograph, and the feeling of having avoided the tourist version. These are not automatically scams. The surcharge may be printed. The experience may be delivered. The bros often request the premium because they cannot bear to be ordinary tourists.

The Fiaker Fiasco demonstrates the harder edge: damage and debt accumulate as real consequences, even while the men narrate the day as cultural immersion.

### 7.6 Intellectual vanity and cultural gatekeeping

**Core locations:** Café Existenz; MusikGasse; opera-house public spaces; Grounds for Concern.

People use culture as craft, identity, status, armor, and weapon. Café debate can drain Bravado like combat. Musical knowledge can open a conversation or end one. Espresso purity becomes an ethical system. These scenes work when the gatekeeper actually knows the subject and the bros know just enough to be dangerous.

Avoid making every intellectual secretly stupid. The satire targets performative hierarchy and the bros’ vanity, not learning itself.

### 7.7 Gossip, masks, reputation, and blackmail

**Core location:** The Baroness Ballroom.

The ballroom is a social market in which everybody performs identity while knowing that the masks conceal less than their wearers imagine. Secrets have exchange value. A rumor can damage access. A title may be inherited, assumed, or institutional rather than a simple legal identity. The Baroness should not be casually reduced to an exposition machine; her uncertainty is part of the venue’s power.

### 7.8 Aristocratic cosplay and borrowed authority

**Core location:** Crown & Cask; ambient royal-jingle system.

König Klaus is a genuinely excellent brewer in a fake crown whose act and belief have fused. He is not pathetic, and the joke is not that everyone humors a harmless fool. The bros believe *they* are humoring *him*, while willingly entering his rules and buying his beer.

Royal critique remains ambient, weary, and oblique. The four authored jingles never name Klaus. The public decree names him but is ignored. The horse-stall payoff must not be explained.

### 7.9 Art markets, auctions, forgery, and collectible speculation

**Core routes:** hush-hush auction catalogue; Bassline backstage terminal; Grounds for Concern filter; Aaron’s table.

Sinfonia’s NFT leg makes cheap or ugly objects feel considered by adding wall text, controlled access, and a fine-art acquisition ritual. The people handing them over need not lie about value. The bros supply the valuation themselves.

The first three Sinfonia NFT artifacts still contain author-write slots. Preserve their acquisition routes and do not write final wall text or replace the approved roster without a ruling.

### 7.10 Sexual pursuit, hospitality, and the professional morning after

**Core material:** Sinfonia Couchsurfing Chronicles; opera divas; career understudy; Helga; Eternal Student; Lady Geheimnis.

Sexual pursuit belongs in the city, but its tone is adult competence rather than conquest-fantasy validation. Sinfonia’s hosts are accomplished adults with jobs, histories, preferences, and control of their own spaces. They are not fooled by the bros and do not need to humiliate themselves to prove it.

Use implication, cutaway, aftermath, costume, altered social state, and invoices. No nudity and no graphic sex. Refusal or deferral must not silently destroy unrelated rewards. Every relevant encounter must route correctly for absent, active, and suppressed Herp states.

The comic center is often the next morning: a precise charge, a lift, a ruined piece of furniture, a face of makeup left intact, or a standing invitation issued with unnerving professionalism.

### 7.11 Surveillance, invasive hospitality, and contractual intimacy

**Core location:** The Gilded Suite.

The Gilded Suite can use fake reviews, aggressive hospitality, observation, and absurd house rules. Write it as polished invasion and contractual pressure—not a survival-horror trap and not a lesson in stranger danger. The bros are actively seeking access and remain convinced the property is exclusive even as its systems classify and monitor them.

### 7.12 Theft, heists, and high-culture criminal fantasy

**Core routes:** Gold Circle Coin heist option; auction and art-market material.

The bros read museums, formal retailers, and backstage corridors as invitations to perform a heist movie. A theft route can exist, but it must respect actual item ownership, quest state, and consequence. Do not let a “heist” become a new compulsory main plot or make every Sinfonian institution secretly part of one criminal syndicate.

### 7.13 Coffee, pastry, food, and respectable compulsion

**Core locations:** Grounds for Concern; Café Sachertorte; Torte Royale; future Golden Cutlet.

Coffee, cake, and fried food are the daylight mirror of the city’s nightlife: ritual, expertise, excess, and status all operating around a consumable appetite. Jitters, gluttony, pronunciation, recipe claims, and authenticity premiums provide vice without requiring criminality.

### 7.14 Debt, billing, and consequence as social memory

Sinfonia remembers what is owed. Vendors can react to an outstanding Fiaker balance. The Backroom holds collateral. Hosts invoice damaged property. Premium shops notice spending tier. This memory makes the city feel coherent.

Never use debt only as a punchline and then reset it without authorization. If the number matters, designate one source of truth rather than duplicating it across dialogue, location, and item files.

## 9. How Sinfonia Treats the Bros

### Lord Pilsner

Sinfonia offers him the dangerous possibility that titles might be real if everybody uses them with sufficient ceremony. He responds to crowns, heraldry, formal address, heritage claims, and rooms that appear to recognize rank. The city should give him enough ritual validation to make his overreach plausible, then enforce a rule he never bothered to learn.

He is not frightened by powerful women, doormen, titled figures, or social rooms. He reads them as peers, rivals, or future members of his court.

### Chadwick

Sinfonia gives him endless proof that access can be filmed: velvet ropes, masks, backstage doors, secret menus, formalwear reveals, certificate moments, and curated collectibles. He interprets a controlled customer journey as exclusive discovery. His content often documents the transaction more accurately than his commentary does.

The city can value his camera as marketing, tolerate it as tourist behavior, or prohibit it as etiquette. It should never make him suddenly media-literate or embarrassed into stopping.

### Bradley

Sinfonia gives him systems to decipher: tiers, ratios, house rules, provenance, menu language, etiquette, and apparent arbitrage. He may correctly identify an individual mechanism and then use that insight to justify joining it. His pride turns partial comprehension into commitment.

He is not the scam-aware straight man. If he spots the surcharge, he explains why the premium package is still the rational choice.

### As a trio

The city separates them by aspiration and reunites them at the bill:

- Pilsner wants recognition.
- Chadwick wants evidence of access.
- Bradley wants mastery of the mechanism.
- Sinfonia can sell all three desires through the same doorway.

## 10. NPC and Institution Tone

### Professionals

Sinfonia’s strongest NPCs are at the top of a craft or have survived long enough to understand it completely: principal singers, career understudies, bartenders, dealers, tailors, doctoral lifers, doormen, drivers, café staff, and former social sovereigns.

Write them with:

- specific operational knowledge;
- efficient boundaries;
- practiced lines that still sound human;
- private motives beyond “teach the bros a lesson”;
- the ability to let silence perform the judgment.

### Gatekeepers

A gatekeeper should enforce a legible threshold: attire, reservation, collateral, reputation, skill, behavior, or invitation. Avoid arbitrary “no” scenes. The fun comes from how the bros pass, fail, purchase, perform, or circumvent a meaningful standard.

### Locals

Locals need not share one personality. They can be tired of tourists, economically dependent on them, proud of their craft, contemptuous of a monarch, addicted to applause, lonely beneath polish, or sincerely delighted by spectacle. What unites them is social fluency: they generally understand the room better than the bros do.

### Women

Sinfonia’s women must remain adult agents rather than rewards, scolds, or omniscient truth-tellers. They can desire the bros, use them, hire them, bill them, sleep with them, reject them, invite them back, beat them socially, or enjoy the story. Their competence should not require coldness, and sexual availability should not erase professional dignity.

### Criminal and quasi-criminal figures

Keep their reach bounded. A card shark controls a game, not the entire city. A rumor broker controls information within a circuit, not universal truth. A private auction can be shady without connecting every venue to a single conspiracy.

## 11. Scene Grammar

A reliable Sinfonia scene usually contains six beats:

1. **The visible threshold.** A sign, dress code, menu, title, rope, password, collateral amount, or etiquette expectation exists before the bros act.
2. **The professional.** Someone competent owns or administers the space.
3. **The self-flattering interpretation.** The bros explain why the threshold proves they belong.
4. **The voluntary commitment.** They pay, perform, wager, drink, sign, boast, sneak, or accept the invitation.
5. **The exact consequence.** The city delivers the service and the cost with equal precision.
6. **The triumphant retelling.** The bros preserve their self-image without learning a lesson.

Do not use all six as a rigid formula in every conversation. Use them as a diagnostic. If a scene feels like a generic scam or generic party, it is probably missing the threshold, the professional, or the exact consequence.

### Dialogue rhythm

- Let Sinfonian staff be shorter and more exact than the bros.
- Allow terminology to do social work: “collateral,” “assessment,” “supplement,” “heritage tier,” “nightcap,” “guest,” “client,” “patron.”
- Do not translate every custom for the audience.
- Let the bros fill silence with the wrong conclusion.
- Avoid a final NPC line that states what the scene meant.

### Stage direction

Use physical evidence instead of narration:

- the quartet continues playing;
- a receipt prints;
- a doorman straightens a borrowed lapel;
- nine of eleven views come from Chadwick’s own accounts;
- a server replaces the correct fork without looking at him;
- a sign forbids a song nobody stops singing;
- the bill total is correct.

## 12. Adult Content and Vice Boundaries

The game’s ceiling is adult comedy without nudity. Sinfonia can include sex workers if authored, hookups, alcohol, drugs as setting material, gambling, cheating, theft, surveillance, and social exploitation. The following preserve tone:

- sexual content uses implication, cutaway, costume, dialogue, and aftermath;
- no nudity or graphic sexual description;
- adult workers and hosts are professionals/people, not scenery or disposable punchlines;
- the bros seek debauchery and do not become prudish, frightened, or morally awakened;
- do not write instructional detail for obtaining illegal goods or evading enforcement;
- consequences can be harsh without turning the scene into a moral lesson;
- the joke never depends on a vulnerable person’s exploitation being invisible or consequence-free;
- no encounter contradicts the Herp routing rules.

## 13. Visual and Audio Language

### Visual palette

- gold, ivory, velvet red, dark wood, tarnished brass, black formalwear;
- baroque massing contrasted with cables, scanners, LED spill, phone screens, receipt printers, and club lighting;
- pristine public fronts with busy service corridors;
- framed certificates, engraved plaques, ropes, masks, silver chips, crown motifs, musical notation, and itemized paper.

Avoid making every interior a red-and-gold palace. Café daylight, shop neutrals, backstage utility, crypt stone, city-park green, and fluorescent morning aftermath keep the city legible.

### Audio palette

- pompous waltzes that can absorb a bass drop without becoming a different city;
- rehearsal scales, applause, carriage noise, cups and saucers, quartet music, muffled club bass, receipt printers;
- royal jingles as brief ambient fragments, never political exposition;
- strategic silence when a room has collectively noticed a bro.

## 14. Continuity and System Rules

### Open-city structure

The intended city order is Publandia → Debaucheryville → Sinfonia → Shamsterdam, but all cities remain open. Write optional scenes so that they work on first arrival, a late return, or a post-game visit. Do not assume a player completed another Sinfonia sidequest unless a clear prerequisite explicitly says so.

### Herp state

Every relevant sexual or bodily-intimacy encounter supports:

1. no Herp;
2. active Herp;
3. suppressed Herp while the Gold Circle Coin is equipped by any bro.

Suppression is not a cure. No temporary suppressant exists elsewhere unless the author adds one.

### Reputation

Sinfonia reputation is best treated as a sophistication spectrum, not a morality score. It changes reception, confidence, access, and prices. It does not make the bros wiser.

### Economy

- Use SOVS in all player-facing material.
- Prices and debts persist when their source quest says they persist.
- Prefer a single source-of-truth block for any number reused across multiple files.
- Expensive does not automatically mean fraudulent; cheap does not automatically mean honest.

### Optionality

All sidequests remain optional. Sinfonia’s culture should be discoverable through overlapping clues rather than one mandatory tour.

## 15. Material That Must Not Be Reintroduced

- The Holy Keg plot or any replacement compulsory Sinfonia main plot.
- A required city boss/finale in Sinfonia.
- Mandatory Rival Trio showdowns by city.
- A narrator, conscience character, or truth-teller who explains the satire.
- Player-facing real-world city, country, dynasty, language, or nationality names.
- Gold, dollars, euros, or other currencies where SOVS belongs.
- Bros who become afraid of nightlife, disgusted by vice, scam-aware, or permanently wiser.
- Sex scenes with nudity or graphic detail.
- A universal Sinfonian conspiracy connecting every casino, salon, café, club, and auction.
- Klaus written as merely delusional, pitiable, or harmless.
- The Baroness casually unmasked and explained.
- The royal jingles turned into protest anthems or political lore dumps.
- The Gilded Suite written as victim-centered survival horror.
- Old Maestro/city-finale material treated as current simply because it remains in a legacy document.
- The contaminated Debaucheryville block from the old `sinfonia.md`.
- New Golden Cutlet floors, bosses, vault rosters, or trial details invented before the author decides them.

## 16. Available but Non-Canonical Expansion Palette

Real-world research can suggest additional textures for Sinfonia, but research is not automatically game canon. Any future addition must first be selected and transformed by the author, then renamed and fitted to Sinfonia’s rules.

Potentially compatible seams include:

- regulated adult entertainment operating beside informal or illegal markets;
- old nightlife districts whose reputations outlive their actual venue mix;
- cellar bars, late-night music, private clubs, and venues selling “secret” access;
- legal gambling beside unauthorized machines or back-room games;
- strong drinking culture, late-night food, and public-transit aftermath;
- stimulant, party-drug, and harm-reduction contexts presented through character and consequence rather than procurement detail;
- art-market laundering, forged provenance, or auction manipulation at a bounded venue scale;
- ticket resale, pickpocketing, fake petitions, inflated currency exchange, and other tourist-facing extraction;
- political nostalgia, costume imperialism, and purchasable aristocratic fantasy;
- wellness, spa, sauna, or “gentlemen’s club” spaces where discretion itself is the premium product;
- subcultures that resist the city’s official self-image rather than merely mirroring it.

## 17. Quick Consistency Test

Before approving a new Sinfonia scene, ask:

1. What appetite is being sold?
2. What ceremony makes it feel elevated?
3. Who is the competent professional in the room?
4. What threshold or rule is visible before the bros commit?
5. Why do the bros want this even after seeing the price?
6. What exact, persistent consequence follows?
7. Can the scene end without anyone explaining the joke?
8. Do the bros remain eager, vain, and uneducated by the outcome?
9. Are SOVS, open-city continuity, and Herp state handled correctly?
10. Does the scene add a distinct vice or angle rather than duplicating an existing venue?

If the scene can be moved unchanged to another city, it probably is not Sinfonian enough.
