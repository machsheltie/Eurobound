## 0. Editor's charge: Producer's Circle versus Legends Wall

**Current preference: Producer's Circle as the sole paid prestige mechanism; Legends Wall as free environmental satire. But the Circle is not as load-bearing as Aion claims, and my audition overwrites its punchline.**

Only two payoffs genuinely require the Circle:

1. The pre-show announcement, Chadwick half-rising, and **"Only one name was supplied."**
2. Max's conditional opener, **"You heard your name and remained seated."**

Pilsner signing later interactions **Beer Lord** and treating the title as office flows from required event 4, not from the purchase. GLM's flag-conditional critic drop is not a reason to preserve the Circle; that drop should be rejected on its own merits below. Aion's "four jokes die" argument therefore overstates the dependency.

My own Circle scene also explains the 48-SOVS acknowledgment **three times**: Bradley calculates it before purchase, the receipt itemizes it afterward, and Pilsner then says "Forty-eight SOVS..." aloud. That is joke explanation, not escalation. Keep Bradley's pre-purchase arithmetic so the player enters the transaction with open eyes; keep the receipt as optional inventory flavor; cut Pilsner's explanatory replay. The receipt should not be asked to carry the whole scene.

The stronger character payoff is available for free: Pilsner pays to hear **Lord Pilsner** announced, then instantly prizes Max's unsolicited, faintly insulting **Beer Lord** more than the acknowledgment he bought. He does not recognize that the free title is the more devastating read. That contrast should be the central vanity joke, not the receipt by itself.

The **Legends Wall** is stronger as scenery than as a transaction. Its existing proposed material — "THE AMERICANS — RETURNING CHAMPIONS, ANNUAL SINCE 1994" — makes the honor look routine without another menu or another 120+ SOVS. Buying an inscription after Max's encounter would repeat the Circle's mechanism after the dramatic climax and slow the exit. No paid wall inscription. No automatic inclusion of both systems.

## 2. Three weaknesses to fix in the emerging proposals

### 1. Aion's restraint-branch "genuine win" is factually broken as drafted

The example has Pilsner correct Max about **Shark Tale** and **Pearl Harbor** as summer-2000 culture. *Shark Tale* is 2004; *Pearl Harbor* is 2001. The entire proposal depends on Pilsner being unexpectedly, verifiably right. If his correction is wrong, the scene becomes ordinary ignorant bluster and Max's delight makes no sense.

Keep **"That was the correct amount of useless."** Replace the trivia with an airtight period artifact. One possible direction: Max asks about the Baha Men's forgotten follow-up to "Who Let the Dogs Out"; Pilsner immediately names **"You All Dat" (2001)** because he owned the CD single and played the wrong track for six months. The exact artifact can be selected in drafting, but its date and answer must be checked. A genuine win cannot rely on the writers misremembering the era as badly as Pilsner does.

### 2. GLM's Producer's Circle → `retracted_review` drop makes purchased vanity genuinely powerful

A critic softening or retracting a review because Pilsner's name was announced as a paying patron gives the Circle real social authority. That contradicts the intended joke — the acknowledgment confers no status — and makes the critic corrupt without a character setup. It also adds a conditional drop-table dependency for a joke that is weaker than the static enemy material.

If a review ever changes, it should change because the critic witnessed a specific player-caused public fiasco, and it should become **more** eager to print, not more respectful. The cleaner current-engine solution is a fixed, resolvable drop and no review telemetry.

### 3. Aion's wall-based CHADWIK rescue turns technological stupidity into generic stupidity

Chadwick handwriting his own name incorrectly on a wall is not rooted in his character. It makes him randomly unable to spell **Chadwick**, and it adds minor vandalism plus a permanent venue alteration to preserve one typo.

Move the typo into the ruled vintage technology: the camcorder's primitive title generator allows seven characters in its main credit field. **CHADWICK** has eight. The title card emerges **CHADWIK**. He notices he can retype it and chooses not to: **"Enigma sells."** Same joke, but now the machine, his impatience, and his brand delusion cause it. No wall damage and no new venue state.

## 3. Original proposal — "The Chain Decides the Frame"

The q1 ruling says the camcorder's chain should contribute to the physical comedy. Use it to determine what the dangerous footage actually is.

During the fixed post-show exchange, after **"Do I?"**, Chadwick asks Max to say his name into the lens. He yanks the camcorder up; its attached chain snaps taut around the chair/his belt hardware and wrenches the camera down to belt height. The live dialogue remains unchanged and unobscured. The tape accidentally records:

- the playbill held rigidly at Bradley's waist;
- the lower edge of Max's formalwear and their voice, perfectly clear;
- the full name-denial exchange and Max leaning toward the lens to say **"Tip your server"**;
- Chadwick's own off-camera voice, louder and needier than he remembers.

The image is accidentally dynamic and the audio is unmistakable. Chadwick reviews a crotch-height, chain-jerked shot and calls it **"Dogme 95. No tripod. No rules."** He then gives it the seven-character **CHADWIK** title card.

This gives the post-versus-vault decision a real object. Posting does not merely expose Bradley; it publishes Chadwick begging for his own name and being used as a public-service-announcement delivery surface. Vaulting protects both of them while Chadwick describes the suppressed tape as lost cinema. The clip is good enough to be dangerous without requiring modern livestreaming or instant upload.

It also creates useful continuity: the drink branch can append Bradley's drunken "technical clarification," while a Reset branch can append one piece of physical slapstick. Thus D3 changes what D4 would publish rather than merely changing a line before an identical exit.
