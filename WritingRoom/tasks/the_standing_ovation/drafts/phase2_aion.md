## 0. MODERATOR'S CHARGE FIRST: PRODUCER'S CIRCLE vs. LEGENDS WALL

**Verdict: Producer's Circle survives as the quest's single vanity purchase. Legends Wall is demoted to free environmental satire. The wall inscription as a purchasable dies.**

Grounds:

1. **The receipt is the joke; the inscription is the same joke at a worse price.** Kimi's itemized receipt ("Public acknowledgment — 48 SOVS") exposes Pilsner, not the venue — it's the only fee structure that satisfies editor note 9 (venue not a villain, satire aimed at bro self-delusion). The Legends Wall inscription (120 SOVS + 22/letter) is the identical "paying for honor" beat, post-climax, at double the price, and it re-opens the stacked-spend problem this room spent round 1 killing.
2. **The Producer's Circle generates free downstream material.** Dramatisch's pre-show announcement; "Only one name was supplied" (Chadwick's best humiliation beat in any draft — see strength 2); Max's "You heard your name and remained seated" callback; Pilsner signing subsequent venue interactions "Beer Lord" with zero conferred authority; GLM's flag-conditional critic drop. The wall generates a typo. Kill the Circle and four jokes die with it; kill the wall purchase and one joke (the typo) needs a new home — see below.
3. **The Legends Wall's best material is already free.** GLM's pre-existing entry — "THE AMERICANS — RETURNING CHAMPIONS, ANNUAL SINCE 1994" — does more work than any purchase: it proves the bros' singular honor is an annual routine the venue has been running since before the bros peaked. That's the BEER LORD HALL beat I defended in round 1, arriving pre-installed and at zero SOVS. My corkboard can die satisfied.
4. **Cost curve:** 72 is reachable; 120+22 stacks toward the ~388 problem.

**Condition of survival:** the refusal branch stays fully written. Kimi's "wing position" / "tactical sightlines" rationalization is the best free-path comedy in the room and the reason this fork is a genuine decision and not a tollbooth.

**The CHADWIK typo survives the wall-purchase death — relocated, free:** when nobody is looking, Chadwick inscribes his own name on the Legends Wall with the house pen. He misspells it. He notices. He leaves it ("Enigma sells"). No fee, no staff involvement, no transaction — just a man committing minor vandalism against a monument to himself and getting his own name wrong. Dramatisch eventually frames it. Zero SOVS; the typo keeps its full life and the venue keeps its honesty. (PROPOSED, obviously.)

## 2. THREE WEAKNESSES TO FIX

1. **GLM's 150-SOVS napkin — even Chadwick-initiated — now fails editor note 5.** The ruling limits us to one substantial vanity purchase and calls the PRESS credential "an embarrassing, trivial acknowledgment" with no expensive transaction attached. GLM's inversion (Chadwick demands the fee; Dramatisch, who cannot lie, tries twice — "It is a NAPKIN, sir" — then pockets an idiot's money on request) was the right fix for the shakedown problem, but it's still a second priced vanity beat in a one-priced-vanity quest. **Fix:** the stamp is free. Chadwick asks where press check-in is; Dramatisch stamps a cocktail napkin "PRESS" at no charge because the request delights him; Chadwick treasures it like a credential and will later laminate it. Same treasured-object comedy, zero SOVS, and the venue's honesty stays intact. "Every fee enters through bro vanity" becomes "no fee enters at all except the Circle."
2. **Every draft's D4 assumes instant posting. q1 killed that.** The ruling requires a plausible transfer/upload path, no wireless uploads, vintage media. All four drafts have Chadwick's clip going from tape to internet inside the scene. **Fix — and it's an upgrade:** the upload is a *delayed fuse*. Chadwick hands the tape to a period-plausible pipeline the next morning (an Opera District cyber café — a Y2K institution he would trust absolutely — or the hotel's "business center," which is a printer and a fax machine). He announces the post that night ("Dropping Friday. Building anticipation is a strategy."), and the actual consequence — 63 views, "is the jersey guy ok," Brenda's mom — lands a day or two later as a callback. The gap between his announcement and the upload is the joke: he's promoting content that does not exist yet. `footage_posted` becomes a delayed flag; flag as PROPOSED.
3. **The restraint branch is underpowered in every draft.** Editor note 6: a free choice should not feel like the boring route. Kimi's water branch gets four seconds of silence and "This is worse" — good, but thin as a *payoff* for the player who held the bros back. GLM's restraint option just relocates the "puka shells" line backstage. **Fix:** see my original proposal — restraint's payoff is Pilsner's one genuine win.

## 3. ONE ORIGINAL PROPOSAL — "THE ONE HE ACTUALLY KNEW"

Nobody in any draft gives the bros a real victory — the room's cut list has quietly eliminated every competence beat, and the brief explicitly wants "occasionally genuine successes that the bros exaggerate beyond recognition." This slots into the restraint branch of the q5 crowd-work decision:

During the wind-down, Max runs a non-interactive bit with the room — the revue's premise is excavating the year 2000, so Max throws a deep-cut reference at the audience expecting silence ("In the summer of 2000, America's number one movie was about a shark who wanted to be a lawyer. Who funded this? Anyone?"). The young room is silent. Pilsner answers instantly, correctly, with context nobody requested ("Shark Tale was a fall release, but the *summer* was all *Pearl Harbor* — we were there, it was a cultural moment —"). Max — genuinely, briefly delighted, two seconds, no more: **"That was the correct amount of useless."** A free pour, or nothing at all; the win is the moment.

Pilsner retells it as a consulting relationship by the time they reach the street ("They're going to want me for the writing process. That's how The Producers starts").

What this does: makes restraint the funny route (editor note 6); gives the player who declines the crowd-work fork a distinct reward instead of skipped content (q5); costs zero SOVS; doesn't touch the locked eleven events; and gives the player the one thing no draft has — a moment where the bro is *actually* good at the thing he claims to be good at, immediately overinterpreted into a career. That's the delusion engine running on its cleanest fuel.

## 4. ONE IDEA TO CUT

**Cut the "drink sent to Max" from the drink-through branch (Kimi audition §6B, 36 SOVS).** Three reasons:

1. **It reroutes the branch's comedy into pursuit.** The branch's joke is Bradley out-drinking an erection ("Alcohol is a depressant. It suppresses vascular dilation. It's medical.") — self-inflicted gym science, my lane, the right joke. Adding Max's raised glass in thanks ("Drink accepted. That's basically a date.") bends the beat back toward Bradley-as-pursuer, which d1 and the dossier both warn against.
2. **It re-opens Max's attention after event 11 locks it shut.** "Max moves on with their evening" is a fixed beat; a thank-you toast from across the room is a small violation of the sequence's spirit — Max has already assigned the bros to each other and left.
3. **The delusion survives without it.** Bradley can misread the *bartender's* professionalism ("He poured it in front of me. Personally.") for the same "European dating" effect at zero interaction cost.

Branch cost drops 36 → 24, which converges Kimi's and GLM's drafts on the same number.

Works in every D4 branch, costs zero SOVS, and keeps the room's single best piece of Chadwick physical comedy alive under the new ruling.
