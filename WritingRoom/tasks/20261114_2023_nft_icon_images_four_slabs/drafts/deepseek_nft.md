## 1. Napster (Mint #01) — three image options

Canon fixes the artifact's name and its owning route (Shadow Exchange ledger backroom); it does not fix the image, so all three below are proposals for the author to pick from. All three are Napster-era (1999–2001), all three are American, none is location-derived, and all three are a still image of something the bros would call a mint condition original.

### Option A — **"The Cat At Four Percent"** *(my top pick)*
A 640×480 screenshot of the Napster client window, taken whole, with the Windows 98 taskbar still along the bottom. The cat-with-headphones mark sits large in the upper two-thirds of the window; under it a single file is downloading: one green bar filled about a twentieth of the way, a speed readout, an ETA in minutes that is not decreasing. The mouse arrow is parked mid-window, mid-click, where the screenshot was grabbed.
**Why:** the strongest silhouette in the set. A cat face with headphones reads at 32 px, and it is the one Napster image a woman who was there recognizes instantly and a woman who wasn't can still parse as "something ancient and internet." The joke sits in the framing: the bros bought a screenshot of a logo they could have pressed Print Screen on, and the screenshot has a taskbar on it.
**Format crime:** a screen grab, not a file. The Windows taskbar and Start button are cropped in at the bottom, the cursor is baked into the image forever, and the whole thing is JPEG'd hard enough that the window chrome has a dirty halo.

### Option B — **"The Cake"**
A 50-pack CD-R spindle on beige shag carpet, lid off and set aside, one disc lifted clear of the stack and held flat so the flash hits it and throws the rainbow. The stack reads as a solid block of silver; the lifted disc reads as a bright ring. Sharpie numbers on the lifted disc's face.
**Why:** the best *object* read — it looks like bullion at thumbnail size, which is exactly what the bros think it is.
**Format crime:** the flash blows out the disc's centre hole to pure white and the whole photo has the flat orange cast of a single-use camera indoors at night.

### Option C — **"The Burn"**
A beige tower's slot-load CD-R drive, disc half-ejected and stopped there, the blank face out and a Sharpie title half-written across it, the writing stopping mid-word where the pen ran out. A coiled IDE cable and a tangle of phone line in the background.
**Why:** the most textural and the least logo-dependent; it's about the act, not the brand.
**Format crime:** the disc is caught mid-eject so the composition is off-balance and the top third of the frame is a featureless beige wall.

**Top pick: A.** It is the only one of the three where the image is *the brand doing the thing the brand did*, which is the whole reason the bros think it's a blue chip.

## 2. Meme Lord's First Selfie (Card 4) — two photo options

Canon (`legacy_vault_cards.md:136-150`) fixes the subject to "clearly a stolen MySpace photo," "shot from above, on a bedroom ceiling light," at the angle that has since been reproduced nine hundred million times. It does not fix whose photo it is. Both options are an **invented, anonymous person with no identifiable face** — no real person, no real face, no likeness of anyone living or dead. The face is the one thing neither option shows.

### Option A — **"Flash Blind"** *(my top pick)*
A 320×240 webcam-era frame. The camera is held at arm's length overhead and the flash fires on the way up, so the face is a white oval with no features in it at all — just a blowout. The bedroom ceiling light sits directly behind and above the head as a bright bulb with a bloom ring, exactly the angle the canon describes. Straightened dark hair fans out across the bedspread below. A corner of a beige CRT is lit blue at the frame edge.
**Why:** the face isn't hidden by a trick, it's destroyed by the light — so no face is shown and nobody is being mocked. The bright bulb behind the head is the single element that makes it *the* angle, and it survives at 128 px as a glowing ring. It is also the funniest: the most-copied photograph in human history is unusable.

### Option B — **"The Curtain"**
Same overhead angle, flash off, one desk lamp. A curtain of straightened hair falls over the whole face from the forehead down, one eye and a sliver of nose visible between the strands, one arm out of frame holding the camera. Fisheye distortion from a cheap clip-on lens bows the doorframe.
**Why:** more recognizably a *chosen* composition — she posed for this, which is truer to what people actually posted.
**Format crime:** heavy fisheye, and the strap of the camera bag intrudes at the top corner.

**Top pick: A.** No face, no likeness, and the ceiling light — the canon's only fixed detail — is the brightest thing in the picture.
