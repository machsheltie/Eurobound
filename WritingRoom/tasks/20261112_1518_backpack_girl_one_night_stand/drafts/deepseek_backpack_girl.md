## 1. THE APPROACH, THE OFFER, AND WHO GOES

*Absinthe Arcade. She is on the floor, back against a cabinet, half a kebab in her hand, ukulele across her knees. One string is visibly wrong.*

**Backpack Girl:**
> "Oh — hey. Hey. You want the rest of this? I'm serious, take it. I've eaten. Sit down, you look like a story."

*They sit. The notebook is out before anyone has said a name.*

**Backpack Girl** *(writing, not looking up)*:
> "Okay. Don't say the thing you say when you meet people. Say the thing you'd say if you were already bored of me."

**Lord Pilsner:**
> "I'm divorced."

*A beat. She writes faster.*

**Backpack Girl:**
> "That's not what I — okay. No, that's good. That's a *hook*."

*She closes the notebook. She looks at all three of them the way a person looks at a shelf.*

**Backpack Girl:**
> "Right, so — logistics. There's floor at my place. There's *always* floor at my place, that's kind of my whole practice. And there's my room. One bed."

**Backpack Girl** *(no shame in it at all, because shame has never once occurred to her)*:
> "One of you. The other two get the floor and the common room and the book. I'm saying it plainly because being weird about it wastes a night and I've had a lot of nights."

**Chadwick:**
> "The book?"

**Backpack Girl:**
> "The roommates run a book on how long guests stay. You're all in it. Everybody's in it. It's not personal, it's *data*."

*All three of them step forward at the same time. It is the most competitive any of them have been all trip. Nobody wins, because nobody is competing with anything.*

**Backpack Girl:**
> "I genuinely don't mind which one. You're all the same amount of interesting."

## 2. MORNING-AFTER OPTIONS

Both options pay the same canonical payload: **−10 Bravado** and the **Ballad of the Bros** collectible. No new stat numbers, no new statuses, no new items. The two bros who slept on the floor are in the room for both, because they are the audience.

---

### OPTION A — "THE BOOK TAKES THE RING"
**Which bro:** Lord Pilsner
**Result used:** **stolen from** — routed to the roommates' betting book, not to her. She is asleep in the other room for the whole of it and never steals anything.

**Why him:** Pilsner is on this trip to celebrate a divorce he is nowhere near finished with. He is also the only bro who would stake something on his own legend and lose it on a technicality. The ring is the one thing he brought that he cannot make a story out of, and the roommates take it without ever raising their voices.

**The night, implied:** the door, the wrong string, the tuning. At 05:40 the door opens and he comes out, and she comes out *behind* him with the notebook and sits on the edge of the third mat because the bed is now covered in pages. Nobody in the room says anything about it. He goes back in at six. He is back on the mat by 06:50.

**The morning — 07:40. Hostel common room.**

*Pilsner is on a third mat, which he did not have at 02:40. His hair is up on one side. He is awake and he is not moving, because moving would mean admitting where he is.*

*She is in the doorway of her room with the notebook, mid-line, not looking up. Behind her the bed is a snowdrift of paper.*

**Backpack Girl** *(not looking up)*:
> "Don't go anywhere. I'm at the bit where you're tall."

**Lord Pilsner** *(to the bros, instantly, quietly, thrilled beyond speech)*:
> "I'm in the song."

**Chadwick:**
> "You're on the *floor*."

**Lord Pilsner:**
> "I gave her the bed. She needed the bed. It's a *writing* bed."

*Roommate 2 comes out of the kitchenette drying a mug. There is a ring on his pinky finger. It is very obviously not his ring and it is very obviously too small.*

**Roommate 2** *(holding up the hand, to nobody)*:
> "It's tight. Is it supposed to be tight?"

**Lord Pilsner:**
> "That's a wedding ring."

**Roommate 2:**
> "Yeah. You bet it."

*Pause.*

**Lord Pilsner:**
> "I bet it *what*?"

**Roommate 1** *(opening the book, reading it back flatly)*:
> "'Nobody hears it before me.' You bet the ring that she'd play it for you first. You went in at one. She played it at six."

**Roommate 1:**
> "In this room. To us. And the vending machine."

**Roommate 2:**
> "I've run this book nine times. It's never been a ring."

**Lord Pilsner** *(to the bros, with total conviction)*:
> "I was asleep for the first movement. That's how you're *supposed* to hear it. You hear it after."

*Roommate 2 holds the ring out, palm up, offering it back. Pilsner looks at it for a second.*

**Lord Pilsner:**
> "Keep it. It's a better story on your hand."

**Roommate 2:**
> "...It's a *wedding* ring."

**Lord Pilsner:**
> "Yeah. Not mine anymore."

*She looks up from the notebook for the first time.*

**Backpack Girl** *(delighted, appalled, sincere)*:
> "Oh, you *lost* it. That's — okay, that's not a verse, that's a *bridge*. Give me twenty minutes and don't leave the building."

*She tears a page out, crosses the room, and puts it in his hand. It is the ballad. Verse two is the aura. Verse three is him. Verse three has the divorce story in it four times, because he told it four times.*

**Backpack Girl** *(hand on his shoulder, meaning every word)*:
> "It's called *The Ballad of the Bros*. Nine o'clock, this room, there's a vending machine, and it's going to be beautiful. Bring the other two. They're the chorus."

**Backpack Girl** *(already back at the notebook)*:
> "You don't have to sing. You just have to stand there and be *accurate*."

**RESULT**
- **−10 Bravado** (no sleep, 05:40 ukulele, and a technicality)
- **Gain collectible: Ballad of the Bros**
- **The wedding ring is on Roommate 2's pinky.** No mechanical effect, no item entry, no SOVS. It is simply gone and nobody in the room is going to be the one to bring it up again.

**RATIONALISATIONS**

**Lord Pilsner:**
> "Bros. She kept the ring and she gave me the song. That's a *trade*. That's more than a trade. That's a signing bonus."

**Chadwick:**
> "You paid to be in a song. That's the Renaissance, bro."

**Bradley:**
> "You should get it back, man."

**Lord Pilsner:**
> "It's too small for him."

**Bradley:**
> "Yeah. Sausage fingers."

**Lord Pilsner:**
> "It's genetic."

### OPTION B — "SHE'S GONE, AND THE BOOK IS VOID"
**Which bro:** Chadwick
**Result used:** **she's gone.** She is not gone in anger and she is not ghosting. She is gone because the free breakfast across the square closes at 7:30 and she has priorities. Nothing is stolen; she genuinely intends to come back and says so in writing.

**Why him:** Chadwick's entire personality is being present at and documenting his own life. He got the whole night on a phone that cannot hold it, in a room that was empty of her for most of it, and she left while he was awake. He is the only bro who would consider a six-hour recording of a wall to be a *deliverable*.

**The night, implied:** the door closes at 01:10. The N-Gage comes out. He does not put it down. At some point it is pointed at the ceiling. At some point it is pointed at a wall. At some point it is on the floor, still recording, while the ukulele does something complicated behind the door.

**The morning — 08:20. Hostel common room.**

*Chadwick is asleep sitting up in the doorway of her room, phone in his lap, one arm still raised in the filming position. The recording timer reads 06:41:12.*

*The room behind him is empty. The bed is made — made properly, tightly, the way someone makes a bed they have made nine hundred times. On the pillow there is a page torn out of the notebook, and a note on the back of it.*

*The three roommates are up. The book is open. Nobody is happy, which is new.*

**Chadwick** *(waking, seeing the timer, hushed, reverent)*:
> "I got all of it."

**Roommate 1** *(closing the book)*:
> "You got a wall."

*He picks up the page. The ballad is on the front. The note is on the back, in biro, tilted, running off the edge.*

**Backpack Girl** *(the note, verbatim)*:
> "Breakfast across the square closes at 7:30 and it's the one that's open, not the real one. Back before two. Verse three is about your hands. Do NOT leave, the last line needs your face."

**Chadwick** *(reading it a second time, out loud, slowly)*:
> "'Verse three is about my hands.'"

**Bradley:**
> "What about your hands?"

**Chadwick:**
> "I don't know. I don't remember doing anything with them."

*He turns the page over. Verse three is about his hands. Verse three is about how he held the phone with both hands, at chest height, for six hours, and did not touch anything else in the room.*

**Roommate 2:**
> "Two days. I said two days."

**Roommate 1:**
> "I said one. He's got the face of a one."

**Roommate 1** *(closing the book and putting it down, with the finality of a man who has done the arithmetic twice)*:
> "Neither of us won. He's still here and she's not. The book's void."

**Roommate 3** *(small, gutted)*:
> "The book's never been void."

**Roommate 1:**
> "The book's void."

*Chadwick is not listening. Chadwick is reading verse three for the fourth time, and then a fifth, and then he laughs at one of the lines in a way that suggests he thinks it is about someone else.*

**Chadwick** *(looking up, wet-eyed)*:
> "Bros. She rhymed 'ceiling' with 'feeling.'"

**Bradley:**
> "That's not a rhyme."

**Chadwick:**
> "It's a *near* rhyme."

**Bradley:**
> "It's not near."

**RESULT**
- **−10 Bravado** (no sleep, no her, and a made bed)
- **Gain collectible: Ballad of the Bros** (the page from the pillow; verse three is about him and it is not flattering)
- **Nothing is stolen.** She took nothing of his. He tries to give Roommate 1 a tip on her behalf and is refused without anyone looking up.

**RATIONALISATIONS**

**Chadwick:**
> "Bros. She left the *material*. That's a handoff. That's an editor trusting you with the cut."

**Lord Pilsner:**
> "Notes mean she's coming back. Nobody writes a note to a one-night thing."

**Bradley:**
> "She said don't leave and then she left. That's not the same thing as being left."

**Chadwick** *(already walking out with the page, not looking back)*:
> "I'm going to get breakfast. It's not the real one, but it's the one that's open."

## 3. INTEGRATION FLAGS (not my section — for Kimi and Mistral)

I have not rewritten States 2 or 4. Two things in them break against this rewrite and need the smallest possible pass:

1. **State 2**, "The floor's a no, obviously. But thank you." — the offer is no longer floor-for-everyone, so "floor" reads wrong. Change one word: **"The room's a no, obviously. But thank you."**
2. **State 4**, "Floor's still a no. That was never about the rash." and "—no, okay, no, get in. GET IN." — same problem, plus "get in" implies shuffling sideways on the floor. Smallest fix: **"The room's still a no. That was never about the rash."** and **"—no, okay, no, come on. COME ON."**

Everything else in both states survives untouched, including the ninety-second composition, the eleven people and the vending machine, the euphemism ballad, and the post-cure reversal for the material.

**Third flag:** State 2 and State 4 both assume the evening's offer was available to all three bros at once. With the pick in item 1, an infected party reaches her *before* anyone is chosen, so the refusal fires on the group rather than on one bro. That works as written and needs no change — but the moderator should confirm the pick beat is skipped in State 2, not played and then refused.

## 4. RULES CHECK (self-audit)

- **She never steals, never scams, is never humiliated, and wins the book twice.** Option A's ring is lost by Pilsner, to a roommate, on a bet Pilsner made out loud. Option B's absence is a breakfast run with a note.
- **She is the one with agency in both mornings.** She is composing, she is performing, she is going to breakfast. Nobody's morning happens *to* her.
- **The ballad is about him, is not flattering, and she means every word.** Verse three in both options is a precise, tender, public humiliation delivered with total sincerity.
- **The bros are sincere doofuses.** No bro reads a situation correctly. Pilsner's best line is a rationalisation, not a joke. Chadwick thinks a wall is a deliverable. Bradley's sharpest observation is that a ring is too small.
- **The roommates carry every cutting line.** The roommates are unnamed, as canon requires.

- **No real place names.** No brands invented; the only hardware named is the N-Gage, which is Chadwick's canonical pocket.
- **Money:** no transaction occurs, so no price can be wrong.
- **All dialogue lines under 280 characters.**
- **No narrator, no explanation of any joke, no lesson at the end of either option.**
