## 1. THE APPROACH, THE KEBAB, THE OFFER — ONE BED, ONE BRO

*Absinthe Arcade. She is on the floor, back against a cabinet, half a kebab in her hand, ukulele across her knees. One string is wrong and she knows it.*

**Backpack Girl:**
> "Oh — hey. Hey. You want the rest of this? I'm serious, take it. I've eaten. Sit down, you look like a story."

*They sit. The notebook is out before anyone has finished saying a name.*

**Backpack Girl** *(writing, not looking up)*:
> "Don't say the thing you say when you meet people. Say the thing you'd say if you were already bored of me."

### The pitches — all three flop

**Lord Pilsner:**
> "I'm recently divorced."

**Backpack Girl** *(writing faster, delighted)*:
> "That's SO interesting. Okay. Keep going."

*Pilsner produces the divorce papers. They are laminated.*

**Lord Pilsner:**
> "I laminated them. So they last."

*She looks at the papers for exactly as long as it takes to find a rhyme for "laminate." She does not find one. She keeps writing.*

**Chadwick:**
> "We're kind of, like, cultural ambassadors? Unofficially."

**Backpack Girl**:
> "Say the word 'unofficially' again? I want the exact stress pattern."

**Chadwick:**
> "Unofficially."

**Backpack Girl**:
> "Perfect. That's the hook."

**Chadwick:**
> "I should probably mention my following. It's—"

**Backpack Girl** *(not looking up)*:
> "How many?"

**Chadwick:**
> "Four-oh-six."

**Backpack Girl** *(writing)*:
> "Mm. Okay. That's a number."

**Bradley** *(sincere, hands out)*:
> "I can carry you. I carried a keg once. It was mostly empty. Still counts."

**Backpack Girl** *(looks at his arms, then at the ukulele, then writes)*:
> "The ukulele weighs less. But noted. In the song."

*None of them reacted. The notebook never closed.*

### The offer — one bed, one bro

*She closes the notebook and looks at the three of them the way a person looks at a shelf of jars.*

**Backpack Girl:**
> "Right. Logistics, because I like to be transparent. There's my room at the hostel. One bed. And there's the common room, and there are mats, and there are the roommates, and the roommates run a book on how long guests stay. So. One of you gets the bed. Two of you get the mats and the book."

**Chadwick:**
> "The book?"

**Backpack Girl:**
> "The book. Everybody's in the book. It's not personal, it's data. I'm not going to make you pick in front of each other, that's rude. You pick among yourselves."

*All three stand up at once. It is the most competitive any of them have been all trip.*

**Backpack Girl:**
> "I genuinely don't care which. You're all the same amount of interesting. That's not an insult, that's just — okay, it's a little an insult, but kindly."

### The pick — per-bro variants (her reasons, never their pitches; any morning-after plugs in)

**If she takes Lord Pilsner** — for the book, not the papers:

**Backpack Girl:**
> "You. Roommate two has money on a two-day stay and nobody's ever made two days. You're the only one who offered paperwork-adjacent material."

**If she takes Chadwick** — for the hardware:

**Backpack Girl:**
> "You. You've got the phone with the little screen. Verse three needs a prop and you're already holding it."

**If she takes Bradley** — for the verse and the proximity:

**Backpack Girl:**
> "You. You're closest to the bed, and the bed's the good spot, and I'm not carrying anyone's bag up those stairs. Also you're easy to rhyme."

### She takes one and goes

*She stands, pack still on, and holds out a hand to the chosen bro. The other two stay seated against the cabinet.*

**Backpack Girl** *(to the two left behind, warm as a lamp)*:
> "Common room's through there. Mats in the cupboard. Don't move the mats, they've got a system. There's a vending machine. It's not a good vending machine, but it's ours."

**Backpack Girl** *(from the door, entirely sincere)*:
> "The roommates settle the book at seven. Try to be worth the odds."

*The door closes. Chadwick films it.*

**Chadwick:**
> "Bros. A door. Doors are green flags."

**Bradley:**
> "Three hours. Tops. I'm timing him."

### Cutaway — 02:40, hostel common room

*Two mats. Three roommates. One lamp. The book, open.*

*Through the wall: a ukulele being tuned. It takes a long time. Then one chord. Then the wrong string, held deliberately, far longer than a mistake needs to be held. Then quiet. Then, much later, a different quiet.*

**Roommate 1** *(not looking up)*:
> "Two days. I said two days."

**Roommate 2:**
> "I said one. He's got the face of a one."

**Roommate 3** *(writing)*:
> "I've got the common room before seven. And the vending machine."

*Bradley is doing push-ups on a mat. Chadwick is filming Bradley doing push-ups.*

**Chadwick:**
> "This is going to do numbers."

**Bradley** *(on the floor)*:
> "Forty-one."

### OPTION A — "THE SOUVENIR GLOWS" — Bradley sleeps with her, wakes up with Regret Rash

**Which bro:** **Bradley**
**Result used:** **an STD** — routed to **Regret Rash** (One-Night Stand Roulette), applied to Bradley only.

**Why him:** Bradley's entire self-concept is physical performance. A rash he cannot flex past, sung about in verse three by a woman who means every word, is the one souvenir his body actually files correctly.

**The night, implied:** the door closes at 01:50. The wrong string is re-tuned twice. Nobody in the common room sleeps; nobody in the room sleeps either, for different reasons.

**The morning — 06:10. Hostel common room.**

*Bradley is in the doorway of her room, still in his basketball jersey, one eye open. She is in the common room already, cross-legged by the vending machine, strumming, notebook open. Two roommates have the book. The third is making breakfast and has not looked up.*

**Backpack Girl** *(strumming, delighted, not looking up)*:
> "Okay, okay, I got it at four-thirty — it's called *The Ballad of the Bros* and the second verse is about the way your aura screamed 'tourist.' Not in a mean way! In a documentary way! Sit down, I only need eleven minutes."

*Bradley sits down on the floor. His neck is red. It is not shaped like anything a mouth makes.*

**Roommate 2** *(not quietly, to Roommate 1)*:
> "Two days. I said two days."

**Roommate 1:**
> "I said one. He's got the face of a one."

**Roommate 3** *(from the kitchenette, not looking up)*:
> "Dude. Your neck."

**Bradley:**
> "Hickey."

**Roommate 3:**
> "It's not shaped like a hickey."

**Bradley** *(touching his neck, sincere)*:
> "It's a rash. From the European air. The air is different here, it's more... authentic."

*She looks up from the notebook for the first time and sees his neck. She does not flinch. She writes it down.*

**Backpack Girl** *(warm, appalled, delighted — all three at once)*:
> "Oh. Oh, that's — hold on. That's a bridge. Don't move, I'm getting the third verse."

*She writes for ninety seconds. Then she tears the page out and hands it to him. It is the ballad. Verse two is the aura. Verse three is about him and about last night, and it does not name anything, and she means every word of it.*

**Backpack Girl** *(hand on his shoulder, meaning it)*:
> "Nine o'clock, this room, the vending machine, and it's going to be beautiful. You're the third verse. You don't have to sing. You just have to stand there and be accurate."

**RESULT**
- **−10 Bravado** (no sleep, 04:30 ukulele, and a souvenir)
- **Gain collectible: Ballad of the Bros**
- **Status applied to Bradley: Regret Rash** (existing One-Night Stand Roulette status — −Luck for 3 battles, shame dialogue overlays). Not `the_herp`. No new disease.

**RATIONALISATIONS**

**Bradley:**
> "Bros. It's a European souvenir. Like a passport stamp, but on me."

**Chadwick:**
> "Free souvenir, bro. Europe's generous."

**Lord Pilsner:**
> "FlagMan's system doesn't have a flag for this. That's off the chart."

**The morning — 06:40. Hostel common room.**

*Pilsner is on a mat in the corner, sitting up slowly, one lip split, his polo untucked. She is at the vending machine, strumming, notebook open. Two roommates have the book. The third roommate — small, wearing a hoodie — is standing over Pilsner holding a set of keys.*

**Roommate 3** *(to the others)*:

*A man comes in from the hallway behind her — tall, unnamed, in a towel, the way a man dresses when he has somewhere to be and nowhere to explain. He looks at Pilsner. Pilsner looks at him. The man shrugs, once, the way a man shrugs when the shrug is the whole conversation.*

**The man** *(to the roommate, not to Pilsner, already at the door)*:
> "Tell her I'm not mad. Just disappointed. That's worse, right?"

*The door closes. The roommate goes back to the book.*

**Backpack Girl** *(still strumming, not stopping, delighted)*:
> "Okay so I got it at five — it's called *The Ballad of the Bros* and the second verse is about the way your aura screamed 'tourist.' Sit down, I only need eleven minutes, and you look like you need to sit down a lot."

*She writes. She looks up at Pilsner's face. She writes that down too.*

**Backpack Girl** *(tearing out the page, handing it to him, hand on his shoulder, meaning every word)*:
> "Verse three is about him. It's about last night. It's not flattering and it's not a mean way — it's a documentary way. Nine o'clock, this room, the vending machine. You're the chorus."

**Roommate 1** *(closing the book)*:
> "Two days. I said two days. Nobody's ever paid in blood before."

**Roommate 2:**
> "I said one. He's got the face of a one. And now the fist of one."

- **Gain collectible: Ballad of the Bros** (verse three is about him and the man in the towel; it is not flattering; she means every word)

**RATIONALISATIONS**

**Chadwick** *(lowering the phone)*:
> "Eleven seconds, bro. That's a clip."

**Bradley:**
> "Should we call anyone? Like, to tell them? This is huge."
