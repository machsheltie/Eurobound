# Synopsis Satire-Voice Flags

1. **“They’ve got a mission: Sleep in questionable places, drink in questionable quantities, and experiment with everything a substance loving bro wishes to indulge in.”** — “Questionable” twice is euphemistic hedging and the third clause is vague filler, the exact “vague, toothless” swap the voice doc bans when the bible’s bar is hyper-specific degradation like the champagne-rash cease-and-desist graffiti.
2. **“Join them on their journey across Europe as they lurch through the continents’ nightlife, seedy underbellies, and cultural hot spots.”** — “Join them on their journey” is a sincere travel-brochure invitation that recruits the player to root for the bros instead of laugh at them, propped up by a generic noun-list and the real-world “Europe” sitting right next to minted canon “Euromemeia.”
3. **“The more they win, the more ridiculous they get. The more they lose, the harder you laugh. And somehow… they might just pull it off.”** — The final sentence pivots into an underdog-success hook that invites rooting for the bros, undercutting the voice rule that the player remains a superior, amused observer of their failures.
4. **“Players explore pixel-art overworlds, interact with quirky NPCs, and battle over-the-top enemies in a front-view, turn-based combat system that parodies JRPG tropes.”** — The neutral, generic pitch-deck phrasing (“quirky NPCs,” “over-the-top enemies”) reads like a standard corporate store blurb rather than delivering actual satirical voice, biting specificity, or cynical comedy.
5. **“Combat features a rolling HP meter, ‘SMAAAASH!’ crits, and status effects like Hungover, Grease Fingers, and Embarrassed.”** — Listing mild, mundane status effects like “Hungover” and “Embarrassed” alongside EarthBound mechanics severely undershoots the game’s actual unhinged MA benchmark (such as “Blackout Drunk” or hyper-specific fashion-crime “Existential Crisis”).
6. **“In Debaucheryville, a Velvet Curtain Club boss who ‘upsells’ them into buying fake VIP wristbands.”** — “Upsells” is corporate sales-floor jargon, the exact “corporate/professional” register villains are banned from speaking in, and a wristband scam is a polite, low-stakes tourist anecdote instead of real menace played for laughs with a devastating, personal humiliation attached.
7. **“In Sinfonia, a baroque crime lord who manipulates them into performing embarrassing ‘courtly duties’ for status.”** — “Embarrassing” is told, not shown — the line announces a joke instead of making one, giving us a quoted “courtly duties” placeholder where the voice doc demands a hyper-specific, fully-formed degrading image the reader can picture.
8. **“In Publandia, a bar that’s a ‘fashion show’ for the local fashionistas, and a ‘gigolo’ who’s a ‘gigolo’ for the local gigolos.”** — The circular, repeated phrasing reads like an unfilled placeholder rather than a specific joke, so the example gestures at vice without a sharp target, vivid characterization, or a punchline.
