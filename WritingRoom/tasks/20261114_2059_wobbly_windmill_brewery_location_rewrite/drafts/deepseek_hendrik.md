## §2 Replacement: Core Concept

### What He's Actually Doing
Running a genuinely working 1680 mill as a genuinely working brewery and taking both halves with total seriousness, in a building that has been wrapped in a tourist machine since 2008 and is now loud enough to be heard from the train. The floor vibrates because the stone is actually turning. The room rotates because it is a windmill. The flight climbs from 5% to 11% in five glasses sold as a journey and delivered as a staircase. None of this is a trick. All of it is the reason nobody can walk straight by the second floor, and the reason there is a Survivor Pack display ninety centimetres from his till, which he has never once looked at.

He is the only person in the building who is not being processed by it. The turnstile has never counted him. He goes around it, through the staff gap, and has done since it was installed, because he was not asked.

### What Bros Believe
That they are having an exclusive craft experience in a historic windmill. Lord Pilsner calls it ICONIC on arrival and asks why it is moving forty seconds later. Chadwick considers the grinding mechanism CONTENT. Bradley, three beers in, says "actually, this is really good, the spinning adds… something," which is the closest anyone in the party gets to understanding what Hendrik is doing.

### The Comedy Gap
Hendrik is not selling disorientation. He is selling grain and beer, and the disorientation is a physical property of the machine he inherited, which he considers self-evident and stopped explaining in 2011. The gift shop sells motion-sickness pills at 3 SOVS, the dispenser on the second floor is refilled hourly and empty hourly, and Tessa does not find either fact funny any more. Hendrik has never joined the two facts up. Nothing here is fake, which is the trick: the cigarette-burned tablecloths, the zip-tied gear guard and the sheer volume of merch are not what he thinks he is running. He thinks he is running a mill that pays for itself.

### What He Has Never Once Looked At
The main sign, which is wider than his door, backlit, and flickers at two letters. The plywood cutout with three face holes. The laminated "WAIT FROM HERE: 45 MIN" planted two metres out, every day, for a party of three. The Survivor Pack. He walks under all of it four times a day at the same pace. When the flicker catches his eye he looks at it the way a man looks at weather.

### The Thing He Is Quietly Proud Of
There is a seagull at The Salty Swallow, on the other side of the city, named Hendrik. It was named after him because he once threw a herring at it and the bird caught it clean out of the air. Maarten at the herring stand has never corrected anyone. Hendrik is aware. Hendrik considers it an honour. He will not raise it, but if a player mentions the bird, it is the single warmest reaction available from him in the entire location — warmer than the post-boss respect, warmer than the Windmill Grist.

## §3 Replacement: Appearance

### Thumbnail read
A dead-vertical man in a grain-grey apron with a strip of tape across the chest, standing in the middle of a room where every other person is leaning.

### Physical Description
| Attribute | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| Age | Late 50s |
| Build | Barrel-chested. Grain-sack shoulders. Built by three generations of the same work, and it is holding. |
| Height | Tall enough to have hit every beam in this building at least once, and there are low beams |
| Energy | Reverent, immovable, alarmingly sincere |
| Silhouette | A square. In any room with a spinning element in it, he is the only vertical line that is not a beam. |

### Clothing
- **Apron, not costume.** Heavy canvas, worn since the 2008 conversion, and it is no longer any colour you could name — grain-gold dust has gone into the weave. Two pockets. The pockets are the modernisation.
- **Name tape.** A strip of gaffer tape across the chest, HENDRIK in marker, and this is the third strip. The first two are under it. Nobody at the brewery has ever suggested embroidery, because nobody at the brewery suggests things to him.
- **Under the apron**, the house polo, collar gone soft, with the 2008 logo on the chest: a windmill that does not look like this building and never did.
- **Beard that could filter beer.** Not a metaphor he would enjoy hearing. It is a working beard on a working man and it is enormous. Grain dust lives in it permanently, at every hour of the day, including nine in the evening.
- **Accessories.** A brass hydrometer in the apron pocket, drawn and read and returned constantly; a copy of his own self-published book, "Respect The Grain," in the other pocket, corners furred, which he will offer to sign before the player has asked for anything.
- **Hands.** One fingernail black and staying black. The right thumb knuckle is a knot. These are the hands that dressed the stone.
- **Footwear.** Heavy boots, cracked across both toes and re-laced with a bootlace that used to be something else.
- **Lanyard.** House staff lanyard with his ID photo from 2009, in which his hair is a different man's. He does not know this is visible.

### Distinguishing Features
- Absolutely stable on a rotating, vibrating floor. Everyone else grabs a rail. He does not. This is the location's best silent joke and it should be visible in every frame he shares with a tourist.
- Speaks about grain in the register other people use for family members
- Grain dust in the beard, permanently, at all times of day
- Points at the millstone rather than at people
- Steps around the turnstile. Every time. In his own building, in front of a queue.

## §4 Replacement: Personality

### Archetype
**The Sincere Zealot of Grain** — Hendrik is passionate beyond reason about a genuinely worthwhile thing. He is a third-generation miller who added brewing to the family trade and treats both as sacred inheritance. He is not pretentious in the craft-beer sense; he does not use tasting-note vocabulary and he does not sneer at anyone's palate. He is a man who believes barley deserves respect and is visibly disappointed by everyone who does not get it, which is everyone.

### Personality Traits
| Trait | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| Reverent past the point of comfort | The grain is family. He means this at full strength, four times a day, to groups of six. |
| Absolutely serious | Not one line is a joke. Every laugh in this location is at the gap between him and the room. |
| Physically unshakeable | Never grabs a rail. Never mentions the spinning unless asked. Steps around his own turnstile. |
| Commercially fluent, entirely unashamed | He knows every price and quotes it before you ask. The glasses are 18 SOVS and the beer is 6 SOVS and he will say both numbers in the same sentence, at the same volume, hearing nothing. |
| Structurally disappointed | In everyone. Not cruelly — the way a teacher is disappointed, and with the same total job security. |
| Genuinely generous | The lecture is free. The knowledge is real. The 5% gift shop discount for listening is a real discount, and he considers it a substantial gesture. |
| Suspicious of nothing | A phone camera is attention paid to the mill. A full queue is the grain being honoured. The Survivor Pack display is a shelf he has not read. |

### Internal Monologue
> "They arrive and they photograph the outside and they say the word 'authentic' four times before they have entered the building. And then they come up to the first floor and the millstone is turning — the actual stone, the one my grandfather dressed with a hammer in 1961 — and they say it is 'cool.' Cool. And then I speak for forty minutes about what the grain is doing and where it came from and by the end there are two of them still standing. Two. And of those two, one is from a big-city paper and writing everything down. That one, I would give the recipe to. But I cannot give the recipe to only one man with a notebook. So I give the speech to everybody and I wait."

### Motivation
Continuity. His grandfather ground grain in this mill, his father ground grain in this mill, and Hendrik grinds grain and brews beer in this mill. The tourism pays for the mill. The mill is the point. Everything else — the 25 SOVS ticket, the 30 SOVS T-shirts, the branded glasses at 18 SOVS when the beer is 6 SOVS, the eleven hats and one toupee in the blade-level bin — is the cost of keeping a 1680 windmill turning in 2026, and he would rather be doing it for four people who cared.

## §9 Replacement: Voice Direction

### Accent
▲ Countryside accent, lowland vowels, deep register. A reverent baritone with real weight behind it. Slower than city speech. He does not use filler and does not trail off. Every sentence is finished, and several of them are about barley.

### Tone
- **Welcome**: Ceremonial. He has said it four thousand times and he means it every time.
- **The Speech**: Escalating passion. Not shouting — the volume is constant and the intensity climbs, and it has to climb over a whooshing blade and a retail radio without ever acknowledging either. Forty minutes of this is the joke and two minutes of it must be enough to make players believe in the other thirty-eight.
- **Opener check**: Immovable. Not hostile. He is a door.
- **Approval**: Almost nothing. Two words at most. It is worth more because everything else is worth so many.
- **The seagull beat**: This is the exception. Warmer, slower, faintly delighted, and completely without irony. He is genuinely honoured by a bird.

### Key Delivery Notes
- Not one line is a joke and he must never be played as if it might be. He is the straight man to an entire building.
- "It is a windmill." — flat, after a long silence, with no exasperation. It is the answer to the question.
- "We are... proud? We are proud." — the hesitation is real. He worked out how he felt about the golem mid-sentence and stands by the conclusion.
- The seagull story must be delivered at half his normal pace. It is the only time he slows down.
- Never let him acknowledge that tourists are struggling. He does not perceive the struggling. He does not perceive the Survivor Pack, the motion-bag dispenser, the queue sign or the flickering letters either. That is the joke and it is load-bearing.
- When he says "Cool" — he does not say it. He only reports that they said it, and the contempt is in the quotation.
