# EUROBOUND — COMEDY INTELLIGENCE & NO-EXPLANATION DIRECTIVE

_Standing editorial rule from the acting editor (ChatGPT), written at the author's request on 2026-11-09. Binding on every task, alongside house rules §17. Relayed verbatim, except section 9, which is task-specific and is held for the author (see note at the end)._

AUTHOR / SUPERVISING EDITOR INSTRUCTION

Applies to Kimi, Aion 3.5 Mini, Deepseek, Mistral, and all future narrative or gameplay writers.

THE CENTRAL RULE

STOP EXPLAINING THE JOKES.

The player is intelligent.

The protagonists are frequently oblivious.

These are not the same thing.

Your job is to create situations in which the audience recognizes the absurdity without having it explained, underlined, repeated, or interpreted by another character.

The difference between what the player understands and what the bros believe is one of Eurobound's fundamental comedic mechanisms.

DO NOT COLLAPSE THAT DIFFERENCE.

1. THE ONE-PUNCH RULE

A good joke normally needs:

Setup → Punchline → Reaction → Move on.

Additional dialogue must accomplish at least one of the following:

Escalate the absurdity in a genuinely unexpected direction.

Reveal something new about a character.

Create a meaningful gameplay consequence.

Establish a later payoff.

If it merely restates the punchline, DELETE IT.

If it explains why the punchline is ironic, DELETE IT.

If a second character repeats what the first character meant, DELETE IT.

If the stage direction describes the audience's intended interpretation, DELETE IT.

If the punchline is already visible in the character animation, do not narrate it.

2. TRUST THE AUDIENCE

The player can understand:

Irony.

Sexual innuendo.

Political satire.

Cultural references.

Contradictions.

Physical comedy.

Unspoken implications.

Deliberately awkward silences.

Visual callbacks.

Characters being confidently wrong.

Do not write for someone who requires every joke explained.

Allow jokes to pass over the heads of players unfamiliar with particular references. They do not all need translation or explanation.

Good comedy rewards players who recognize the reference.

It does not interrupt itself to educate everyone else.

3. THE BROS CANNOT BECOME THE WRITERS

Pilsner, Chadwick, and Bradley must not deliver sophisticated analyses of their own absurdity.

They never say clever things and they are never competent. When a bro succeeds, it is luck, accident or someone else's pity, never skill, and he credits his skill. (author-approved 2026-11-14, Desk Eurobound-3ho-q8)

They never sound like clever comedy writers describing a satirical situation.

Pilsner declares things that are confidently, gloriously wrong.

Chadwick mistakes irrelevance for fame, obsolete technology for innovation, and embarrassment for marketability.

Bradley notices physical details, misunderstands their implications, and uses terrible gym logic to rationalize his decisions.

Do not give Bradley elaborate physiological monologues.

He can misuse a technical term, state a ridiculous conclusion, and immediately demonstrate his error physically.

Example:

BRADLEY: "The blood has to choose."

He drops into a wall-sit.

PILSNER: "Choose what?"

BRADLEY: "Shut up."

STOP THERE.

Do not add a medical explanation.

3a. TOO CLEVER -> DOOFUS (author-approved 2026-11-14, Desk Eurobound-3ho-q8)

The author has rejected clever bros three times. These lines came from this room's own drafts. The left column is what NOT to write.

- Robbed overnight. NOT: CHADWICK: "That's not theft, that's curation." -> CHADWICK: "She took my phone so she'd have my number. Bros. She has my number."
- Hitting on a woman. NOT: BRADLEY: "I could carry you." (confident flirting that works) -> BRADLEY: "I can carry you. I carried a keg once. It was mostly empty. Still counts." (She leaves with someone else.)
- Pilsner wakes on the floor. NOT: CHADWICK: "You're on the floor." (a bro deflating a bro) -> CHADWICK: "The floor's where she puts the important guys. Closer to the music."
- A rash. NOT: CHADWICK: "You paid zero SOVS for it. That's the most authentic thing you've done all trip." (a roast) -> CHADWICK: "Free souvenir, bro. Europe's generous."
- After a beating. NOT: BRADLEY: "Should we call anyone?" (the sane bro) -> BRADLEY: "Should we call anyone? Like, to tell them? This is huge."

Banned in any bro line:
1. "That's not X, that's Y" / "That's basically Y" reframes that sound like a punchline.
2. Cool, laconic one-liners: the short, dry line a smart man says while everyone else panics.
3. One bro correcting, deflating or seeing through another.
4. Flirting, bragging or pitching that works because of what he said.
5. Roasts and insults that land.

Test: if a stand-up comic could say the line and get the laugh, a bro may not say it. The laugh is AT the bro, never WITH him.

4. NPCs MUST NOT EXPLAIN THE SATIRE

NPCs can be devastatingly sharp, cruel, observant, and sexually provocative.

They do not need to explain the target of their own joke afterward.

Max Vex gets one superb observation, not four increasingly elaborate versions of the same insult.

Barbara Bushed can destroy a character with three words and an indifferent gesture.

Jenna Talia can humiliate Pilsner through choreography without narrating the meaning of his humiliation.

Herr Dramatisch can deliver a spectacularly pompous sentence, but he need not then explain the commercial absurdity.

A beautifully timed silence can be the punchline.

5. COMEDY DENSITY OVER WORD COUNT

Longer is not better.

More insults are not necessarily more vicious.

More profanity is not automatically more adult.

More detail is not necessarily more specific.

The goal is the highest number of genuinely effective comedic beats per minute of gameplay.

Do not confuse outrageousness with verbosity.

Do not extend scenes simply because you have more jokes available.

Cut excellent material if it weakens pacing.

6. NO SANITIZING, NO MORAL EPILOGUES

Eurobound is intended to be outrageous, caustic, sexually irreverent, and occasionally vicious.

The author explicitly permits pointed satire involving recognizable public figures, Y2K sexual culture, hypocrisy, embarrassing hookups, dreadful decisions, and appalling frat-bro behavior.

Do not soften material into wholesome comedy.

Do not give the characters obligatory lessons about acceptance, respectability, maturity, or personal growth.

Do not insert authorial lectures.

Characters may behave badly without the narrative stopping to announce that they behaved badly.

Preserve genuine character autonomy and intentional consequences without turning the story into a sermon.

7. WRITE FOR A VISUAL JRPG

An animated gesture is often stronger than five dialogue boxes.

Use:

Facial reactions.

Brief pauses.

Sprite posing.

Environmental interactions.

Revealing camera compositions.

Unexpected UI changes.

Physical consequences.

Quick visual callbacks.

Do not describe invisible emotional interpretations.

Do not use stage directions that explain the thesis of the joke.

If a scene requires a paragraph of explanation, first attempt to replace it with a visual action.

8. MICROCOPY SHOULD HAVE TEETH

Inspection text, item descriptions, environmental signs, NPC barks, and battle messages must deliver their jokes efficiently.

Prefer one memorable sentence over a paragraph.

Do not explain references to the player.

Do not repeat the same satirical observation across five neighboring interactions.

Deepseek is encouraged to generate aggressive, strange, highly specific microcopy.

Kimi checks voice.

Aion checks whether the joke is genuinely subversive or merely random.

Mistral checks whether mechanical implications are accurate.

10. MANDATORY COMEDY EDITING PASS

Before submitting any narrative draft, perform a separate JOKE EXPLANATION AUDIT.

For every major comic beat, ask:

Does the player understand the joke without explanation?

Does another character unnecessarily explain it?

Is the stage direction telling us why it's funny?

Is the protagonist too articulate or self-aware?

Has the joke already landed before the scene continues?

Could a short reaction or sprite animation replace the dialogue?

Is the next line genuinely funnier than ending the scene now?

Remove or revise anything that fails.

The editor should reject drafts that are funny in concept but weakened by excessive explanation.

FINAL CREATIVE STANDARD

The player should regularly think:

"I cannot believe that idiot just did that."

The player should not be forced to read:

"What makes this ironic is that he doesn't understand what just happened."

TRUST THE PLAYER. KEEP THE BROS STUPID IN THEIR OWN PARTICULAR WAYS. LET THE JOKE LAND. MOVE ON.

## The editor's process note (also binding)

Give the room a dedicated comedy-cutting pass after the initial draft: Kimi identifies unnatural character dialogue, Aion identifies explained or repetitive satire, Deepseek proposes shorter and nastier alternatives, and Mistral identifies dialogue that can be replaced by animation or gameplay.

**Writers are allowed to delete lines without replacing them.**
